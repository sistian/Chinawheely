Overview: Photography in Kunming

Kunming is built around a lake at 1,886 metres with a range of peaks along its western shore, and its photographs are mostly of those two shapes. There is also a third, older city of towers and stone that the modern skyline grew around: a bronze hall on a wooded hill, a restored market town, and a military airport from the war years that is now a memorial. The light is generous because of the altitude, the air is clear for most of the year, and the reason the city is called the City of Eternal Spring is the same reason the photographs are easy to take.

The Western Hills above the Lake (西山)

The Western Hills, formerly called Bijishan, are the group of peaks of Biyao, Huating, Taihua and Luohan in Xishan district, 15 kilometres from the city, running 35 kilometres along the western shore of Dianchi from Bijiguan in the north to Haikou in the south. The profile of the range looks like a reclining figure, which is why it is also called the Reclining Buddha Mountain or the Sleeping Beauty Mountain, and its highest point, Luohan Peak, reaches 2,511 metres. The forest is dense and the plant variety is unusually broad for the latitude, and the range is a national key scenic area rated 4A, with the Longmen cliff section, Huating Temple, Taihua Temple, the tomb of the composer Nie Er and a small stone forest among its features. The rock cut into the cliff at Longmen is the passage that gives the walk its reputation.

best light: Early morning from the ridge, when the peaks separate one behind another, or late afternoon when the haze stacks the layers. · mountain and cliff walk

Getting there: On the western shore of Dianchi, reached by the road up the range to the Longmen section, then by foot or cable car for the ridge.

  • The stacked ridgelines over the lake are the motif, and they build through the afternoon haze.
  • The cut path at Longmen is narrow, so a wide lens handles it better than a long one.
  • The tomb of Nie Er sits on the route and makes a natural break in the walk.

Dianchi and the Haigeng Dike (滇池与海埂大坝)

Dianchi, also called Kunming Lake, was known in antiquity as the Diannan Marsh and is the largest plateau freshwater lake in Yunnan and the sixth largest freshwater lake in China, standing at 1,886 metres with a stomach-shaped outline and a flat bottom. It belongs to the Yangtze system as the head of the Pupu river and takes 29 rivers, the largest of them the Panlong river, while the Haikou river, the Tanglangchuan, carries the water out. The basin covers 2,855 square kilometres across six districts and counties of the city. The hills close it in on every side, with Jinma Shan to the east, Bijishan to the west, Snake Mountain to the north and Crane Mountain to the south, and those ranges are what make it a basin. The silted Haigeng embankment divides the northern Caohai from the outer lake, which is 96.4 per cent of the total area, and since the Xiyuan tunnel was completed in 1996 the two halves no longer connect.

best light: Sunset over the water, with the Western Hills in silhouette on the far shore and the gulls in front of them. · lake and embankment

Getting there: The Haigeng embankment runs along the southern shore of the lake in the centre of the city, on the road between the old town and the Western Hills.

  • The long embankment gives a clean horizon with the range behind it, which is the frame for the lake.
  • Evening brings the gulls and the fishing boats; both need the low light.
  • The lake is large and shallow and the shore is reed, so the foreground needs a longer lens than the distance suggests.

Yunnan Nationalities Village (云南民族村)

The village stands on the shore of Dianchi 8 kilometres south of Kunming and covers about 1,300 mu. Twelve settlements are open, for the Bai, Dai, Yi, Wa, Naxi, Tibetan, Lahu, Jino, Blang, De'ang, Hani and Jingpo peoples, together with a Mosuo household, and they are laid out at different scales and orientations so that each reads as a place rather than an exhibit. Guides in minority dress explain the marriage, funeral and festival customs of each group, and the festivals are staged in the village as well, including the Bai March Fair, the Yi Torch Festival, the Dai Water Splashing Festival, the Jingpo Munao Festival and the Lisu Blade Pole Festival. It is the most reliable way to photograph the architecture of a dozen minority traditions in one afternoon.

best light: Late afternoon, when the rooflines catch the light and the village squares are in shadow. · open-air museum

Getting there: On the south shore of Dianchi, 8 kilometres from the centre, on the road towards the Haigeng embankment.

  • Each village has its own roof form and material, so a straight-on frame of the roofline is the way to compare them.
  • The staged festivals are the only place where the customs are visible rather than described.
  • The site is flat and open, and the ground is wide enough for a wide lens on every settlement.

Guandu Old Town (官渡古镇)

Guandu lies 10 kilometres south-east of the centre, on the north shore of Dianchi and the lower Baoxiang river, and is a national 4A site and one of the named characteristic scenic towns of the country. It is regarded as one of the birthplaces of Dian culture: the ground was a rise on the eastern shore of the lake called Wo Dong, with people settled there 3,500 years ago, and it became a ferry crossing when the Nanzhao king Fengjiayi set up the landing and officials and scholars passing through paused there to write. The town has more than a thousand years of history and keeps the local architectural fabric of old Kunming. A restoration and reconstruction ran from April 2001 to April 2003 at a cost of about 120 million yuan. The site is organised in named sets of halls and temples, eight views, five hills, six temples, seven pavilions, eight local cult temples, two palaces and two rivers.

best light: Early morning, when the market is running and the light is still low along the old streets. · restored market town

Getting there: In the south-east of the city between the lake and the Baoxiang river, with the morning market running from early.

  • The eight views and the seven pavilions are spread along the river, and the river is the axis of the town.
  • The market is the reason to come early, and it is at its best before the coach groups arrive.
  • The reconstruction is recent and regular, which reads best in a straight street view rather than in detail.

The Stone Forest (石林)

The Stone Forest, also called Lunan Stone Forest or Yunnan Stone Forest, is a karst formation in Shilin Yi Autonomous County, 120 kilometres from Kunming across an area of more than 400,000 mu. It was approved on 8 November 1982 as one of the first batch of national key scenic areas, under the legal name Lunan Stone Forest Scenic Area, and its protection regulations took effect in 1991. It became a national geopark in March 2001, one of the first UNESCO Global Geoparks in February 2004, and a World Natural Heritage site on 27 June 2007 as part of the South China Karst. The sections include the large and small stone forests, also called Liziqing, the Naigu stone forest, Zhiyun Cave, Long Lake and the Great Waterfall. It is the country of Ashima in Yi legend, and it was named in 1931 by the Yunnan governor Long Yun, whose calligraphy was destroyed during the Cultural Revolution and later rebuilt from characters collected on the Cuan Baozi stele.

best light: Early morning or the last hour of the day, when raking light separates the towers from each other. · karst landscape

Getting there: 120 kilometres east of Kunming by road or by the Kunming to Shilin train, with the site entered through the main gate and a circuit path through the karst.

  • Side light is what makes a karst read; the towers are separate forms at low sun and a wall of rock at midday.
  • The smaller stone forest is quieter and has the better viewing platform than the main circuit.
  • The path between the sections is long, so the internal transport is worth taking rather than walking the whole site.

The Bronze Hall of the True Warrior (太和宫金殿)

The Taihe Palace Bronze Hall stands on Jifeng Mountain in Kunming and is a national key cultural relic, and its main hall is dedicated to the True Warrior deity. It is the surviving metal temple that gives the city one of its defining images: a fully constructed bronze building in a forest on the mountain above the city, rather than a timber hall in a walled compound. The image matters because the whole complex was assembled elsewhere and brought up the mountain, and the fact that it survives at all is unusual for a structure of its kind.

best light: Late morning in the dry season, when the light comes through the trees onto the copper roofs. · temple in the forest

Getting there: On Jifeng Mountain in the northern part of the city, with the hall at the end of the tree-lined approach.

  • The approach through the cypress trees is the approach in every published image of the hall, and it is still the strongest frame.
  • The copper roof takes light differently from tile or stone, so expose for the roof and let the trees go dark.
  • The hall is small, and a long lens from the approach separates it from the trunks behind.

The Wujiaba Airfield and the Flying Tigers (昆明巫家坝机场旧址)

During the Second World War Kunming was a Chinese military centre and the headquarters of the American forces in China, Burma and India, and Wujiaba airport was the home of the First American Volunteer Group of the Republic of China Air Force, the unit known as the Flying Tigers. Kunming was also the terminus of the Burma Road, the supply route that ran from the Burma coast through Lashio to China, and the city was the last point on that road. The airfield is the place where that history physically happened, and the old runways and the surrounding streets are what remain of it.

best light: Any light; the subject is the ground and the buildings, not the sky. · wartime aviation site

Getting there: In the south-east of the city near the modern airport, on the old airfield area between the city and the lake.

  • The subject is the ground plane: the old runway alignments read from any high point in the district.
  • The old terminal and the surrounding blocks are ordinary urban fabric, which is exactly what makes the comparison with 1942 work.
  • Late light on the concrete aprons is what makes the runway lines visible.

Gear and planning for photographing Kunming

At 1,900 metres and just north of the Tropic of Cancer, Kunming gives you long light and clean air, and the kit can be simple. A wide lens covers the lake from the Haigeng embankment and the plateau horizon; a short telephoto covers the karst at the Stone Forest, where the towers are separated by side light rather than by distance; and a 70-200 handles the ridges from the Western Hills, where the layers stack in haze. A polarizer earns its keep on the lake and on the wet rock at the karst, and a graduated filter is more useful than a heavy ND because the sky over the plateau is bright and the foreground is not. Tripods are worth carrying for the dawn shots from the ridge.

Plan around altitude and around the lake, since those two facts decide most of the day. The city sits at 1,900 metres, so the first morning is slightly slow and the air is dry and thin, which flatters long lenses. The lake is the largest single feature and the light crosses it twice, once from the east over the city and once from the west behind the Western Hills. The Stone Forest and the villages of the Nationalities Village are the two trips that need a full day each, and everything else, the old town, the bronze hall, the lake dike and the airfield site, sits inside the ring road and can be taken in a single long afternoon.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the classic Kunming view?

The lake with the Western Hills behind it. Dianchi is the largest plateau freshwater lake in Yunnan and the sixth largest freshwater lake in China, at 1,886 metres, fed by 29 rivers and drained by the Haikou river, and the hills close it in on every side with Jinma Shan to the east and Bijishan to the west. The Haigeng embankment on the southern shore gives the long foreground, and the range behind it runs for 35 kilometres.

Why is the lake split in two?

The Haigeng embankment was built out of silt and divides the northern Caohai from the outer lake, which is 96.4 per cent of the total area. Since the Xiyuan tunnel was completed in 1996 the two halves have been separate bodies of water, and the northern section has gone on shrinking and shallowing while the lake as a whole has entered its ageing stage.

Which day trip is the essential one for a photographer?

The Stone Forest, 120 kilometres east, approved in November 1982 as one of the first batch of national key scenic areas, a national geopark in 2001, a UNESCO Global Geopark in 2004 and a World Natural Heritage site in 2007 as part of the South China Karst. It is a karst landscape of more than 400,000 mu, and it reads as a set of separate towers only in raking light, which is an argument for going early.

What is the oldest thing to photograph in Kunming?

The Bronze Hall of the True Warrior on Jifeng Mountain, a national key cultural relic and a fully built metal temple standing in forest above the city. Its main hall is dedicated to the True Warrior deity, and the approach through the trees is the composition that the site is known for.

How old is the town of Guandu?

The site has been settled for 3,500 years, on a rise on the eastern shore of the lake called Wo Dong, and it became a ferry crossing under the Nanzhao king Fengjiayi. As a town it is more than a thousand years old and it keeps the local architectural fabric of old Kunming, after a restoration that ran from April 2001 to April 2003 at a cost of about 120 million yuan.

Where is the wartime history in Kunming?

At Wujiaba airport. Kunming was the headquarters of the American forces in China, Burma and India during the Second World War, Wujiaba was the home of the First American Volunteer Group, the Flying Tigers, and the city was the terminus of the Burma Road. The old runways and the blocks around them are what is left of it.
