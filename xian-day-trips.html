Xi'an is the only Chinese city where the centre is itself a day trip, and the surrounding sites carry an oddness the city centre cannot: the Terracotta Army is a real army buried in real ground, Huaqing Palace is a genuine imperial hot-spring complex in a genuine state of decay, and Mount Hua is a granite plug with a chain ladder up its most fearsome face. All three are within two hours, and all three reward the sort of car that lets you leave when the light is right rather than when the tour bus says.

Terracotta Army (兵马俑)

The army was discovered on 29 March 1974 by a group of villagers digging a well about 1.5 km east of the tomb mound of the First Emperor of Qin, at the foot of Mount Li in Lintong. The warriors stand in three excavated pits, in hollow earthen galleries sealed since 210 BC, and are life-sized at 1.75 to 2 m with officers noticeably taller. They were painted in full colour originally, which is why the bare clay you see now is such a shock. The finds include 8,000 terracotta figures, 40,000 bronze weapons including 160 crossbow triggers, and a chariot with a 4.3 m long shaft with 36 blades that fired six bolts at once. The site is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the reason 40 percent of overseas Chinese who have visited China say they came here.

How the day runs: Leave central Xi'an at 8 am and take the Lintong exit off the G5, about 60 minutes in normal traffic. Arrive at the entrance for 8:30, when the first tour groups have not yet descended. Go straight to Pit 1, which is the largest and the one with the full T-shaped corridors and the reconstruction of the crossbow volley formation, and then to Pit 3, the smallest and most complete, with 14 surviving life-size figures including an archer missing his arm. Pit 2 has cavalry, infantry and war chariots and is thought to represent a guard. The flanking museum of pit reconstructions and the bronze chariot hall take another hour. Finish at the site of the tomb mound itself, marked by the timber-framed structure and the tree that has been growing out of it. Have lunch in the Lintong town and be back in the city by 2 pm.

40 km from Xi'an · about 60 min by car from Xi'an · half-day trip · best in Any month except the National Day week; from December to March the museum is busy with school groups, but the numbers are lower than the summer school-holiday peak.

  • The site is a huge visitor complex; the pits themselves are only about an hour's walk, so leave the rest of the day for the museum halls if you have them.
  • Buses from Xi'an Railway Station and from the Bell Tower run frequently to Lintong for a few yuan and take about 70 minutes.
  • The famous moustached figure and the kneeling archer are replicas; the originals are the ones recovered from Pit 3.

Huaqing Palace and Mount Li (华清宫)

Huaqing Palace occupies 1,300 mu on the northern slope of Mount Li, about 25 km east of Xi'an, and it is one of the few imperial hot-spring sites in the world where the springs are still flowing at a usable temperature. Tang Taizong built the Tangquan Palace here in 644, and in 747 Tang Xuanzong renamed it Huaqing Palace and expanded it into a large palace and garden complex. The excavated remains include eight bathing pools for the emperor, his consorts and the imperial family, from east to west the Nine Dragons Pool, the Consort Yang's Pool, the Star Pool, the Crown Prince's Pool and the Long Pool. The site was listed as a national key cultural relic protection unit in 1996, and its association with the emperor Xuanzong and the consort Yang Guifei is so strong in literature that most visitors come for the story rather than the archaeology.

How the day runs: Combine it with the Terracotta Army since both are on the same side of the city and only 10 km apart, which is the obvious way to use a car here. Start at Huaqing at 8:30, before the tour coaches. Follow the excavated pools in sequence, and pay particular attention to the Nine Dragons Pool, which is the one made famous by the Tang poems, and the Consort Yang's Pool, which is the one the archaeological material is richest in. Walk up into the West Ridge garden area to the view point over the Lintong plain, then eat noodles in the town below. Depart for the Terracotta Army by 11 am to arrive before the midday peak.

30 km from Xi'an · about 50 min by car from Xi'an · half-day trip · best in October to April; the site is exposed with almost no shade and summer heat makes the stone platforms punishing.

  • The Emperor's Pool areas are still live springs and the temperature is genuinely hot, so take water and a towel.
  • The Mount Li cableway, the Huaqing cableway and the Lintong hot spring hotels all cluster in the same area if you want to add a soak.
  • Buses from Xi'an run to Huaqing Palace and Lintong on the same route, so a car saves time mainly by letting you do both sites in one trip.

Mount Hua (华山)

Mount Hua rises to 2,154.9 m at the South Peak and is 120 km east of Xi'an, close enough to Huayin on the Yellow River to be a serious day's drive. It is a granite fault-block peak, one of a small number of such geological features on Earth, and the reason it is famous is that it is barely climbable: the classic route involves the Changkong Zhandao, a plank path cut into near-vertical rock and famously called the 'heart-throbbing plank road', where climbers are chained to a handrail on exposed stone with a drop below. The mountain was sacred long before it was difficult, with a Daoist shrine at its base from the 2nd century BC, and it is one of the five great mountains of China. The full route from the south gate to the North Peak is 6 km and involves several thousand steps.

How the day runs: This needs an early start. Leave Xi'an at 6 am and you arrive at the Yuntai Mountain Gate around 8:30, which is when the cableway begins. Take the cableway to the North Peak area and walk out to the Changkong Zhandao section, where you can see the planks without committing to them. Continue towards the South Peak, the highest of the five, for the real exposure and the best views over the Qinling range. If you are fit, continue along the ridge to the West Peak, which involves some scrambling and takes another two hours. Be back on the ridge by 2 pm for the cableway down, because the descent queue in high season is long. Drive home by 6 pm. If you would rather not hike at all, the cableway plus a short walk to the South Peak and the Zhenhai Temple is a reasonable compromise.

120 km from Xi'an · about 150 min by car from Xi'an · full-day trip · best in April to June and September to November; the south peak is exposed at 2,154.9 m and the north route closes in winter ice.

  • The cableway saves about two and a half hours of the climb each way and is the only sensible option in high summer.
  • Prices are per person per section and are not included in the entrance ticket, so budget for two or three cableway legs.
  • A rope or chest harness can be rented at the gate, which is standard practice on the Changkong Zhandao rather than a sign of inexperience.

Famen Temple (法门寺)

Famen Temple is in Famen town in Fufeng County, 120 km west of Xi'an in the western Shaanxi plain, and it exists because of what was found beneath it in 1987: an underground chamber holding the finger bone of the Buddha, the only surviving Buddhist relic in China. That discovery transformed the temple from a declining provincial site into a national one, and the new cultural scenic area added 150 acres to the complex. The True Relic Pagoda was rebuilt in 1579 into a 13-storey brick pavilion-style structure of about 47 m, which is what you see now, and the temple's Tang-period wooden hall was moved here from a site near the Wei River. The relic itself is displayed only on certain days in the Treasure Hall.

How the day runs: Leave Xi'an at 7 am for the drive west along the G30, allowing two and a half hours. Go to the Treasure Hall first, where the finger bone is kept, and then the Old Temple with its relocated Tang wooden hall and the 13-storey pagoda. Allow a good two hours for both, and then take the aerial tramway up to the reconstructed Old Relic Terrace overlooking the valley, which gives the best view of the complex and the landscape. Lunch in Famen town. The drive back is the same two and a half hours, so leave by 3 pm to avoid the dark. If you are also visiting Xianyang, Famen is a logical pairing on the same western axis.

120 km from Xi'an · about 150 min by car from Xi'an · full-day trip · best in October to April; the temple sits in a narrow valley and the approach is shaded, but winter brings ice on the steps.

  • The finger bone is only shown to visitors on designated days, so check the schedule before travelling.
  • The new scenic area is large and spread out, with a shuttle bus and an aerial tramway between the sections.
  • There is a similar temple complex at the Songyue Pagoda in Dengfeng near Zhengzhou, which was built in 523.

Xianyang Museum and the Qin Capital (咸阳博物院)

Xianyang was the Qin capital and the museum there is the one to see if you want the archaeology without the coach-tour crush. It was founded in 1962 in a former Taoist temple and holds more than 10,000 objects including a large number of terracotta figures, bronzes and coins from the Qin and Han periods. The Qianling and Han imperial tomb mounds lie in the surrounding district, and the museum's collection is built around material from them, including the famous jade burial suit and the Qin terracotta cavalry. It sits only 25 km from Xi'an across the Wei River, which makes it a genuinely easy pairing with the Terracotta Army on the same day.

How the day runs: Drive north-west from Xi'an across the Wei River bridge, 40 minutes, and park at the museum. Start with the bronzes, which include ritual vessels of a formality and weight that photographs well against a plain background. The outdoor bronze display area is worth walking through in the late afternoon, where the larger pieces are set in a garden rather than a warehouse, and the light is warmer. The museum also covers the Qin unification and the Han successor state in a sequence of galleries that works as a good introduction before or after the Terracotta Army. Allow two hours and stop for lunch in Xianyang's old city streets, which are a short drive from the museum.

25 km from Xi'an · about 40 min by car from Xi'an · half-day trip · best in October to April, with the outdoor bronze display areas most comfortable outside the summer months.

  • The museum is free and rarely crowded, which is unusual for a site of this quality in the region.
  • Combine it with the Terracotta Army on a single day trip, since both sit north of Xi'an along the same road.
  • The Qianling tomb mounds, with their Tang dynasty stone guardian figures, are nearby and worth a short additional drive.

Qinglong Temple and the Tang Buddhist Monuments (青龙寺)

Qinglong Temple in Yanta District is the temple that gave Japan its best-known view of Tang Buddhist architecture, because the Japanese monk Kukai studied here in 804 before travelling back to establish Shingon Buddhism. The temple's pagoda is a Tang brick tower of nine storeys, and a set of four Tang dynasty carved stone Buddhist pillars from the site, showing the sarcophagus of a monk and carved in high relief, is now held in the Tokyo National Museum, having been taken from the site during a period of political tension. The modern cherry blossom planting has turned it into one of the most photographed spring locations in the city, and the surrounding area is a genuinely quiet part of Xi'an, far from the tourist core.

How the day runs: This works as an early-morning or a late-afternoon trip, since the temple is only 12 km from the centre and the blossom crowds arrive mid-morning. Go at 7 am during the blossom season and walk the temple precinct while the flowers are still lit from the side by low sun. The pagoda is at the highest point and the climb up through the courtyards takes about ten minutes. Spend a further hour at the site's pagoda forest, where more than a dozen Tang and Ming pagodas stand in a single enclosed compound, which is a rare concentration. Then eat noodles in the Yanta District night market area a short drive away, which is one of the best places in Xi'an to see the city eating rather than sightseeing.

12 km from Xi'an · about 30 min by car from Xi'an · half-day trip · best in Late March to mid-April for the cherry blossom that made the temple famous, and any autumn week for clear light on the pagoda.

  • The blossom season brings heavy visitor numbers; early morning on a weekday is the only quiet approach.
  • The Tokyo National Museum's Tang stone pillars were returned to China in 2007 and are now exhibited at the museum in Tokyo on long-term loan.
  • Pair with the Small Wild Goose Pagoda and the Xi'an Museum, which are 10 minutes apart and also Tang Buddhist monuments.

Planning around the Qinling

The geography of Xi'an rewards a car for the eastern and western axes but not for the centre. Mount Hua to the east, Mount Li and the Terracotta Army to the northeast, and Famen Temple to the west are all long drives on good expressways, and each one is a genuine half-day or full-day commitment. The old city itself, with the wall, the Bell Tower, the Muslim Quarter and the Stele Forest, is walkable.

Traffic on the G5 towards Lintong is the worst in the system, and it is genuinely bad between 8 am and 10 am on a weekend, when tour buses and private cars compete for the same lanes. Leaving before 7 am makes the Terracotta Army and Huaqing Palace combination possible in a single day, which it is not if you start at 9.

Ticket reservations for the Terracotta Army are made through the official online platform and are required in high season, and the same applies to Famen Temple. Both are worth booking a few days ahead rather than turning up and queuing.

Qinglong Temple and the Tang Buddhist Monuments, Famen Temple and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Xi'an day trip route — Qinglong Temple and the Tang Buddhist Monuments sits closest at 12 km, roughly 30 minutes by car, while Famen Temple reaches furthest at 120 km, about 150 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Mount Hua, Famen Temple, Xianyang Museum and the Qin Capital each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Xi'an itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I visit the Terracotta Army and Huaqing Palace in one day?

Yes, and it is the efficient way to do it. They are both in Lintong District on the same side of Xi'an, only about 10 km apart, with Huaqing Palace first on the route because the hot spring complex opens earlier and the morning light on the excavated pools is better than the afternoon light. Doing Huaqing from 8:30 to 11 and the Terracotta Army from 11:30 to 2 covers both comfortably, and both are reachable by bus from Xi'an if you want to skip the driving.

How long does the drive to Mount Hua really take, and is it worth it?

About two and a half hours each way, 120 km east of the city, which makes it a full-day commitment rather than a morning trip. It is worth it if you have three or four hours of walking available and reasonable fitness, because the Changkong Zhandao plank path and the South Peak at 2,154.9 m are unlike anything else in the region. If you are not keen on heights or exposed rock, the cableway up with a short walk to the South Peak gives most of the view for much less effort.

What should I book in advance for Xi'an day trips?

The Terracotta Army and Famen Temple both use timed advance reservations through their official platforms, and the Terracotta Army especially sells out during the National Day holiday and in July and August. Mount Hua tickets are less tightly controlled but the cableway queues are long, so booking the cableway time slot is worthwhile. The Qinling Wildlife Park and Huaqing Palace are more flexible but still worth pre-booking at weekends.

Is the Terracotta Army worth it if I have already seen the Forbidden City?

It is a completely different proposition, because it is an archaeological site rather than a palace. What you see is a sealed pit with hollow brick vaults and standing figures in formation, not decorated rooms. The detail that surprises people is that the figures were originally painted in full colour, and seeing the surviving pigment on a few of them makes the bare clay around them read differently.

Which is better for a Xi'an day trip, Famen Temple or Mount Hua?

They suit different travellers. Famen Temple is a smaller, calmer archaeological and religious site centred on the 1987 discovery of the Buddha's finger bone, with an aerial tramway up the valley, and it works for people who would rather look at a pagoda and a collection than climb a mountain. Mount Hua is a physical day with real exposure and real height. If you can only manage one comfortably, choose Famen for interest and Mount Hua for the sense of achievement.

Are there day trips that work well for a family with young children?

The Terracotta Army is the best of them, because the excavation halls and the pit reconstructions hold children's attention and there is a shuttle bus between the sections. The Small Wild Goose Pagoda and the Xi'an Museum, which share the Jianfu Temple site and have a replica of the Tang wooden hall, is a gentler second choice and costs nothing to enter.
