Name Origin of Hotan

Hotan (和田) is a Uyghur name for the settlement, and the city grew out of the oasis towns strung along the Yurungkash and Karakash rivers at the southern foot of the Kunlun. Its old walled core, the Tuancheng, still keeps the grid of lanes, courtyard houses and bazaars that shaped the city.

Hotan Education & Universities

Higher education in Hotan is provided by the city's local colleges, vocational schools and teacher-training institutions rather than by large research universities. Most students who want a full degree head for Urumqi or Kashgar.

Getting Around Hotan

Hotan Kungang Airport (HTN) is 12 km from the city centre and handles domestic services, with the military and civil functions the airport has carried since it opened. Hotan Station on the Kashgar–Hotan railway links the city with Kashgar, and buses and taxis serve the urban area.

Hotan Natural Resources & Environment

Hotan sits in a desert oasis at the southern edge of the Taklamakan, watered by the Yurungkash and Karakash rivers and backed by the Kunlun mountains. The contrast is the point: date palms and walnut groves inside the oasis, bare gravel desert and snow peaks a short drive away. Summers are hot, winters cold, and the growing season depends entirely on meltwater from the Kunlun snowfields.

Getting to Hotan: Airports & Railway Stations

How to reach Hotan by air, rail and road

✈️ Hotan Airports

✈️ Hotan Kungang Airport (HTN)

Main gateway

Hotan Kungang Airport (HTN) is the main gateway to Hotan, about 12 km from the city centre. It is a joint civil-military field. Our private car service meets you inside the terminal with a name board, tracks your flight and drives you straight to your hotel.

🚄 Hotan Railway Stations

🚄 Hotan Station

Main rail station

Hotan Station on the Kashgar–Hotan railway is the principal rail station for the city, with services running west to Kashgar. Taxis and buses meet arrivals at the station forecourt; a pre-booked private car is the easiest way to reach hotels and the old town.

🛣️ By road

Highways and expressways

Hotan is reached by the G315 national highway from Kashgar and by the desert highway along the southern rim of the Taklamakan. Driving distances are usually 2–5 hours, and a private car is often the most comfortable way to cover the old town and the oasis sites in one day.

Food & Shopping in Hotan

What to eat and what to bring home from Hotan

🍽️ Must-Try Hotan Cuisine

The kitchen is built on oasis produce: the melons, grapes and mulberries of the Tuancheng gardens, lamb and pulled noodles from the bazaar, and the walnut and almond pastries the old traders carried on the Silk Road.

🛍️ Shopping in Hotan

Buy Andale silk carpets, Hetian jade, walnut kernels and rose products. The bazaars of the Tuancheng old town are where the carpet traders and jade sellers keep their shops.

🍵 Tea Culture in Hotan

Traditional Hotan teahouses serve loose-leaf tea in covered bowls, and the regional green teas are the usual house pour. Your driver can point you to a teahouse that specialists use.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

A private car and driver lets you start at the morning market, cross the city for lunch, and finish at an evening teahouse without worrying about parking or the last bus back to your hotel.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Hotan

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Hotan

🍵 Tea Houses of Hotan

Tea culture runs through the day in Hotan: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
