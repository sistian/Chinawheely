The Humble Administrator's Garden occupies 78 mu, about 5.2 hectares, at 178 Northeast Street in Suzhou's Gusu District, making it the largest of the city's classical gardens. Built from 1513 on the site of a burnt temple by the retired Ming official Wang Xianchen, it was listed among the first National Key Cultural Relics Protection Units in 1961 and joined UNESCO's World Heritage List in 1997 as part of the Classical Gardens of Suzhou. Suzhou itself has no civil airport, so most visitors arrive through Shanghai Hongqiao, 86 kilometres to the east.

Why transfer to the Humble Administrator's Garden?

The garden is the reference point for Jiangnan private garden design. Laid out around a central water sheet in the Low Bridge and Loft region, it counts the Hall of Distant Fragrance and the boat-like Fragrant Isle among its principal structures, and Chinese listings rank it as one of the four great gardens of China alongside the Summer Palace, the Mountain Resort at Chengde and the Lingering Garden. Its 5.2 hectares put it first among Suzhou's gardens in area.

The site carries verifiable dates. Wang Xianchen, a censor under the Zhengde emperor, began work in 1513 after retiring to Suzhou, converting the grounds of the ruined Dahong Temple. The complex passed through private hands for four centuries, and the modern conservation listing arrived in 1961 with the first batch of national key sites, followed by UNESCO inscription in 1997 and a national 5A scenic rating shared by the garden with the surrounding Pingjiang quarter.

  • Founded in 1513 by Wang Xianchen on the site of the Dahong Temple ruins
  • First-batch National Key Cultural Relics Protection Unit, designated in 1961
  • UNESCO World Heritage listing in 1997 within the Classical Gardens of Suzhou
  • Address: 178 Northeast Street, Gusu District, Suzhou, Jiangsu

What to do at the Humble Administrator's Garden once you arrive

The standard circuit runs east, central and west sections in that order. The central section, the oldest part, is organised around the water and the Hall of Distant Fragrance, while the western section contains the Hall of Thirty-Six Pairs of Mandarin Ducks and the fan-shaped Pavilion of the Reflection of Reality. The eastern section, rebuilt latest, carries lawns and the Furong Pavilion; allow roughly two hours for the full loop at museum pace.

Exit at the north gate area and the Suzhou Museum, designed by I.M. Pei and opened in its current building in 2006, stands across the lane, with the Lion Grove Garden a 600-metre walk south through the Pingjiang lanes. The garden opens daily with seasonal hours, typically 07:30 to 17:30 from mid-March to mid-November, and daily tickets are capped, so morning entry before 10:00 avoids the densest groups.

  • Book timed-entry tickets in advance; same-day counters sell out on weekends and holidays
  • The central water court around the Hall of Distant Fragrance is the core composition; start there
  • Pair the visit with the Suzhou Museum next door, closed Mondays
  • Metro Line 4's North Temple Pagoda station is about 1 kilometre west if you continue onward

Arriving at Hongqiao or Shuofang for Suzhou

Hongqiao pairs a domestic terminal, T2, with the older T1 used largely for regional services, and the airport connects directly to Shanghai Metro Lines 2 and 10 as well as the Hongqiao rail hub, where high-speed trains cover Hongqiao to Suzhou station in about 25 to 30 minutes. The intercity coach at 50 yuan takes an hour; a private car covers the 86 kilometres on the G2 in 75 to 90 minutes clear, longer at the Friday-evening peak.

Shuofang (SUN) handles a thinner network from major Chinese cities, and the walk from its single terminal to the kerb is short; the drive east into Suzhou takes under an hour in normal conditions. Meet drivers at the arrivals exit by the coach bays rather than in the multi-storey car park, since pickup lanes at both airports turn over quickly. Have your hotel or the garden address written in characters, as Northeast Street is partly pedestrianised and final approach is on foot from Dongbei Street.

What the transfer costs

From Hongqiao, a private sedan to the garden runs roughly 450 to 650 yuan depending on vehicle class and hour, which covers the G2 tolls and a standard waiting allowance; the rail alternative costs 39 to 40 yuan second class and takes half the time, though it terminates at Suzhou station 3 kilometres north of the garden. From Shuofang, sedan transfers price nearer 150 to 220 yuan for the shorter cross-city run.

Whichever airport you land at, quote the destination as the Humble Administrator's Garden, 178 Northeast Street, so the driver routes to the Dongbei Street entrance and not the coach station. Chinawheely lists fixed fares by car class for both SHA and SUN runs, and the meter is not used on these pre-quoted intercity routes.

Northeast Street limits vehicle access during the day; most drivers set down at the crossroads by the Suzhou Museum and the walk to the ticket gate is about 300 metres. Agree this set-down point in advance rather than expecting a kerb directly outside the gate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which airport should I fly into for Suzhou?

Suzhou has no civil airport. Shanghai Hongqiao (SHA), 86 kilometres away, has the densest schedule and a 25-to-30-minute high-speed rail link plus hour-long coaches; Shanghai Pudong (PVG) lies 120 kilometres off; Wuxi Shuofang (SUN) is the nearest field for domestic flyers. Hongqiao suits most itineraries, cutting the road leg to under 90 minutes.

How long does the drive from Hongqiao Airport take?

The distance is 86 kilometres and the run on the G2 Shanghai-Nanjing Expressway takes 75 to 90 minutes with clear traffic, rising past two hours on Friday evenings and holiday eves. The coach shuttle schedules one hour; the high-speed train from Hongqiao rail station does it in 25 to 30 minutes but requires a transfer at Suzhou station.

Is there a train from the airports to Suzhou?

Hongqiao airport and Hongqiao rail station share one complex, with trains to Suzhou every few minutes through the day at 39 to 40 yuan for second class and a journey time of 25 to 30 minutes. Shuofang airport is 5 kilometres from Wuxi New District station on the same Beijing-Shanghai line, adding a short taxi hop.

How much time should I spend in the Humble Administrator's Garden?

Two hours covers the east, central and west sections at a steady pace, including the Hall of Distant Fragrance and the western pavilions. Add another hour if you plan to cross to the Suzhou Museum next door, which needs its own timed reservation and closes on Mondays. Early entry at 07:30 gives the lightest crowds.

Can a car drop me right at the garden entrance?

Northeast Street is a limited-access street in the daytime, so vehicles stop at the junction near the Suzhou Museum, roughly 300 metres from the ticket gate, and the final stretch is on foot. Tell your driver this set-down in advance. The garden's own car park is small and fills by mid-morning in peak season.

What does entry to the garden cost?

Timed tickets are tiered by season, with the peak-season rate around 70 to 80 yuan and the off-season rate lower; student and senior concessions apply with ID. Daily numbers are capped, so book online for weekends and Chinese public holidays. Tickets are checked at the main gate on Northeast Street.
