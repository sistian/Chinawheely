Ancient Culture Street, or Guwenhua Jie, is a pedestrian complex on the west bank of the Hai River in Tianjin's Nankai District, rated AAAAA by the national tourism administration and opened to the public on New Year's Day 1986. Its Qing-style gates, shopfronts and kiosks enclose the Niangniang Palace, a Mazu temple first constructed in 1326 under the Yuan dynasty, making the compound the city's best-documented historic street. From Tianjin Binhai Airport the carriageway west runs beside the Jingjintang Expressway to the riverside in under an hour outside peak periods.

Tianjin Binhai International Airport (TSN)

Binhai is the single civil airport serving Tianjin, in Dongli District east of the city centre. The main passenger terminal, opened in 2006 and expanded around 2010 to 116,000 square metres with 47 jet bridges, sits beside the Jingjintang Expressway, which is the direct carriageway west into the Nankai riverside quarter where the Ancient Culture Street begins.

Why transfer to Ancient Culture Street?

The street holds Tianjin's oldest standing religious structure. The Niangniang Palace, variously recorded as the Tianhou Palace, Tianfei Palace or Xiaozhigu Tianfei Palace, was first built in 1326 and repaired repeatedly across the Ming and Qing periods; it is one of the three great Mazu temples recorded in China and the anchor around which the 1986 street reconstruction was planned. The AAAAA rating it shares with the surrounding area is the top grade in the national system.

The 1986 rebuild followed the Qing-dynasty shopfront idiom rather than modern retail architecture, and the layout pairs the temple forecourt with two long rows of two-storey shop buildings stretching toward archway gates at each end. Crafts associated with the city — niren painted clay figures, kites, New Year prints from Yangliuqing workshops — are sold from the units, and the street functions as the compact introduction to the port-city heritage that grew around the Hai River trade.

  • Opened on New Year's Day 1986 as a purpose-built Qing-style pedestrian street
  • Niangniang Palace, first built in 1326, stands at the centre of the complex
  • AAAAA national scenic rating, the highest grade in China's tourism system
  • Location: west bank of the Hai River, Nankai District, Tianjin
  • The temple is among China's three principal Mazu temples by traditional listing

What to do at Ancient Culture Street once you arrive

Walk the full axis first. The street runs between its two archway gates with the Niangniang Palace midway, and the palace forecourt opens onto side lanes holding smaller shrines and craft stalls; the main rows carry roughly two hundred shop units in Qing-style timber fronts. Entry to the street itself is free, and the palace charges a modest admission, commonly in the region of 10 yuan.

The niren clay figure shops and the Yangliuqing woodblock print sellers are the documented trades of the quarter, both crafts inscribed on the national intangible heritage list. The Hai River bank immediately east of the street carries walkways with boat departure points, and Tianjin Eye, the 120-metre Ferris wheel on the Yongle Bridge, stands under 2 kilometres north along the water if you extend the walk.

  • The street is pedestrian; vehicles set down on the ring roads at either archway gate
  • The palace opens daily except late-afternoon closings; morning is quieter before coach parties arrive
  • Clay figures and prints are the two crafts documented to this street since the 1980s rebuild
  • Metro Line 2's Dongnanjiao station lies about 800 metres south for onward travel

Arriving at Tianjin Binhai International Airport

Binhai handled 19,862,196 passengers in 2025, ranking 26th among China's airports, and its 2006 passenger terminal — tripled in size by the expansion completed around 2010 — operates as one consolidated building with 47 jet bridges, so there is a single arrivals hall to meet in. Metro Line 2 connects the airport station to the city grid, and the Jingjintang Expressway parallels the line into town for road traffic.

For the street, the practical meeting point is the kerb outside domestic arrivals, where pickup bays are marked; drivers on booked transfers wait there with a name sign and track the flight number, so a late touchdown does not change the pickup. The carriageway west to Nankai District takes under an hour in clear conditions and stretches in the weekday evening peak. Beijing passengers should note the alternative: intercity trains from Beijing South reach Tianjin station in about 30 minutes, and the street lies across the river from that station, a 15-minute taxi hop.

What the transfer costs

A metered taxi from Binhai to the Nankai riverside costs roughly 60 to 90 yuan by urban tariff, while a pre-booked private sedan on this site quotes a fixed fare in the 120 to 180 yuan band inclusive of waiting at arrivals; the MPV class for families prices a step above. The fixed quote suits arrivals at night, when metered availability thins after the last wave of departures.

For travellers combining the street with the Tianjin Eye or the Italian-style quarter, hourly hire runs about 100 to 150 yuan per hour with a three-hour minimum on most platforms. Compare against Metro Line 2 at a few yuan before committing a car for the whole day, since the street's own car park is small and the archway gates are the only drop points.

Direct your driver to the south archway gate on Shuangxiangdao rather than the riverside lane, which is closed to general traffic. If the driver is to wait, the coach parking bays behind the north gate hold vehicles while you walk the street; agree the return time before leaving the car.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Ancient Culture Street from Tianjin Binhai Airport?

The airport lies in Dongli District east of the centre, and the road run to the Nankai riverside quarter via the Jingjintang Expressway takes under an hour in clear traffic, typically 30 to 45 minutes. Allow longer in the weekday evening peak when the inner ring slows, and on holiday mornings when the street area draws heavy coach traffic.

Is there a metro from Tianjin airport to the old town?

Metro Line 2 serves a station at the terminal, running west through the city grid; Dongnanjiao station on Line 2 sits about 800 metres south of the street's south archway. The fare is a few yuan and trains run at short headways through the day, so the line is the budget alternative to a booked car.

What is there to see at Ancient Culture Street?

The core is the Niangniang Palace, a Mazu temple first built in 1326 and repaired many times since, standing midway along a Qing-style pedestrian street opened in 1986. Around it sit roughly two hundred shopfronts selling niren painted clay figures, Yangliuqing prints and kites, plus the Hai River walkways immediately east of the gates.

Do I need a ticket for the street and the palace?

Walking the street costs nothing; it is a public pedestrian way between two archway gates. The Niangniang Palace charges a small admission, in the order of 10 yuan, with standard opening hours through the day. Allow one to two hours for the street and the temple forecourt together.

Can I visit from Beijing as a day trip?

Yes. Beijing South to Tianjin on the intercity line takes about 30 minutes, and Tianjin station stands across the Hai River from the street, a 15-minute taxi ride or a short metro transfer away. Arriving by air at Binhai instead, the expressway run delivers you to the archway gates in under an hour off-peak.

Where does the car drop me off?

The street itself is pedestrian-only, so vehicles stop on the ring roads at either the south or north archway gate. The riverside lane alongside is closed to general traffic. Confirm the gate with your driver in advance, and if you are being collected later, use the coach bays behind the north gate as the meeting point.
