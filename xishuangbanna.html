Name Origin

Xishuangbanna means 'twelve thousand rice fields' in the Dai language: xi is ten, shuang is two, and banna is a rice field. The twelve districts were carved out of the old Dai kingdom of Mengleng (勐泐) in 943 AD, and the name has been in continuous use since.

Education & Universities

Higher education is provided by Xishuangbanna University for Nationalities and Xishuangbanna Vocational and Technical College, with a distinctive focus on tropical agriculture, forestry, tea science and the Dai, Hani, Jinuo, Yao and Yi languages of the prefecture.

Getting Around

Getting around Xishuangbanna is easy now that the China–Laos railway is open: Jinghong Station is on the line from Kunming to Vientiane. The expressway from Jinghong to the Myanmar border at Mohan takes about two hours, and Jinghong's airport connects the prefecture with Kunming, Chengdu and Bangkok. Within the prefecture, buses and cars run between Jinghong, Menghai, Mengla and Mengjing.

Natural Resources & Environment

Xishuangbanna is the best-preserved tropical ecosystem in China, protected from the northern cold air by the Ailao and Wuliang ranges. Forest covers 1.55 million hectares, and six national nature reserves — Mengyang, Mengla, Menglun, Shangyong, Manggao and the Naban River basin — hold about 4 million mu, including 700,000 mu of untouched primary forest. There are more than 5,000 higher plant species, 1,724 medicinal plants, and the Lancang-Mekong river runs through the middle of the prefecture.

Food & Shopping in Xishuangbanna

What to eat and what to bring home from Xishuangbanna

🍽️ Must-Try Xishuangbanna Cuisine

The food of Xishuangbanna is Dai: lemon-grass grilled fish (香茅草烤鱼), bamboo-tube rice (竹筒饭), sour bamboo shoot soup, sapie (a bitter-herb salad), and a market full of tropical fruit. The prefecture also produces much of the Pu'er tea sold in China, from the six famous tea mountains.

🛍️ Shopping in Xishuangbanna

Buy tea directly from the Jinghong and Menghai tea shops, along with elephant-foot-wood-free rainforest products, rattan and bamboo craft, and the silk and cotton the Dai weave. The night market at Gaozhuang is the easy option.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Pu'er tea (普洱茶) is the prefecture's other great product, and the mountain teas are still pressed by hand in the villages of Menghai and Mengla. Tea drinking follows the Dai custom of a strong cup in a small clay pot, refilled many times through the day.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

A private car and driver lets you start at the morning market, cross the city for lunch, and finish at an evening teahouse without worrying about parking or the last bus back to your hotel.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Xishuangbanna

The living traditions of Xishuangbanna — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The Dai New Year water-splashing festival (泼水节) on 13–15 April is the biggest event on the prefecture's calendar, with dragon boat races and sky lanterns; the Hani Gatangpa, the Jinuo Temaoke, the Yao Pangwang and the Yi Baishijia festivals follow through the year. The temple architecture, the drum (象脚鼓), the Dai script and the Buddhist pagodas of Jinghong are the living heritage of the region.

Best Time to Visit Xishuangbanna

November to April for the dry season and clear skies; mid-April for the water-splashing festival; May to October for the green rainforest.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — the hottest and driest part of the dry season, with the water-splashing festival in the middle of April. (daytime 31.9–33.4°C, nights around 14.4°C)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — wet and humid, with the rainforest at its most vivid and the rivers running high. (daytime 30.5–31.5°C, nights around 22.4°C)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–November — the rains ease, the clouds burn off by morning and the harvest festivals start. (daytime 26.3–30.6°C, nights around 16.4°C)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · December–February — cool, dry and clear, the best months for trekking and for seeing the mountains across the Mekong. (daytime 23.9–28.6°C, nights around 11.6°C)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Xishuangbanna

What to drink in Xishuangbanna, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Xishuangbanna

Pu'er tea (普洱茶) is the prefecture's other great product, and the mountain teas are still pressed by hand in the villages of Menghai and Mengla. Tea drinking follows the Dai custom of a strong cup in a small clay pot, refilled many times through the day.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Xishuangbanna

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Xishuangbanna

🍵 Tea Houses of Xishuangbanna

Tea culture runs through the day in Xishuangbanna: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
