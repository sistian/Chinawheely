Qingdao is the one city on this list where the airport and the attraction sit on opposite sides of the metropolis. Jiaodong Airport is 39 km out in Jiaozhou, while Laoshan rises 30 km north-east of the old town, so the two are roughly 60 km apart and the drive takes about eighty minutes. What is at the end of it is the highest coastal mountain in China, a granite range whose peaks reach 1,132.7 metres and which has been a centre of Taoism since the Han dynasty, with a hundred temples and hermitages at its height.

Why transfer to Laoshan?

Mount Lao is a landscape, not a building, and it needs a vehicle and a plan rather than a couple of hours. The massif is granite, shaped by glaciers during the Quaternary and then by the meltwater that covered the Shandong peninsula in the late Pleistocene. It rises straight out of the Yellow Sea on the eastern edge of the Shandong peninsula, so from the lower slopes you look down over open water and, on a clear day, across to the old German-style waterfront of Qingdao itself.

The religious history is as substantial as the scenery. Laoshan counts as one of the cradles of Taoism and was where the Complete Perfection school developed; the Buddhist pilgrim Faxian landed nearby in 412 on his way home from India. At the peak of Taoist observance about a thousand monks and nuns lived on the mountain. The biggest surviving complex is the Temple of Supreme Purity, Taqing Gong, whose present buildings date from the Ming Wanli period, with the Three Pure Ones Hall and the Three Emperors and Three Officials halls flanking it.

The literary connections run just as deep. Pu Songling, author of the Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio, visited often and set two of his best-known stories on the mountain, one of which features the camellia tree that still grows in the temple courtyard. Qiu Chuji, founder of the Dragon Gate school, climbed the mountain three times and left inscriptions and a grave robe behind. The Qing reformist Kang Youwei is buried on its western slope. In 2013 the Taoist buildings of Laoshan were listed as the seventh group of national key cultural heritage sites.

  • National park covering 446 sq km, about 30 km north-east of central Qingdao
  • A Taoist centre for 1,600 years, with the Taqing Palace complex dating from the Ming Wanli era

What to do at Laoshan once you arrive

The mountain is split into named lines, and the choice matters more than usual here. The southern route covers the Taqing Palace, where the Taoist halls and the ancient camellia of the Pu Songling story are, and the giant granite peaks of Jufeng, the highest point on the coast. The eastern route through Yangkou is shorter and gentler. The central route is Nine Waters, a gorge of streams and rock steps. Most first-time visitors take the southern line, since it combines the major temple with the highest summit.

Fares are usually built as a ticket plus a scenic-area shuttle, and the shuttle legs are genuinely long, so plan for a full day rather than a quick stop. Between the Taoist halls, the granite peaks and the sea views from the upper ridges, four to six hours is a sensible length. Bring proper shoes: the paths are stone, steep in places and often wet, and the weather on the ridge changes quickly even when the city below is clear.

  • A one-way pass with the scenic shuttle commonly comes to 80 yuan for the ticket plus around 40 yuan for the shuttle
  • Qingdao Metro Line 4 and the Blue Valley Express reach the north end of the mountain; the southern lines need the tourist coach from the visitor centre
  • Take the loop rather than the out-and-back on each line, the walking times are longer than the map suggests
  • Winter and early spring bring hard frost and ice on the stone steps; check the trail conditions before setting out

Arriving at Qingdao Jiaodong International Airport

Jiaodong is a new airport on the coastal plain in Jiaozhou, about 39 km from the city centre, and it replaced the old Liuting strip in 2021. It was designed for 35 million passengers a year and handled 26.9 million in 2025, making it the largest airport in Shandong. The terminals are large and the transfer time to a car is long, so allow more margin than at a smaller airport. Traffic on the road back into the city builds heavily in the late afternoon, and for Laoshan you are heading away from the centre rather than towards it.

What the transfer costs

The TAO to Laoshan run is one of the longer ones, at around 60 km and eighty minutes of driving through Jiaozhou and the northern suburbs. The fare is calculated on distance and vehicle class, with fuel and tolls included, and the waiting time at the mountain should be built into the booking because you will be on the trails for hours. For a group, a van that stays with you all day costs less per head than two separate car hires.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Laoshan from Qingdao Jiaodong airport?

Jiaodong Airport is about 39 km from the city centre in Jiaozhou, while Laoshan rises 30 km north-east of central Qingdao on the opposite side of the city. The two are roughly 60 km apart and the drive takes about 80 minutes. Because you cross the urban area rather than circling it, this transfer is longer than the airport distance alone suggests.

How much does a day at Laoshan cost?

The mountain is sold by line, and a typical combined ticket plus the scenic-area shuttle comes to around 80 yuan for admission and about 40 yuan for the shuttle, with additional charges for some cable cars and shuttle segments. Prices vary by line and by season, so treat these as the standard figures and confirm at the visitor centre.

Which line should I take, the south, the east or Nine Waters?

The southern line is the best first choice, combining the Taoist Temple of Supreme Purity with the Jufeng granite peaks and the highest viewpoints on the coast. The eastern Yangkou line is shorter and easier. Nine Waters in the centre is a narrow gorge of streams and stone steps, good for a half-day rather than a full one.

Is there public transport from Qingdao to Laoshan?

Yes. Qingdao's metro network reaches the north side of the mountain, with Line 4 and the Blue Valley Express serving stations in the Laoshan district, and tourist coaches run from there to the southern lines including Taqing Palace and Jufeng. Coming by car from the airport is quicker and lets you pick the line you want first, but it is entirely possible without one.

How physically demanding is Laoshan?

Moderate to demanding. The southern line involves long stone paths and real climbs, and the shuttle only covers the stretches between nodes, not the walking. Nine Waters in particular is a steep stepped gorge. Comfortable shoes and roughly five hours of walking on a full southern loop are a fair expectation.
