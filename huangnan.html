Name Origin

Huangnan means "south of the river": the Yellow River loops first along the prefecture's southern edge and then again along its north, giving the name a double frame of water. When the autonomous region was founded in 1953, its government was first seated at Rongwo Monastery in Tongren — the prefecture's seat today is Longwu Town in the same city.

Education & Universities

Huangnan is served by local colleges, vocational schools and teacher-training institutions rather than by large research universities.

Getting Around

No airport or railway is recorded for Huangnan; it is compact road country — the prefecture spans only 175 km east to west and 235 km north to south — between Haidong (and Xining beyond it) to the north, Guoluo and Hainan to the west, and Gansu's Gannan to the south and east.

Natural Resources & Environment

Two Qinghais in one prefecture: the north (Tongren and Jianzha) drops into warm Yellow River valleys as low as 1,900 m, while the south (Zêkog and Henan counties) is 3,500 m-plus cold pasture — the prefecture's herding base. The Yellow River frames first the southern then the northern edge, gathering the Zéqu and Longwu rivers; the northern foot of the Amne Machin range and the Xiqing range define the high south.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Huangnan

The living traditions of Huangnan — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

Tongren is the heart of Rebgong art, a Tibetan and Tu tradition that took shape in the Longwu valley from the 13th century onward, when Thangka painting from the Manthang school met Han Chinese painting and local folk craft. The repertoire covers thangka, murals, appliqué, sculpture in wood, clay and stone, sand mandalas, woodblock prints, butter sculpture and temple painting, and it survives in active workshop villages such as Wutun, Nianduhui, Gashari and Guomari. Tongren itself was named a national historical and cultural town in 1994, and about three quarters of its residents are Tibetan.

Huangnan Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Huangnan like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

Pack for two climates: northern Tongren and Jianzha average 6.6-8.5 °C, the southern pasture counties -0.9 to 0.3 °C — one day's drive can cross both.

🥢 Skip the tourist menu

Best Time to Visit Huangnan

Warm-valley summer suits everything; the high south stays cool even then — two climate zones, one prefecture.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · 3-5月 — Rain and warmth arrive together (雨热同季); late frost risk on the southern pastures.

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · 6-8月 — Warm in the northern valleys (annual means 6.6-8.5 °C), short and cool on the 3,500 m pastures — the herding season.

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · 9-10月 — Brief; valley frost returns as the north's 147-173 frost-free days run out.

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · 11-2月 — Long, dry and cold (冷季漫长干冷); the southern counties average below 0 °C all year and roads ice.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Huangnan

What to drink in Huangnan, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Huangnan

No specific tea custom is recorded; in the high southern counties the herding households' drinks are dairy-based, while the warm northern valleys grow their own produce along the river.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Huangnan

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Huangnan

🍵 Tea Houses of Huangnan

Tea culture runs through the day in Huangnan: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
