Hefei has been the home of Bao Zheng, the Song official nicknamed Lord Bao, for a thousand years, and the park built for him now covers a stretch of the lake his family is said to have drained. Xinqiao Airport sits 31.8 km from the city centre, so the transfer takes about 45 minutes, and it lands you at Wuhu Road No. 72 with the Bao Gong shrine, the tomb, Qingfeng Pavilion and the floating village of Fuzhuang inside one walled green space on the southern edge of the old city.

Hefei Xinqiao International Airport (HFE)

33 km from the Hefei terminal, about 45 minutes by car.

Xinqiao lies 31.8 km from the city centre in Shushan District and replaced the old Luogang strip in 2013. The route runs north from the terminal, joins the ring road and continues into the Baohe District on Wuhu Road, where the park entrance sits.

Why transfer to Lord Bao Park?

Bao Zheng served as a magistrate and judge in the Northern Song and is remembered less for the office he held than for the tales that grew around it, the most famous being the two-headed dragon and the tiger guillotines that were said to have executed a prince in his presence. The shrine here, formally the Bao Xiaosu Gong Ci, is the longest continuously maintained Lord Bao temple in China, and it has been a provincial protected site since 1961. Its Main Hall holds his statue, the four attendant figures of Wang Chao, Ma Han, Zhang Long and Zhao Hu, and three bronze blades.

The park is more than a shrine. Qingfeng Pavilion, built for the thousandth anniversary of Bao Zheng's birth, is a large Song-style complex, and Fuzhuang is a village on the water made up of cottages linked by covered walkways. The park also covers the Baocheng Lake shoreline, and the story goes that Bao Zheng's family drained and cleaned the lake, which is where the blue-green water around the islands comes from today.

Lianquan Pavilion holds the best-known single detail. The well inside it is said to have turned cloudy for corrupt officials and clear for honest ones, and the Hefei brewery once named a beer after it. The pavilion was pulled down in 1966 and rebuilt in 1980, which is worth keeping in mind when reading accounts of the site, since much of what you see has been rebuilt within the last century.

  • China's longest continuously maintained Lord Bao shrine, a provincial protected site since 1961
  • Main Hall displays Bao Zheng's statue, four attendants and the three bronze guillotines
  • The shrine sits on Wuhu Road in Baohe District, 31.8 km from the airport
  • Qingfeng Pavilion and the lakeside floating village Fuzhuang are inside the same park

What to do at Lord Bao Park once you arrive

Go to Bao Gong Ci first, because it is the part with a fixed opening rhythm and the part most visitors come for. The entrance gate carries the couplet on loyalty and fame, and the side gates are labelled with phrases about incorruptibility and moral courage. Inside the Main Hall, look at the three bronze blades on the right side, then the family genealogy and Hefei history displays, and the portrait tablets of the Qing officials who funded the rebuilding.

From the shrine, follow the path past Lianquan Pavilion to Qingfeng Pavilion, which is the largest of the buildings and worth the climb for the view back over the lake. The floating village of Fuzhuang is on the water side and can only be reached on foot, so allow fifteen minutes each way. The surrounding Baohe Park lake path is the local evening walk and fills with people after sunset.

  • Bao Gong shrine, tomb and Qingfeng Pavilion generally open from 09:00 to 17:00 with last entry around 16:30
  • Individual sites inside the park are ticketed separately rather than under one park pass
  • The floating village is reached on foot only, so allow extra time for the return walk
  • The park is close to the Xiaoyaojin park area, an easy addition to the same afternoon

Arriving at Hefei Xinqiao International Airport

Xinqiao is a single-terminal airport in Shushan District, 31.8 km from the city centre, opened in May 2013 to replace the old Luogang airfield. It handled over 12 million passengers in 2025 and has grown into a base for international charter work, including a short-lived Frankfurt route. The road into town crosses the western ring road, and traffic is heaviest in the hour before the evening peak.

What the transfer costs

The HFE to Lord Bao Park run is a mid-length city transfer of roughly 33 km, priced on distance and vehicle class, covering fuel and tolls on the ring road. A sedan handles one to three passengers, a van suits a family or a small group with luggage. If you want the driver to wait while you tour the shrine and the floating village, ask for the waiting time to be included in the quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Lord Bao Park from Hefei Xinqiao airport?

Xinqiao Airport is 31.8 km from Hefei city centre and Lord Bao Park is in Baohe District on the southern side of the old city, so the road distance is about 33 km. The drive takes roughly 45 minutes, and about an hour in the afternoon peak when the ring road around the city fills with commuter traffic.

What are the opening hours and tickets at Lord Bao Park?

Bao Gong shrine, Bao Gong tomb and Qingfeng Pavilion are generally open from 09:00 to 17:00 with last entry at 16:30. The sub-sites are sold separately rather than under a single park pass, and the floating village Fuzhuang is reached on foot inside the grounds. Check current prices at the entrance, since individual site charges have changed over the years.

Who was Bao Zheng and why is there a whole park for him?

Bao Zheng was a judge and official of the Northern Song, remembered for impartial rulings against the powerful, which is why he appears in Chinese drama and storytelling as the archetype of the honest official. The park commemorates him with a shrine, his tomb and memorial halls, and it is the most important of the surviving Lord Bao cult sites in the country.

Is the park suitable for a short visit before a flight?

Yes. The shrine alone takes under an hour and the park sits close to the southern ring road, so a morning visit followed by a return to the airport works well. If you only have an hour, concentrate on the Main Hall and the bronze blades, and skip the floating village, which is the part that needs the most walking.

Can I visit the Bao Gong tomb?

The tomb is one of the separate ticketed sites inside the park and is worth including, since the whole site exists because of where Bao Zheng was buried. It is a short walk from the shrine and the signage explains the site in both Chinese and English. Combine it with the shrine visit rather than treating it as a separate stop.
