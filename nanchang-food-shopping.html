Overview: Eating and Shopping in Nanchang

Nanchang eats in earthenware. The city's best-known dish is a soup held in a sealed clay jar over charcoal for more than six hours, and it is eaten at dawn beside a bowl of cold mixed rice noodles. Around that pairing sits a documented commercial geography: a riverfront plaza of 87,000 square metres, a memorial museum that opened in 1959, a Taoist temple founded in 312, and a shopping street that the travel guides list alongside the city's department store.

What to eat

Charcoal-simmered earthen jar soup (瓦罐汤)

Small clay pots of ingredients are stacked inside a larger earthen vessel and heated over charcoal for more than 6 hours, so the soup never meets an open flame. The classics set the standard as 5 flavours and 3 materials brought to the boil 9 times and changed 9 times. One account traces it to a Song-dynasty scholar; another to a Ming official who fed the hungry from a vat.

Where: Breakfast joints across the old city, from the lanes behind Zhongshan Road to the riverside blocks, where the jar is carried out with the mixed noodles as a set.

Cold-dressed mixed rice noodles (拌粉)

Rice noodles dropped into boiling water, drained and turned in a bowl with 6 seasonings, among them chopped chilli, peanuts, radish, cooking oil, soy sauce and spring onion, the proportion left to the customer. The noodles are served cold rather than in broth, which makes them a breakfast dish, and are ordered with the jar soup as one meal.

Where: Street-side kitchens on Shengli Road and the side streets of Zhongshan Road, and the counters of restaurant chains that group mixed noodles and jar soup under one menu.

Soup dumplings (汤包)

Dumplings whose broth is set as jelly inside the filling and released as liquid when steamed. The form was recorded in the Northern Song capital of Kaifeng, sold as 'cave-plum-blossom buns' among 72 principal restaurants. Huang Jishan founded the First Floor bun house in 1922 and developed a 3-soft, 3-hard method of 3 water-attachments and 3 dough-attachments, then moved to small baskets.

Where: Breakfast counters in the Nanchang old town, where the small-basket format is standard alongside the jar soup and mixed noodles.

Jiangxi roast and braised meats (煌上煌)

A Jiangxi brand of Chinese roast and soy-braised poultry and meats, run from a headquarters on Xiaolan Middle Avenue in Nanchang. The company was founded in 1993 by the Xu Guifen family, developing out of an earlier roasted-poultry society. Its 2012 listing on the Shenzhen exchange raised 30.9 million new shares for 123.87 million yuan. The braised cuts are sold by weight, with offal and eggs as take-home items.

Where: Company stores across Nanchang and other cities, and the counters of deli-style shops carrying the house brand of braised duck and seasoned cuts.

Century egg and pork wontons (皮蛋馄饨)

Wontons filled with century egg and fresh pork mixed with aromatics, wrapped in a wrapper thinner than ordinary wonton skin. The broth underneath carries 2 additions, dried shrimp and laver, so the base and the parcels are meant to be eaten together. It is listed among the Nanchang snack dishes alongside mixed noodles and jar soup, and belongs to the Gan tradition of seasoning plain home dishes strongly.

Where: Snack shops and small restaurants in the old city, sold by the bowl as a light meal or a late-night dish.

Jiao ban tofu skin (脚板)

A soy product unique to Nanchang, made by wrapping 2 intact sheets of tofu skin around crushed tofu skin, which is highly absorbent and swells when cooked. It is added to the local pot dishes, and its use is what makes Nanchang's poaching style distinguishable, since the Jiangxi seasoning in the same dishes turns the technique spicy. It changes the body of the soup it cooks in.

Where: Included in the pot-cooked soups served at Nanchang soup houses, where it is added to the clay jar before the long charcoal simmer.

Where to shop

Qiushui Square (秋水广场)

A crescent-shaped plaza on the Gan River, completed on 28 January 2004 and named for a line in the preface to the Pavilion of Prince Teng. The riverbank runs 1,100 metres, the widest point measures 110 metres, and the site covers 87,000 square metres, of which the fountain basin takes 12,000. The music fountain holds 22 features along 800 metres, driven by 636 pumps and 1,591 nozzles, Asia's largest.

Where: Honggutan New District, on the Gan River bank opposite the old city.

Bayi Uprising Memorial Museum (八一起义纪念馆)

A state first-class museum and national 4A attraction on the site of the old uprising headquarters at 380 Zhongshan Road, founded in 1956 and opened on 1 October 1959. The headquarters building was the Jiangxi Grand Hotel, put up in 1923 as a 5-storey brick-and-timber block 26 metres wide, 42 metres deep and 23.5 metres high, with 96 rooms over 4,800 square metres of floor space.

Where: Xihu District, on Zhongshan Road at the western edge of the old city.

Wanshou Palace historic block (万寿宫)

A Taoist temple dedicated to Xu Jingyang, whose cult is associated with the protection of Jiangxi, founded in the 6th year of the Yongjia era, 312. Imperial names followed: Iron Pillar Observatory under the Tang, Jingde Temple under Song Zhenzong, and Miaojì Wanshou Palace granted by the Jiajing emperor in 1546. The building burned in the Cultural Revolution and the original site reopened in 2021.

Where: Xihu District, on Cuihua Street inside Guangrunmen, north of Zhongshan Road.

Shengli Road shopping street (胜利路)

One of the 2 principal retail streets named in the standard travel listings for the city, the other being Zhongshan Road, which carries the Baihuodalou department store. Shengli Road runs through the old commercial core and holds the dense concentration of small food shops, noodle counters and snack outlets that the mixed-noodle and jar-soup breakfast tradition depends on. Its importance is as the address for eating rather than for browsing.

Where: Xihu District, running north from the Zhongshan Road junction in the old city.

Eating and shopping in Nanchang: practical notes

Eat the jar soup first, and eat it early. The charcoal process needs more than six hours, so the pots are filled overnight and the counters open at dawn with the mixed noodles alongside them; the pairing is the city's default breakfast and is ordered as one dish rather than two courses. Around midday the same counters switch to quick noodle and rice dishes, so if you want the clay-jar version, arrive before nine in the morning and do not expect the same pots in the afternoon.

For shopping, note that Nanchang is split by the Gan River and the two halves offer different things. The old city east of the river holds the Wanshou Palace quarter, the Bayi museum on Zhongshan Road, the Shengli Road food streets and the Temple of Prince Teng with its 653 foundation and 1980s reconstruction; the new Honggutan district west of the river holds the 87,000-square-metre Qiushui Square and its 1,591-nozzle music fountain. The two are joined by the Bayi and Nanchang bridges, so a single evening crossing covers both halves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Nanchang's signature breakfast?

Clay-jar soup with cold mixed rice noodles. The jar soup is heated over charcoal for more than six hours in a large earthen vessel holding small pots, and the mixed noodles are boiled rice noodles dressed with chopped chilli, peanuts, radish, oil, soy sauce and spring onion. They are ordered together, and both are eaten in the morning rather than at midday.

How old are the techniques behind Nanchang's soups?

The classics describe the standard for simmering as five flavours and three materials brought to the boil nine times and changed nine times, and the practice is attributed to two separate origins: a Song-dynasty scholar's cook in the Jiayou era who buried a sealed pot in the embers of a spent fire, and a Ming official in the third year of Chongzhen who ran a large vat for the hungry and was remembered as the 'three soups' for his clean government, bitter integrity and restorative charity.

Which museums and monuments can be visited in Nanchang?

The Bayi Uprising Memorial Museum on Zhongshan Road was founded in 1956 and opened on 1 October 1959, and it became free after repairs completed in 2007, with 2.0865 million visitors in 2010. The Wanshou Palace Taoist temple dates from 312 and its rebuilt original site opened in 2021. The Pavilion of Prince Teng was first built in 653, destroyed in 1929 and rebuilt in the 1980s, and the pagoda in the old city, Shengjin Tower, is a Tang brick structure of seven storeys with opening hours from 08:00 to 21:00.

Where do people shop in Nanchang?

In the old city east of the Gan River, with the Wanshou Palace quarter on Cuihua Street and the retail streets around Zhongshan Road and Shengli Road, where the Baihuodalou department store stands. Across the river in Honggutan, Qiushui Square combines tourism, shopping and sightseeing over 87,000 square metres, with a 1,100-metre riverfront, and its music fountain group of 22 water features, 636 pumps and 1,591 nozzles runs along 800 metres.

How should a day in Nanchang be paced?

Start early for the jar soup and mixed noodles, then work the old city: the Wanshou Palace quarter, the Bayi museum at 380 Zhongshan Road and the Shengli Road food counters are all within the eastern historic core. Cross to Honggutan in the late afternoon for Qiushui Square and the riverfront, and check the Pavilion of Prince Teng, whose reconstruction is a landmark on the same bank. The city divides cleanly along the Gan, so the day works best as one circuit rather than two.
