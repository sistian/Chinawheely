Overview: Photography in Changsha

Changsha's photographs are split between the Han-era and the hyper-modern, and the city puts them within a few hundred metres of each other. On the west bank the wall section of the old city still stands above the traffic with a Ming tower on it, and a hundred metres away a spring that has fed the city since the Warring States runs out of granite into a stone trough. On the east bank a Tang poet's pavilion looks back across the river at the sandbar, while two streets further south an arcade of glass towers holds a KAWS sculpture on its roof garden and a food hall dressed as a 1980s mall.

Tianxin Pavilion and the Old City Wall (天心阁)

Tianxin Pavilion was originally called Tianxing Ge and stands on the highest ground of the old city, in Tianxin district beside the Helong sports centre. The name comes from the Shangshu, in the line about one virtue being shared and thereby enjoying the heart of heaven, and the pavilion was the place where the southern Song prince Zhao Huang and his advisers held out against the Mongol troops of the Hubei regime. The city wall was taken down after 1911 and only this section survives: 226.2 metres long, 13.4 metres high, 6.1 metres wide at the top and 5,125 square metres in area. It was repaired in 1983 with provincial and municipal money, listed as a provincial protected unit in 1993, and named a national key cultural relic on 5 March 2013, the seventh batch.

best light: Late afternoon, when the sun comes across the wall from the west and the tiled roof of the tower takes the last light against the sky. · city wall and tower

Getting there: In the south of the old city in Tianxin district, at the junction of Caie South Road and Chengnan Road, within walking distance of the riverside and of the Bamboo Slips Museum.

  • The surviving wall section is short and the street is busy, so a long lens from the far pavement compresses the wall and the tower into one frame.
  • The wall top is narrow at 6.1 metres wide, so the photographs from above work better with a wide lens than a long one.
  • The Baisha well is a few hundred metres away and the two together make a coherent walk on the old city ridge.

Du Fu River Pavilion (杜甫江阁)

The pavilion stands at Xihu Bridge on the east bank of the Xiang, at the junction of Xiangjiang Road and Xihu Road, as part of the river scenic belt, opposite Orange Isle and Yuelu Mountain and less than a kilometre from Tianxin Pavilion. It was built in 2005 and covers a garden of more than 6,000 square metres with a building area of over 3,800 square metres. The main structure has four storeys and rises 18 metres, and the whole thing is modelled on Tang practice rather than being a period building: vermilion columns, lattice windows and white walls, with a roof of dark blue-green glazed tiles, and the main hall, corridor, stele pavilion and teahouse are laid out along the water. Inside is a memorial hall for the poet, with his statue, a set of paintings and calligraphy collected for the building. Du Fu came to Tanzhou, the city of Changsha, in 768 to see a former patron who had died, and the pavilion marks that visit.

best light: Blue hour from the river bank, when the vermilion columns and the roof line are lit against a cooling sky. · modern memorial pavilion

Getting there: Xihu Bridge on the east bank in Yuelu district, on the Xiangjiang Road and Xihu Road junction, with the riverside promenade along its southern side.

  • The whole building is a night subject: it is lit after dark and the reflections in the river carry the colour into the frame.
  • The garden and the pavilion are separated by water, so the riverbank gives the cleanest angle on the whole elevation.
  • Across the river, Orange Isle and the Yuelu Mountain ridge make a useful background rather than an empty sky.

Baisha Ancient Well (白沙古井)

The well lies on Baisha Road in the south of the city, west of the Helong sports centre and just south-east of Tianxin Pavilion, and it is known as the first spring of Changsha and one of the famous wells of the Jiangnan region. It has four spring mouths, and the granite lip of the main one measures about 67 centimetres long, 33 centimetres wide and 70 centimetres deep, with a stone tablet set to the east. It was first recorded in the Spring and Autumn and Warring States period, when it was a single outlet; in the late Ming it divided into two and later into four, and until piped water arrived it was the city's main supply. It was listed for municipal protection in 1964, repaired in 1983, listed again in 1993 and made a provincial protected unit in 2006. A dispute over land acquisition in 1999 halted construction work beside it, which is why the setting remains an ordinary courtyard rather than a landscaped park.

best light: Overcast or late afternoon, when the granite and the wet stone read by texture rather than by shape. · spring

Getting there: Baisha Road in the south of the city, a short walk west from Tianxin Pavilion, between the old wall and the Helong sports centre.

  • The four mouths are at different levels, so a low viewpoint works better here than a standing one.
  • The surrounding buildings are ordinary, and that is the point of the photograph; the well works because it is not staged.
  • Water level and access change with local works, so check the condition on arrival rather than assuming the old view.

Pozi Street (坡子街)

Pozi Street runs about 1,000 metres from Huangxing South Road west to the Xiangjiang Avenue in Tianxin district, and it is the city's folk food street. The ground was the core of the city in the Warring States period. The whole street burned in the Wenti fire of 1938 and was rebuilt afterwards; in 1971 the upper and lower Pozi Street were joined with Xiaoximen Main Street and the Xiaozheng wharf to form the present street, and at the end of the twentieth century it was demolished and rebuilt in imitation Ming and Qing style with old-branded businesses brought back in. The result is a reconstructed arcade, which is why the photography here is about light, signage and repetition rather than about surviving fabric.

best light: Blue hour, when the signboards are lit but the sky still carries colour, or early morning before the shutters go up. · food street

Getting there: Central Tianxin district between Huangxing South Road and the river, on the metro and within a short walk of the IFS complex.

  • The street is narrow and the signage is dense, so shoot upward along the row rather than across it.
  • The imitation architecture is best read at the eaves and the shopfront brackets, which is where the detail survives.
  • Early evening gives both the lit signs and the open kitchens; mid-morning is empty but flat.

Changsha Bamboo Slips Museum (长沙简牍博物馆)

The museum at Baisha Road in Tianxin district is the first institution in China built specifically around bamboo slips, combining collection, conservation, study and display, and it is a sub-county-level public body under the city's culture and broadcasting bureau. Hunan has produced more than 200,000 slips since the 1950s, and Changsha itself has produced more than any other city in the country. The scale of that comes from October 1996, when more than 100,000 slips of the Three Kingdoms state of Sun Wu were uncovered at the Pinghetang building site on Zoumalou Street, which was more than everything found in China before put together. The find was named one of the top ten archaeological discoveries of 1996 and one of the hundred archaeological discoveries of the twentieth century. The research centre was approved in 1999, the building started on 8 June 2000, the museum was approved on 16 November 2002 and the galleries opened at the turn of 2006.

best light: Indoor; the raking light on the wooden slips is the subject, and it does not depend on the hour. · specialist museum

Getting there: Baisha Road in Tianxin district, a few minutes' walk from Tianxin Pavilion and the Baisha well, so all three sit inside one short walk.

  • The gallery lighting is designed for reading slips, which is exactly what makes the wood grain work in a photograph.
  • The 1996 excavation material is the reason to come; the general cases are a supporting exhibit.
  • Combine with the Baisha well and Tianxin Pavilion, which are close enough to walk between.

Changsha IFS and the Tower One Sky Garden (长沙IFS国金中心)

The complex stands in Furong district at 188 Jiefang West Road, at the junction with Huangxing Road and Wuyi Avenue, with metro lines 1 and 2 at Wuyi Square. It cost more than 20 billion yuan and covers about 1.02 million square metres, of which some 250,000 square metres is commercial. Two towers carry the skyline: Tower One has 94 floors and reaches 452 metres, the tallest building in the city, and Tower Two has 63 floors and 308 metres. The land was bought by Wharf for 5.63693 billion yuan on 18 February 2011, and the retail level carries the sculpture garden on the seventh floor, including the bronze KAWS work called SEEING and WATCHING. The building is the standard subject for anyone photographing the contemporary Changsha skyline.

best light: Blue hour from across the road, when the towers are lit and the sky is still bright enough to separate them. · modern architecture

Getting there: Furong district on Jiefang West Road, opposite the Huangxing Road pedestrian street, with Wuyi Square station on metro lines 1 and 2 directly beneath.

  • The two towers are best separated from the south-east corner, where the gap between them opens.
  • The seventh-floor garden is a hard ticket; the same building from street level gives the same verticals without it.
  • The facade lighting changes through the evening, so the second visit at blue hour is rarely the same as the first.

Super Wenheyou (超级文和友)

Wenheyou began in Changsha in 2011 and in 2018 rebuilt the Hisense Plaza branch as Super Wenheyou, a food hall that recreates the interior of a 1980s shopping mall inside a block of flats, with the stalls set along a sloping street and the service counters built to resemble old shopfronts. The model was exported: a branch opened in Guangzhou's Taikoo Hui in July 2020 with more than twenty traditional Guangzhou snack businesses, and queues there ran to four and a half hours, followed by a Shenzhen branch in April 2021 and a name change. The traffic at the out-of-town branches then fell, the Shenzhen site was rebranded twice before suspending operations in September 2023 and reopening with Hunan cooking, and the Guangzhou expansion plan was quietly dropped in 2022. The Changsha original is the version worth photographing.

best light: Interior, any hour; the light comes from the neon and the signage, and the point is the density of it. · interior

Getting there: Hisense Plaza in the centre of Changsha, near the Xiang river and the Pozi Street food street, with metro access nearby.

  • The illusion only works if you include the ceiling and the staircases; the mid-level view loses the trick entirely.
  • A wide lens at low height reproduces the slope, which is what makes the space read as a mall.
  • The signage is the subject, so slow down and shoot the counters rather than the crowd.

Gear and planning for photographing Changsha

The two halves of this city need different kit. The old city, with the wall section, the spring and the pavilion, is small in scale and best walked, so a 35mm or 50mm with a hood is enough and a polariser helps with the wet granite at the well and the glazed tiles on the pavilion roof. The modern half, the IFS towers and the Wenheyou interior, is about verticals and about light that the building provides for itself, so the useful tools are a wide lens for the towers from a distance, a fast lens for the interior, and a tripod for the long exposures the neon needs. One small tripod in the bag covers both halves of the day.

The light in Changsha is generous in winter and hard in summer, and the city sits on the Xiang with humidity from the river, so the morning is usually the cleanest window. Two practical notes for the streets: Pozi Street is entirely pedestrian and works best early or at blue hour, while the Baisha well and Tianxin Pavilion are three hundred metres apart and can be taken in one short walk with the Bamboo Slips Museum between them, which is indoors and covers the midday heat.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the historic site to photograph in Changsha?

Tianxin Pavilion and the surviving section of the old city wall. The wall is 226.2 metres long, 13.4 metres high and 6.1 metres wide at the top, and it stands on the highest point of the old city, where the southern Song prince held out against the Mongol troops. It is a national key cultural relic since 5 March 2013, and the pavilion takes its name from a line in the Shangshu.

Which spring is worth a short walk?

Baisha Ancient Well, on Baisha Road in the south of the city, known as the first spring of Changsha and one of the famous wells of the Jiangnan region. It has four mouths, the granite lip of the main one measuring about 67 by 33 by 70 centimetres, and it dates from the Spring and Autumn and Warring States period. Until piped water arrived it was the city's main supply, and it has been protected in one form or another since 1964, with provincial status since 2006.

Is there a museum worth going to in Changsha?

Two. The Hunan Provincial Museum holds the finds from the three Western Han tombs at Mawangdui, including the body of Xin Zhui, the plain gauze robe and the silk manuscripts. The Changsha Bamboo Slips Museum, on Baisha Road, is the first museum in China built specifically around bamboo slips; the collection rests on the 1996 excavation of more than 100,000 Three Kingdoms slips, which was more than everything found in the country before combined and was named one of the top ten archaeological discoveries of that year.

What does Wenheyou actually do?

It rebuilds a food hall as the interior of a 1980s shopping mall inside a block of flats, with stalls along a sloping street and counters made to look like old shopfronts. The first opened in Changsha in 2011 and the Super Wenheyou version at Hisense Plaza in 2018. It was copied in Guangzhou in 2020 and Shenzhen in 2021, and both out-of-town versions ran into trouble, but the Changsha original is the one that established the style and the one worth photographing.

Where do you photograph the modern skyline?

Changsha IFS in Furong district, at 188 Jiefang West Road. It cost more than 20 billion yuan over 1.02 million square metres, with Tower One at 94 floors and 452 metres, the tallest building in the city, and Tower Two at 63 floors and 308 metres. The land was bought by Wharf for 5.63693 billion yuan in February 2011, and the seventh floor carries a sculpture garden with the bronze KAWS work SEEING and WATCHING.

Is Pozi Street authentic?

The street itself is 1,000 metres long and runs from Huangxing South Road to the Xiangjiang Avenue, and it was the core of the city in the Warring States period. It burned in the Wenti fire of 1938, was rebuilt, and was reorganised in 1971; the present arcades date from the end of the twentieth century, when the street was demolished and rebuilt in imitation Ming and Qing style with old-branded businesses brought back in. The food and the street life are local, the buildings are not, and the photographs are better for that reason.
