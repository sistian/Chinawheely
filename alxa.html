Name Origin

Alxa is generally held to be a transcription of the Helan Mountains, and the Mongolian name means "a place of many colours". The name also circulates in a Turkic legend as the name of a mythical beast. Whatever the origin, the league has been a corridor for the peoples of the steppe and the silk route for two thousand years — the Xiongnu, the Xiyu, the Tang garrison towns and the Western Xia all held ground here.

Education & Universities

Higher education is served by the Alxa branch of Inner Mongolia University for Nationalities and local vocational colleges, with programmes in teacher training, Mongolian studies, desert ecology, husbandry and site management.

Getting Around

Getting around Alxa is by road, and distances are big. The G6 and G30 expressways skirt the eastern desert edge towards Yinchuan and Dunhuang, the roads to Ejin Banner and the border run south through the Gobi, and the Jiuquan and Dongfeng space centres are reached from the highway. There is no passenger railway inside the league, but Bayan Hot Airport (IATA AXF) opened in December 2013 and links the seat at Bayan Hot, so many visitors fly in and then drive out from Yinchuan or arrive overland via Dunhuang.

Natural Resources & Environment

The league is a high desert plain: mountains and ridges ring an interior of desert and gravel, with the Badain Jaran desert and the Tengger desert in the west and the Helan Mountains along the eastern edge — 250 km long, the western limit of the Chinese monsoon and the divide between the Yellow River basin and the inland basins. The highest point is the Helan's Darhan Huobi at 3,556 m, the lowest the Yingen basin at 740 m. The Yellow River runs along the eastern margin and the Heihe flows north into Juyan Lake. Of the league's 248,100 km², 88,400 km² is desert and 91,000 km² is gravel.

Food & Shopping in Alxa

What to eat and what to bring home from Alxa

🍽️ Must-Try Alxa Cuisine

Alxa's table is that of the desert herders: hand-grabbed lamb (手把肉), whole roast lamb, salted milk tea, dried milk and cheese, and the wild sea buckthorn, desert onion and mushrooms that grow on the dune margins.

The bazaars of Alxa Left Banner and Ejin sell dried milk, camel products, desert honey, the jade and stones of Ejin, and carpets and blankets. Ejin is the place to buy jade — go with someone who can read the stone.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Alxa

The living traditions of Alxa — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The league carries several thousand years of desert heritage: the Western Xia garrison town of Heicheng and the Tang ruins of the Ejin and Juyan area, the petroglyphs of the Mandala mountains, the Khelmg Mongolian singing and long song, the camel-herding and badger-hunting traditions of the western banners, and the architecture of the Alxa monasteries — Guangzong Temple (the South Temple) in the south and Fuyin Temple (the North Temple) in the north. The traditional Mongolian festival of the banners, with wrestling, horse racing and archery, is the living face of the culture.

Best Time to Visit Alxa

June to October for the desert; mid-October for the Ejin poplars; September and October for the clearest air and the best light.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — still cold in the desert, with hard frost and wind; the dunes are empty and the light is clean. (daytime 10.5–26.2°C, nights around -4.9°C; about 5 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — hot by day and cold by night in the desert, with the best walking hours at dawn and dusk. (daytime 30.8–32.9°C, nights around 15.5°C; about 24 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — the best season, with the poplar forest of Ejin at full colour and the desert nights clear enough to see the stars. (daytime 7.7–25.2°C, nights around -6.3°C; about 6 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–February — very cold, with the desert hard and frozen and the dune ridges sharp; few travellers, occasional ice. (daytime -1.6–3°C, nights around -16°C; about 1 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Alxa

What to drink in Alxa, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Alxa

Salted milk tea is the constant companion on the steppe, poured from the long-simmering pot and refilled all day. In the monasteries of the league, butter tea is served to visitors before anything else.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Alxa

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Alxa

🍵 Tea Houses of Alxa

Tea culture runs through the day in Alxa: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
