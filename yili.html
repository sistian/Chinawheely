Name Origin

Ili takes its name from the Ili River and the valley it drains. The prefecture was established in 1954 for the Kazakh, Uyghur and other pastoral peoples of the region, and it remains one of the few parts of China where a Kazakh autonomous prefecture sits directly on the national border.

Education & Universities

Higher education is provided by Xinjiang Normal University and Yili Normal University, together with local vocational colleges, with a strong emphasis on teacher training, agriculture, veterinary science and the languages of the region.

Getting Around

Getting around Ili is mostly by road. The G30 expressway links the prefectural seat Yining with Urumqi to the east and with the Kazakhstan port of Khorgos to the west, and the county seats — Yumin, Chabuchar, Nilka, Gongliu, Tekes, Zhaosu and Xinyuan — are linked by intercity coaches and by the national rail line to Urumqi.

Natural Resources & Environment

Ili is a mountain-basin country of three ranges: the Altai in the north with peaks above 4,000 m, the Junggar range in the middle, and the Tianshan in the south, whose Nalti range reaches 7,443 m. Between them lie the forest belts, wild-flower pastures and the irrigated oasis of the Ili River — one of the most fertile and best-watered parts of Xinjiang.

Food & Shopping in Ili

What to eat and what to bring home from Ili

🍽️ Must-Try Ili Cuisine

Ili is the meeting point of Han, Uyghur and Kazakh kitchens. Expect hand-pulled noodles (拉条子), Uyghur polo and laghman, big plates of big plate chicken, and on the Kazakh side jar-cooked lamb (缸子肉) with bread and sour mare's milk.

🛍️ Shopping in Ili

Yining's Kazan bazaar and the covered bazaars of Tekes and Zhaosu sell the region's produce: apricots, walnuts, wild honey, dried horse meat and the embroidered shapan and kamzul (knitted riding coat) of the Kazakh herders.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

In the Kazakh yurt, tea is served in a shared bowl with milk and salt and passed round in a fixed order — the person who pours for everyone drinks last. Green brick tea (砖茶) with milk is the everyday drink, and shubat (soured mare's milk) is served alongside it.

Best Time to Visit Ili

June to September for the grasslands and the festivals; September and October for the autumn colours in the Tianshan forest belt.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — the almond and apricot blossom in the Yining plain, and the first meltwater making the mountain roads passable. (daytime 9–24.8°C, nights around -2.5°C; about 25 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the main season: the grasslands are at their greenest, yurt camps are open and the Askiya festival is held. (daytime 28.5–31°C, nights around 14°C; about 21 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — golden birch and larch in the Tianshan valleys, honey gathering, and the first snow on the high passes. (daytime 8.7–25.8°C, nights around -2.8°C; about 23 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–March — cold and dry with heavy snow in the mountains; the cities stay open but the high-altitude routes close. (daytime -2.1–1.3°C, nights around -15°C; about 21 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Ili

What to drink in Ili, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Ili

In the Kazakh yurt, tea is served in a shared bowl with milk and salt and passed round in a fixed order — the person who pours for everyone drinks last. Green brick tea (砖茶) with milk is the everyday drink, and shubat (soured mare's milk) is served alongside it.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Ili

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Ili

🍵 Tea Houses of Ili

Tea culture runs through the day in Ili: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
