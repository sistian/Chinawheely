Overview: Eating and Shopping in Jinan

Jinan's table is written in water and grain. The city sits on a karst aquifer that pushes artesian springs to the surface, and the kitchens that grew up around those springs turned cheap staples into named dishes with recorded origins: pork belly bound with string, a peppered millet porridge, and a beef-shank sauce stewed nine times over. Four commercial streets document the other side of the same city, from a 432-metre old lane to a rebuilt axis dated to 2001.

What to eat

Braised pork belly with string (把子肉)

Rectangular blocks of layered pork belly, browned with soy sauce or caramel colouring, then stewed until the fat turns translucent, and tied with cord before cooking. It belongs to both Shandong and Xuhai cooking, centred on Jinan, Jining and Xuzhou. The plate is a system: 4-meat balls, grass carp, sausage, ribs and hock join dried tofu, gluten, bean curd skin and tiger-skin egg.

Where: Sold across the Jinan metro area as a working lunch, with dedicated counters on the older commercial streets near Furong Street and Quancheng Road.

Peppered millet porridge (甜沫)

Thick porridge of millet, vegetables and a heavy hand of white pepper, so its name misleads: the taste is savoury and mildly hot rather than sweet. One origin story credits a Tian-family porridge shop in the late Ming or early Qing that stretched thin gruel with vegetable leaves for refugees, who named it after the foam in the pot. The craft entered Jinan's 4th batch of heritage lists in 2013.

Where: Morning counters on the old-town lanes and around the Confucian Temple, served alongside fried rings and flatbread in the traditional breakfast order.

Wheat-kernel chicken gruel (糁汤)

Wheat kernels and shredded chicken in broth simmered from beef bones until milky white, finished with spring onion, garlic, ginger, coriander and white pepper. Eaten for its heat, pepper and fat, with an egg sometimes broken into the bowl. Registered in the 3rd batch of Shandong provincial intangible cultural heritage, and the character used today, sà, differs from the older grain-and-meat sense recorded in the Book of Rites.

Where: Breakfast stalls across Jinan, Shandong, Henan and northern Anhui, typically sold from small kitchens that open before sunrise and close once the morning rush passes.

Nine-turn braised pork intestine (九转大肠)

The flagship dish of the Jinan branch of Shandong cooking: pig intestine boiled soft with aromatics, fried in oil, then braised repeatedly with cardamom, cinnamon and cassia. Created in the first years of the Guangxu reign, when the Jiuhualou restaurant in Jinan served it as red-braised intestine. The present title comes from the Taoist legend of the 9-turn immortal elixir, 9 also being the number the owner preferred.

Where: Ordered in Jinan banquet halls and Lu-cuisine restaurants, with the original Jiuhualou name still used to describe the dish's braising method.

Sweet-and-sour Yellow River carp (糖醋鲤鱼)

A whole carp deep-fried, then flooded with a sugar-and-vinegar sauce until the surface is lacquered and the flesh stays tender. Yellow River carp is the preferred fish, and the dish is listed among the representative plates of the Jinan school of Shandong cuisine, one of China's 4 great traditions. Authentic sauce is built from sugar and vinegar only, without ketchup, which is why the colour is amber.

Where: Served in Jinan's larger Lu-cuisine restaurants and at celebratory banquet tables, where the fish is presented whole and carved at the table.

Water from the Baotu springs (趵突泉)

Three vents in a row inside the Baotu Spring pool, each pushing water up in a continuous column that the Qianlong emperor named the First Spring Under Heaven. The spring heads the city's list of 72 named springs. It ceased on 30 March 1976 and again for 926 days from 14 March 1999, returning on 6 September 2003; level reached 30.18 metres on 18 October 2021.

Where: Baotu Spring Park, at 1 Baotuquan South Road in Lixia District, alongside Shuyu, Mapo, Huanghua and Jinsian springs, with the Li Qingzhao memorial hall at Shuyu.

Where to shop

Furong Street (芙蓉街)

The oldest and busiest general commercial street in Jinan, running 432 metres from the middle of Quancheng Road to the south entrance of Xihuaquanzi Street at the Confucian Temple, with an average width of 4.6 metres. It took its name from the Furong Spring at mid-block, first recorded in the 1771 Licheng County Gazetteer. Shops were built in the front-shop, back-house form typical of Ming and Qing trade streets.

Where: Lixia District, running north from Quancheng Road to the temple frontage.

Quancheng Road (泉城路)

One of the 2 commercial axes of the old city, the other being the Daming Lake Road leading through the old East Gate, and it was rebuilt in 2001 to become Jinan's main shopping street. The middle section contains the city's oldest commercial lane, Furong Street, which anchors the pedestrian core. Department stores, snack counters and bank branches line the route between the Spring City Square and the temple district.

Where: Lixia District, crossing the old town between the Spring City Square and Daming Lake Road.

Qushuiting Street and Baihuazhou (曲水亭街)

A waterside lane now part of the Baihuazhou historic block, named for a floating-cup pool built at the neighbouring Wangfu Pool in the Northern Wei period. Scholars gathered each 3rd day of the 3rd lunar month to float wine cups downstream and drink where the cup stopped. The street is lined with grey-brick houses drawing spring water from their own taps, with willows before the doors.

Where: Lixia District, beside Daming Lake, the Confucian Temple, Zhenzhu Spring and Wangfu Pool.

Daming Lake Park (大明湖)

A spring-fed lake of 0.58 square kilometres with an average depth of 2 metres and about 830,000 cubic metres of water after the 2010 dredging, fed by the Black Tiger, Baotu, Wulongtan and Zhenzhu spring groups. It became a park in 1955 and a national 4A attraction in 2003; works from 2007 to 2009 raised the grounds from 74 to 103.4 hectares. Admission is free.

Where: Lixia District, north of the old town and linked to the moat by excursion boats.

Eating and shopping in Jinan: practical notes

Plan the morning around breakfast rather than lunch. Tianmo, shen soup and jiaozi-style steamed buns are sold in the first two hours after dawn, and the porridge counters close once the lunch trade starts, so the shantang and tianmo kitchens around Furong Street and the Confucian Temple are the practical target. Two consecutive red days on the old-town map — the temple at 124 Daminghu Road, founded in the Xining era between 1068 and 1077 and rebuilt in 1369, then Furong Street along its eastern flank — put the shopping and the food within a single walk of about 500 metres.

For shopping, treat the old town as one circuit and the wider city as another. Furong Street runs 432 metres and holds the dense souvenir and snack layer; Qushuiting and Baihuazhou give the quieter waterside blocks; Quancheng Road, rebuilt in 2001, carries the department stores and banks. The springs set the timetable: the Baotu Spring Park publishes a daily groundwater reading for Baotu and Black Tiger in the city's newspapers, and the level reached 30.18 metres in October 2021, so the paving around the three vents is busiest in the two hours after dawn and again in the early evening.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Jinan dish is the one to try first?

Ba zi rou is the broadest entry point: braised pork belly tied with string, served with dried tofu, gluten, four-meat balls and day vegetables on one plate, and it belongs to a family of cooking centred on Jinan, Jining and Xuzhou. If you prefer a single bowl, tianmo is the local signature, a peppered millet porridge dating from the late Ming or early Qing and listed in Jinan's fourth batch of municipal intangible cultural heritage in 2013.

Is the water at Baotu Spring safe to drink?

The park draws its water from a confined limestone aquifer under an impermeable granite base, and the three vents at the pool have been in continuous spouting since they returned on 6 September 2003 after a 926-day stoppage from March 1999. The city monitors the level daily and issues yellow, orange and red warnings at 28.15, 28 and 27.60 metres, so the state of the springs is a matter of published record rather than local memory.

How long is Furong Street and what is it like now?

The street runs 432 metres with an average width of 4.6 metres, from the middle of Quancheng Road north to the temple entrance, and it is named for the Furong Spring at its centre, first recorded in the 1771 Licheng County Gazetteer. A protection programme began in 1999; the first phase covered the southern section from 2006 to May 2007 and the northern phase started in May 2008, restoring temples and the spring itself. Dining dominates the current tenant mix.

What does a full day's eating plan look like in Jinan?

Begin with tianmo or shen soup in the first two hours after dawn, since both are breakfast plates and the porridge kitchens close once the lunch trade starts. Walk the 432-metre Furong Street and the Qushuiting lane behind the Confucian Temple, then take the free Daming Lake park for the afternoon, where the lake covers 0.58 square kilometres after the 2010 dredging. Finish with jiu zhuan da chang or sweet-and-sour carp at a Lu-cuisine restaurant.

Which Jinan sights can be visited without a ticket?

Daming Lake park charges nothing and is the only one of the city's three great sights that is free; it became a national 4A attraction in 2003 after expansion raised its area from 74 to 103.4 hectares. Baotu Spring Park is the other common stop, and the museum buildings in the city, including the Shandong Library rebuilt on the lake edge between 2007 and 2009, are reached through the same park.
