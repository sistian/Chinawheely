Name Origin

Jinchang (Chinese: 金昌; pinyin: Jīnchāng) is a prefecture-level city in the centre of Gansu province, People's Republic of China, bordering Inner Mongolia to the north.

Education & Universities

Jinchang is served by local colleges, vocational schools and teacher-training institutions rather than by large research universities.

Getting Around

Getting around Jinchang relies on road transport, with buses and taxis covering the urban area and long-distance coaches serving the surrounding counties.

Natural Resources & Environment

Jinchang sits in its provincial hinterland, where the surrounding hills, rivers and farmland shape the local landscape and cuisine.

Top Jinchang Attractions

From the world's largest nickel mine to ancient Silk Road watchtowers, explore the desert edge with a private driver.

🏭

🌊 Jinchuan Industrial Tourism Zone

Nickel Capital · World's Top 3 Nickel Mine

Jinchuan's claim to fame — the Jinchuan Non-Ferrous Metals Company's mine is one of the top three largest nickel mines in the world. The company's industrial tourism zone offers guided tours of the surface facilities, the museum of mining history, and the metallurgy exhibition center. Fascinating for anyone interested in heavy industry and rare earth metals.

🏜️

⛰️ Tengger Desert Edge & Juyan Oasis

Gobi Desert · Rare Desert Wetland

At the northern edge of the Tengger Desert, the Juyan Oasis (居延绿洲) is one of the most ecologically significant areas in western China — a rare wetland in the desert fed by underground water from the Qilian Mountains. Poplar forests, reed beds, and migratory birds make it a unique destination. Camel rides and desert hiking are available.

🏯

🍵 Ming Dynasty 紫金苑 — Jinchang Section

Ancient Fortifications · Desert Watchtowers

Well-preserved sections of the Ming Dynasty 紫金苑 run through the hills and desert around Jinchang, including watchtowers and earthen ramparts. Unlike the restored tourist sections near Beijing, these are raw, unrestored remnants — authentic Ming Dynasty fortifications in a dramatic desert landscape. Best visited with a local guide.

🛤️

🌸 Jinchang Desert Railway Park

Railway Heritage · Industrial History

The Jinchang–Zhangye desert railway is one of China's most scenic train journeys — cutting through the edge of the Gobi Desert with views of the Qilian Mountains. The Desert Railway Park in Jinchang celebrates this engineering achievement with heritage locomotives, museum exhibits, and a section of the original railway.

🌸

🛕 Purple Lotus Temple (紫金苑)

City Park · Flower Gardens

A large urban park in the city center featuring themed lavender gardens (the "Purple Lotus" name refers to the lavender fields), walking paths, artificial lakes, and recreational areas. Popular with local families, especially during the summer months when the lavender is in bloom. Free entry.

⛰️

🏛️ Yongchang Ancient City Ruins

Han Dynasty · Silk Road Fortress

The ruins of Yongchang Ancient City, about 60km east of Jinchang, was once one of the most important Silk Road fortresses on the Hexi Corridor. The Han Dynasty earthworks and remnants of the ancient city walls are still visible. Nearby is the Yongchang Museum with artifacts from the Han Dynasty garrison period.

Jinchang's Best Photo Spots

Capture China's Nickel Capital from its most dramatic angles.

🌊 🏭 Jinchuan Mine Overlook — Dawn Light

Best at sunrise

The vast open-pit mine of Jinchuan, one of the largest in the world, is most dramatic at sunrise when the low golden light catches the exposed rock layers of the terraced pit walls. The mine is visible from the hills to the east of the city. Photography requires permission from the company — the industrial tourism zone is the official way to view it.

🏜️ Desert-Oasis Transition

Best in late afternoon

The boundary where the golden dunes of the Tengger Desert give way to the green poplar forests and reed beds of the Juyan Oasis is one of the most striking landscape contrasts in western China. The color gradient from gold to green is most vivid in the late afternoon light. A drone shot from above captures this transition beautifully.

🏯 紫金苑 Watchtower at Sunset

Best at sunset

The unrestored Ming Dynasty watchtowers along the Jinchang 紫金苑 section, silhouetted against the orange desert sunset with the Qilian Mountains in the background, create one of the most atmospheric 紫金苑 photography opportunities in Gansu. The earthen watchtowers are much more ancient-feeling than the restored sections near Beijing.

🌸 Purple Lotus Temple Lavender Fields

Best in July–August

The Purple Lotus Temple's lavender gardens bloom in July and August, creating a sea of purple that is unusual for the normally brown and gold Gansu landscape. The contrast between the cultivated lavender and the distant brown hills and desert is striking. The best vantage point is from the hilltop pavilion overlooking the garden.

🛕 🚂 Desert Railway — Train Crossing the Dunes

Best from elevated viewpoints

The Jinchang–Zhangye railway cuts through the edge of the Gobi Desert, and photographing a high-speed train crossing the dune landscape is a favorite among transport photographers. The elevated sections of track offer the best vantage points. Consult your driver for the best shooting locations along the route.

⛰️ 🌅 Qilian Mountain Alpenglow

Best at dawn

The snow-capped peaks of the Qilian Mountains to the south and west of Jinchang are often lit by alpenglow in the early morning — the pink and orange light on the white snow against the deep blue pre-dawn sky is one of the most beautiful natural phenomena visible from Jinchang. The mountains are visible from most elevated points in the city.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts of Jinchang

Traditional crafts and cultural traditions at the Gobi Desert edge of the Silk Road.

🐪 Hexi Corridor Camel Wool Crafts

Camel wool (驼绒) weaving is a traditional craft along the Hexi Corridor, using the thick winter coats of Bactrian camels that roam the Tengger Desert edge. The wool is spun into yarn and woven into blankets, scarves, and coats of exceptional warmth. Jinchang's camel wool products are considered among the finest in Gansu — available at local markets and the night market.

Where to find: Jinchang Night Market, local specialty stores.

🎭 Silk Road Folk Music & Dance

The Hexi Corridor has a rich tradition of Silk Road folk music — featuring the pipa (lute), suona (double-reed horn), and Mongolian throat singing that reflects the cultural crossroads nature of the corridor. Jinchang's local cultural center regularly performs traditional Hexi folk music, including pieces that blend Han Chinese, Mongolian, and Tibetan musical traditions.

Where to experience: Jinchang Cultural Center (金昌市文化馆), local festivals.

🏺 Gansu Pottery & Earthenware

The Gansu region has a long tradition of earthenware pottery — unglazed terracotta vessels for water storage, cooking, and burial. The distinctive ochre and terracotta tones of Gansu pottery reflect the desert landscape. Local workshops in Jinchang produce both traditional functional earthenware and contemporary sculptural ceramics.

Where to find: Jinchang markets, local craft workshops.

✂️ Gansu Paper-Cutting (剪纸)

Paper-cutting is a beloved folk art throughout Gansu Province, with designs that reflect the Silk Road heritage — camels, horses, flying dragons, and geometric patterns borrowed from Central Asian textile designs. Jinchang's paper-cutting tradition features bold, graphic designs in red paper — particularly popular during Chinese New Year and weddings.

Where to find: Folk craft markets, Jinchang Cultural Center.

🐎 Mongolian Horse Head Fiddle (马头琴)

The Mongolian communities near Jinchang keep the tradition of the morin khuur (horse head fiddle) alive. This two-stringed instrument with a carved horse head is central to Mongolian musical expression — its mournful, singing tone perfectly captures the atmosphere of the Gobi Desert steppe. Performances are occasionally held at the Jinchang Cultural Center.

Where to experience: Jinchang Cultural Center, local Naadam festival events (July–August).

🍜 Gansu Noodle-Making Tradition

The art of hand-pulled noodles (拉面) is deeply embedded in Gansu's food culture. In Jinchang, the local variant — slightly thicker and with a different gluten texture than Lanzhou noodles — is taught in local noodle restaurants. Some restaurants offer hands-on noodle-making experiences where visitors can learn to pull their own noodles.

Where to experience: Local noodle restaurants near Jinchang Railway Station area.

Jinchang Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Jinchang like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

紫金苑 is the sight everyone should build the day around. Ask your driver to arrive early, when the coaches have not arrived yet, and to wait nearby rather than circling the car park.

🥢 Skip the tourist menu

Eat where the queues are shortest: the morning market and the small noodle shops a block back from the main square usually feed locals at a fraction of the tourist-restaurant price.

⏱️ Plan the driving time

Allow extra time between the airport or main station and the old town — the city spreads out, and the distance that looks short on a map can take half an hour in traffic.

Day Trips from Jinchang

Explore the Silk Road and Gobi Desert beyond Jinchang with a private car and licensed driver.

🏔️

Zhangye — Rainbow Mountains & Grand Buddha

~200 km · 2.5 hr drive · UNESCO Geopark

Zhangye's Danxia Rainbow Mountains are one of the most spectacular geological landscapes in China — striated red, yellow, and orange sandstone hills that look like frozen watercolors. Also visit the Giant Buddha Temple (大佛寺), home to China's largest indoor Buddha statue. A long but spectacular day trip from Jinchang.

From $130

🏙️

Wuwei — Leitai Temple & Han Dynasty Tomb

~110 km · 1.5 hr drive · Han Dynasty capital

Wuwei, the largest city in the eastern Hexi Corridor, is home to the magnificent Leitai Temple (雷台汉墓) — the tomb complex of a Han Dynasty military commander, famous for the "Heavenly Horse" bronze sculpture unearthed there. Also visit the Han Dynasty 紫金苑 museum and the largest underground Han tomb in China.

From $80

🏜️

Dunhuang — Mogao Caves & Singing Sand Dunes

~500 km · 6 hr drive · UNESCO World Heritage

Dunhuang is one of China's most extraordinary destinations — the Mogao Caves (莫高窟) with 45,000 m² of Buddhist murals and statues, the Singing Sand Dunes (鸣沙山) that hum with the wind, and the Camel Caravan Market. Best as an overnight trip from Jinchang, or as a point-to-point transfer with a stop.

From $280

🏛️

Lanzhou — Yellow River & Gansu Museum

~370 km · 4 hr drive · Provincial capital

Gansu's capital, sitting on the Yellow River with the famous "Mother River" sculpture and historic waterwheels. The Gansu Provincial Museum has one of China's finest collections of Silk Road artifacts, including the Hexi Corridor collection from the Dunhuang manuscripts. Excellent for a first-time visitor to Gansu.

From $200

Best Time to Visit Jinchang

Each season reveals a different face of China's Nickel Capital on the Gobi Desert edge.

🌸

Spring

Mar–May · 5–20°C

The sandstorm season in the Gobi Desert — Jinchang can experience several major sandstorms in March and April. These are dramatic but can disrupt travel plans. The upside is that post-storm desert photography is extraordinary, and the city's parks begin to show new growth. Not ideal for desert excursions.

Highlights: Post-storm desert photography, wild herb foraging

☀️

Summer

Jun–Aug · 18–32°C

The best time to visit Jinchang. Long days, warm temperatures, and the Purple Lotus Temple lavender in bloom (August). The desert and oasis are most accessible. Evening street food culture is at its peak. The Jinchang Cultural Festival usually takes place in July–August. Book accommodation early for August.

Highlights: Lavender bloom, desert tours, cultural festival

🍂

Autumn

Sep–Nov · 5–18°C

Cool and dry with clear skies — excellent for photography, especially the Qilian Mountain alpenglow at dawn and the autumn colors in the Juyan Oasis poplar forests. The first cold snaps bring the best lamb of the year. Good time for the Jinchuan industrial tour and indoor cultural attractions.

Highlights: Clear photography weather, autumn oasis colors, best lamb

❄️

Winter

Dec–Feb · -10 to 3°C

Cold and clear, with occasional snow. The city is peaceful, hotel prices are at their lowest, and the hotpot restaurants are at their most welcoming. Desert tours are not recommended due to cold and ice on desert roads. The Jinchuan mine tour is available year-round and is a good option for cold winter days.

Highlights: Warm hotpot restaurants, low prices, industrial tours
