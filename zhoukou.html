Name Origin

Zhoukou means "Zhou ferry" in Chinese, referring to the ancient ferry crossing on the Sha River. It is located in eastern Henan and is one of the most populous prefecture-level cities in China.

Education & Universities

Zhoukou Normal University and Zhoukou Vocational and Technical College are the main institutions.

Getting Around

Zhoukou has no commercial airport; Zhengzhou Xinzheng Airport is the nearest. Zhoukou Railway Station connects to Zhengzhou and other cities on the Xiangyang–Chongqing railway. Buses and taxis serve the city.

Natural Resources & Environment

Zhoukou lies on the Huang-Huai Plain in eastern Henan with a continental climate. It has the Sha, Ying, and Jialu rivers and is a major agricultural region.

Zhoukou Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Zhoukou like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

Fuxi is the sight everyone should build the day around. Ask your driver to arrive early, when the coaches have not arrived yet, and to wait nearby rather than circling the car park.

🥢 Skip the tourist menu

Eat where the queues are shortest: the morning market and the small noodle shops a block back from the main square usually feed locals at a fraction of the tourist-restaurant price.

⏱️ Plan the driving time

Allow extra time between the airport or main station and the old town — the city spreads out, and the distance that looks short on a map can take half an hour in traffic.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Zhoukou

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Zhoukou

🍵 Tea Houses of Zhoukou

Tea culture runs through the day in Zhoukou: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
