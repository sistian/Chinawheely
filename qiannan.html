Name Origin

Qiannan means "the south of Guizhou", the prefecture having been created in 1956 from parts of the old Dushan, Xingyi and Guiding prefectures. It is the homeland of the Buyi and Miao people, and it is the closest part of Guizhou to the sea — 360 km in a straight line from the coast at Fangchenggang — which is why it is wetter, warmer and greener than the rest of the province.

Education & Universities

Higher education is served by Qiannan Normal University for Nationalities and local vocational colleges, with programmes in teacher training, Buyi and Miao studies, agriculture, forestry and hospitality management.

Getting Around

Getting around Qiannan is by road and rail. The Shanghai–Kunming railway and the high-speed line to Guiyang run through Duyun and Guiding, the G60 and G65 expressways link the prefecture to Guiyang, Hunan and Guangxi, and the Qing Shui River bridge and the Pingtang bridge carry the cross-river traffic. The villages themselves are reached by provincial and county roads through the mountains, so allow more time than the map suggests.

Natural Resources & Environment

Qiannan lies on the slope between the Guizhou plateau and the hills of Guangxi, with the land falling from north-west to south-east. Average altitude is 997 m, and the range runs from the 1,961 m Doupo Mountain between Guiding and Duyun down to 242 m where the Red river leaves Luodian — a difference of 1,719 m. It is one of the wettest parts of Guizhou, with more than 1,200 mm of rain a year, and many of the Wu, Red, Qing Shui and Du Liu rivers rise here, which makes it a source region for both the Yangtze and the Pearl systems.

Food & Shopping in Qiannan

What to eat and what to bring home from Qiannan

🍽️ Must-Try Qiannan Cuisine

Qiannan's cooking is Buyi and Miao country food: the sour soup (酸汤) with river fish and hot chili, glutinous rice with beans and nuts, cured pork and blood curd from the winter butchering, and the tea from the mountain slopes.

🛍️ Shopping in Qiannan

The markets of Duyun and Guiding, and the bazaars of the county towns, sell Buyi indigo-dyed cloth, embroidered aprons and baby wraps, Miao silver, bamboo weaving, wooden farm tools and the teas and dried foods of the villages. Buy from the makers where you can.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Tea grows on the slopes above Duyun and Guiding and is drunk as a plain, strong green tea in the villages, refilled all day from the pot. It is also the base of the oil tea with fried rice cakes that the Buyi and Miao households make in the cold months.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Qiannan

The living traditions of Qiannan — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The Buyi people of the prefecture are famous for their indigo dyeing and their plain but precise embroidery, and for the San Yue San festival of the third lunar month, when the villages fill with song and dance. The Miao culture of the northern counties keeps the silver ornaments, the buffalo festival and the Lusheng of the reed-pipe dance. The Shui people of Sandu have their own end-of-year festival, the wooden stilt houses and the rice-and-fish ponds of the Duliujiang basin, and the Sangji and zang song of the south. The Gejing Bridge, the Fuquan city walls, the Qiannan Water ethnic tomb group and the Huishui cave burials are all listed national heritage sites.

Best Time to Visit Qiannan

April to June and September to November, when the rain is lighter and the karst country is at its clearest.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — the Buyi <em>San Yue San</em> festival in the third lunar month, and the fields in green across the karst hills. (daytime 16.1–25.6°C, nights around 8.5°C; about 122 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the wettest season, with heavy rain, high river levels and the most dramatic waterfalls. (daytime 27.8–30.1°C, nights around 20.3°C; about 227 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–November — the clearest weather of the year, with the rice harvest and the best light in the limestone country. (daytime 17.6–27.2°C, nights around 9.9°C; about 85 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · December–February — damp and cold in the mountains, with mist in the valleys and the quietest villages. (daytime 9.4–12.6°C, nights around 3.4°C; about 35 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Qiannan

What to drink in Qiannan, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Qiannan

Tea grows on the slopes above Duyun and Guiding and is drunk as a plain, strong green tea in the villages, refilled all day from the pot. It is also the base of the oil tea with fried rice cakes that the Buyi and Miao households make in the cold months.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Qiannan

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Qiannan

🍵 Tea Houses of Qiannan

Tea culture runs through the day in Qiannan: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
