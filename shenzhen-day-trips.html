Overview: Day Trips from Shenzhen

Shenzhen is young in a way that no other city of its size is: it was a fishing village until 1980 and now has three of the country's tallest towers. But the interesting day trips are not all in the skyscraper district. Dapeng has a Ming dynasty coastal fortification, Yantian has beaches and a fishing harbour that is still working, and the Dafen oil painting village puts an entire street of artists in repurposed urban village housing.

Dapeng Fortress (大鹏所城)

Dapeng was built in 1394 under the Hongwu Emperor as one of the great coastal defence garrisons of the Ming, and its surviving walls enclose a town of about 250,000 square metres with a 600 year continuous military function. It is a genuine walled town rather than a single fort, with a Barbican, a moat and remnants of the original rammed-earth core beneath the brick facing, and it is one of the largest surviving Ming garrison towns in the Pearl River Delta. The 1980s naval base nearby made it a sensitive military zone for decades, which is why it is still noticeably less developed than the rest of Shenzhen.

How the day runs: Leave at 7:30 am and take the Shenzhen Expressway east through Yantian and on towards Pengcheng, where the turn-off is signposted for Dapeng. Allow 90 minutes in light traffic but considerably longer on a weekend morning. Park outside the old walls, because the interior streets are narrow. Walk in through the southern gate and go first to the Dapeng Museum, which has good exhibits on the Ming coastal defence system and is where you pick up the historical context. Then walk the wall circuit, which takes about an hour and gives views over both the modern town and the sea, and note the 1906 and 1911 revolutionary activity that the old drill ground and ancestral halls commemorate. Eat in the seafood restaurants near the east gate, then drive out to the coast at Sanya Bay or Jiaochangwei for the beach. Back by 6 pm.

60 km from Shenzhen · about 90 min by car from Shenzhen · full-day trip · best in October to April; the sea is calmer and the light clearer, and summer brings typhoon rain and a spectacular but dangerous shoreline.

  • Dapeng and its beaches were placed under a no-entry permit for non-local vehicles during the 2022 restrictions, so check the current rules before driving in.
  • The town walls were partly destroyed in a typhoon in September 2024 and sections are still under repair, so the walk may be partly diverted.
  • Combine with a visit to the Pengcheng Museum, which covers the broader history of the Dapeng administrative region.

Dafen Oil Painting Village (大芬油画村)

Dafen grew out of a real estate boom in the 1980s when Hong Kong artists moved into the urban village to paint copies of famous Western masters for the export market. What began as an oil painting trade has since produced an entire village economy: Dafen now houses more than 2,000 painting studios and 3,000 painters in an area of only 0.7 square kilometres, and the gallery of copied masterpieces, including a version of the Mona Lisa, is the surreal centrepiece of the place. Frames are cut, stretched and gilded on the street in full view of passers-by.

How the day runs: Drive out along Buji Road and turn toward Dafen Village, about 40 minutes from the centre in the Luohu and Longgang direction. Arrive mid-morning, when the frame workshops are active but before the midday heat. Start at the main street gallery complex to see the copied masterpieces, which is a short and strange visit, then move into the back alleys where the actual studios are. Several open their doors to visitors and you can watch a painter work through a piece in real time. Buy a small original oil directly from a studio, which costs a fraction of gallery prices in Europe, and ask for a certificate. Lunch in the village, then either continue to the adjacent Buji Buddhist temple or head back. Two to three hours is the right length.

20 km from Shenzhen · about 40 min by car from Shenzhen · half-day trip · best in Any time; the village is busiest on weekends when the frame-and-paint stalls operate at full volume.

  • A car gets you to the edge only; the studio alleys are pedestrianised and full of delivery scooters.
  • Prices in the studio alleys are per square metre, so a 40 by 50 cm canvas is a manageable first purchase.
  • Ask about varnish and framing before you commit, since the frames are often made on site and can be ordered in a day.

OCT Loft and Shekou (华侨城创意文化园)

OCT Loft is a 2004 redevelopment of a former domestic appliance factory of the China Overseas Group, and the project deliberately kept the industrial sheds rather than demolishing them. The result is a compound of two-storey workshops, exposed concrete and steel trusses with gallery spaces inserted into them. The adjoining Sea World district in Shekou has its own history: the foreign firms that built Shenzhen in the 1980s established their base there, and the Shekou ferry terminal opened in 1981 as the first passenger link between Hong Kong and the mainland. The mix of original port architecture and repurposed factories is what makes the walk worthwhile.

How the day runs: Park at the north entrance of the OCT Loft and walk the central spine, which takes about ninety minutes with stops at the gallery spaces and the sculpture park. The coffee shops and design stores are concentrated along the main covered walkway, which is shaded and works well in bright light. Cross towards Shekou for the harbour: the Sea World plaza and the ferry terminal building are 10 minutes on foot, and the former foreign-suppliers' housing along the waterfront is worth a slow look. The Minghua ship, a retired passenger vessel moored by the plaza, gives a strong foreground object for the harbour. Eat seafood at the Waterfront restaurants, then drive back via the tunnel rather than the elevated road if the traffic is heavy.

15 km from Shenzhen · about 35 min by car from Shenzhen · half-day trip · best in October to April, when the courtyard galleries host outdoor screenings and the banyans along the waterfront are at their best.

  • OCT Loft is a working design and arts district, so most studios are open by weekday but some close at weekends.
  • The waterfront in Shekou is best in the last hour of light, when the harbour takes on the sky.
  • The Shekou ferry terminal building is a good example of early 1980s modernist architecture and is worth the walk.

Window of the World and Splendid China (世界之窗)

The Window of the World covers 48 hectares on the western side of the city and contains around 130 replicas of world monuments at their original relative scale, from the Eiffel Tower and the Sydney Opera House to the pyramids and a 118 m Arc de Triomphe. Next door, Splendid China Folk Village covers 300,000 square metres and presents 82 miniature scenes at a scale of 1:15, including a full replica of the Forbidden City and a working village with real performers. Together they make one of the largest single-attraction complexes anywhere in Asia, built in the 1990s to attract the overseas Chinese business community.

How the day runs: Combine the two parks, which sit close together and can be covered in one day. Open at 9 am at Splendid China, where the morning light is best on the 1:15 Forbidden City replica and the outdoor folk performance stage, then walk across to the Window of the World for midday in the international area with the Eiffel Tower and the Taj Mahal. Have lunch in the complex's restaurants. The reason to come is after dark: the illumination comes on across the whole 48 hectares, and there is a large-scale evening show combining laser, flame and projection in the central plaza. Allow 30 minutes of driving between the two parks if you want to park properly at each.

20 km from Shenzhen · about 40 min by car from Shenzhen · full-day trip · best in Any time; the evening illumination and the twice-nightly performance make it far stronger after dark than during the day.

  • Both parks are large; walking them fully takes most of a day, and the sightseeing train inside the Window of the World saves a lot of time.
  • Evening performances and illumination schedules are posted at the gate and change seasonally.
  • The two parks are close enough that a taxi between them is cheap, which avoids moving the car twice.

Yantian District and Dameisha (盐田)

Yantian, formerly a salt works, has become the eastern edge of Shenzhen and has the best coastline in the city. The old Yantian Harbour, in use since the 19th century, remains one of the few working fishing harbours in the Pearl River Delta and is reached through a channel between the breakwaters lined with fishing boats and dense residential towers on the hills above. Dameisha Beach is the main bathing beach, with coarse golden sand and a backdrop of the Dapeng hills, and a few kilometres further east at Xiaomeisha the sea is cleaner. The absence of crowds makes this the coast to come to for a quiet morning.

How the day runs: Drive out along the Yantian Expressway and get to the harbour by 7 am, when the fishing fleet is still unloading and the light is low and clean across the water. Walk the channel side, photograph the boats and the stacked crab pots, and the fish market activity on the quay. Then drive 15 minutes south to Dameisha Beach for a couple of hours; the early morning and the late afternoon are best, and there are beach bars along the back of the sand. Lunch at the harbour, on the seafood the boats landed that morning. If you are there in winter, the offshore islands and the hazy coastline give a strong long-lens subject. Head back by 4 pm.

30 km from Shenzhen · about 55 min by car from Shenzhen · half-day trip · best in May to October for swimming; September and October are the best for photography, with the clearest skies over the harbour.

  • The old harbour is a working port, so treat it with respect and keep clear of the mooring lines and the unloading areas.
  • Weekends in summer draw a lot of traffic to Dameisha; weekday mornings are almost empty.
  • The coastal road east towards Dapeng gives a sequence of cove viewpoints that is one of the better drives near Shenzhen.

Shenzhen driving notes

Shenzhen is spread along a 120 km narrow coastal strip, and distances that look short on the map take an hour because the expressways run through dense urban sections and the Shenzhen Bay crossing is the bottleneck. The expressway tolls are modest, but the real cost is time in the two peak windows, roughly 7:30 to 9:30 am and 5:30 to 7:30 pm, when the Yantian and Buji approaches crawl.

Parking at the industrial-district attractions, OCT Loft and Dafen, is on-street and charged by the hour through a local parking app. At the water towns and the Dapeng fortress, parking is free and plentiful but the walk in is the price.

Note that Shenzhen, Hong Kong and Macau share a land border, and vehicles from mainland China cannot cross into Hong Kong without special documents. If your itinerary includes both, the border crossing at Huanggang or the ferry from Shekou is a separate and time-consuming operation, not something to attempt on a day trip.

Planning a day trip from Shenzhen

OCT Loft and Shekou, Dapeng Fortress and the 3 stops in between set the two ends of this Shenzhen day trip route — OCT Loft and Shekou sits closest at 15 km, roughly 35 minutes by car, while Dapeng Fortress reaches furthest at 60 km, about 90 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Dapeng Fortress works best October to April; the sea is calmer and the light clearer, and summer brings typhoon rain and a spectacular but dangerous shoreline., while Dafen Oil Painting Village suits Any time; the village is busiest on weekends when the frame-and-paint stalls operate at full volume. — in Shenzhen that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

OCT Loft and Shekou, Window of the World and Splendid China, Yantian District and Dameisha each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Shenzhen itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Dapeng Fortress from central Shenzhen and how long does it take?

About 60 km east of the city centre and roughly 90 minutes by car on a weekday morning, going east past Yantian towards Pengcheng. Allow more at weekends when the coastal roads fill with day-trippers and beach traffic. The old town itself is small enough to walk in a couple of hours, so the drive is the long part.

Is Dafen Oil Painting Village worth visiting, or is it a tourist gimmick?

It is a working art village, and the working part is the interesting part. Dafen houses more than 2,000 studios and 3,000 painters in 0.7 square kilometres, and the frame-making and picture-stretching happens in the street in front of you. The gallery of copied masterpieces is strange but brief; the value is in the back alleys, where studios will often let you watch a painter work.

Can I combine the Window of the World and Splendid China in one day?

Yes, and it makes more sense than doing either alone. Splendid China covers 300,000 square metres with 82 scenes at 1:15 scale including a Forbidden City replica, and the Window of the World covers 48 hectares with around 130 monuments at original scale. They sit close together, but each is big enough that you will want to allow a full day and use the internal sightseeing train in the Window of the World.

Which Shenzhen beach is the best for a half-day?

Dameisha for the classic Shenzhen beach experience, with golden sand and the Dapeng hills behind it, and Xiaomeisha a few kilometres further east for cleaner water and more of a sports scene. The Yantian harbour fishing fleet next to Dameisha is a better subject than the sand itself, and it is best seen in the early morning when the boats are landing.

Is driving into Shenzhen from Hong Kong straightforward?

Not as a day trip. Mainland Chinese vehicles cannot cross the border into Hong Kong without separate documents and permits. The cross-border coach services from Shekou and Huanggang are the normal option, and they add a substantial immigration and vehicle transfer process on top of the journey. Plan on doing the Shenzhen side in one day and Hong Kong separately.

What is the least known Shenzhen day trip worth doing?

Yantian harbour, the old fishing port, which is one of the few remaining working fishing harbours in the Pearl River Delta with the fleet still unloading in the early morning. Most visitors go to Dameisha beach or the theme parks, so the harbour gets little attention despite being within an hour of the centre and surrounded by a striking wall of residential towers.
