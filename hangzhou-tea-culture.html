Overview: Tea Culture in Hangzhou

Hangzhou has grown tea for more than twelve centuries: Lu Yu's Classic of Tea, compiled around 760-780 CE, records tea from the Tianzhu and Lingyin temples here. The name that dominates today is West Lake Longjing, grown in five core villages - Shifeng, Longjing, Yunqi, Hupao and Meijiawu - in the hills west of the lake, inside a national geographical-indication protection zone approved in 2021.

The tea

Longjing (龙井茶)

Longjing is a pan-fired green tea named after Longjing village in the hills behind West Lake in Hangzhou. The buds and first leaves are picked before Qingming (around April 4-5), fired by hand in a hot wok using ten distinct traditional hand movements, and pressed into flat, leaf-shaped pieces. The spring picking made before Qingming is the most prized grade.

How to have it here: Grading runs by date: Mingqian, picked before Qingming around April 4-5, is the top tier, followed by Yuqian, picked before Grain Rain around April 20. In Hangzhou the tea is poured in glass cups at farmhouse tastings in Longjing Village and Meijiawu in the hills west of the lake; the classic local pairing is spring water from Hupao Spring, about 5 km south.

How Hangzhou drinks Longjing

Longjing is brewed simple: about 3 grams of leaf in a 150-200 ml glass, water cooled to 80-85°C, one to two minutes for the first infusion. The glass, not a lidded bowl, is deliberate - the flattened leaf stands on end and settles slowly, and the cup shows it. Two or three infusions are normal; hot-but-not-boiling water keeps the sweetness in the leaf.

Refill etiquette is practical: drink down to about a third of the glass before the host tops it up, and a good host never lets a guest's glass run dry. Tapping two bent fingers on the table acknowledges a pour, a common gesture across Jiangnan tea tables. Cups stay small; Longjing is meant to be refilled slowly, not drained in one go.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to buy Longjing?

The top grade is Mingqian, picked before Qingming around April 4-5; the next cut is Yuqian, before Grain Rain around April 19-20. Prices fall sharply after Qingming as the leaf grows larger, so mid-to-late April is the practical window for good value at the village shops.

Where can I taste Longjing at the source?

Longjing Village, Shifeng and Meijiawu sit in the hills west of West Lake, all reachable by bus in under an hour from downtown. Farmhouses pour tastings through the spring season, and Hupao Spring, whose water has been paired with the tea for centuries, is a separate park about 5 km south of the lake.

How do I brew Longjing at home?

Use 3 grams of leaf per 150 ml glass, water at 80-85°C, and steep one to two minutes. Boiling water scorches the bud and turns the liquor bitter. Two to three infusions are usual; keep about a third of the liquid in the glass when refilling so the second round stays even in strength.

How can I tell real West Lake Longjing apart?

A national geographical-indication zone covering the West Lake area was approved in 2021, so look for the GI mark and the three-zone labelling - West Lake, Qiantang and Yuezhou - on the pack. Genuine leaf is flat, smooth and yellow-green, and the origin line on a properly labelled pack is traceable to one of the three zones.

Is Longjing still pan-fired by hand?

Partly. The traditional craft uses ten hand movements in a hot wok and holds a national intangible-heritage listing; most volume today is machine-fired, with hand-finishing kept for top Mingqian lots. Village workshops in Longjing and Meijiawu still demonstrate the wok during the April season.
