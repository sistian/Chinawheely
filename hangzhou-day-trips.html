Overview: Day Trips from Hangzhou

Hangzhou's day trips are unusually close and unusually rewarding. The West Lake itself is 6.39 square kilometres with a 15 km shoreline and no entrance gate at all, because the city removed the ticketing and the wall in 2002 and has been managing it as an open park ever since. Within half an hour you can be in the Lingyin temple complex at the foot of Feilai Peak, or among the camphor trees and plank bridges of the Xixi wetland, and in an hour you can be sitting in a tea house above the Longjing tea terraces.

West Lake (西湖)

The lake covers 6.39 square kilometres, 3.2 km north to south and 2.8 km east to west, with a shoreline of nearly 15 km, an average depth of 2.27 m and a water volume of 14.29 million cubic metres. Its basic pattern of one mountain, two pagodas, three islands, four causeways and five areas of water was set by hand: the Su Causeway was built in 1089 by the prefect Su Shi from the mud dredged out of the lake and completed in 1111, and the Bai Causeway was built later the same way. It became China's first batch of national key scenic areas in 1982, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2011, and since 2002 the whole lakeside has been free to enter.

How the day runs: Start at 7 am at the Broken Bridge on Bai Causeway, when the light is low and the water is flat, and walk south along the Bai Causeway to the Solitary Hill island and the Zhejiang Museum. Cross the causeway to the Three Pools Mirroring the Moon, where the 1 m stone pagodas and the small island pavilion give the classic reflected composition. Continue along the Su Causeway westward, which is 2.8 km with six arched bridges and is best walked rather than cycled because of the willows on the water side. Take lunch at a farmhouse restaurant on the causeway at the First Bridge. Finish at the Leifeng Pagoda hill around 4 pm to catch the pagoda against the light, or at Su Causeway's western end where the road climbs into the tea hills. Total walking is about 5 km with the option to break it up.

8 km from Hangzhou · about 25 min by car from Hangzhou · half-day trip · best in March to April for the cherry and peach blossom and the willow greening, and October to November for the autumn colour; the lake is unbearably humid in July and August.

  • There is no gate and no ticket anywhere around the lake, so a car only helps with comfort rather than access.
  • The lakeside road is closed to through traffic at weekends and on holidays, which makes the car more trouble than it is worth on a busy day.
  • Cycling the Su Causeway is possible from either end but shared with pedestrians, and bicycles are not allowed on the Bai Causeway in the morning peak.

Lingyin Temple and Feilai Peak (灵隐寺)

Lingyin was founded in 326 AD by the Indian monk Huili, who is credited with giving the hill its name, meaning abode of the spirits. It is one of the largest and wealthiest Buddhist temples in the country, with a 33.6 m Great Hall of the Buddha containing a 19.6 m Sakyamuni statue carved from a single block of Zhejiang sandstone, one of the largest in China and carved in the Ming. Immediately above the temple stands Feilai Peak, a 168 m limestone outcrop with some 470 Buddhist carvings dating from the Eastern Jin to the Song, including the distinctly Tang-style laughing Buddha relief, the most photographed carving in Hangzhou.

How the day runs: Arrive at 8 am, before the coaches from the city, and follow the stream up through the gorges with the stone pagodas on both sides. The walk from the ticket gate to the temple is about 1.5 km and takes 25 minutes, passing the two towering Five Pagodas and the natural rock clefts that gave the area its reputation for stone Buddhist art. Enter the temple complex and go straight to the Great Hall of the Buddha for the 19.6 m sandstone Sakyamuni, then work through the Arhats halls. Climb the 200 steps up to Feilai Peak afterwards, where the carvings are distributed along the cliff in a linear sequence and the Tang laughing Buddha is a ten-minute walk further along the ridge. Finish at the spring below the peak. Allow four hours for the whole thing, and go early.

15 km from Hangzhou · about 35 min by car from Hangzhou · half-day trip · best in Any month; the temple is at its best in the first half of the year for the blossom in the temple grounds, and the mountain paths are quietest on weekdays.

  • The laughing Buddha carving is on the far side of Feilai Peak from the main staircase, so allow 20 minutes to reach it.
  • The temple's own path is busy but the cliff carvings above are almost empty if you keep walking.
  • The ticket covers the temple and Feilai Peak; the Yuhuang Pavilion viewpoint over the West Lake below is a separate small ticket.

Xixi Wetland (西溪湿地)

Xixi covers about 11.5 square kilometres of secondary wetland on the western side of the city, 5 km from the West Lake, and was once called one of the Three Xis together with West Lake and Xiling. It has been left largely in its natural state while the surrounding city grew around it, which is why it holds a sequence of camphor forests, ponds and mud flats with a dense population of waterbirds. Around 90 percent of the site is covered with water, plum, willow, persimmon and camphor, and the park is run on a low-intervention model with hand-pulled rowing boats and a bamboo walk through the reed beds rather than a large-scale boardwalk complex. It is a national 5A scenic area.

How the day runs: Enter through the Hezhu Street gate, which has the main car park, at 9 am, and take the electric boat or a rowing boat for the first section, which is the only way to reach the inner reed beds without walking 5 km. Get out at the Fu Embankment and walk the plank path through the persimmon and willow groves, which is the part with the best light and the fewest other visitors. Cross the bridges to the water village area where the traditional houses sit on piles over the water. The observation tower at the north end is worth the climb for the view over the ponds. Have lunch at the restaurants at Hezhu Street on the way out. Allow three hours, and go early because the weekend coaches arrive around 10 am.

10 km from Hangzhou · about 30 min by car from Hangzhou · half-day trip · best in October to November for the persimmon and reed colour, and March to April for the rape blossom; summer is green and humid and the boardwalks are busy at weekends.

  • The rowing boats are hand-pulled, which takes a passenger on a slow circuit of the inner water and is the best way in.
  • October brings the persimmon trees into colour along the Fu Embankment, which is the signature image.
  • The wetland is much quieter on weekday mornings than any of the other Hangzhou sites.

Longjing Tea Village and the Tea Museum (龙井村)

Longjing village sits in the hills west of the West Lake, and the tea grown here is what Dragon Well green tea has always been. The China National Tea Museum, founded in 1991 at the foot of Shuangfeng hill and known as the Double Peak Pavilion, is dedicated to the history, cultivation and processing of tea and holds more than 10,000 items. The villages of Longjing, Meijiawu and Shifang sit among terraces where the bushes were planted between the 16th and 18th centuries, and the picking is famously by hand, one bud and two leaves, with the pan-firing done in the village kitchens within hours of picking.

How the day runs: Drive out along the Longjing Road, which climbs into the hills, and stop first at the China National Tea Museum, which has a good café and terrace and explains the whole process in a way that makes the afternoon visit more meaningful. Continue up to Meijiawu, where the terraces are steeper and the tea houses are better. Walk a terrace path in the mid-afternoon, with the rows of tea bushes running in clean contour lines. Have tea in one of the village houses, where you can watch the pan-firing and buy by the tin. Come down the mountain road at dusk, which is when the light rakes across the terraces and the whole valley turns gold. Allow four hours for the museum and the village together.

20 km from Hangzhou · about 40 min by car from Hangzhou · half-day trip · best in Late March to early May for the picking season, and any autumn week for the colour on the terraces; the spring picking days are extremely busy.

  • Buy tea in the villages rather than at the city shops, and you can watch it being fired in the pan in front of you.
  • During the spring picking season the best tea goes quickly and the villages fill with buyers, so an early arrival is worthwhile.
  • The Meijiawu terraces are higher and steeper than Longjing village itself, with better views over the valley.

Faxi Temple and the Upper Tianzhu Road (法喜寺)

Faxi, formerly called Upper Tianzhu or the Upper Heavenly Pillar, is the highest of Hangzhou's three great Buddhist mountains, and it has been described as the most beautiful temple in the world in the Chinese travel literature. It is a working nunnery with around 300 resident nuns, which is unusual and gives the complex an atmosphere quite different from the tourist-managed Lingyin. The approach road from the Upper Tianzhu Pagoda, built in 259 AD, climbs for about 1.5 km with the Qiantang river below on one side, and the turning road with its stone pagodas is the most photographed approach in Hangzhou.

How the day runs: This works best as a quiet alternative to Lingyin rather than as an addition to it. Drive out to the Upper Tianzhu Pagoda area and start with the pagoda itself, a six-storey brick and stone tower from the Three Kingdoms period with a pagoda forest around it. Walk the stone-paved road up to Faxi, which takes about 45 minutes and is steep in the last section, with the river visible through the trees on the left. At the temple, the covered stairway up to the main hall is the strongest image, with the incense and the yellow walls and the light coming through the open sides. Finish at the upper terrace for the river view. Four hours including the walk.

22 km from Hangzhou · about 45 min by car from Hangzhou · half-day trip · best in March to April for the blossom and the incense-free early mornings; late autumn for the clear views over the Qiantang river from the Upper Tianzhu terrace.

  • Faxi is a working nunnery, so be quiet in the halls and ask before photographing the nuns.
  • The incense is much heavier here than at most temples, which some visitors find atmospheric and others do not, so go early in the morning.
  • Combining the Upper Tianzhu Pagoda with Faxi makes a satisfying half-day; combining it with Lingyin makes a long one.

Qiandao Lake (千岛湖)

Qiandao Lake, the Thousand Island Lake, is a reservoir formed by the Xin'an River dam completed in 1959 and named for its 1,078 islands, of which 1078 are above a certain size, in an area of 573 square kilometres with a shoreline of 800 km. It is 150 km from Hangzhou, which makes it a genuine day's drive rather than an outing, and the water clarity is among the highest in China because of the deep basin and the forest cover on the slopes. The islands are clustered in the north-east and the south-east sections, which is where the boat routes go, and the forest plantations on the island slopes date from the 1960s.

How the day runs: Leave at 6:30 am, because the drive via the G25 and the expressway to Chun'an is close to three hours. Arrive at the Qiandao Lake pier at around 9:30 to catch the first boat, which runs the central island circuit and takes about six hours including the stops. The main islands worth landing on are Meizhan, known for its high-voltage towers in the water, and Yulong, with its waterfalls and temple. Eat lunch on board, because the boat restaurants are the practical option. Be back at the pier by 5 pm for the return drive, which gets you into Hangzhou around 9 pm. Book the boat and the bus from the pier together, since the transfers are timed to the boat schedule.

150 km from Hangzhou · about 190 min by car from Hangzhou · full-day trip · best in October to November for the clearest water and the autumn hills; June has the occasional plum rain, and the island roads can be misty in early morning.

  • The two-hour-plus drive each way means this is a full day and not a spontaneous decision.
  • The boat, not the drive, is the point; book the central circuit boat and a bus transfer to the pier as one package.
  • A small number of islands are uninhabited and closed, so check the current route before assuming a particular island is accessible.

Moving around the Hangzhou area

West Lake is not somewhere a car helps, because the whole lakeside area has been open and free of charge since 2002 and the roads around the north and east of the lake are closed to through traffic at weekends and on public holidays. Park once at a station on the south side and use the electric shuttle buses and the extensive bicycle network instead.

The hills west of the lake are where a car becomes useful, because the tea villages and Faxi are on winding roads that the public buses serve only intermittently. The Longjing Road is the main artery and it climbs steeply from the lake's western end.

Qiandao Lake is the exception that proves the rule: at 150 km and nearly three hours each way, it is a full-day commitment, and the correct arrangement is to book the boat and the transfer from the pier together so that the timings line up.

Planning a day trip from Hangzhou

West Lake, Qiandao Lake and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Hangzhou day trip route — West Lake sits closest at 8 km, roughly 25 minutes by car, while Qiandao Lake reaches furthest at 150 km, about 190 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

West Lake works best March to April for the cherry and peach blossom and the willow greening, and October to November for the autumn colour; the lake is unbearably humid in July and August., while Lingyin Temple and Feilai Peak suits Any month; the temple is at its best in the first half of the year for the blossom in the temple grounds, and the mountain paths are quietest on weekdays. — in Hangzhou that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

Xixi Wetland, Longjing Tea Village and the Tea Museum, Faxi Temple and the Upper Tianzhu Road each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Hangzhou itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does West Lake charge an entrance fee?

No. It became the first free-admission 5A scenic area in China in 2002, when the city removed the gates and the fences and began managing the whole lakeside as an open park. Some individual sites inside it still charge, such as the Yuhuang Pavilion viewpoint and the three-pagoda island when you take the boat, but the lake itself is free and ungated.

Is a car useful in Hangzhou at all?

Only for the hills and the far side of the region. Around the West Lake the roads are restricted and there is an excellent electric shuttle bus and bicycle network, so a car mostly creates parking problems. For Longjing, Meijiawu, Faxi and the Upper Tianzhu Pagoda, or for Qiandao Lake, a car is genuinely the right tool.

How long does the drive to Qiandao Lake take, and is it worth it?

About 150 km and close to three hours each way, which makes it a full-day trip rather than a morning excursion. What you are buying is the boat trip among the islands of a 573 square kilometre reservoir with 1,078 of them and 800 km of shoreline. The best value is the central circuit boat, which runs for about six hours with island landings, and the practical arrangement is booking the boat and the bus transfer together.

Lingyin or Faxi, which temple should I choose?

Lingyin if you want the famous things: the 33.6 m Great Hall of the Buddha with its 19.6 m single-block Sakyamuni statue, and the roughly 470 cliff carvings on Feilai Peak including the Tang laughing Buddha. Faxi if you want quiet and a working nunnery atmosphere with 300 resident nuns, plus a much more demanding climb to reach it. Lingyin is busier and more commercial; Faxi is steeper, smaller and much calmer.

What is the least crowded time to visit West Lake?

Before 8 am on a weekday. That is when the Broken Bridge and the Bai Causeway are empty, the light is lowest and the water is flattest for reflections, and the boat traffic to the Three Pools island has not started. From around 10 am the tour groups arrive and remain through the afternoon, and the sunset hour is the single busiest of the day.

Can I cycle around West Lake?

Yes, and it is the standard way to cover the 15 km shoreline, with public bicycle stations around the lake and dedicated lanes on the causeways. The Su Causeway run of 2.8 km with its six arched bridges is the best section. Bicycles are not allowed on the Bai Causeway during the morning peak, and the roads are closed to through traffic at weekends and holidays, which makes the lanes safer and calmer those days.
