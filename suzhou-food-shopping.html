Overview: Eating and Shopping in Suzhou

Suzhou's kitchen is a catalogue of techniques rather than a list of signature dishes: whole fish scored into a squirrel shape and flooded with sweet-sour sauce, eels finished with a ladle of smoking hot oil, noodles boiled in a broth simmered for half a day, and silk-thin green tea rolled into spirals. This page records six foods and four shopping streets, each with the numbers a visitor needs — years of founding, lengths of lanes, counts of shops.

What to eat

Squirrel mandarin fish (松鼠鳜鱼)

A mandarin fish boned, scored in a crosshatch, coated in egg yolk batter, deep-fried until the flesh curls away from the bone, then flooded with a boiled sugar-and-vinegar sauce poured over it at the table. The scoring gives the dish its name: the flesh opens like a squirrel's tail, in a legend tied to the Qianlong emperor's 18th-century southern tours. Elsewhere in China, yellow croaker is used instead.

Where: Songhelou, the restaurant named in the dish's best-known origin story, sits on Guanqian Street; the preparation is also served across the old town.

Sizzling eel paste (响油鳝糊)

Finely shredded eel, blanched and cut from small to medium eels, then stir-fried at high heat with 7 seasonings from Shaoxing wine to white pepper and thickened with starch water. A single ladle of oil heated to smoking point is poured on at the end, which is what makes it sizzle on arrival. Jinhua ham and winter bamboo shoot are common additions.

Where: A standard item on Shanghainese and Wuxi-leaning Suzhou banquet menus, ordered at restaurants across the old town.

Suzhou-style soup noodles (苏式汤面)

Thin noodles that drink broth quickly, arranged in the bowl in a shape traditional servers call a crucian carp's back. The broth is simmered over low heat with pig knuckle and the leftover eel bones from cooking eel dishes, and comes in 2 forms: red, with soy sauce, and white, without. Customers give shorthand orders such as 'wide broth' or 'more garlic'.

Where: Zhuhongxing, Tongde and Luchangxing are the named old noodle houses; the craft was listed as a Suzhou municipal intangible heritage item in 2024.

Fresh pork mooncake (鲜肉月饼)

One of the Su-style mooncakes: a filling of minced pork, sometimes with shrimp or crab roe, enclosed in layered flaky pastry that is baked the same day it is sold. The pastry is high in oil, so the cakes are meant to be eaten fresh and refrigerated within 24 hours if kept. Sold year-round across the Jiangnan region, with sales peaking in the weeks before the Mid-Autumn Festival.

Where: Cai Zhizhai and Qian Sheng Yuan, both old Guanqian Street houses, are the best-known Suzhou counters for the fresh pork version.

Bilongchun green tea (碧螺春)

One of China's ten famous teas, and in narrow terms a green tea made only from the hills of Dongting East Mountain and West Mountain on Lake Tai. The leaves are picked as 1 bud with 1 unfolded leaf and rolled by hand into tight spirals. Infused, the liquor stays pale green and clear. The state designated it as the tea served at the Great Hall of the People.

Where: Sold as loose leaf from the Dongting mountain workshops; the wider Yangtze delta tea houses and the markets around Taihu stock it year-round.

Yangcheng Lake hairy crab (阳澄湖大闸蟹)

The Chinese mitten crab of Yangcheng Lake, registered by the Chinese state as a protected geographical indication product. Four features mark it: a blue-grey back, a white belly, yellow leg hair and golden claws that can stand on glass. The lake is the first place where the Yangtze's fresh water meets the sea, its water level held near 2 metres. Its sales network reaches 2,000 outlets in 133 cities.

Where: Cooked and sold through the autumn season at specialist counters across the old town, with the busiest trade on Guanqian Street and around Pingjiang Road.

Where to shop

Guanqian Street (观前街)

The pedestrian commercial street at the centre of the old walled city, running from Renmin Road in the west to Cufangqiao and Lindun Road in the east, with the thousand-year-old Xuanmiao Temple on its axis. It was paved with small stone blocks until 1965 and asphalted after. Merchants returned after 1863, and by 1927 the street held over 120 firms in close to 40 trades.

Where: Central Suzhou old town, immediately north of the Xuanmiao Temple.

Pingjiang Road (平江路)

The historic canal-side lane of Suzhou, laid out along the Pingjiang River in a pattern preserving the street grid of the Southern Song city, when the town was itself called Pingjiang. The main street measures 1,606 metres, with 51 side alleys cutting into it and about 3 kilometres of canal inside a historic district covering 0.4 square kilometres. Named one of the first nine famous historical streets in 2009.

Where: Northeastern old city, running south from the Humble Administrator's Garden along the canal.

Shantang Street (山塘街)

The seven-li street on the northwest side of the old city, running beside a canal from Changmen in the east to Tiger Hill in the west, about 3,830 metres long. The Shantang Canal was begun in 825 under Bai Juyi, prefect of Suzhou, and the spoil from the dig was piled into the Baigong Dyke on the north bank, on which the street was later built.

Where: Northwest of the old city, from the Changmen gate toward Tiger Hill and Fengqiao.

Xuanmiao Temple and its square (玄妙观)

The Taoist temple that gave Guanqian Street its name, called Tianqing Guan in the Northern Song and renamed Xuanmiao Guan in the Yuan. The street in front was called Tianqingguan Front in that era, was nicknamed Suijin Street in the Ming for the peach blossoms in the temple grounds, and was renamed Zhongshan Road in 1937 before Guanqian Street was restored in 1980.

Where: Guanqian Street, Suzhou old city, Suzhou, Jiangsu.

Eating and shopping in Suzhou: practical notes

Suzhou's food rules are written into the noodle order itself. At a Su-style noodle house you will hear shorthand for broth volume, garlic quantity and the balance between noodles and topping, so the safest approach is to listen to the counter staff or point at the wall menu. Noodle shops in this tradition tend to run breakfast and lunch rather than dinner, with several of the long-established names keeping short hours.

For shopping, pair the two old cores. Guanqian Street carries the department stores, banks and time-honored food counters, while Pingjiang Road, with its 51 alleys and 3 kilometres of canal, is the lane of small craft shops, teahouses and converted houses. Shantang Street reaches north to Fengqiao, and it is the natural extension of a walk that ends at Hanshan Temple, whose 108-ton bell cast in Wuhan in 2007 stands 8.5 metres tall.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Suzhou's most famous dish?

Squirrel mandarin fish, a boned and crosshatched fish deep-fried and covered in sweet-sour sauce, credited to the Songhelou restaurant on Guanqian Street. Sizzling eel paste and Su-style soup noodles with slow-simmered broth are the other two entries every local would list.

Why does the eel dish make noise on the table?

A ladle of oil heated to smoking point is poured over the shredded eel at the last moment, which is the step the name records. The hiss happens in front of the guest, so the dish is normally carried directly from the kitchen to the table.

How long is Guanqian Street and what sells there?

It runs from Renmin Road in the west to Lindun Road in the east through the centre of the old walled city. Tea and confectionery houses such as Cai Zhizhai, rice and noodle shops, silk stores and clock and watch dealers were among the trades established from the 1860s, and department stores followed in the 1920s.

Which street should I walk for atmosphere rather than shopping?

Pingjiang Road, whose canal-side layout preserves the Southern Song street pattern with stone bridges and waterside alleys. Its main run is 1,606 metres long with 51 side alleys, it was named one of the first nine national famous historical and cultural streets in 2009, and the southern stretch is the busiest part, with food stalls and craft shops operating from morning into the evening.

When can I buy the fresh pork mooncakes?

They are sold year-round in Jiangnan, but the weeks before the Mid-Autumn Festival are when the counters are busiest and the queues longest. Because the pastry is oily, they are sold to be eaten the same day, and leftovers are best refrigerated and finished within 24 hours.
