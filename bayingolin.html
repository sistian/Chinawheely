Name Origin

Bayingolin is the Mongolian name for the region, and the prefecture is the home of the Oirat Mongols, who moved here with the Torghut people in the eighteenth century. The Chinese name means 'rich spring', after the rivers that rise on the Tianshan and run into the Tarim. The area itself is far older: Loulan, Qiemo, Ruoqiang and the other ancient city-states of the Tarim Basin stood here two thousand years ago.

Education & Universities

Higher education is served by the Bayingolin campus of Xinjiang University and local vocational colleges, with programmes in teacher training, petroleum engineering, horticulture and desert agriculture, and a growing international student body from the neighbouring states.

Getting Around

Getting around Bayingolin is by road and rail. The southern Xinjiang railway runs from Turpan through Korla, Hejing and Ruoqiang to the desert, and the G218 highway links Korla with Ruoqiang and the Kunlun crossing to Tibet, while the G315 runs west to the desert highways of Aksu and Hotan. Korla airport is the hub of the southern Xinjiang network, with the Ruoqiang airport serving the far south.

Natural Resources & Environment

This is the largest prefecture-level division in China — 471,526 km², a quarter of Xinjiang — and the geography is a giant U: the Tianshan across the north, the Tarim Basin in the middle and the Kunlun along the south, with the eastern Kunlun reaching 7,723 m at Muztagh Ata. The mountains, basins, rivers, lakes, gravel and deserts of the prefecture include 69 lakes covering 2,398 km², of which Bosten Lake is the largest, and the Tarim gas field, one of the three great gas fields in China.

Food & Shopping in Bayingolin

What to eat and what to bring home from Bayingolin

🍽️ Must-Try Bayingolin Cuisine

Bayinbulk's cooking is southern Xinjiang oasis food: big plate chicken (大盘鸡), hand-pulled noodles, laghman, and the apricots and melons of the Korla and Yuli orchards dried in the sun. The lake fish from Bosten is the local delicacy.

🛍️ Shopping in Bayingolin

Korla's bazaars and the Ruoqiang market sell the region's dried fruit, apricots, raisins and walnuts, the Ruoqiang jade, and the cotton and textiles of the state farms. The apricot orchards around Yuli are worth a stop in July.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Tea is drunk strong and black with milk and salt in the oasis towns, the standard across southern Xinjiang, and in the Mongol camps of the north the tea is the same, taken with milk and salt from a pot on the stove.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Bayingolin

The living traditions of Bayingolin — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The prefecture holds two very different heritages. The Uyghur and Han oasis towns carry the old Silk Road culture of the Tarim Basin, with the Loulan, Qiemo and Ruoqiang archaeological sites and the melons and music of the oases. The Mongol side, which arrived with the Torghut in the 1770s, has the grassland culture of Bayinbuluk and the Oirat herders — the summer camps, the horse culture and the long song. The region also has the famous Lop Nur wild camel reserve and the Tsaidam and Ruoqiang desert heritage.

Best Time to Visit Bayingolin

April to June for the apricot blossom and the grasslands; September and October for the best weather and the clearest desert light.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · April–May — the grasslands come alive, the apricots blossom around Yuli and the temperatures are perfect for the desert. (daytime 13.3–27.6°C, nights around 1°C; about 3 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — hot in the desert and oasis cities, cool on the grassland; the wildflowers on Bayinbuluk are at their best in July. (daytime 30.9–32.8°C, nights around 17.9°C; about 10 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — the best season, with clear skies, golden reeds at Bosten Lake and comfortable days across the whole prefecture. (daytime 8.8–27°C, nights around -2.9°C; about 4 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–February — hard frost in the desert and snow on the Tianshan, but clear days and very few tourists. (daytime -1.5–4.8°C, nights around -11.8°C; about 2 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Bayingolin

What to drink in Bayingolin, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Bayingolin

Tea is drunk strong and black with milk and salt in the oasis towns, the standard across southern Xinjiang, and in the Mongol camps of the north the tea is the same, taken with milk and salt from a pot on the stove.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Bayingolin

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Bayingolin

🍵 Tea Houses of Bayingolin

Tea culture runs through the day in Bayingolin: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
