Changshui Airport opened in 2012 and sits 24.5 km north-east of Kunming at 2,100 metres above sea level, while the Stone Forest lies about 90 km east of the provincial capital in Shilin Yi Autonomous County. Kunming Changshui International Airport (KMG)

85 km from the Kunming terminal, about 100 minutes by car.

Changshui opened in 2012 in a graded mountainside 24.5 km northeast of the city centre at 2,100 m elevation. The road to Shilin runs east on the G78/Shilin expressway and takes roughly an hour and a half to the gate of the Greater and Lesser Stone Forest.

Why transfer to the Stone Forest?

The formations are genuine geology rather than a themed park. Around 270 million years ago this was a shallow sea; sandstone and limestone accumulated in the basin through the Permian period, the region was uplifted, and then wind and running water carved the joints into the karst pillars that stand today. Two areas, the Naigu Stone Forest and Suogeyi Village, were inscribed in 2007 as part of the South China Karst World Heritage site, and the whole district became a UNESCO Global Geopark in 2004.

It is also the place where the Yi ethnic minority's most famous story lives. Ashima, the heroine of the epic, was turned to stone by the flood, and the pillar formations around the Greater and Lesser Stone Forest are read as her people. The annual Torch Festival on the 24th day of the sixth lunar month draws large crowds, and the local government prints the name in calligraphic characters collected from an ancient stele, with the attribution to Long Yun, who named the site when he passed through in 1931.

Because the drive is long, the visit is best treated as a full-day rather than a stop on the way somewhere else. The scenic area itself is divided into seven parts spread over 400 sq km, and the two most visited, the Greater and Lesser Stone Forest and Naigu, are 9 km apart. Shilin county has hotels if you want to stay near the gates, but most visitors do the long run in and out from Kunming on a single day trip.

  • About 90 km east of Kunming, roughly 100 minutes by car from Changshui Airport
  • Two sections are UNESCO World Heritage as part of the South China Karst, inscribed 2007
  • The karst developed in Permian limestone around 270 million years ago
  • Seven scenic areas in all, including Naigu, Zhiyun Cave, Long Lake and Dadieshui Waterfall

What to do at the Stone Forest once you arrive

Enter at the Greater and Lesser Stone Forest first, where the classic sword-shaped pinnacles stand most densely and the paths loop between them over undulating ground. The Lesser Stone Forest is the tighter and more atmospheric half, and there is an observation platform at the far end. Stone paths were laid through the karst so the formations are walked around rather than through, and the change in light between the narrow clefts is the part people remember.

Naigu is the quieter and geologically different half, 9 km away and developed in dolomite rather than pure limestone, with wider canyons and a lake. For the space between the two, the scenic area runs shuttle buses and an electric cart around the loop. If time allows, add Long Lake and the Dadieshui Waterfall on the northern side, where the karst meets a 17 m drop of water.

  • Combined tickets for the Greater and Lesser Stone Forest and Naigu are sold at the gate, with electric cart and shuttle legs priced separately
  • Wear shoes with grip; the paths are polished limestone and often wet
  • The Torch Festival falls on the 24th day of the sixth lunar month and changes the crowd profile completely
  • Shilin county has a railway station on the Kunming to Yuxi line, an alternative to a long road return

Arriving at Kunming Changshui International Airport

Changshui is a large modern airport in a graded mountainside north-east of the city, built by SOM with Arup as engineers and opened in June 2012 to replace the old Wujiaba strip. It handled close to 50 million passengers in 2025, the tenth busiest in China, and it doubles as the main gateway for flights into Southeast Asia and South Asia. At 2,100 m elevation the walk from gate to car is short and the air is noticeably thin, so take the transfer slowly on arrival.

What the transfer costs

This is a long-haul airport run and the fare reflects it: a one-way car from KMG to the Shilin gates covers a little over 100 km of driving, plus fuel, tolls on the expressway and the driver's time. A round trip with several hours of waiting is the usual way to book it. Because the day is long, many travellers take the same driver for both legs so that the vehicle is guaranteed at the stone forest when they finish.

Tell us your flight number so the driver can time the pickup for a morning departure, and say if you want the return leg to wait rather than run back to the airport empty.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to drive from Kunming Changshui airport to the Stone Forest?

Allow about 100 minutes for the 85 to 90 km run from the airport to the scenic area gate. The road takes the expressway east through Kunming's eastern suburbs and then across open plateau before reaching Shilin county. Traffic is light most of the day, but weekend mornings near the Torch Festival and during the August holiday period add a queue at the ticket office.

Is there a train or bus from Kunming to the Stone Forest?

There is a railway station in Shilin county on the Kunming to Yuxi line, and the county is 16 km from the scenic area, so a rail-plus-taxi combination is possible. It is slower and less convenient than a direct car, which is why the road transfer is the usual choice for a day visit from the airport.

Do I need a whole day for the Stone Forest?

Yes. The drive from the airport is 100 minutes each way, and inside the park the Greater and Lesser Stone Forest and Naigu are 9 km apart with shuttle legs between them. Most visitors spend five to six hours on site. A morning departure from Kunming, lunch in the county, and an afternoon return is the standard shape of the day.

How much do the tickets cost?

The main ticket covers the Greater and Lesser Stone Forest, and a separate ticket covers Naigu, with combined packages available. Shuttles between the sections and the electric cart inside the park are priced separately again. Figures change with the season and with discounts, so check the current price on arrival at the gate rather than assuming a fixed rate.

Is the Stone Forest suitable for children or older visitors?

Mostly yes, on the paved main routes. The Lesser Stone Forest has steps and narrow passages, and the Lesser Stone Forest paths are not step-free, so the electric cart is worth taking for anyone with limited mobility. The karst is dramatic rather than technical, and the tourist infrastructure around it is well developed with toilets and shelters at the main nodes.
