Overview: Photography in Guangzhou

Guangzhou's best photography is a mix of the exotic and the domestic: a 610 m tower with a 1,000-step skywalk, and a 19th-century Lingnan ancestral hall covered in carved brick and ceramic roof sculpture. The Pearl River Delta light is also unusually generous, with a long subtropical day that stretches golden hour well past 6 pm for much of the year, and the city's older west end gives you arcades, banyan trees and repurposed factory architecture within a few kilometres of the CBD.

Canton Tower (广州塔)

Canton Tower rises 610 m above the Pearl River, with 454 m of tower body and a 146 m antenna mast, and was the tallest structure of its type in the world when it opened in October 2010. Its signature feature is the skywalk on the outside: 1,088 steps spiralling between 168 m and 334.4 m, the longest open-air skywalk anywhere. The facade carries 1,080 LED nodes driven by computer-controlled circuits, and three lasers at the top sweep over the river, which is why the tower photographs either as a lit object after dark or as a lattice silhouette against the sunset.

best light: sunset · modern

Getting there: Line 3 to Canton Tower station. A car can reach the west plaza on Yizhou Road, and the riverside path along the south bank gives the classic full-length framing across the water.

  • Shoot from the south bank on the Panyu side at sunset so the tower is front-lit by warm light before the illumination takes over.
  • The laser colour at the mast tip changes daily with the day of the week, which local photographers track.
  • The skywalk needs a separate ticket and closes in high wind, so allow flexibility in the schedule.

Shamian Island (沙面岛)

Shamian is an artificial island of about 0.3 square kilometres in the Pearl River, filled in from 1859 and leased by Britain and France in 1861 under a treaty that gave the British four fifths and the French one fifth. The street plan mixes British and French consulates with neoclassical, Renaissance and Baroque merchant houses built in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and the island retains over 150 old trees including banyans planted at the time. Because the island is separated from the city by a moat-like channel, light arrives from both sides, and the wide car-free avenues make it one of the easiest street portraits in China.

best light: golden hour · street-life

Getting there: Shamian North Street runs directly off the Renmin South Road roundabout, with a metro entrance on the square. A car can reach the north bridge, but the island interior is pedestrianised with bollards.

  • The banyan avenue on Shamian North Street with the low sun coming through the branches is the signature image.
  • The former French and British consulate buildings on the western half are grander and less crowded than the eastern commercial section.
  • Shoot from the east bridge in the early morning to catch the mist on the river with the buildings behind it.

Chen Clan Ancestral Hall (陈家祠)

Built between 1888 and 1894 as the ancestral hall for the Chen clans of Guangdong's 72 counties, the Chen Clan Ancestral Hall is the most complete surviving example of Lingnan decorative arts in the country, and it now houses the Guangdong Folk Arts Museum. It covers more than 15,000 square metres in a three-bay, three-courtyard, nine-hall layout, with the central Juxian Hall rising 14.5 m. The surfaces are what matter: six brick-carved murals each about 4 m long carved in high relief from single grey blocks, ceramic sculpture on the roof ridges of 19 halls, and plasterwork totalling 2,562 m in length.

best light: all day · landmark

Getting there: Metro Line 1 to Chen Clan Academy station, with the hall immediately outside the exit. There is no convenient car park; use the metro.

  • The grey sculpture on the east and west outer walls is the biggest single surface, and raking afternoon light picks out its depth.
  • The roof-ridge ceramic figures are best seen from the elevated gallery in the inner courtyard.
  • Entry to the museum is included with the Guangzhou Museum pass and the hall generally opens from 9 am with last entry earlier.

Guangdong Museum (广东省博物馆)

The Pearl River New Town branch of the Guangdong Museum, designed by Rocco Yim and opened in May 2010, is shaped like a treasure box. The entrance floor is lifted 23 m above ground and reached across a sloping lawn, and the central hall beneath it is 6,000 square metres and 38 m high under a daylight roof. From the steps and the upper terrace the view back across the Pearl River to the old city is the best elevated shot of Guangzhou, with the IFC and CTF towers in the frame and Shamian's rooftops in the middle distance. Late afternoon light rakes across the bronze-coloured stone.

best light: golden hour · modern

Getting there: Line 5 to Wuyangcun or Line 3 and Line 8 to Zhujiang New Town. There is underground parking under the building, which is the practical way to reach it by car.

  • The museum requires real-name advance reservation through its WeChat mini program, and it closes on Mondays, so plan the visit around that.
  • It opens 9 am to 5 pm with last entry well before close, so an afternoon slot still leaves time for the terrace light.
  • The lawn in front of the raised entrance is the correct place to shoot the building's mass against the sky.

Liwan Lake and Xiguan Arcades (荔湾湖公园)

The four lakes of Liwan Park were dug in 1958 to relieve flooding in Xiguan, and they sit beside Enning Road, which preserves the longest continuous row of Xiguan arcade architecture in the city. The arcades are the subject: a covered footpath at street level that let merchants move in from the river whatever the weather, and which survives in stretches here in a way it does not elsewhere in Guangzhou. The lake edges are lined with mature banyans and willows, and rowers and water-bike riders are on the water from early morning. The low sun coming down the arcade line is the strongest golden-hour street frame in the city.

best light: golden hour · street-life

Getting there: Buses to Zhongshan 8th Road, Pantang or Longjin West Road serve the four lakes. Driving is possible on Longjin West Road and Longjin Middle Road with roadside parking.

  • Shoot along the arcade rather than across it, so the covered walkway recedes into the frame.
  • Early morning from about 7 am gives the arcades to yourself, with the rowers and early tai chi groups as the only movement.
  • The evening food stalls along Enning Road and Lychee Bay add people and colour to the same architecture.

Baiyun Mountain Summit (白云山)

Moxing Ridge at 382 m is the high point of Baiyun Mountain and the reason the whole massif was named, since clouds so often settle over it. The case here is atmospheric rather than architectural: from the summit platform you look north over the whole city to the radio and television tower, and on a spring morning the summit is often above a cloud layer with the city beneath it. Sunrise is the strongest option because the light comes from the east over the industrial districts, which then light up before the residential areas. Blue hour gives the reverse, with the tower and city lights switching on in sequence.

best light: sunrise · nature

Getting there: Take the Sanyuanqiao exit and follow the mountain road up to the Yuntai Garden entrance area. A chairlift and a cableway both run from the garden towards the ridge, and buses run from the foot up on weekends.

  • Clouds filling the valley below the ridge are common and best seen in the early morning, so the earlier you start the better.
  • A long lens compresses the city lights below into a dense band, which works well from the ridge.
  • Private cars are sometimes restricted on the summit road at peak times, so check before relying on driving to the top.

Yongqing Fang (永庆坊)

Yongqing Fang opened in September 2016 in the Enning Road district of Xiguan, on the site of the historic Tai Ping, Yong Qing and Bao Hua lanes and the partially demolished Jinsheng Cinema, of which one original wall was retained and built into the new structure. The project is a good example of adaptive reuse: the arcade streets and the old shikumen and Western-style merchant houses were kept and repaired, while new insertions sit deliberately alongside them. At dusk, when the arcade lighting is warm and the lane is busy with bars and restaurants, the juxtaposition of the salvaged cinema wall and the new retail is the shot to make.

best light: blue hour · street-life

Getting there: Metro Line 1 to Huangsha or Line 6 to Yide Road, both a short walk away. Parking is in the surrounding streets and is paid by the hour.

  • The retained cinema wall on the western side of the square is the strongest single frame in the development.
  • Late evening on a Friday gives the busiest lanes with the most people, which suits street photography.
  • The lanes are narrow, so a 28 mm or wider is more usable than a zoom.

Working with Pearl River Delta light

The latitude and the coastal humidity in Guangzhou give you long, low sun angles for much of the year, and the haze on a humid afternoon scatters the light into a warm glow that suits the arcades and the Shamian trees. The practical consequence is that you can shoot well into the evening without resorting to high ISO, and that the hardest light to handle is the flat white midday overcast that gives the decorative surfaces on the Chen Clan Ancestral Hall no shadows at all.

A polariser is less useful here than in, say, Shanghai, because much of the light is already diffused. It earns its place on the Pearl River itself, where the surface glare will otherwise blow out any attempt to include the Canton Tower's reflection, and on the wet granite of the temple carvings after rain.

For the interiors, the Guangdong Museum's bronze and jade galleries and the Chen Clan Ancestral Hall's side halls are lit at levels that need either a high ISO or a tripod. The single most useful piece of kit for a Guangzhou photography day is a small tabletop tripod, because so many of the best frames are vertical shots of a hall or a roof ridge.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time of day is best for photographing Canton Tower?

Sunset from the south bank on the Panyu side, working from about an hour before the sun drops. The tower is lit by warm frontal light while the sky behind still holds colour, which is the version most people want. After dark the 1,080 LED nodes and the three sweeping lasers take over, which is a different and more graphic subject, and from the north bank at the Haizhu Bridge you get the tower reflected in the river.

Is the Chen Clan Ancestral Hall easy to photograph inside?

The building itself is easy to reach on Metro Line 1 and the decorative surfaces are the reason to go, but the crowds peak in the middle of the day. Mornings at opening are best. The large brick-carved murals on the east and west outer walls and the ceramic roof sculpture are the details worth finding, and overcast or shaded conditions actually reveal the relief better than direct sun.

Do I need to book the Guangdong Museum in advance?

Yes. The museum requires real-name advance reservation through its WeChat mini program, and it is closed on Mondays except for public holidays. Entry is otherwise free, and the building's terrace and sloping lawn are the main photographic targets, so an afternoon reservation still lets you shoot the exterior in good light.

Which Guangzhou spot works best at night rather than by day?

Shamian Island and the Xiguan arcade streets around Enning Road, because both are lit at night with warm architectural lighting and both have very little through traffic. Shamian's neoclassical consulates and merchant houses are floodlit, and the Enning Road arcades are lit along their length, which gives the covered walkway a strong receding perspective after dark.

Is Shamian Island worth photographing, or is it just a pretty park?

It is genuinely worth it, because of the tree canopy and the 19th-century architecture rather than the island itself. Shamian was filled in from 1859 and leased to Britain and France under the 1861 treaty, which split it four fifths British and one fifth French, and the street plan and building styles still reflect that division. Over 150 old trees remain. The banyan avenue on the north street in early morning light is the image to take.

What about drone photography over the Pearl River?

It is possible in the outer districts but heavily restricted in the central city, and the CBD area around the Guangdong Museum, the Canton Tower and the riverfront is closely controlled. The safer and more interesting options are the elevated terraces and the arcade streets, which give the same layered perspectives without the regulatory problem.
