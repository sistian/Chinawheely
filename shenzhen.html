From Village to Metropolis

Name Origin

The name "Shenzhen" (深圳) means "Deep Ditches," referring to the deep irrigation channels in the area's rice paddies. Before 1980, it was a small fishing village of 30,000 people just across the border from Hong Kong. In 1980, it became China's first Special Economic Zone (SEZ), opening to foreign investment and free-market reforms. Today, it's one of the world's wealthiest cities, home to tech giants like Huawei, Tencent, and DJI.

Education & Universities

Shenzhen has rapidly developed its educational infrastructure with world-class universities. Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech) is ranked as one of China's top young universities, and Shenzhen University is known for its strong engineering and design programs. The city also hosts campuses of international universities like Tsinghua Shenzhen and OCT East Shenzhen. The city's focus on innovation has created a unique educational ecosystem.

Metro & Transportation

Shenzhen's metro system has 17 lines covering 550+ km, making it one of the most efficient in China. The airport line connects Bao'an Airport (SZX) to Futian and Luohu districts in 40 minutes. The city also connects directly to Hong Kong via the Shenzhen Bay Port and Futian Checkpoint. However, with luggage, the metro can be challenging. Our private car service offers door-to-door convenience from the airport to any hotel or business district.

Natural Resources & Environment

Despite its rapid urbanization, Shenzhen has invested heavily in green spaces. The city has over 1,000 parks, including the massive Shenzhen Bay Park along the waterfront. The Wutong Mountains in the east offer hiking trails with views of Hong Kong. The city is located on the Pearl River Delta with a subtropical climate. Shenzhen's air quality is among the best in China's major cities due to strict environmental policies.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts of Shenzhen

Meet the traditional arts that have defined Shenzhen for centuries — and the artisans keeping them alive.

🎭 Cantonese Opera (Yueju)

UNESCO-listed intangible heritage. Though Shenzhen is a young city, Cantonese Opera remains an important cultural tradition brought by migrants from Guangdong. The elaborate costumes, face painting, and unique singing style continue to entertain audiences.

Where to experience it: Shenzhen Cantonese Opera Theatre or the Nanshan Cultural Center. Ask your driver to book tickets.

🎨 Dafen Oil Painting Village

Once the world's largest producer of oil paintings, Dafen Village in Shenzhen is home to thousands of artists who create reproductions of masterpieces and original works. The village has evolved from a copycat hub to a genuine art community with galleries and studios.

Where to find it: Dafen Oil Painting Village in Longgang District. Your driver can take you there for a fascinating art-buying experience.

🎋 Fish Lantern Dance (Shatoujiao)

A traditional folk dance from the Shatoujiao area of Shenzhen, performed during the Spring Festival. Dancers carry fish-shaped lanterns through the streets, symbolizing abundance and good fortune. The tradition reflects Shenzhen's fishing village origins before it became a metropolis.

Where to experience it: Shatoujiao during the Spring Festival, or at the Shenzhen Folk Culture Village.

🧵 Migrant Worker Poetry

A unique cultural phenomenon born from Shenzhen's factory culture. Migrant workers have formed poetry clubs and published anthologies that capture the dreams, struggles, and hopes of China's urbanization. The annual Migrant Worker Poetry Festival celebrates this grassroots literary movement.

Where to find it: Shenzhen Book City or the annual Migrant Worker Poetry Festival events.

🎤 Hakka Mountain Songs

The Hakka people, one of Shenzhen's original ethnic groups, have a tradition of improvised mountain songs that express love, labor, and daily life. These call-and-response songs are sung in the Hakka dialect and are recognized as an intangible cultural heritage.

Where to experience it: Hakka cultural villages in eastern Shenzhen, or during the Shenzhen Folk Culture Festival.

🎨 Shenzhen Design Week

As a UNESCO City of Design, Shenzhen hosts an annual Design Week that showcases innovative product design, architecture, and digital art. The city's design culture reflects its identity as a place where creativity and technology intersect.

Where to experience it: Various venues across the city during April. Check the official Design Week schedule.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Shenzhen

Shenzhen inherited the Cantonese tea habit wholesale, and it is one of the places where the yum cha ritual is most visibly alive: a numbered ticket, a trolley of dim sum, and jasmine, pu-erh or chrysanthemum tea poured at the table.

Cantonese-style morning tea runs from an early start, around 5 or 6 am, until roughly 11 am or half past, and it is now a full-day habit that runs straight through into lunch rather than ending at a fixed hour. The tea houses are two to three storeys tall, and on a weekend you take a number when you arrive and wait for it to be called.

Shenzhen in particular has made this format its own: Cantonese-style morning tea has a broad audience in the city, and the Cantonese kitchen it comes with, light and fresh rather than heavy, now holds the mainstream position in the city's larger restaurants. What sits on the trolley is also standardised, with a single plate normally carrying two to four pieces, so a table of three orders in multiples of two.

The ritual has its own paperwork. In 2022 the practice of drinking tea in Guangfu was entered on the eighth batch of the Guangdong provincial list of intangible cultural heritage, which put a folk habit on paper. The same custom produces the two-piece shorthand of one pot and two dishes, and the old rhyme that a cup of tea cost one li, with 72 li making a silver mill, remembers just how cheap it once was.

☕ Tea Culture & Tea House Guide in Shenzhen

Shenzhen has a long tea tradition. Pu-erh is what you will find most often in the city's tea houses — some are local products, others are the everyday teas carried in from the producing areas. This guide covers where to drink them, what to order, and how the customs work.

☕ Tea Culture

☕

Shenzhen Modern Tea Culture

Venue: the waterfront, Futian CBD, Shekou waterfront teahouses   Hours: Year-round

Shenzhen, as a young cosmopolitan city, has developed a modern tea culture blending traditional Chinese tea with contemporary aesthetics. Many stylish tea bars and boutique teahouses serve single-origin teas from famous growing regions. Premium tea experiences available in the waterfront and Futian financial districts.

☕

Shenzhen Tea Markets - Huaqiangbei Tea City

Venue: Huaqiangbei, Nanshan Tea Market   Hours: Year-round, daily 09:00-18:00

Shenzhen has major tea wholesale markets where tea merchants from across China gather. The Huaqiangbei Tea City and Nanshan Tea Market offer enormous variety of teas including pu-erh, Tieguanyin, Longjing, and white tea at competitive prices with knowledgeable vendors providing tastings.

☕

Shenzhen and Hong Kong Cross-Border Tea Culture

Venue: Shekou, Luohu, Futian food streets   Hours: Year-round

Shenzhen has unique connection to Hong Kong tea culture, with many tea houses serving both Cantonese morning tea and imported Taiwanese and Hong Kong-style milk tea varieties. Cross-border cultural exchange has created distinctive Shenzhen tea scene drawing from multiple traditions.

☕ Herbal & Flower Tea

☕

Shenzhen Health Tea Culture - Liangcha (凉茶)

Venue: Citywide specialty shops especially in old neighborhoods   Hours: Year-round

Traditional Chinese medicinal tea (凉茶, liangcha) is important part of life in humid Pearl River Delta. In Shenzhen, numerous specialty shops sell homemade liangcha for various health concerns. Cooling herbal teas made with chrysanthemum, honeysuckle, and other herbs are consumed daily to balance tropical climate.

⭐ Tea Experiences

⭐

Shenzhen Tea Art and Tea Ceremony Schools

Venue: Nanshan and Futian tea art schools   Hours: Year-round, book in advance

Shenzhen has emerged as center for tea art education with numerous schools teaching traditional Chinese tea ceremony. Schools offer courses in Gongfu Tea ceremony, tea flower arrangement, and tea-related calligraphy. Visitors can participate in tea ceremonies at dedicated tea art centers.
