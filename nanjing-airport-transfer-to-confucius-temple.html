The Confucius Temple quarter of Nanjing sits on the north bank of the Qinhuai River in Qinhuai District, centred on a temple first built in 1034 on the site of an Eastern Jin academy. The complex groups the Confucius shrine, the old prefectural school and the Jiangnan Examination Hall, and the surrounding street market has served the district since the Ming era. Lukou International Airport lies about 38 kilometres south, a drive of roughly 50 minutes on the expressway.

Nanjing Lukou International Airport (NKG)

38 km from the Nanjing terminal, about 50 minutes by car.

Lukou sits in suburban Jiangning District more than 35 kilometres south of the centre. The airport expressway runs north beside the S1 rail corridor, past the southern rail hub, and continues onto the inner ring before dropping into the Qinhuai river quarter where the temple stands on Gongyuan Street north of the river.

Why transfer to the Confucius Temple?

The compound documents the examination culture of late imperial China in one place. The Jiangnan Examination hall was founded in the Southern Song and grew into the largest testing compound of the Ming and Qing dynasties, holding just over 20,000 examination cells at its height; the display areas restore rows of these cells exactly as candidates sat them. The temple proper, rebuilt after Taiping-era destruction in the Tongzhi reign, follows the classical layout with the Dacheng Hall at its core, which charges 30 yuan to enter.

The district around the temple adds the layer that most temple quarters lack. From the Song onward the aristocratic families of the Six Dynasties period settled here, and the streets behind the shrine still operate as a continuous market, the form that gave the quarter its temple-and-bazaar character. During the Spring Festival the Qinhuai lantern fair fills the river banks, the single largest annual event of the Nanjing calendar; the rest of the year the water level is set by the boats and the restored walls of the compound itself.

  • Temple first built in 1034 as an expansion of an Eastern Jin academy
  • Jiangnan Examination Hall grew past 20,000 cells at its Qing-era peak
  • Dacheng Hall admission is 30 yuan; the surrounding streets are free
  • Present buildings date from the Tongzhi reconstruction after Taiping damage
  • Qinhuai lantern fair each Spring Festival is Nanjing's largest annual event

What to do at the Confucius Temple once you arrive

Work the compound from south to north. The screen wall by the river and the archway gate open onto the central axis: Lingxing Gate, the Dacheng Gate, and then Dacheng Hall, where a statue of Confucius and a set of ritual bells and chimes are displayed. Behind it, the Mingde Hall and the old school buildings complete the line. The examination hall occupies the east side, where a small museum explains the quota system that funnelled Jiangnan candidates into the imperial bureaucracy.

The river side is the second half of the visit. Painted pleasure boats run circuits of the Qinhuai between the Wuding Bridge and the Zhanyuan Garden section, and the banks are lined with restored late-Qing shopfronts selling duck dishes and local snacks. Zhan Garden, the oldest surviving garden in the city, lies two blocks east, and the Zhonghua Gate fortress of the Ming city wall stands ten minutes on foot to the south, both worth folding into the same walk.

  • Fuzimiao station on Metro Line 3 and Sanshanjie on Line 1 both border the quarter
  • Entry to the streets is free; tickets apply to Dacheng Hall, the examination hall and boats
  • Evening boat circuits run after the lanterns light, longer hours during festival weeks
  • The quarter is at its densest on weekend evenings; mornings are the quiet window

Arriving at Nanjing Lukou International Airport

Lukou operates two passenger terminals. Terminal 2 opened ahead of the 2014 Youth Olympics after three years of construction and carries the bulk of domestic trunk traffic, while Terminal 1 handles the remainder and international services after its own renovation. The airport counted 19.9 million passengers in 2020 despite the pandemic year and 31.4 million in 2025, ranking it among the ten busiest fields in eastern China.

For a private pickup the driver monitors the landing and moves the meet point to whichever terminal the aircraft docks at, so a last-minute stand change does not matter. Bags and any arrivals checks take around twenty minutes. Two road options exist: the expressway all the way to the southern ring, or the S1 metro line from the terminal basement to the South Railway Station with a swap to Line 1 or 3; the car wins clearly with luggage or when several people travel together.

What the transfer costs

Expect 130 to 190 yuan for a standard saloon between Lukou and the Qinhuai quarter, quoted inclusive of fuel, expressway tolls and a meet-and-greet at arrivals. Five-seat execution is the default; larger groups generally book the business MPV tier at 170 to 240 yuan, which absorbs four adults and full-size suitcases with margin.

Hire that runs beyond a point-to-point drop is priced by duration. A two-hour dwell at the temple fits inside most standard allowances, while a loop that adds Zhonghua Gate and the Ming wall usually tips into half-day territory. Late-night arrivals after the metro stops carry a percentage loading on the base rate, disclosed in the quote rather than added at the kerb.

Gongyuan Street itself is pedestrianised; ask for the drop at the taxi bay on Pingjiangfu Road at the north-east corner of the quarter, which leaves a two-minute walk to the temple axis. Drivers unfamiliar with the quarter should be told the bay name, since several one-way approaches surround it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Lukou Airport from the Confucius Temple?

The temple stands in the Qinhuai river quarter just south of central Nanjing, and the airport sits 35-plus kilometres further south in Jiangning District, so the road distance is around 38 kilometres. The expressway run takes about 50 minutes clear and up to 75 minutes when the southern ring slows at the evening peak.

Can I take the metro from the airport to the temple?

Yes, in two legs. The S1 line leaves the terminal basement for the South Railway Station in roughly 35 minutes; there you change to Line 1 toward Sanshanjie or Line 3 toward Fuzimiao, either of which borders the quarter. Total journey time is close to an hour and a quarter, against about 50 minutes by car in flowing traffic.

How much are tickets at the Confucius Temple?

The streets, the river banks and the lantern-lit evening market are free to walk. Admission to Dacheng Hall, the main hall of the shrine, is 30 yuan, and the Jiangnan Examination Hall and boat circuits are separately priced. Combined tickets covering several parts circulate at the visitor centre on the square.

What was the Jiangnan Examination Hall?

It was the testing compound attached to the temple, founded under the Southern Song and expanded repeatedly until it could seat more than 20,000 candidates in cellular rooms during the Ming and Qing. Provincial graduates across the lower Yangtze sat its triennial sessions. Restored cell rows and a museum on the quota system occupy the site today.

When is the best time to visit the quarter?

Weekday mornings are the calmest for the temple axis and the examination hall. In the evenings the riverside lanterns come on and the boat circuits run later, which is the view the quarter is known for. For the Qinhuai lantern fair, come between the start of the Spring Festival and the fifteenth day, when installations line both banks.
