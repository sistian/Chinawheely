Name Origin

Ganzi (甘孜) is the Tibetan name of the prefecture, traditionally part of the Kham region, and the seat was known as Dajianlu (打箭炉) — 'the archery kiln' — from the Han dynasty until it was renamed Kangding in 1904. The name Kangding, meaning 'the city of peace', was chosen by the Qing court at the same time the Tusi system was abolished and the region was brought under direct Qing administration.

Education & Universities

Higher education is served by Sichuan Minzu College's Garze campus and local vocational colleges, with programmes in teacher training, veterinary science, plateau agriculture and Tibetan medicine.

Getting Around

Getting around Ganzi is by road, and roads are the point. The G4217 Ganzi–Lhasa and G4218 Chengdu–Lhasa expressways now cross the prefecture, the Chengdu–Kangding expressway reaches the seat in about four hours, and the Kangding–Lhasa highway crosses the 4,518 m Zheduo Mountain pass. Kangding Airport (IATA KGT) links the seat with Chengdu and Chongqing, and the Chengdu–Kangding railway is still under construction, so the prefecture is reached by air and road.

Natural Resources & Environment

Ganzi is the largest prefecture-level division in Sichuan — about 149,700 km² of the Hengduan mountains, where the Shaluli range in the west and the Daxue range in the east are separated by the Yalong, Dadu and Jinsha rivers. It has five peaks above 6,000 m, more than 200 above 5,000 m, 2,471 high-altitude lakes and over 200 glaciers, with Mount Gongga at 7,556 m the highest summit in the province. Grassland covers about 60% of the prefecture, and the province's main pastoral region lies here.

Food & Shopping in Ganzi

What to eat and what to bring home from Ganzi

🍽️ Must-Try Ganzi Cuisine

Ganzi's table is the Tibetan plateau table: butter tea (酥油茶) and tsampa (糌粑) for breakfast, yak meat hotpot, Tibetan momo and thukpa noodles, and the dairy of the summer camps — butter, cheese and dried yak milk.

🛍️ Shopping in Ganzi

The county towns of Kangding, Dege and Litang sell the best of Tibetan craft: thangka paintings, silver and turquoise jewellery, hand-woven woollen chuba, prayer wheels, and the butter and yak cheese of the pasture camps.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Butter tea is the drink of the whole prefecture, churned in a wooden churn with yak butter and salt and served in a wooden bowl, refilled all day from the same pot. In the monastery towns, sweet tea with butter is served to visitors after the butter-tea itself.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Ganzi

The living traditions of Ganzi — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

Ganzi is the heart of Kham, and its heritage is world famous: the Kham Kagyu monasteries, the printing house of Dege and its Tibetan-language canon, the wooden villages of the Jueba and Danba valleys, and the crafts of the Tibetan minority itself — thangka painting, silverwork, weaving and the carved mani stones. The Yushu and Litang horse festivals and the kham guozhuang festivals keep the singing and dancing traditions alive.

Ganzi Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Ganzi like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

Altitude here is serious: 3,000 m at the prefecture seat and 4,000 m-plus on the passes. Give the first day a day, drink plenty of water, and don't run on the second day.

🥢 Skip the tourist menu

The best light on Mount Gongga is at dawn from the Moxi or Hailuogou side — a driver who knows where to park is worth more than a guide here.

⏱️ Plan the driving time

Dege's printing house and Jueba hot springs are on the same road from Kangding; combine them in one long day rather than two short ones.

📍 Ask the driver

On the Tibetan plateau, always carry cash and warm layers — even in July, the pass temperatures can drop close to freezing by evening.

Best Time to Visit Ganzi

May to October, with the peak walking season in June and September; winter is for the snow roads and the winter festivals.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · April–May — the passes reopen, the rhododendron and peach blossom break out, and the plateau is clear and cool. (daytime 9.8–16.6°C, nights around -0.6°C; about 70 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the main season, with the grassland at its greenest and the monasteries busiest; rain usually arrives in the evening. (daytime 18.1–20.4°C, nights around 10.1°C; about 139 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — the best weather of the year, with clear air over the passes and the barley harvest in the valleys. (daytime 9–16.7°C, nights around 0°C; about 69 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–March — very cold, with snow on the passes and some roads closed; the winter horse festival in Litang is worth the cold. (daytime 3.9–5.9°C, nights around -5.6°C; about 8 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Ganzi

What to drink in Ganzi, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Ganzi

Butter tea is the drink of the whole prefecture, churned in a wooden churn with yak butter and salt and served in a wooden bowl, refilled all day from the same pot. In the monastery towns, sweet tea with butter is served to visitors after the butter-tea itself.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Ganzi

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Ganzi

🍵 Tea Houses of Ganzi

Tea culture runs through the day in Ganzi: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
