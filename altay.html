Name Origin

Altay means "golden mountain" in the local Turkic tongues, and the name was taken from the Altai Mountains themselves — the range the Turkic peoples have grazed and ridden across since before written history. The area was the country of the Xiongnu and then of the Western Turkic Khaganate, and the Dzungar and Kazakh communities of today descend from those pastoral traditions.

Education & Universities

Higher education is served by Altay Vocational and Technical College and the Altay campus of Xinjiang University of Finance and Economics, with programmes weighted towards teacher training, animal husbandry, veterinary science and border trade.

Getting Around

Getting around Altay is by road, with flights into Altay, Keketuohai and Burqin. The G216 and G217 highways run the length of the prefecture from the Kazakh border in the west to the Mongolian border in the east, and the border crossings at Tachengken, Hongshanzui, Jimunai and Aheituobaike handle the trade with Kazakhstan and Mongolia.

Natural Resources & Environment

Four fifths of the prefecture is mountain. The Altai Mountains rise to 4,374 m at Youyi Peak, the Sawur range runs across the west with Musidao Mountain at 3,835 m, and the rivers all start in the range: the Irtysh, the Ulungur and the Jimunai, with 56 rivers in all. The low point is 317 m at the Three Springs depression in Fuyun, and the lakes include Ulungur Lake, Kanas Lake and Jili Lake. The climate is continental and cold — annual mean temperatures of 0.7 to 4.9 °C, an extreme low of -51.5 °C, and long dry summers with strong sunshine.

Food & Shopping in Altay

What to eat and what to bring home from Altay

🍽️ Must-Try Altay Cuisine

Altay is pastoral country: hand-torn boiled lamb (手抓肉), smoked horse sausage (熏马肠), grilled mutton skewers, and milk products from the summer camps. The Kazakh families are mostly Muslim, so the meat is halal throughout.

🛍️ Shopping in Altay

The Altay, Burqin and Jeminay bazaars sell smoked sausage and dried meat, dried milk and cheese, felt rugs and shyrdak embroidery, and the region's honey and berries. Cash is still the usual way to buy from the pastoral families.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Tea and milk tea (suute han) are the everyday drinks in the Kazakh and Tuvan households, poured with milk and salt from a pot that stands on the stove all day. Green brick tea is the standard.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Altay

The living traditions of Altay — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The prefecture is the homeland of the Kazakh and Tuvan peoples, and their heritage is the reason to come: the Asik song contests with dombra players, the Kurtash wedding and horse-racing customs, the felt shyrdak wall hangings, and the wooden log architecture of the mountain villages. The Kazakh eagle hunter tradition survives in the practice of training golden eagles for hunting in the winter.

Best Time to Visit Altay

June to September for the pastures, the lake and the long daylight; late September for the first frost and the golden larch; December to March for snow and the eagle hunters.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · April–May — the snow is still on the high passes and the mountain roads open slowly; the light is clean and the lakes are frozen. (daytime 1.4–21.9°C, nights around -9.8°C; about 14 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the main season, with green pastures, wildflowers and the lake at its best; nights on the high passes are cold. (daytime 26.6–28.2°C, nights around 13.3°C; about 22 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — the larch and birch turn gold, the light is the clearest of the year and the harvest festivals fill the bazaars. (daytime 1.3–21.2°C, nights around -8.3°C; about 18 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–March — hard cold, deep snow and very few roads open, but this is the season for eagle hunting, dog sledding and the Kazakh New Year. (daytime -9.1–-6.1°C, nights around -20.4°C; about 17 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Altay

What to drink in Altay, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Altay

Tea and milk tea (suute han) are the everyday drinks in the Kazakh and Tuvan households, poured with milk and salt from a pot that stands on the stove all day. Green brick tea is the standard.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Altay

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Altay

🍵 Tea Houses of Altay

Tea culture runs through the day in Altay: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
