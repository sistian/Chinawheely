The Chengdu Panda Base began with six animals rescued from the wild and by the end of 2024 was home to 260 giant pandas. It opened in November 1987 on the low hills of Futou Shan in Chenghua District and expanded from 1,035 mu of ground to 3,570 mu when the new section opened on 1 January 2022. A car transfer from the airport puts you at the south gate before the morning crowd, which matters on a site that caps daily visitors at 85,000.

Chengdu Shuangliu International Airport (CTU)

30 km from the Chengdu terminal, about 45 minutes by car.

Shuangliu sits about 16 km southwest of central Chengdu and the base lies in Chenghua District in the north of the city, so the two ends of the trip point in opposite directions. The route runs up the Airport Expressway, through the western ring road and then north on the Third Ring Road towards the Panda Avenue exit.

Chengdu Tianfu International Airport (TFU)

50 km from the Chengdu terminal, about 60 minutes by car.

Tianfu opened in June 2021 with three runways and two terminals and took the international and cargo services from Shuangliu. It lies southeast of the city, which makes the run to the panda base in the north longer, at roughly 50 km by road.

Why transfer to the Chengdu Panda Base?

This is not a zoo display but a working research and breeding institution, and the difference shows. The site is administered by the Chengdu Municipal Bureau of Parks and Greening, and it also runs a second breeding centre at Dujiangyan called Panda Valley. The expansion that opened in January 2022 more than tripled the grounds, and the newer area is where most of the young animals and the birth houses are. As well as giant pandas the base keeps 201 red pandas, alongside golden snub-nosed monkeys and other endangered species.

The pandas themselves are the reason people travel, and the viewing arrangements are unusually good. The enclosures are set into wooded gullies so that animals can be out of sight when they choose to be, which sounds like a drawback but is exactly what the animals need. The most reliable times to see active pandas are early morning when they are feeding, and late afternoon as the temperature drops. A private car gets you there for the breakfast feed rather than for the mid-morning crush.

  • The base opened in November 1987 and started with six pandas rescued from the wild; the population reached 260 by the end of 2024
  • Grounds expanded from 1,035 mu to 3,570 mu when the new section opened on 1 January 2022
  • Adult admission is 55 yuan, students 27 yuan, and a single-person annual pass is 168 yuan
  • The site caps daily visitors at 85,000 and requires every guest to book online with a real-name registration

What to do at the panda base once you arrive

Enter at the south gate and turn left towards the Moon Hill area, which holds the main giant panda enclosures and the birth houses for the cubs. The second phase opened in 2022 is reached further round and is where the red pandas and the other endangered species live, with a dedicated conservation education centre along the way. The whole walk is around 5 km of paths with plenty of steps, and it takes three to four hours if you stop at every enclosure.

Time of day matters more than anything else here. The pandas are active for feeding early in the morning and again in the late afternoon, and they spend the middle of the day asleep in the trees, which is why midday visits often look empty. The gates open at 07:30 between March and October and at 08:00 from November to February, so arriving at opening time is what gives you the animals actually awake and moving. The second phase is the better bet for cubs and for species other than the giant panda.

  • Every visitor must book online in advance with the ID used for the reservation, and the daily cap is 85,000 people
  • Entry runs 7:30 to 17:00 from March to October, closing at 18:00; from November to February it is 8:00 to 16:30, closing at 17:30
  • Take Chengdu Metro Line 3 to Panda Avenue station and change to the number 408 shuttle bus for the south gate
  • The base is on low hills with long paths, so allow three to four hours and wear shoes with grip

Arriving at Chengdu airport for the panda base

Both of Chengdu's airports work for this trip, but they sit on opposite sides of the city and the distance between them and the base differs by twenty kilometres. Shuangliu is the busier domestic hub and the shorter run. Tianfu is newer and takes the international and cargo services, so most overseas visitors land there and should expect around an hour on the road. Shuangliu has been the city's main gateway since 1957, and with traffic regularly above its designed maximum capacity the international services were moved across to Tianfu when it opened in June 2021.

Allow fifteen minutes for bags and immigration, then meet your driver in the arrivals hall. Because the base requires a timed reservation in your own name, tell the driver your booked entry slot so the timing works; a driver who monitors your flight number will also absorb any delay without you needing to call. Both airports are busy in the morning, so leave the terminal a little ahead of the slot you booked rather than treating it as a fixed start time.

What the transfer costs

The run from Shuangliu to the panda base is typically priced at 120 to 170 yuan for a sedan, and from Tianfu 180 to 240 yuan, with the quote covering fuel, tolls and the driver's waiting time at the gate. A sedan suits one or two people with hold luggage; a seven-seat MPV or van suits a family or a group and comes to roughly 160 to 220 yuan from Shuangliu and 220 to 280 yuan from Tianfu.

Waiting time at the gate is normally included for the first two hours, which covers the first stretch of the walk through the enclosures if you want the driver to meet you at a different gate later. Vehicle options run from a standard sedan through a seven-seat MPV for a family, and the quote is worked out from the airport you are flying into and the class of vehicle you choose rather than from a meter in the car.

Give the driver the south gate as the drop-off, since that is the entrance closest to the parking and to the main panda enclosures. Have the booking reference for your timed ticket ready, because entry checks the ID against the reservation and drivers cannot wait outside the gate with the car for long.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is it from Chengdu airport to the panda base?

The panda base is in Chenghua District in the north of Chengdu. From Shuangliu Airport, which is about 16 km southwest of the centre, the road distance is roughly 30 km and takes about 45 minutes. From Tianfu Airport to the southeast it is closer to 50 km and around an hour, so an international arrival means a longer first leg of the journey.

How much is the panda base ticket and do I need to book in advance?

Adult admission is 55 yuan and students pay 27 yuan; a single-person annual pass costs 168 yuan a year. Every visitor must make a real-name online reservation and show the matching ID at the gate, and the site caps numbers at 85,000 per day. Walk-up tickets exist only if there is spare capacity at the window, so book before you fly.

What time should I arrive to actually see pandas?

Come at opening. The gates open at 07:30 from March to October and at 08:00 between November and February, and the animals are active for feeding in the first hours of the morning. By late morning they are usually asleep in the trees, which is why many midday reviews say there were no pandas to be seen. A late afternoon return is the other good window.

How long does a visit take and how much walking is involved?

Allow three to four hours. The site covers a series of wooded valleys and hills, and the full loop through the giant panda enclosures, the birth houses and the newer second-phase area is roughly 5 km of path with a fair number of steps. If you only want the giant pandas, the first area near the south gate holds most of them and can be done in about ninety minutes.

Can I see baby pandas?

Birth houses are visible from the visitor path and the number of cubs varies with the season, so it is never guaranteed. The most reliable months for young animals are generally autumn, when births peak in the wild as well as in captivity. Keep your voice down near the enclosures, since noise and visitors crowding the viewing windows are the main reason mothers move their cubs away from the front.
