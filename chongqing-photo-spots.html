Overview: Photography in Chongqing

The photographs that are taken in Chongqing are usually taken at 500 metres of altitude, and the ones that are taken in the mountains around it are better. The river city stacks itself in three tiers against the Yangtze and the Jialing, and the karst country west of the city rises to 2,251 metres with snow for four months of the year. What is left between the two is a cliff of Buddhist sculpture and a fortress at the head of a gorge. The whole municipality covers about 80,000 square kilometres, and the subject in every case is the gap between the river below and the mountain above.

The Three Natural Bridges (天生三桥)

The three natural bridges at Xianvushan in Wulong district stand over the Yangshui river, a tributary of the Wujiang, and were entered as part of the South China Karst on the UNESCO World Natural Heritage list on 7 June 2007. The protected core covers about 22 square kilometres and includes the three bridges, the Qinglong and Shenying sinkholes, the Longshui Gorge, the Chishiyuan and Xishi yuan sinkholes, the cave system of the Seventy-two Forks with the Longquan Cave, and the karst canyon of the river. The proportions are what the photographs have to carry: from the upper end of the Tianlong Bridge to the lower end of the Heilong Bridge the straight-line distance is only 1,500 metres, and the two sinkholes between the bridges measure 276 to 285 metres in depth and 300 to 522 metres across.

best light: Late morning, when the light reaches the bottom of the sinkhole and still rakes across the bridge faces. · karst landscape

Getting there: In Wulong district, 180 kilometres from the centre of the city, with the walking route entered at the visitor centre and the exit reachable by the internal transport and a lift.

  • The three bridges are crossed in sequence and the light changes completely between them, so keep the walk going rather than stopping at the first one.
  • The sinkhole floor and the bridge deck are far apart in scale, which is what a wide lens is needed for.
  • The exit lift saves the last climb, which matters after two and a half hours of steps.

The Carvings at Baodingshan (宝顶山石刻)

The Dazu cliff sculptures were begun in 650 and run through the late Tang, the Five Dynasties, the two Song dynasties and the Ming and Qing, with more than 70 sites and more than 100,000 statues in five clusters, of which Baodingshan is the largest and the most concentrated. The cliff here is not a cave wall but a sheer rock face with a walkway cut along it, and the relief is carved in high relief into the softer beds and modelled in the harder ones. Baodingshan and Beishan were listed as national key cultural relics in 1961 and the whole group was inscribed as a World Heritage site in 1999, and the programme, while Buddhist in subject, is the leading example of the fusion of the three teachings in late Chinese cave art.

best light: Raking side light in the morning, which reaches into the recesses under the overhangs. · cliff sculpture

Getting there: In Dazu district, 90 kilometres west of the city, with the cliff reached on foot along a single walk of about two kilometres with the numbered groups in sequence.

  • The figures sit under overhangs, so side light reaches deeper into them than a frontal view does.
  • The Thousand-Armed Avalokitesvara is the group people come for; the thirty or so around it are why to stay.
  • The path is level and the wall is vertical, so the problem is the light rather than the walking.

Baidi City over the Qutang Gorge (白帝城)

The fortress stands on the north bank of the Yangtze, 8 kilometres east of Fengjie, at the mouth of the Qutang Gorge, with the mountain behind and water on three sides. It was announced as a sixth-batch national key cultural relic on 25 May 2006. The name comes from Gongsun Shu, the warlord of Shu, who built a fortress there and took the title of Bai Di when a well gave off white vapour like a white dragon, and the temples built on the site include shrines to Zhuge Liang and to Liu Bei, who in the second month of 223 entrusted his son and his kingdom to Zhuge Liang from this place. The gate carries three characters written by Guo Moruo, and Du Fu's lines on the region are cut on the stone. Since the Three Gorges project raised the water, the site has become an approach by boat with water on three sides.

best light: Late afternoon, when the gorge wall behind the fortress is in shadow and the terraces are lit. · cliff fortress

Getting there: In Fengjie county, 280 kilometres upriver from the centre of the city, reached by road or by excursion boat on the Yangtze.

  • The steps from the landing to the gate are the steepest part of the visit and the light changes as you climb.
  • The gorge wall behind the terraces is in shadow for most of the day, so the terraces are the lit half.
  • With the raised water level, the boat approach is now part of the composition rather than a way in.

The Geleshan Prison and Cemetery (歌乐山与烈士陵园)

Geleshan is a 693-metre peak in Shapingba district, a karst hill of limestone with old coal workings under it, and it carries the heaviest concentration of memorial sites inside the city. On the hill are the former site of the Sino-American Special Technical Cooperation Office, the secret intelligence headquarters of the war years, the prisons of Baigongguan and Zhazidong from the second civil war, preserved in the state they were left, and the Geleshan Martyrs' Cemetery, where the martyrs of the anti-Japanese war and of the liberation struggle are buried. The hill is a short drive from the centre of the municipality and the local chilli chicken takes its name from it, which is the one thing about the place that is not a memorial.

best light: Any light; the subjects are buildings, stonework and the terraces of a hill. · memorial site

Getting there: In Shapingba district, 12 kilometres from the centre, with the three sites a short walk apart along the road up the hill.

  • The prison buildings are preserved as they were, which photographs very differently from a restored monument.
  • The cemetery and the prison sites are separate, and the walk between them is where the hill itself comes into the frame.
  • Early morning is quiet; the midday buses bring the school groups.

The Waterfalls of Simian Mountain (四面山)

Simian Shan lies in Jiangjin district on the northern spur of the Dalou mountains, 130 kilometres from the centre of Chongqing and 90 kilometres from the seat of Jiangjin. It covers 213.37 square kilometres between 1,000 and 1,500 metres, with the highest point, Wugongling, at 1,709.4 metres and the lowest at 560, and it holds 128 core scenic points. The waterfalls are the defining feature, more than 100 of them and eight of around a hundred metres, the largest being the Wangxiangtai fall at 152 metres high and 40 metres wide, the highest in China. The area was listed as the third batch of national key scenic areas in 1994 and rated a national 5A site in October 2015, and its primeval forest is described as a natural gene bank of wild plant resources.

best light: Late summer for volume, October to December for the colour of the forest around the water. · waterfall and forest

Getting there: In Simian Mountain town, Jiangjin district, 130 kilometres from the city, with internal buses running the loop between the five core sections.

  • The Wangxiangtai fall needs a low viewpoint and a wide frame; 152 metres does not fit in a normal lens length.
  • The internal transport is the practical way round the loop, and the forest between the sections is half the subject.
  • The colour of the forest in late autumn is the reason to come in the low season.

The Cliffs of Jinfo Mountain (金佛山)

Jinfo Shan stands in Nanchuan district on the Dalu range and is built from three peaks, Jinfo, Qingba and Baizhi, with the main summit Fengchui Ling at 2,251 metres carrying snow for four months of the year. It was inscribed as a World Natural Heritage site for its biodiversity and its karst landforms, and it holds eight national designations, among them a national key scenic area, a national forest park and a national nature reserve. The name records what happens on the mountain rather than what it looks like: on a clear evening in late summer the low sun turns the layered cliffs gold, and the range resembles a row of golden Buddhas, which is the origin of the name and of the famous sunset over the sea of clouds.

best light: The hour before sunset on a clear evening in late summer, when the cliff face turns gold. · mountain and cliff face

Getting there: In Nanchuan district, 180 kilometres from the centre of the city, with the internal transport reaching the summit section from the scenic gate.

  • The golden cliff light is a clear late summer evening, not a winter one, so the two best photographs need different trips.
  • The snow season from December to March is the least crowded time and the best for the summit.
  • The sunset needs a clear western horizon, which is why the forecast matters more than the hour.

The Karst Canyon of the Yangshui River (羊水河喀斯特峡谷)

The river that runs through the three bridges and the two sinkholes at Wulong is the Yangshui, and its canyon is part of the same protected area, listed alongside the bridges, the sinkholes, the Longshui Gorge, the seventy-two caves and the karst gorge of the river. It is the part of the site that most visitors drive past, because the walking route between the bridges is a separate path, and it is the part that carries the vertical scale, with the cliff faces rising straight out of the water and the river reading as a thread far below. Together with the Longquan Cave, the longest cave in Asia, it is the reason the protected core was drawn at 22 square kilometres rather than around the three bridges alone.

best light: Late afternoon, when the canyon walls take the last light and the water below stays in shadow. · river canyon

Getting there: Within the Wulong scenic area, 180 kilometres from the city, reached by the internal transport and the paths that connect the upper and lower sections.

  • The canyon reads best from the upper paths, where the drop is visible in the frame rather than implied.
  • The Longquan Cave section is the one that needs a torch and a slower shutter.
  • The relationship between the river and the bridges is clearest on the descent into the sinkhole, so shoot it on the way down and not only on the way up.

Gear and planning for photographing Chongqing

The mountains are the equipment driver. At Wulong the subject is a 500-metre sinkhole seen from a walkway, at Jinfo Mountain it is a 2,251-metre summit, and at Simian Mountain a 152-metre waterfall: all three need either a wide lens and a vertical frame, or a telephoto and a tripod, and none of them are comfortable with a single zoom. A 16-35mm for the sinkholes and the gorge, a 70-200mm for the cliff faces and the snow, and a small tripod for the low-light work at Baidi City and the canyon. The cliffs at Dazu and the carvings need nothing special except a short focal length for the recesses and the patience to wait for the side light, which is the whole technique there.

Plan around the altitude and the road, because the two together set the day's shape. The four destinations over ninety kilometres away are all in the same mountain system but on different sides of it, and the sun sets behind the western expressway traffic, so the late returns are the slow ones. Baidi City is the exception: it is upriver, on the river roads, and it is 280 kilometres of the same valley, which makes it a full day rather than a trip. Between them the city offers the hill at Geleshan, 12 kilometres out, and the riverfront, and both work when the mountains are shut by weather or when the day is only half a day long.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most photogenic place in Chongqing?

The Three Natural Bridges at Wulong, where the UNESCO World Heritage listing of 7 June 2007 covers about 22 square kilometres of bridges, sinkholes, caves and canyon. The three spans cross only 1,500 metres of the Yangshui river, and the two sinkholes between them measure 276 to 285 metres deep and up to 522 metres across. The photograph that works is the vertical one, from the walkway above the sinkhole floor.

Are there carvings worth photographing?

Dazu, and they are a different subject from the Buddhist cave art people expect. The group was begun in 650, runs through the Tang and the Song, and numbers more than 100,000 statues at more than 70 sites in five clusters. The cliff at Baodingshan is a sheer rock face with a walkway cut along it, the relief is carved in high relief into the softer beds, and the programme unites the three teachings rather than illustrating one school.

What about snow in a subtropical city?

Jinfo Mountain in Nanchuan district reaches 2,251 metres at Fengchui Ling and holds snow for four months of the year, which makes it a winter trip in December to March. The same mountain is also the place for the golden sunset on the layered cliffs on clear late summer evenings, which is what gave it its name, so the two best photographs need different seasons.

Can I photograph anything at the river?

The Yangshui canyon inside the Wulong protected area is the river subject, with the cliff faces rising straight out of the water and the Longquan Cave, the longest in Asia, in the same area. Baidi City is the other river subject, 280 kilometres upriver at the mouth of the Qutang Gorge, where the fortress sits with water on three sides and excursion boats now reach it after the Three Gorges project raised the level.

Is there anything to shoot close to the city?

Geleshan, 12 kilometres out in Shapingba district: a 693-metre karst hill with the former Sino-American Special Technical Cooperation Office, the prisons of Baigongguan and Zhazidong and the Geleshan Martyrs' Cemetery on it. It is three hours, it works in any weather, and the preserved prison buildings photograph differently from the restored monuments elsewhere in the country.

How big is Chongqing?

About 80,000 square kilometres, with a municipality of some thirty million people in the middle of it, which is why the distances here are mountain distances rather than the flat-country kind. Wulong, Dazu, Simian Mountain and Jinfo Mountain are all between 90 and 180 kilometres from the centre but sit on opposite sides of the same range, and Baidi City at 280 kilometres up the Yangtze is a full day in a different landscape.
