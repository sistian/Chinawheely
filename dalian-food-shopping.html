Overview: Eating and Shopping in Dalian

Dalian was laid out three times: as a Russian radial plan in 1899, as a Japanese grid after the Russo-Japanese war, and as a Chinese commercial city after 1945. That layered history sits underneath a kitchen built on the meeting of the Yellow Sea and the Bohai. This page covers six foods, one historic restaurant and four shopping streets, each with a measurement, a date or a documented standard attached.

What to eat in Dalian

Dalian seafood cuisine (大连海鲜)

The city's standard is seafood cooked with Shandong technique, and the classic plates keep their quantities on the page: red-roasted prawns are scored, pan-fried with ginger and scallion, then simmered with Shaoxing wine, sugar and salt until the sauce reduces; scored fish is steamed for 15 minutes; and a banquet dish uses six kinds of seafood, with sea cucumber and scallop standing in for two figures.

Where: Restaurants across Zhongshan, Xigang and Shahekou districts, with the heaviest concentration around Shengli Square and Qingniwa.

Dalian menzi starch cake (大连焖子)

A street snack of sweet potato starch loosened with a little potato starch, set into a cake and slow-fried in a pan, then dressed with garlic oil, soy sauce and sesame paste. The cake must keep some springiness. Restaurants have since folded the local seafood into it as a 3-seafood menzi, and a bronze sculpture of a woman frying menzi stands in the Dalian Modern Museum.

Where: Old-city snack counters in Zhongshan District, including the traditional shop Qunying Lou on Tianjin Street.

Sugar drum flatbread (糖鼓火烧)

A flatbread traced to the late Qing period, made from a white flour dough with white or brown sugar worked into the layers, then baked over a hearth until the skin is thin, crisp and golden with sesame. The 2 halves separate around the sugar filling into a drum-like hollow. It is on Dalian's municipal intangible cultural heritage list, and in 2014 it was sold in only 2 shops.

Where: Lüshunkou District, where in 2014 it was still sold in only two shops, both in the Shuishiying subdistrict.

Stinky tofu rolled in cold noodle (臭豆腐冷面卷)

A Dalian street snack that appeared around 2008, when fried stinky tofu skewers were rolled into grilled cold noodles, a northeast standard. The cold noodle is prepared in an oil bath rather than on a flat griddle, so the finished roll stays soft and sticky rather than dry. It is also called fried cold noodle wrapped in stinky tofu, or cold noodle with tofu.

Where: Evening street stalls across the central districts, particularly the lanes behind Tianjin Street and around Shengli Square.

Iron-plate grilled squid (铁板烤鱿鱼)

Squid grilled whole or cut into strips, usually small ones about the size of a hand, laid on the grill with chilli and threaded onto skewers, 1 squid to a skewer. Where coal smoke is restricted, the squid is instead fried on an iron plate, which produces a comparable result and suits shoppers who eat while walking. Vendors set up around fishing harbours, where better squid comes from open water.

Where: Harbour-side and night-market stalls along the coast, with additional pitches on the approach streets to Zhongshan Square and Qingniwa.

Dalian sea cucumber (大连海参)

The species that anchors many local banquet tables, used in the Eight Immortals Crossing the Sea for the black-robed figure in one version of the recipe. Sea cucumber is a cold-water echinoderm: four types of tentacle arrangement are recognised, animals carry between 10 and 20 tentacles, and one rough species is recorded with roughly 20 million internal ossicles, which is why the skin feels coarse.

Where: Seafood restaurants and dried-goods dealers throughout Dalian, with the Jinshitan and Zhuanghe coastal zones the best known source areas.

Qunying Lou old-style Dalian cooking (群英楼)

One of the city's oldest named eating houses, founded in the early twentieth century and originally a small snack shop serving Dalian laocai, a local cooking style close to the Fushan branch of Shandong cuisine. It was converted into an ordinary restaurant during the Cultural Revolution and had its old-name status restored in 1983. It now sits among the long line of food outlets on Tianjin Street.

Where: Tianjin Street in Zhongshan District, where the street's buildings are almost entirely commercial premises.

Where to shop in Dalian

Tianjin Street (天津街)

Dalian's oldest commercial district, laid out by the Japanese in 1909 and originally 1,720 metres long, later trimmed to 1,054 metres in a 2003 rebuild. The finished street was 16 metres wide with a 9-metre carriageway, was closed to vehicles in 1983, was named one of the country's ten best commercial streets in 2005, and drew 60 million yuan of district support in 2009.

Where: Zhongshan District, curving around Zhongshan Square north of Youhao Square, east of Dalian railway station.

Qingniwa Bridge quarter (青泥洼桥)

The trading heart of the old city, built around a wooden bridge over a stream where a fishing village had stood in the Ming and Qing periods. The Japanese rebuilt it in concrete as the Tokiwabashi Bridge in 1905 and raised the Changpan Bridge Market, now part of the Dashang group, and the Mitsukoshi store nearby. Dalian people still call the district street life.

Where: Zhongshan District around Shengli Square, immediately north of Dalian railway station, with an underground shopping passage.

Russian Style Street (俄罗斯风情街)

A rebuilt historic quarter known in Russian as Rossiya, 500 metres of street north of Shengli Bridge that was renamed Tuanjie Street after 1945. The redevelopment began in 1999 and finished in October 2000, restoring eight Russian-period buildings including the former mayor's residence and adding six new ones. In 2001 the restored blocks opened with matryoshka dolls, seafood and jewellery stalls.

Where: Tuanjie Street in Zhongshan District, north of Shengli Bridge, now used as the Dalian Municipal Art Museum.

Xi'an Road commercial district (西安路)

Dalian's western commercial centre, built along a road 2.1 kilometres long running south to Heping Square and north to Shahekou station, with the retail stretch from Xinggong Street to Huanghe Road. Parimont arrived in 1997, Carrefour opened the city's first branch in 2000, the Jinhui mall followed in 2001, and the Changxing seafood market opened in 2002.

Where: Shahekou District, several kilometres west of the Qingniwa quarter, along the Xi'an Road corridor.

Eating and shopping in Dalian: practical notes

Plan around two clusters. The historic group sits south of Shengli Bridge in Zhongshan District, where Tianjin Street, Qingniwa and the Russian Style Street all sit within a short walk of one another, and where the mall-heavy Xi'an Road district begins a few kilometres further west. Qingniwa is the older of the two commercial areas, a 1990s note in the city's own records describes Xi'an Road as the newer district ranked second only to Qingniwa's long-established centre.

Seafood standards in the city are written down. A Dalian Cuisine and Cooking Operations Standard for seafood was issued on 22 January 2011 and was approved as a Liaoning provincial standard in September of that year, with a separate standard for pork dishes published on 30 November. Ordering from a restaurant that works to these rules is the most direct way to check that the portion sizes match the published specifications.

Frequently Asked Questions: Booking in Dalian

What seafood is Dalian known for?

Seafood dominates the local table because the city sits where the Yellow Sea meets the Bohai. The canonical dishes are red-roasted prawns, scored steamed fish served after 15 minutes in the steamer, and a banquet preparation called Eight Immortals Crossing the Sea that uses six kinds of seafood. Local sea cucumber, which appears in that banquet dish, is the country's best-known marine product from the region.

How long is Dalian's oldest shopping street?

Tianjin Street in Zhongshan District was Dalian's first commercial district and was laid out by the Japanese in 1909. It originally ran 1,720 metres and was reduced to 1,054 metres during a 2003 reconstruction. It was closed to motor vehicles in 1983, was rated one of the country's ten famous commercial streets in 2005, and received 60 million yuan in district support in 2009.

What is Dalian's menzi and why is it on the street food list?

Menzi is a starch cake of sweet potato starch loosened with a little potato starch, slow-fried in a pan and topped with garlic oil, soy sauce and sesame paste, with springiness as the key test of a good one. It was long a Dalian street snack, and restaurants have since added local seafood to it as three-seafood menzi. A bronze of a woman frying menzi stands in the Dalian Modern Museum.

Where can I see Dalian's Russian architectural heritage?

The Russian Style Street is a 500-metre historic quarter north of Shengli Bridge, laid out by Russian engineers after 1898 and called Rossiya during the Japanese occupation. It was rebuilt between 1999 and October 2000, restoring eight Russian-period buildings including the old mayor's residence and adding six new ones. Restoration was criticised in 2012 for altering the original facades of the colonial-era buildings.

Is there a quieter local snack worth seeking out?

Two municipal listings give an idea. The sugar drum flatbread dates to the late Qing period and is on Dalian's intangible cultural heritage list; in 2014 it was still being sold in only two shops, both in the Shuishiying subdistrict of Lüshunkou. The stinky tofu and cold noodle roll, by contrast, is a recent local form that only appeared around 2008, when fried tofu skewers were wrapped in grilled cold noodles and fried rather than griddled.
