Wuhan has three rivers and two historic towns, and Yellow Crane Tower stands on Snake Hill on the Wuchang side of the Yangtze, about 30 km from Tianhe Airport across the northern half of the city. The tower itself is a 51.4 m concrete reconstruction built between 1981 and 1985, on a site a kilometre from where the original stood for sixteen centuries. Arriving by car puts you at the mountain gate with the tower's five upturned eaves right above you, a far better first impression than circling the block looking for a car park.

Wuhan Tianhe International Airport (WUH)

30 km from the Wuhan terminal, about 40 minutes by car.

Tianhe sits about 26 km north of the city centre in Huangpi District. The route runs south on the Airport Expressway, then the expressway ramp onto Jiefang Avenue, dropping you near the Wuchang riverfront below Snake Hill.

Why transfer to Yellow Crane Tower?

The tower is the anchor of a literary rather than a religious site. Cui Hao's eight-line poem about the migratory crane that stopped here on its way north, written in the Tang period, made the name famous, and Li Bai, Bai Juyi and Lu You all wrote about it afterwards. More than a dozen emperors left their own inscriptions on the structure. What stands today is a modern concrete frame clad to look like Qing timber construction, but the floors inside hold calligraphy, carved steles and murals that keep the Tang and Song poem cycle alive.

The climb is short but generous in reward: five external storeys give the illusion of height while the interior hides nine levels, the extra floors carrying exhibitions on the tower's rebuilding history and on the poets who wrote it. The second floor holds the record of the tower carved in stone, and the third carries portraits of the Tang and Song poets with their verses cut into the walls around them. From the upper galleries you look over the Yangtze towards the Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge, which took the original tower's footprint as the start of its approach viaduct in 1957.

Snake Hill and the tower park form a 150 mu site, and the surrounding Wuchang district deserves an extra hour. Shouyi Alley, Hubu Alley and the Snake Hill path climb the hill behind the tower for free, and a walk down to the river puts you at the wharves where sightseeing boats depart for the islands in the middle of the Yangtze.

  • The current tower was built in 1981 to 1985 on Snake Hill, one kilometre from the original site
  • Five storeys outside, nine levels inside, with a 51.4 m ridgeline over the Wuchang district
  • Tianhe Airport is about 26 km north of the city centre, roughly 30 km from the tower gate
  • The riverfront and the old Wuchang lanes below the hill are a five-minute walk from the tower

What to do at Yellow Crane Tower once you arrive

Arrive at the eastern gate in the morning if you can. The ticket hall opens at 08:30 and the queue at the main entrance builds sharply after 10:00, when tour coaches from Wuhan Tianhe and the cruise terminals begin arriving. The climb itself takes twenty minutes if you read every panel, and the third floor is the one worth slowing down in, since it holds the portrait murals of the poets whose verses are cut into the walls around them.

After the tower, cross the road to the riverbank and walk west along the embankment towards the bridge. The old customs house building and the row of early Republican-era brick houses near Shouyi Alley are now small cafes and craft shops, and the wharf below Snake Hill runs sightseeing boats onto the Yangtze. A late afternoon here, with the light coming off the water towards the tower, is the reason most people choose this particular landmark over any other in Wuhan.

  • Metro Line 5 reaches Simenkou Yellow Crane Tower station, a few minutes' walk from the south gate
  • Adult admission is 70 yuan, with a lower rate if you buy on the mobile ticketing app a day ahead
  • The ticket hall closes in the early evening, so plan the climb before the afternoon meal
  • Waterfront food stalls along the embankment are informal; the riverside is busiest after 18:00

Arriving at Wuhan Tianhe International Airport

Tianhe is in Huangpi District, about 26 km north of the city centre, and it is a two-terminal airport handling well over 27 million passengers a year. Domestic flights mostly use Terminal 2, while Terminal 3 carries the long-haul and regional services. The drive south into Wuchang crosses the ring road and can take 45 minutes or more in heavy traffic, and because the tower's own car park fills early on weekends, being dropped at the gate is genuinely more convenient than parking on the street.

The WUH to Yellow Crane Tower transfer is a short intercity run priced on distance and car class, and the quote covers fuel, tolls and the driver's waiting time at the gate while you are inside the tower. A sedan suits a couple or a single traveller with light luggage; a van suits a family, a group, or anyone who would rather the driver waited through the whole visit than come back for you.

Frequently Asked Questions: Booking in Wuhan Airport Transfer To Yellow Crane Tower

How far is it from WUH airport to Yellow Crane Tower?

Tianhe Airport lies about 26 km north of Wuhan city centre, and the tower is on the Wuchang side of the Yangtze in the south of the city, so the full road distance is roughly 30 km. Expect 40 minutes with clear traffic and closer to an hour during the morning rush or on a wet afternoon on the expressway.

How much is the ticket to Yellow Crane Tower and do I need to book ahead?

The adult admission price is 70 yuan, with students, senior citizens aged 60 to 65 and children aged 6 to 18 paying a reduced rate of 35 yuan. Visitors over 65 and children under 6 are admitted free. Buying on the official mobile app a day before the visit lowers the adult price, and the ticket covers both the tower and the surrounding park.

Which metro line goes to Yellow Crane Tower?

Line 5 of the Wuhan Metro serves Simenkou Yellow Crane Tower station, which puts you within a short walk of the south side of the tower park. Line 5 also connects at Xunlimen with Line 7, so it is straightforward to combine the tower with a trip onwards to the Yellow Crane Tower district from the airport rail link into the city.

Is the original Yellow Crane Tower site worth visiting as well?

The original site is a low mound of stone on the riverbank about a kilometre south of the present tower, close to where the approach viaduct of the Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge begins. There is little to see beyond a marker and the river view, so most visitors spend their time at the rebuilt tower and then walk down the hill to the embankment instead.

How long does the visit take?

Allow an hour and a half to two hours for the tower itself, including the climb and the exhibitions on each floor, and another hour for the Snake Hill paths and the riverside below. Combined with the drive from the airport, a half-day is the sensible minimum, and a full day leaves room for the Hubei Provincial Museum across the road.
