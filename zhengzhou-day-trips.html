Overview: Day Trips from Zhengzhou

Zhengzhou stands in north-central Henan on the southern bank of the Yellow River, where the valley of the Xiong'er Mountains opens out into the North China Plain, and it has been continuously inhabited for more than 4,000 years. The city is a rail and road hub rather than a scenic one, which is exactly why it works as a base: the Shaolin Temple and the whole Songshan massif lie 80 kilometres to the west, a fortress village lies 50 kilometres to the north-west, and two museums and a memorial tower stand within the ring road.

Shaolin Temple (少林寺)

Shaolin Temple stands beneath the Five Breast Peaks of Shaoshi Mountain in the heart of the Songshan range, at the northern foot of the massif in Dengfeng. It was founded in the nineteenth year of the Taihe era of the Northern Wei, 495, to settle the Indian monk Batuo, and it is now more than 1,500 years old. It is the ancestral court of the Chan school in Chinese Buddhism and today belongs to the Caodong school. Within the group of buildings called the Centre of Heaven and Earth, the temple, its pagoda forest, the Chuzu Hermitage and the permanent residence quarters were inscribed as a World Heritage site. Shaolin is a national 5A rated attraction.

How the day runs: Leave Zhengzhou at 07:00 for the 80-kilometre run west to Dengfeng and arrive at the ticket office by 08:30. The complex is large and the entrance is some distance from the courtyard, so the internal shuttle and the walking route between the pagoda forest, the Chuzu Hermitage and the main halls take most of the morning. The Chuzu Hermitage, above the temple in the ravine, was rebuilt in the Song period and is the earlier and quieter part of the site. The pagoda forest holds the stupa of the eminent monks from the Tang and later periods. Leave at 14:30 for the return, which is about 100 minutes, and be back in the city by 17:00.

80 km from Zhengzhou · about 100 min by car from Zhengzhou · full-day trip · best in April to June and September to October; the mountain paths close in heavy winter weather.

  • The temple, its pagoda forest, the Chuzu Hermitage and the permanent residence quarters are the group inscribed as the Centre of Heaven and Earth World Heritage site, and the four are spread over a wide area.
  • Construction began in 495 for the Indian monk Batuo, which is why the founding date is 1,500 years further back than the visible buildings of the present complex.
  • The pagoda forest is the quietest section and holds the stupa of eminent monks from the Tang and later periods.

Songshan Mountain (嵩山)

Songshan, called Waifang, Songgao or Chongshan in the earliest records, is the central peak of the Five Great Mountains and belongs to the Funiu range. It covers about 450 square kilometres, divided into the Shaoshi and Taishi sections, with 72 peaks, the highest Liantian Peak at 1,512 metres and the main summit Junji Peak at 1,492 metres. It entered the first group of national key scenic areas in 1982, was evaluated as a UNESCO Global Geopark on 13 February 2004, and the Songshan Shaolin scenic area was approved as a national 5A attraction in May 2007. Its geology explains the listing: rock from the Archean to the Cenozoic is exposed together, which Chinese geologists call five generations in one place.

How the day runs: A full day and a walking day, not a sightseeing stop. Drive out at 06:30 and reach the mountain road by 08:10. Choose one section rather than both: the Taishi side carries the Songyang Academy and the Junji summit with the cableway to the upper ridge, while the Shaoshi side carries the temple, the Three Sages Monastery and the steep canyon paths below the Five Breast Peaks. The Taishi route works well as a 07:00 start with the cableway, the summit walk and the descent through the Songyang Academy area. Leave by 14:00 and be back in Zhengzhou around 16:30, since the roads out of the valley fill with traffic in the late afternoon.

70 km from Zhengzhou · about 95 min by car from Zhengzhou · full-day trip · best in May to October, with the clearest air on the ridges in the autumn.

  • The massif has 72 peaks, with Liantian Peak at 1,512 metres and Junji Peak at 1,492 metres, so the two sections are not interchangeable in a single day.
  • The exposure of rock from the Archean to the Cenozoic is the basis of the geopark listing of 2004 and explains the exposed ridges along the walking routes.
  • The mountain roads out of Dengfeng become congested after 14:00, so leaving early in the afternoon is faster than leaving late.

Songyang Academy (嵩阳书院)

Songyang Academy stands three kilometres north of the Dengfeng county seat at the southern foot of Songshan, facing south toward the Shuangxi river and backed by the main peak Junji. It is one of the four great academies of ancient China, alongside Shangqiu, Yuelu and Bailudong. The site began as a Buddhist monastery called Songyang Si, founded in the eighth year of the Taihe era of the Northern Wei, 484, and it was at its height with several hundred monks. It was converted to a Daoist temple in the Sui dynasty, and in 1035 it became an academy and took its present name. The surviving Qing layout covers 9,984 square metres and 106 buildings in five courtyards.

How the day runs: Pair this with the mountain rather than treating it as a separate drive, since it lies inside the Songshan scenic area on the Taishi side. Allow ninety minutes for the academy itself, which is enough to walk the five courtyards in order: the gate, the Hall of the Sages, the lecture hall, the Hall of the Taoist Lineage and the library building. The gate plaque is a modern copy of a hand by Su Shi, and the flanking couplet was written by the Qianlong emperor in 1750. Finish with the large cypresses in the front courtyard and the stele on the southern side of the gate, then continue to the summit path or return to the vehicle.

72 km from Zhengzhou · about 95 min by car from Zhengzhou · half-day trip · best in March to May and September to November; the courtyards are exposed and unshaded in high summer.

  • The site has three distinct identities in its history: a Buddhist monastery from 484, a Daoist temple in the Sui, and an academy from 1035, which is one of the four great academies of China.
  • The surviving complex covers 9,984 square metres with 106 buildings in five courtyards, and the layout dates from the Qing period rather than the Song.
  • The plaque over the gate is a modern copy of a hand by Su Shi, and the couplet on the pillars was written by the Qianlong emperor in 1750.

Henan Museum (河南博物院)

The Henan Museum is one of the country's national key museums and a large modern complex on the Jinshui district's Cultural Road, with a collection of more than 130,000 objects. Its foundation goes back to 1927, when the excavation of over 100 bronze vessels from a tomb at Xinzheng led to a museum being set up in Kaifeng, renamed the National Museum in 1928 and restored to the name Henan Museum in 1930. The present building opened in 1997, after the collection had moved from Kaifeng to Zhengzhou in 1961. The holdings come chiefly from excavations at Xichuan, Luoyang, Sanmenxia, Huixian, Xinzheng and Anyang, and are strongest in prehistoric material, Shang and Zhou bronzes, ceramics, jades and painted tomb walls.

How the day runs: The museum works as a half-day, and the collections are best handled in sections rather than at once. The building has nine exhibition halls on the second floor and the central hall is given over to the painted walls of the tomb of a Han dynasty prince, which are the reason most visitors come. The bronze galleries cover the Shang and Zhou periods and draw on the Xinzheng finds of 1923, and the jade and ceramic halls can be reached from the same landing. Give it two hours and take a taxi to the centre afterwards, since the museum is 6 kilometres from the old city. If the afternoon is free, the Erqi memorial tower is 10 minutes further on.

6 km from Zhengzhou · about 20 min by car from Zhengzhou · half-day trip · best in All year; the galleries are conditioned and work as a wet-weather or high-summer option.

  • The collection exceeds 130,000 objects, with more than 5,000 classed as national first or second grade, which makes the museum second in China by holdings after the Palace Museum.
  • The painted tomb walls of a Han dynasty prince were taken from the tomb at Xichuan, one of the sources named in the museum's own record of where its collection came from.
  • The current building opened in 1997, but the museum dates from 1927 and spent its early decades in Kaifeng before moving to Zhengzhou in 1961.

Erqi Memorial Tower (二七纪念塔)

The Erqi Memorial Tower stands in the middle of the city at Erqi Square and was completed on 29 September 1971 to mark the strike of 7 February 1923. It is a reinforced concrete structure 63 metres high and 14 storeys, of which the base takes three and the shaft eleven, with a bell tower on top carrying six clocks, each 2.7 metres in diameter, and a glass red star at the summit. Its plan is two pentagons joined east to west, so it reads as a single tower from the east and west and as a pair from north and south. A wooden hexagonal tower of 21 metres stood there from 1953 until it collapsed in 1971.

How the day runs: Allow an hour and combine it with the central business district. The tower is reached from the south and the north faces and the plan reads differently from each, so start on the axis where the two pentagons overlap and then move around the corner to see the paired profile. The spiral stair on one side and the exhibition rooms on the other lead to the top, and the tower was the highest building in the city when it was finished, which is why the viewing platform was worth the climb. Before the climb, walk the surrounding square, where the original 1953 plaza and the 2020 plan to enlarge it to about 21,000 square metres are both recorded.

4 km from Zhengzhou · about 15 min by car from Zhengzhou · half-day trip · best in All year; the tower is a short stop that fits between two longer outings.

  • The tower is 63 metres high with 14 storeys, and its top carries six clocks, each with a diameter of 2.7 metres.
  • The plan is two pentagons joined east to west, so the tower appears single from the east and west and paired from the north and south.
  • The first tower on the site was a 21-metre wooden structure built in 1953; it collapsed in July 1971 and the present tower was finished that September.

Kang Baiwan Manor (康百万庄园)

The Kang Baiwan Manor stands at Kangdian in Gongyi, on the north bank of the Luo river below the Mang Mountains, and was begun in the late Ming and early Qing. It is a fortified farmhouse of the type built on the Loess Plateau in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, and the Kang family occupied it through twelve generations across the Ming, Qing and Republican periods and more than 400 years. The family made its fortune in salt, cloth, shipbuilding and land, and held estates in eight counties of the region. The manor was listed as a provincial protected monument in 1963 and as a national key cultural relic in 2001.

How the day runs: Drive out at 08:30 and reach the gate around 09:40. The complex is arranged in a series of courts climbing the slope, with the residential halls, the courtyards, the ancestral hall, the study and the coach house in sequence, and the layout is compact enough to walk in three hours. The northern and eastern sections are the oldest and the outer walls of rammed earth carry the defensive character that gives the fortress reading. Eat in Gongyi, where the river and the old market streets are on the way back, and leave by 15:00 for the 70-minute run back to Zhengzhou, arriving at 16:15.

50 km from Zhengzhou · about 70 min by car from Zhengzhou · full-day trip · best in April to October, with the winter wind on the Loess Plateau the reason for the exposed courtyards.

  • The manor was occupied by twelve generations over more than 400 years and the buildings climb from the foot of the slope to the top, so the visit is a walk uphill rather than a level circuit.
  • It is one of the three great landlord manor houses in China, and the rammed-earth outer walls are what make it a fortress rather than a house.
  • Protection status has accumulated over decades: provincial listing in 1963, national key cultural relic in 2001, and a 4A rating in 2005.

Planning day trips from Zhengzhou

The mountain is the organising fact here. Dengfeng sits 70 to 80 kilometres west of the city, and the roads out of the valley are the slow part of the day rather than the drive in. Shaolin, the Songshan summit walk and Songyang Academy all sit inside the same massif within a few kilometres of each other, so choosing one means accepting that the others are visible but not reachable in the same outing. Fix the departure at 06:30 or 07:00, since the traffic builds after 14:00 and the return through the valley is slower than the outward run.

The second consideration is the split between the city sites and the mountain sites. The Henan Museum and the Erqi tower are 6 and 4 kilometres from the centre and are conditioned buildings, which makes them the right choice for a hot afternoon or a wet morning in July and August. The Kang Baiwan Manor at 50 kilometres is a middle case: a three-hour walk through uphill courtyards on the Loess Plateau, with the wind in winter, that fills a morning and leaves the afternoon for the city.

Songyang Academy, Henan Museum, Erqi Memorial Tower each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Zhengzhou itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Shaolin Temple a full-day trip from Zhengzhou?

It is 80 kilometres west, about 100 minutes by car, and the complex is too spread out for a morning. The group of buildings inscribed as the Centre of Heaven and Earth World Heritage site includes the temple, the pagoda forest, the Chuzu Hermitage and the permanent residence quarters, and the Chuzu Hermitage alone sits above the main courtyard in the ravine. The temple was founded in 495 for the Indian monk Batuo, and it is a 5A rated attraction with a pagoda forest holding the stupa of eminent monks from the Tang and later periods.

What is special about the geology of Songshan?

The range exposes rock from the Archean to the Cenozoic in the same area, which Chinese geologists describe as five generations in one place, and this exposure is the reason the area was evaluated as a UNESCO Global Geopark on 13 February 2004. The massif covers about 450 square kilometres, has 72 peaks, and is divided into the Taishi and Shaoshi sections, with Liantian Peak at 1,512 metres and Junji Peak at 1,492 metres. It entered the first group of national key scenic areas in 1982.

How long does Songyang Academy take?

Ninety minutes is enough. The site began as a Buddhist monastery in 484, became a Daoist temple in the Sui and an academy in 1035, and is one of the four great academies of ancient China. The surviving Qing-layout complex covers 9,984 square metres with 106 buildings in five courtyards along the central axis: the gate, the Hall of the Sages, the lecture hall, the Hall of the Taoist Lineage and the library. It lies 3 kilometres north of the Dengfeng county seat, inside the Songshan scenic area, so it combines with a mountain walk rather than needing a separate journey.

What does the Henan Museum hold that is worth the visit?

The collection runs to more than 130,000 objects, of which over 5,000 are classed as national first or second grade, which puts it second in China by holdings after the Palace Museum. The material comes mainly from twentieth-century excavations at Xichuan, Luoyang, Sanmenxia, Huixian, Xinzheng and Anyang, and the strongest sections are the prehistoric material, the Shang and Zhou bronzes, the ceramics, the jades and the painted walls of the Han dynasty tomb. The museum dates from 1927, moved from Kaifeng to Zhengzhou in 1961, and opened in its present 130,000-square-metre building in 1997.

Is the Erqi Memorial Tower worth stopping at?

It is a short stop in the middle of the city, 4 kilometres from the centre, and it is a genuine landmark rather than a viewpoint: 63 metres high, 14 storeys, with a bell tower carrying six clocks of 2.7 metres diameter. Its plan is two pentagons joined east to west, so it appears single from the east and west and paired from the north and south, and it was the tallest building in the city when it was finished on 29 September 1971. Give it an hour and put the old wooden tower of 1953 in the same story.

When is the best time of year to make these trips?

April to June and September to October for the mountain outings, since the Songshan paths close in heavy winter weather and the Dengfeng valley roads congest from mid-afternoon. The academy courtyards are unshaded and exposed, so spring and autumn suit them better than high summer. The Kang Baiwan Manor works from April to October but the winter wind on the plateau is the reason for its thick outer walls. The museum and the tower are conditioned and open all year, which makes them the wet-weather or high-summer answer.
