Overview: Tea Culture in Nanjing

Nanjing's own tea is Yuhua, a needle-shaped green tea named for the Yuhuatai terrace south of the old city, with records placing its origin in the late Qing county of Jiangning. The city's tea science goes back further in institutions: in 1907 the Jiangnan Tea Planting Bureau, China's first state-run tea research body, opened near Purple Mountain, and its 47 mu of surviving gardens were replanted in the 1950s.

The tea

Yuhua (雨花茶)

Yuhua is a green tea from Nanjing, named after the Yuhuatai terrace and for the way the dried needles resemble falling rain. The leaf is fixed, rolled and fired into straight, pine-needle strands, and it is one of the three teas traditionally associated with the city. Picking runs from late March to mid-April, one bud with one leaf at a time.

How to have it here: In Nanjing it is poured at teahouses around the Confucius Temple (Fuzimiao) quarter on the Qinhuai River and sold in tea shops citywide, graded by the Qingming (around April 4) and Grain Rain (around April 20) date ladder. The Purple Mountain gardens, descended from the 1907 bureau's stock, still turn out small lots each spring.

Drinking Yuhua tea in Nanjing

Yuhua is brewed to be watched: 3 grams of needles in a tall glass at 80-85°C, where they stand upright, then sink in ranks - the whole point of the shape. First steep runs one to two minutes, and two or three rounds are usual. Water straight off the boil bends the needles flat and drags bitterness out of the bud.

On Qinhuai teahouse tables the pot arrives with snacks, and the host tops up guests in order of age before sitting down; tapping two fingers acknowledges a pour. Nanjing pours modest cups - half to seven-tenths full - and a guest who wants no more simply leaves the cup standing full rather than saying so aloud.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Yuhua tea?

A needle-shaped green tea from Nanjing, named for the Yuhuatai terrace and for the way the strands fall and settle like rain in the glass. It counts as one of the three teas traditionally tied to the city. The leaf is hand-shaped into straight pine-needle form from a late March to mid-April picking of one bud with one leaf.

How far back does its history go?

Local records place the tea's origin in the late Qing, in what was then Jiangning county. The institutional thread is firmer: the Jiangnan Tea Planting Bureau founded near Purple Mountain in 1907 was China's first state-run tea research body, and the 47 mu of gardens it left behind were replanted under official programme in the 1950s.

How should I brew it at home?

A tall glass beats a pot, so the needles can be seen standing and sinking: 3 grams of leaf, water cooled to 80-85°C, one to two minutes for the first round. Two or three infusions are normal. Boiling water flattens the strands and pulls the liquor bitter, which is the single commonest mistake with this tea.

Where can I buy or taste it in Nanjing?

Teahouses in the Fuzimiao quarter on the Qinhuai River pour it through the day, and city-centre tea shops sell it graded by picking date - before Qingming around April 4 for the top tier, before Grain Rain around April 20 for the next. Small lots from the Purple Mountain gardens surface in spring and sell out quickly.
