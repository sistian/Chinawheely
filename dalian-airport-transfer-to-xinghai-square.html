Dalian Zhoushuizi International Airport (DLC)

14 km from the Dalian terminal, about 25 minutes by car.

Zhoushuizi lies about 10 km northwest of the city centre in Ganjingzi District. The run goes southeast on the Ganjingzi connector and Huanghe Road towards Zhongshan Road, finishing a few minutes from the north side of the square.

Why transfer to Xinghai Square?

The square was created by landfill and opened to the public free of charge on 30 June 1997, the day after the Hong Kong handover, and the symbolism was deliberate. Its geometry encodes the city's own story: an inner circle 199.9 metres across marked Dalian's centenary, an outer circle of 239.9 metres pointing to the city's fifth centenary in 2399, and a central round altar paved with 999 pieces of Sichuan red marble carved with the heavenly stems, the twelve solar terms and the Chinese zodiac. Nine bronze tripods stand around it, each carrying one character of a phrase on national unity.

The scale is the point. The whole site covers 110 hectares, and the north-south diameter runs to 1,010 metres, so it reads more like a civic park than a plaza. Two parallel avenues and two X-shaped roads cut it into six parts around three levels of circular carriageway, and the 850,000 sq m of lawn is where Dalian residents do tai chi, walk dogs and watch the sunset behind the sculpture group. It is free, always open, and never feels like a ticketed attraction.

The square also works as a starting point rather than an endpoint. The Xinghai Bay Bridge is a kilometre to the south, the convention and exhibition centre and the Dalian Commodity Exchange headquarters are on the north side, and the whole southern coastline down to Bangchuidao is a fifteen-minute walk. Coming straight from the airport lets you spend the late afternoon on the grass and still catch the fountain show and the lights along the bay.

  • 110 hectares of reclaimed land, the largest city square in the world by area
  • Inner circle 199.9 m across for the 1997 centenary, outer circle 239.9 m for 2399
  • Central altar of 999 red marble slabs plus nine bronze tripods with carved characters
  • Lawn covers 850,000 sq m, 77.3 percent of the total surface, and the site is free to enter

What to do at Xinghai Square once you arrive

Come in the late afternoon. The sun drops towards the sea on the western side of the bay, and the sculpture group and the nine tripods throw long shadows across the red marble, while the musical fountain runs in the evenings and the skyline of the southern coast lights up behind it. Locals arrive around 17:00 for the fountain, so the space in the last hour before dark is the liveliest it gets and the best time to be in it.

The north side of the square is where the commercial weight sits, with the Xinghai CBD, the convention centre and the World Expo centre around it, and several luxury car showrooms. Around the edges you will find the usual run of souvenir stalls, sea-salt shops and seafood restaurants serving Dalian's pomfret and sea urchin. Staying on the lawn for the sunset and eating afterwards is a better use of the evening than driving straight out to a shopping mall.

  • Xinghai Square station on Line 1 of the Dalian Metro opens onto the southern side of the square
  • The square, its lawns and the fountain are free and have no closing time
  • Come between May and October for evening fountain and fountain-light performances
  • Coastal wind picks up sharply after 15:00, so bring a layer even in summer

Arriving at Dalian Zhoushuizi International Airport

Zhoushuizi is a compact airport about 10 km northwest of the centre, in Ganjingzi District, and it handled close to 20 million passengers in 2025, making it the busiest airport in Northeast China. It is also at capacity: a replacement, Dalian Jinzhouwan International Airport, is being built on an artificial island to take the traffic in future. For now Zhoushuizi remains the practical arrival point, and the run into the city is short and straightforward compared with most of the distances on this list.

Because the trip is short, this is one of the least expensive airport runs in the set, and the quote is a straightforward distance-based figure for the vehicle class you choose. Frequently Asked Questions: Booking in Dalian Airport Transfer To Xinghai Square

How long is the drive from DLC airport to Xinghai Square?

Zhoushuizi Airport is about 10 km northwest of Dalian city centre and Xinghai Square is 4.5 km south of the centre, so the total road distance is roughly 14 km. In light traffic the drive takes 20 to 25 minutes, and even in the evening peak it rarely exceeds 35 minutes, which makes this one of the quickest airport transfers of any Chinese city.

Is Xinghai Square free to visit?

Yes. The square was opened to the public free of charge when it was completed in 1997 and has never charged an entry fee. There is no ticket for the lawns, the sculpture group, the fountain or the surrounding walkway, and there is no fixed closing time, so late evening and early morning visits are perfectly normal.

What is the musical fountain schedule?

The fountain is programmed for evening performances, with the schedules set seasonally and adjusted for weather. Rather than fixing on a single time, arriving in the hour before dark gives you the chance to see the fountain running at least once, because the programmes shift through the summer months and around public holidays.

Can I drive onto the square itself?

No. The circular roads inside Xinghai Square are for circulating traffic and sightseeing vehicles, and private cars cannot be parked on the lawns or the plaza. Drivers drop passengers at the perimeter roads, which is why having a car meet you at the gate matters here: you get out at the north edge and walk in.

Is the square worth going to in winter?

It is a different place in winter. The lawn goes pale, the sea wind off Xinghai Bay is serious, and the square is far quieter than in summer, but the light on the frozen harbour and the lit-up sculpture group have their own appeal. If you arrive between December and February, dress for the wind rather than the temperature.
