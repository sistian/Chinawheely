Overview: Photography in Chengdu

Chengdu photographs along two lines. One runs through the old city centre, where the Manchu garrison streets of the Qing dynasty were left standing and are now lined with teahouses, and where the shrine of a chancellor stands behind a hundred metres of red wall. The other runs west into the Min river valley, to an irrigation system that has been diverting water for two and a half millennia and to the Taoist mountain beside it. Between them these seven locations cover the city that the Sichuan basin capital is actually made of, rather than the version of it that appears on a list of must-sees.

Kuanzhai Alley (宽窄巷子)

Three parallel east-west alleys in the Qingyang district, Kuan, Zhai and Jing, make up one of the largest surviving groups of Qing dynasty street houses in Chengdu. The ground was the Manchu garrison quarter: in 1718, after the Dzungar campaign, the court built a walled garrison city inside the older wall and allowed only Manchu and Mongol banner households to live inside it. After 1911 the garrison walls came down and the wealthy began building houses here, and the villas of Yu Youren, Tian Songyao, Li Jiayu, Yang Sen and Liu Wenhui are the reason the fabric survived at all. The alleys were renamed after the revolution, from Xingren, Taiping and Ruyi hutongs. The pedestrian street was among the first national demonstration pedestrian streets in the country and is now a designated national night-time culture and consumption cluster.

best light: Early morning before the shops open, for the empty flagstone; or after sunset for the lit shopfronts and lanterns. · old street

Getting there: In the city centre in Qingyang district, near Changshun Street and within walking distance of Tianfu Square, with metro stations on the north-south lines in the surrounding blocks.

  • The three alleys are parallel, so walk one street at a time rather than crisscrossing; the width differences give each a different proportion.
  • The house facades are set back behind courtyards, so a long lens compresses the street better than a wide one.
  • Golden hour falls from the west onto the east-facing facades of the wide alley, so shoot that one later in the afternoon.

Jinli Ancient Street (锦里)

Jinli opened to the public in October 2004 as a reconstruction of a commercial street of the Three Kingdoms period, with the extension along the water, Shui'an Jinli, following in January 2009. In 2005 it was named one of the ten best commercial pedestrian streets in the country, alongside Wangfujing in Beijing, Jianghan Road in Wuhan, Jiefangbei in Chongqing and Heping Road in Tianjin, and it carries the nickname of the first street of western Shu. The Ministry of Culture named it a national cultural industry demonstration base in 2006. The subject is the Three Kingdoms and the folklore of Sichuan rather than a single monument, and the street is at its most photographic where the roofline, the lantern rows and the water edge stack up in one frame.

best light: Blue hour, when the lantern rows and the shop signs are lit but the sky still holds colour. · themed pedestrian street

Getting there: Immediately beside the Wuhou Shrine in Wuhou district, on the east side of the city, with bus and metro services on the surrounding roads.

  • The second section along the water was built later and is less crowded; it has the cleaner sightlines and better reflections.
  • The lanterns come on in the evening, so a blue-hour exposure keeps some ambient light in the sky and lets the lanterns carry the frame.
  • Shoot from the water's edge looking back at the lit facades rather than walking the street, which is where the perspective works.

Wuhou Shrine (武侯祠)

The Chengdu Wuhou Shrine is the best known of the shrines built for Zhuge Liang, chancellor of Shu Han during the Three Kingdoms. Unlike the earlier official shrine at Mian county, erected by the emperor Liu Shan, or the one at Nanyang raised by Zhuge Liang's former general Huang Quan in the Wei and Jin period, the Chengdu shrine dates from the Cheng Han era and is a joint shrine for the ruler and his minister. It is set in Wuhou district and laid out as a series of courts, corridors and halls, with the tomb of Liu Bei inside the grounds and a bamboo planting along the approach. It is a place where the light itself is the subject: red plaster, green bamboo and the grey of the tile roofs give three separate exposures in the same frame.

best light: Late afternoon, when the light rakes across the red wall and the bamboo casts a moving shadow on the plaster. · memorial complex

Getting there: Wuhou district, south of the city centre, adjacent to Jinli Street and served by the metro and by local buses along the main road.

  • The bamboo and the red wall face each other at an angle, so a long lens from the side gives a repeating pattern rather than a single wall.
  • The halls are lit from the sides by covered walkways, so interior frames need a slower shutter and a steady stance.
  • The tomb area has its own rules about photography; check the signs on the gate before working inside.

Dujiangyan Irrigation Works (都江堰)

The works stand on the Min river some 340 kilometres upstream of Chengdu, and they are the reason the plain around the city is habitable at all. The Shiji and the Hanshu record that the governor of Shu, Li Bing, and his son built them between about 256 and 251 BC, with a second tradition putting them in the reign of the ancient Shu king Kai Ming. The system divides into the headworks and the irrigation network: the Fish Mouth divides the river into inner and outer channels, the Feishan Weir spills flood and sediment, and the Baopingkou takes the water, with the inner and outer dikes and the Ren-shaped dike as supporting works. It irrigates the Chengdu plain, the country known as the land of abundance. After two thousand years of repair it still works without a single dam, and in 2000 it was listed with Qingcheng Shan as a UNESCO World Heritage Site for exactly that reason.

best light: Early morning, when the water in the diversion channel is still low and the fish-mouth weir reads clearly. · engineering heritage

Getting there: From Chengdu by train to Dujiangyan and then by local bus or taxi, with the Anlan Bridge crossing the inner channel at the site itself.

  • The Anlan Bridge gives the cleanest frame: the covered walkway with the channel and the weir beneath it.
  • Water levels change through the season, and the weir is a different subject in the low and high seasons; check the local notice.
  • Bring a long lens; the views across the fish-mouth weir and the dike are compressed from the far bank.

Mount Qingcheng (青城山)

Qingcheng Shan is one of the birthplaces of Daoism in China, and was called Zhangren Shan in the old texts. It lies 68 kilometres from Chengdu and 10 kilometres south-west of the Dujiangyan works, with its main peak at Lao Xiaoding reaching 1,600 metres. The front mountain is the main part of the scenic area at roughly 15 square kilometres, with most of the temples and monuments, while the back mountain is harder to reach and keeps more of its natural state because of the terrain and the transport. The range is the eastern spur of the southern Qionglai mountains and dates from an orogeny about 180 million years ago, which is why the rock is broken and folded and the shapes are complicated. Thirty-six peaks stand in a ring, green on all sides like a city wall, which is where the name comes from. It shares the 2000 World Heritage listing with Dujiangyan.

best light: First light on the ridge paths, or late afternoon in the valley where the peaks stack in haze. · mountain and temple

Getting there: About 68 kilometres from Chengdu, reached by train to Dujiangyan followed by a local bus to the mountain gate, or by road along the same corridor as the irrigation works.

  • The ring of peaks is the motif worth photographing; the individual temples are secondary to the ridgeline.
  • Valley haze builds after rain and stacks the peaks in layers, which is the standard image of the site.
  • The mountain paths are stepped and shaded, so a mid-telephoto is more useful on the walk than a wide lens.

People's Park (人民公园)

The park covers 112,639 square metres, about 169 mu, next to Tianfu Square in Qingyang district, and it was the first public park in the history of Sichuan. It began as the Shaocheng park, laid out in 1911 when part of the former Manchu garrison was converted to give the banner families a living, and it was extended in 1914 when the monument to the martyrs of the Railway Protection Movement was erected, a square tapered stele with the inscription written in four different scripts by Zhao Xi, Yan Kai, Wu Bojie and Zhang Kuijie. Outside the east gate stands the monument to the Sichuan army soldiers killed in the war against Japan. The grounds also hold the lotus pond, the nanmu grove, the archery range and the sports ground that once hosted the province's public meetings.

best light: Under the trees in the late morning, where the light breaks through the canopy and falls across the pond. · city park

Getting there: Qingyang district, immediately beside Tianfu Square in the centre of the city, with metro access at the square and on the surrounding streets.

  • The four-script stele is a subject in itself; shoot it square-on in flat light rather than trying to include the surroundings.
  • The nanmu grove and the pond give the park its softest light and its best shade portraits.
  • Teahouse tables under the trees are the standard Chengdu scene, and the light between the branches is better than the light on the street.

Wangjianglou Pavilion (望江楼)

The pavilion, originally called Chongli Ge, was built in 1889 during the Guangxu reign and stands 27.98 metres tall on a timber frame, with four tiers of eaves and an octagonal glazed-tile finial roof. The structure sets inwards storey by storey, the lower two tiers square and the upper two octagonal, which is what makes it unusual. The Zhuojin Lou beside it stands parallel to the river on two levels and reads like a boat hull, five bays wide with carved doors and windows, and the Yinshi Lou is a two-storey, three-eave building with open corners. The complex was listed as a provincial protected unit in 1991 and as part of the Wangjianglou ancient building group among the national key cultural relics on 25 May 2006. The park around it holds the Xue Tao memorial hall and the Xue Tao well; Xue Tao, born 768 and dead 831, was the poet who settled in Chengdu after her father's death and wrote from the bamboo groves along this river.

best light: Late afternoon from the river side, when the glazed roof tiles catch the last light against the sky. · pavilion and garden

Getting there: Wuhou district on the south bank of the Jin river, reached from the city centre by metro and a short taxi or bus ride.

  • The stepped octagonal roof is the detail to isolate; from a distance the tiers merge into one another.
  • Shoot from the river bank so the pavilion is separated from the trees behind it.
  • The bamboo, the well and the tiled roofs make a natural sequence through the park on a single walk.

Gear and planning for a day in Chengdu

The two lines of this city are worked differently. The old centre is pedestrian-scale, so a 35mm or 50mm lens with a hood is the working choice, and the alleys are narrow enough that the light is short and directional in the morning and in the late afternoon. Out at Dujiangyan and Qingcheng Shan the distances are what matter: the weir, the dike and the ridgeline are all photographed across water or across a valley, so a 70-200mm is the useful range and a tripod is worth the weight. The park and the shrine both work at short range, and a polariser earns its place on the pond, the glazed tiles and the old plaster.

Plan the day around the light rather than the itinerary. The old centre, the park and the shrine are all within walking distance of one another and work best in the morning and the last two hours of the afternoon, which leaves the middle of the day for the train west to Dujiangyan and Qingcheng Shan, since the mountain light is at its best early and the works are easier to read before the crowds. Chengdu sits under a basin of cloud and humidity, so expect flat light after rain, which suits the tiled roofs and the red wall, and keep the equipment in a bag rather than on a strap.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Chengdu location is worth the longest trip?

Mount Qingcheng, at 68 kilometres, if the day is about the landscape, and Dujiangyan, 340 kilometres upstream on the Min river, if it is about the engineering. Both share the same 2000 UNESCO listing with Dujiangyan. The irrigation works are the more unusual subject: a diversion system with no dam at all, built around 256 to 251 BC by Li Bing and his son, still irrigating the plain around the city. Both are on the same corridor west of Chengdu, so one road trip can cover them if the time is planned.

Why do the old alleys look the way they do?

Because of the garrison. In 1718 the court walled a Manchu and Mongol garrison city inside the older wall of Chengdu, and only banner households were allowed to live inside it. After the 1911 revolution the walls came down, the wealthy built houses in the freed ground, and the street pattern of the alleys survived along with them. The old names, Xingren, Taiping and Ruyi hutongs, were changed to Kuan, Zhai and Jing after the revolution.

What makes the Wuhou Shrine different from the other Zhuge Liang shrines?

There are three main ones. The official shrine at Mian county was raised by the emperor Liu Shan and is the earliest; the one at Nanyang was built by Huang Quan, one of Zhuge Liang's own former generals, in the Wei and Jin period; the Chengdu shrine, in Wuhou district, goes back to the Cheng Han era and is the best known because it is a joint shrine for the ruler and his minister. It is also the one with the tomb of Liu Bei inside the grounds.

Is the People's Park worth time in a short visit?

It is the first park in the history of Sichuan, laid out in 1911 from part of the old Manchu garrison, and it covers 112,639 square metres next to Tianfu Square. It carries the 1914 monument to the Railway Protection Movement martyrs, with the inscription written in four different scripts, and a second monument outside the east gate to the Sichuan soldiers killed in the war against Japan. For a photograph it is the softest light in the city, and it is two stops from the centre.

What about giant pandas?

The species is Ailuropoda melanoleuca, the last surviving member of its subfamily, and it lives in the mountain forests of Sichuan, Shaanxi and southern Gansu at 2,000 to 3,500 metres, eating bamboo for about half of every day and sleeping through the other half. Around 2,700 individuals remain as of 2025, and the IUCN moved the species from Endangered to Vulnerable at the end of 2016. It has been known to science since 1869 and it is the reason the city has a morning queue.

What is the best light for the city centre?

Late afternoon into blue hour. The alleys run east to west, so the sun reaches the facades of the wide alley from the west in the later part of the day, and the lantern rows on Jinli come on at dusk. The park undergrowth works in the late morning, the shrine in the afternoon when the light rakes across the red wall, and the mountain ridges at Qingcheng Shan in the first hour of the day.
