Why transfer to the Terracotta Warriors?

The mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor, with the terracotta army among its pits, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987 at the eleventh session, under criteria i, iii, iv and vi as reference 441 — among China's earliest inscriptions. Work on the mausoleum began in 246 BCE soon after the future emperor succeeded his father as King of Qin at thirteen, and the historian Sima Qian, writing a century after completion, recorded that the project involved 700,000 conscripted workers.

The necropolis covers approximately 98 square kilometres, and the army is only part of it: other pits have yielded non-military figures including officials, acrobats, strongmen and musicians. The figures vary in height by rank, the tallest being the generals, and they were placed in precise military formation. A museum complex has been constructed over the pits, the largest enclosed under a roofed structure, making the site workable in any weather.

  • Discovered in March 1974 by farmers digging a well 1.5 kilometres east of the tomb mound
  • Necropolis area of approximately 98 square kilometres below Mount Li
  • Mausoleum construction began 246 BCE; 700,000 workers recorded by Sima Qian

What to do at the Terracotta Warriors once you arrive

The pits carry the visit. Pit 1, the largest of four, holds eleven parallel corridors mostly more than 3 metres wide and represents the main army; Pit 2 mixes archers, cavalry and infantry; Pit 3 serves as the command post with high-ranking officers and a war chariot; Pit 4 was left empty, apparently unfinished when work stopped. The majority of the estimated 8,000-plus figures remain in situ, exposed where archaeologists found them.

Beyond the pits, the museum complex covers the finds from the wider necropolis, and the tomb mound of Qin Shi Huang itself stands about 1.5 kilometres west of the discovery site, reachable on foot or by the site's shuttles. The rank hierarchy of the figures — tallest for the generals — rewards close viewing of the front ranks, and the roofs over the pits allow the visit to run in rain or sun without change.

  • Start at Pit 1, the largest, before the tour groups arrive from the city
  • Pit 3's command post and Pit 4's unfinished state complete the sequence of four
  • The emperor's tomb mound lies about 1.5 kilometres west of the pits
  • Allow at least three hours for the pits and the museum halls

Arriving at Xi'an Xianyang International Airport

Xianyang Airport covers a 12-square-kilometre site with terminals T1, T2, T3 and T5, ranking 11th nationwide by passenger traffic; China Eastern, Sichuan Airlines and Hainan Airlines base operations there. The airport stands inside Xianyang city's administrative area, 25 kilometres northwest of downtown Xi'an — on the opposite side of the city from Lintong, where the warriors lie, which shapes every onward option.

Public transport to the site runs indirectly: travellers reach Xi'an first, then take Bus 306, the Tourist Bus number 5, from the lot in front of the train station out to Lintong. A car with driver runs the roughly 60-kilometre route directly and is the option that holds a fixed price across a delayed landing, with the driver meeting arrivals in the hall rather than at the rank, where unlicensed operators are a documented problem at this airport.

What the transfer costs in Xian Airport Transfer To Terracotta Warriors

The warriors sit about 60 road kilometres from Xianyang, double the distance of a downtown Xi'an transfer, so expect the quote to reflect that length: private sedan transfers for the direct run are typically offered in the 250 to 400 yuan band, expressway tolls and the driver's airport waiting included. The metered alternative prices the same distance without a cap on time lost in cross-city congestion.

Day-hire arrangements are common here, since most visitors pair the warriors with the tomb mound and the museum halls and then need a return leg; hiring the car for the block of hours usually beats two separate transfers. Groups of four or more should compare the van class — the Lintong site car parks are built for coaches, so a van drops as easily as a sedan.

Confirm with the driver whether the booking covers the wait at Lintong or a separate pickup, and fix the return meeting point at the main car park rather than the pit exits, which shift as the site directs foot traffic.

Frequently Asked Questions: Booking in Xian Airport Transfer To Terracotta Warriors

How far is it from Xianyang Airport to the Terracotta Warriors?

Roughly 60 kilometres by road. The airport lies 25 kilometres northwest of central Xi'an and the mausoleum site is in Lintong District to the city's east, so the trip crosses the metropolitan area rather than ending downtown. Expect 60 to 80 minutes depending on the ring-road traffic around the city.

When were the Terracotta Warriors discovered?

In March 1974, by local farmers digging a well in Lintong County about 1.5 kilometres east of the emperor's tomb mound at Mount Li. The figures date from the late 200s BCE, buried with Qin Shi Huang in 210–209 BCE, and the site was inscribed by UNESCO in 1987.

How many warriors are there?

Estimates from 2007 put the three pits at more than 8,000 soldiers, 130 chariots with 520 horses, and 150 cavalry horses, the majority of which remain in situ. Other pits across the 98-square-kilometre necropolis have added non-military figures: officials, acrobats, strongmen and musicians.

Is public transport available from the airport to the site?

Only indirectly. The route runs through Xi'an: reach the city first, then take Bus 306, also called Tourist Bus number 5, from the lot in front of the train station to Lintong. The direct alternative is a car with driver on the roughly 60-kilometre cross-city route, meeting arrivals in the hall at the terminal.

Should I book a one-way transfer or a day hire?

A day hire usually fits this site better. The pits, the museum halls and the tomb mound 1.5 kilometres west of the pits take three hours or more, and most travellers want a return leg to the airport or the city. Booking the car for the block of hours fixes the price against two separate transfers and the wait is agreed in advance.
