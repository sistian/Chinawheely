Overview: Photography in Zhengzhou

Zhengzhou is a modern provincial capital with a long documentary record, and the photographs sit in three groups. One is the 1923 strike tower and the museum district around it, all in the centre. The second is the Songshan massif 70 kilometres west, with a 495 AD monastery, a 484 AD academy and a cemetery of 240 brick pagodas. The third is the Yellow River edge and a walled merchant manor south of the city. Each of the seven locations has a recorded date, dimension and protection status.

Erqi Memorial Tower (二七纪念塔)

The tower stands at Erqi Square in the centre of Zhengzhou and was completed on 29 September 1971 to commemorate the strike of 7 February 1923. It is a reinforced concrete structure 63 metres tall with 14 floors: a three-storey base enclosed by white marble balustrades, then an 11-storey shaft whose every level carries pseudo-classical flying eaves covered in green glazed tiles. At the top is a clock tower with a six-faced bell of 2.7 metres diameter, surmounted by a red glass five-pointed star. The plan is two pentagons joined east to west, so it reads as a single tower from the east and a pair from the north.

best light: The hour before sunset, when the tower's west face falls into shadow and the glazed green roofs of the upper tiers still hold light. · memorial tower

Getting there: At Erqi Square in the Erqi district of central Zhengzhou, on the main north-south commercial axis, with the square itself scheduled for enlargement to 21,000 square metres.

  • The plan is two joined pentagons, so the north-south axis gives a double-tower view and the east-west axis a single one; walk a quarter turn between frames.
  • The 2.7 metre bell is inside the clock tower and not visible from the square, so the crown detail is the star and the eave line rather than the bell.
  • The tower was preceded by a wooden structure built in 1951 that collapsed in 1971, which is why the current shaft is much taller than the original.

Henan Museum (河南博物院)

The Henan Museum at 8 Nongye Road in Jinshui district is one of the national key museums, and it traces its origin to a foundation in 1927 under the sponsorship of Feng Yuxiang, which makes it one of the earlier modern museums in the country. Its first building stood at 31 Sansheng Street in the Longting district of Kaifeng, and it moved to Zhengzhou in 1961; the present building opened in 1997. The collection runs to more than 130,000 objects, drawn largely from early twentieth century excavations at Xichuan, Luoyang, Sanmenxia, Huixian, Xinzheng and Anyang, with prehistoric material, Shang and Zhou bronzes, ceramics and jade the strongest sections.

best light: Midday exterior for the building mass, and controlled interior light for the galleries, where the case lighting is dimmer than the hall. · history and archaeology museum

Getting there: On Nongye Road in the Jinshui district of Zhengzhou, north of the city centre between the provincial government quarter and the museum district, with the main entrance on the east side of the road.

  • The gallery lighting is deliberately dim relative to the hall, so the camera needs a higher ISO and a steadier stance than the exterior suggests.
  • The building is the main subject from outside, so shoot it in flat light rather than trying to balance it against a bright sky.
  • Case reflections come from the glass, so a polariser cut down to a few degrees of rotation is usually enough to separate the bronzes from the pane.

Shaolin Monastery (少林寺)

Shaolin Monastery lies at the foot of Wuru Peak on Mount Song in Dengfeng, in the deep grove of Shaoshi from which the name derives. It was founded in the 19th year of the Taihe era of the Northern Wei, 495 AD, and is the ancestral court of the Chan school in Chinese Buddhism, now of the Caodong lineage. The complex includes the abbot's residence, the Pagoda Forest and Chuzu An, inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the Historic Monuments of Dengfeng in the Centre of Heaven and Earth.

best light: Late afternoon in the valley, when the light comes down the Shaoshi side of the mountain and reaches the temple court and the surrounding ridges. · Buddhist monastery and architectural complex

Getting there: On the south-west side of Mount Song in Dengfeng, a county-level city under Zhengzhou, reached by road or coach to the town and then by local bus into the valley.

  • The temple sits low in the valley under Wuru Peak, so the useful light arrives late and the ridges give the backdrop rather than the building.
  • The Pagoda Forest and Chuzu An are separate entries from the monastery itself and are walked from different directions, so plan the route rather than circling.
  • The precinct is an active religious site with its own rules on photography; check the signage at the gate before working inside.

Pagoda Forest of Shaolin (少林寺塔林)

The Pagoda Forest lies on the west side of Shaolin Monastery and is the burial ground of its eminent monks, whose ashes were placed in underground chambers with a pagoda raised above. The size, height and number of storeys of each tower were determined by the depth of the monk's learning, standing and accumulated merit, and among the monuments are bone pagodas and robe-and-bowl pagodas. It is the largest and most numerous surviving group of pagodas at any Chinese Buddhist site, with more than 240 brick and stone tomb towers from the Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming and Qing dynasties.

best light: Morning, when the light rakes along the rows and each tower casts a shadow onto the one behind it. · monastic cemetery of brick and stone pagodas

Getting there: Immediately west of Shaolin Monastery on Mount Song in Dengfeng, a short walk along the valley path from the temple precinct, with the pagodas spread across a walled enclosure in the lower ground.

  • With more than 240 towers packed on a plot of about 14,000 square metres, the rows are tight and a long lens is needed to isolate a single monument.
  • The towers vary in height and storey count, and those variations were deliberate markers of rank, so an elevation of the row reads the hierarchy.
  • Morning light from the east throws each tower's shadow onto its neighbour, which separates the monuments better than a flat frontal frame.

Songyang Academy (嵩阳书院)

Songyang Academy stands on the southern slope of Mount Song about 3 kilometres north of Dengfeng, facing Junji Peak to the north and the Shuangxi river to the south, and is listed as a provincial key cultural relic. The site began as Songyang Temple, a Buddhist establishment founded in the 8th year of the Taihe era of the Northern Wei, 484 AD; it became a Taoist site named Songyang Guan in the Sui dynasty and a school in 1035 AD. It is counted with Yuelu Academy, Bailudong and Shangqiu among the four great academies of ancient China, and the brothers Cheng Hao and Cheng Yi taught several hundred students here.

best light: Early morning, when the light comes across the courtyard from the east and the cypress trunks stand out against the whitewashed hall walls. · Confucian academy

Getting there: On the southern foothills of Mount Song, about 3 kilometres north of Dengfeng in Zhengzhou, at the foot of the range on the Shuangxi valley side.

  • The lecture hall is three bays wide with a hard-mountain and rolled-ridge roof, which is a low horizontal shape and needs a wide frame in an open courtyard.
  • The academy sits on a north-south axis between the peak and the river, so the gate, the courtyard and the hall stack into a single receding view.
  • The site is a school rather than a ruin, so the working elements of a courtyard, the pool and the covered walks are part of the architecture.

Zhengzhou Yellow River Scenic Area (郑州黄河风景名胜区)

The scenic area lies in Guying town in Huiji district, to the north-west of the central Zhengzhou urban area, with the Yellow River to the north and Yueshan to the south, covering roughly 5 kilometres east to west. It began as bare Mangshan hills. In 1952 Mao Zedong inspected the Yellow River here and gave the instruction to do the river work properly, after which a storage station, the Mangshan irrigation lift station and the Yellow River lift station were built, and the ground was set aside as the Zhengzhou Yellow River sightseeing area. Over close to 20 years of construction more than 4,000 mu were planted with trees.

best light: Late afternoon, when the low sun comes across the river and the terraced slopes on the far bank take the light first. · riverside park

Getting there: In Guying town of Huiji district, north-west of the central city on the south bank of the Yellow River, on the Mangshan terraces above the river.

  • The area runs about 5 kilometres east to west along the river, so a single frame needs a chosen arc of the bank rather than an attempt at the whole site.
  • The hills were bare when the area was designated and were planted over roughly 20 years, so the tree cover changes noticeably along the length of the site.
  • Working from the upper terraces gives the river's width in the background, which is the reference the pavilions and the stele forest are scaled against.

Kang Baiwan Manor (康百万庄园)

The manor stands at Kangdian town in Gongyi and was begun in the late Ming and early Qing period. Twelve generations of the Kang family lived there across more than 400 years, and the buildings were extended from the middle of the hillside to the top. It is a typical fortified residence of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries on the Loess Plateau, built with its back to Mangshan and its front to the Luo river, which is why the complex is said to rest on the back of a giant turtle with its head reaching toward the water.

best light: Morning, when the light enters the courtyards from above and the enclosed walls and deep doorways show their depth. · fortified merchant manor

Getting there: At Kangdian town in Gongyi, south of Zhengzhou on the Loess Plateau, approached by road from the provincial capital with the manor on the hillside above the Luo river.

  • The buildings climb the hillside in terraces, so each courtyard looks over the one below; the frames work best looking down the slope.
  • It is a fortified compound with narrow deep doorways, so the light inside is a fraction of the light outside and needs a longer exposure.
  • The compound is one of the three great landlord estates in China and its protection status dates from 1963, with national listing in 2001.

Camera gear for Zhengzhou

The working assumption here is that the light is hard and the surfaces are bright. Zhengzhou sits on the North China Plain, where summer sun is high and shadowless and the dust haze of late spring scatters it into a flat white sheet, so a graduated filter and a sun hood earn their place on the open sites: the Yellow River terraces at Guying and the Mangshan hills above them. The enclosed subjects run the other way, with the deep doorways and courtyards of the Kang manor and the shadowed halls of Songyang Academy needing a slower shutter and a tripod or a braced stance.

On the lens, the choice is between a wide frame for the tower and the academy and a long lens for the pagoda forest. The Erqi Memorial Tower is 63 metres tall and 14 storeys, which a wide angle handles from the base of the square, while the more than 240 towers of the Shaolin cemetery are packed onto about 14,000 square metres and need 70 to 200 millimetres to isolate a single monument. A polariser is worth carrying for the glazed green tile roofs of the tower and for the museum case glass, and a polarising filter also cuts the sheen from the museum's stone plaza in the same way it cuts the glare off the Yellow River.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the tower in the middle of Zhengzhou?

The Erqi Memorial Tower at Erqi Square, completed on 29 September 1971 to commemorate the strike of 7 February 1923. It is reinforced concrete, 63 metres tall with 14 floors, of which three form the base and eleven form the shaft, and it is topped by a clock tower with a 2.7 metre six-faced bell and a red glass five-pointed star. Its plan is two pentagons joined east to west, so it looks like one tower from the east and a pair from the north.

How old is Shaolin Temple?

It was founded in the 19th year of the Taihe era of the Northern Wei, 495 AD, and has been destroyed and rebuilt several times since. It is the ancestral court of the Chan school in Chinese Buddhism and is now of the Caodong lineage. The abbot's residence, the Pagoda Forest and Chuzu An are among the components of the Historic Monuments of Dengfeng inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, and the monastery stands 72 kilometres south-west of Zhengzhou.

What is in the Pagoda Forest?

It is the cemetery of the eminent monks of the monastery, and it is the largest surviving group of pagodas at any Chinese Buddhist site, with more than 240 brick and stone tomb towers from the Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming and Qing dynasties on roughly 14,000 square metres, about 250 metres from the monastery grounds. The height and storey count of each tower recorded the standing of the monk buried there, and some are bone pagodas and robe-and-bowl pagodas rather than standard monuments.

How old is Songyang Academy and what is special about it?

The site was founded as a Buddhist temple in 484 AD, became a Taoist establishment in the Sui dynasty and was turned into a school in 1035 AD, in the second year of the Jingyou era. It is counted with Yuelu Academy, Bailudong and Shangqiu among the four great academies of ancient China. The Neo-Confucian brothers Cheng Hao and Cheng Yi taught several hundred students here, and the Korean Songyang Academy of Kaesong took its name from this one.

Can I photograph the Shang dynasty site in the city?

The Zhengzhou Shang City ruins are a different site from these seven and sit inside the urban area. The city was the early Shang capital, the site was discovered in 1950, the inner city wall was found in 1955, and it was listed as a national key cultural relic in 1961. The wall runs 6,960 metres with 11 gaps, and the palace area lies in the north-east of the enclosure with a stone-lined artificial reservoir at its centre.

Where does the light work best in Zhengzhou?

Late afternoon for the open sites and early morning for the enclosed ones. The Yellow River terraces at Guying take the low sun from across the water, the Shaolin valley lights up from the Shaoshi side only in the afternoon, the Pagoda Forest rows separate best in morning shadow, and the tower's west face falls into shade towards sunset while the green glazed roofs of the upper tiers still hold light. The low sun also throws a hard shadow along the rows of pagodas, which a flat noon frame loses.
