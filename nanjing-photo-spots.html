Overview: Photography in Nanjing

Nanjing photographs well because it has three different kinds of city in one place: the Ming wall and the lakes of the imperial capital, the layered timber roofs of the old town along the Qinhuai, and the Republic-era civic architecture of the Presidential Palace and the former central government sites. Add Purple Mountain's wooded ridges and the autumn colour, and there is a natural cycle of subject matter that runs from dawn to lantern-lit night.

The Spirit Way at Ming Xiaoling (明孝陵神道)

The Spirit Way is 2.4 km of stone avenue lined with paired stone figures, horses, camels, elephants and mythical beasts, running straight through a mature tree corridor, and its geometry is the reason it photographs so well. The avenue runs on a precise axis from the Sifangcheng gate, and the pairs of statues alternate sides in a rhythm that pulls the eye toward the mausoleum. In late autumn the crimson maples are set against the pale grey stone and the yellowing foliage, which is the most saturated colour the Nanjing hills produce. Low sun from the east throws long shadows across the path itself.

best light: golden hour · nature

Getting there: Enter the Ming Xiaoling scenic area through the Sifangcheng gate. Metro Line 2 to Muxuyuan station with a taxi to the gate, and there is a large car park at the entrance.

  • Photograph from low down at the level of the stone animals rather than standing, which compresses the row and hides the modern city behind the trees.
  • The animals are all different, and the pairs are mirrored left and right, so a long lens across the avenue gives you matched pairs.
  • The 700-year-old ginkgo and cypress at the far end of the axis are the oldest trees on the route.

Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum and the 480 Metre Approach (中山陵)

The approach to the mausoleum is designed as a single dramatic axis: 392 stone steps in eight sections climbing 480 m from the Bo'ai Arch to the stone ceremonial arch at the top, with the blue-glazed hall of the main building at the summit. From the upper terrace the composition contains the entire stair run, which is why it is one of the most photographed views in China. The blue glazed tile was chosen for its association with the sky and the afterlife, and at golden hour it reads as a deep saturated blue against the autumn trees, with the orange of the maples below.

best light: golden hour · landmark

Getting there: The Bo'ai Arch entrance is on the south side of the Zhongshan Mountain scenic area, off the Xiaolingwei Road. Metro Line 2 to Muxuyuan plus a taxi, or use the scenic area shuttle from the Ming Xiaoling gate.

  • The classic frame is from the upper terrace looking back down the 392 steps, with the arch framing the distant city.
  • The hall's blue tile is at its richest in the last hour of light and in overcast conditions, when it does not wash out.
  • Climbing to the top before shooting means the return walk down is in the best light of the day.

Linggu Temple and the Wuliang Hall (灵谷寺无梁殿)

The Beamless Hall, built in 1381 as the Yongle Emperor's sarcophagus chamber, is a vault built entirely from brick and stone with no timber beams whatsoever, and it is one of the most impressive structures in Nanjing that almost nobody photographs. The interior is a plain barrel vault of grey brick with the red lacquer of the reconstruction visible on the side walls, and the only light comes from the doorway, which makes a symmetric frame with the vault receding into darkness. The Linggu Pagoda built beside it in 1931 to 1933 is 60.5 m tall, with Chiang Kai-shek's four-character inscription carved in huge characters on each face.

best light: all day · landmark

Getting there: In the Linggu Temple valley on the southeastern foot of Purple Mountain, 1.5 km east of the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum. The scenic area shuttle bus links the sites.

  • Stand in the doorway of the Beamless Hall and expose for the interior, which gives you the vault receding symmetrically.
  • The pagoda's carved characters are legible from the path below, which is the reason to shoot it rather than just the tower.
  • The valley's pines are the subject as much as the buildings, and the light through them is best in the early morning.

The Qinhuai River and Fuzimiao at Night (秦淮河夫子庙)

The inner Qinhuai is 4.8 km of water running through the old city between the West Water Gate and the East Water Gate, and the stretch in front of the Confucius Temple has been lit at night since the area was rebuilt in the 1980s. The photograph that works is from a boat or from the Wenmiao Bridge, with the red lanterns strung across the water, the painted rooflines of the riverside shops, and the reflections doing half the work. The name Qinhuai comes from the Tang poet Du Mu's poem written about this exact stretch of water, so the association with the city is literary as well as architectural.

best light: blue hour · night

Getting there: Metro Line 3 to Fuzimiao station. The night cruise boats leave from the docks on either side of the Wenmiao Bridge, and the Wenmiao Bridge itself is walkable.

  • Go onto the water if you can; the reflections of the lantern lines in the river are half the picture and you cannot get that from the bank.
  • The bridge deck gives a symmetrical frame with the lanterns receding on both sides.
  • A longer exposure of 2 to 4 seconds smooths the boat movement without losing the lantern detail.

Xuanwu Lake (玄武湖)

Xuanwu Lake, called Sangpo before the Northern and Southern Dynasties, is the largest remaining imperial garden lake in the Jiangnan region and the largest urban park in the region, with five islands, Liangzhou, Huanzhou, Yingzhou, Lingzhou and Cuizhou. It was once the training ground for the Southern Dynasties navy, and it has been dredged, filled and re-dug several times, so the current shoreline is largely 20th-century. What makes it photographable is the contrast with the city: the Lake Jade Pagoda, the Xuanwu Gate fortification and the distant skyline all sit against open water and lotus beds.

best light: sunset · nature

Getting there: Metro Line 1 to Xuanwumen station. The lake perimeter road is 9.5 km and is car-free in sections, with bicycle rental at the gates.

  • The Xuanwu Gate wall platform gives you the ancient fortification and the open water in one frame.
  • The lotus beds are at their best in July and August, when the light through the leaves is worth the early start.
  • The Lake Jade Pagoda on the smallest island is the classic landmark, and a long lens from the western shore compresses the pagoda against the city skyline.

Nanjing City Wall and Xuanwu Gate (南京城墙)

The Nanjing wall was built by the Hongwu Emperor between 1366 and 1386 and is still the longest city wall in the world, running 35.3 km around the old capital, with a height of about 14 m and a walkway 2.1 to 4.7 m wide along the top. The best preserved section is the Xuanwu Gate, whose complex barbican is the finest surviving example of a Ming city gate, with a double arched passage, a double barbican and a vaulted tunnel for boats that once ran through the gate wall. Walking the top gives you a raised view across the tiled roofs of the old city to the lake.

best light: golden hour · landmark

Getting there: Xuanwu Gate is on the north side of the old city, with its own car park and entrance, and Metro Line 1 to Xuanwumen station nearby. The wall also has a well-known night section open along the Taiping Rebellion route.

  • Shoot from inside the barbican passage looking out through the tunnel, which frames the light in a heavy dark arch.
  • The wall walk between Xuanwu Gate and Shence Gate is the most intact stretch and gives the best view over the old city roofs.
  • Late afternoon light comes from the west along the wall, which lights the battlements and the merlons in relief.

Presidential Palace and the Republic-Era Civic Quarter (南京总统府)

The Presidential Palace is a complex that reads as three centuries at once: the Ming-era Ming Palace Tower and the Qing-era Grand Audience Hall, both surviving in a Republic-era compound that became the seat of the Republic of China government from 1912. The architecture mixes Chinese palace form with westernised detailing, and the building that matters photographically is the Grand Audience Hall, with a blue-glazed tile roof on a red-columned veranda. Around it, the former government quarter on Zhongshan East Road has the same Republican modernism seen in Shanghai and Tianjin, and the plane trees are large enough to arch over the street.

best light: golden hour · street-life

Getting there: Metro Line 2 and Line 3 to Daxinggong station, with the palace complex immediately north. The main gate is on Zhongshan East Road and there is underground parking inside the site.

  • The Grand Audience Hall's veranda is best shot from the lawn in front with a long lens, which compresses the colonnade.
  • Morning light on the east-facing side of the compound is cleaner than the afternoon, which hits the rear.
  • The Zhongshan East Road plane trees make a good arch over the carriageway on the way in.

Autumn light on Purple Mountain

Nanjing's photographic calendar is decided by the last week of November, when the maples on Purple Mountain and in the Linggu valley turn and the low winter sun comes in under the cloud base. The combination of warm low light and saturated red foliage is the reason to bring a polariser: it will deepen the greens in the tree corridor and cut the haze on the distant city views from the mausoleum terrace.

The other technical situation in Nanjing is the Qinhuai at night. The lantern light is warm and intense but the sky above the old city is a deep blue, which is a genuinely high-contrast scene. Expose for the lanterns and let the sky go slightly dark, and a two to four second shutter will smooth the water and the boat movement without losing the colour of the reds.

For the wall and the old city, the brick and the tiled roofs reward a slight underexposure. The surfaces are mid-grey and the sky in the city is usually bright, so metering for the sky will render the architecture flat. Shooting in the last two hours before sunset gives you both the colour in the masonry and a sky with enough tone left to hold detail.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to photograph the Ming Xiaoling Spirit Way?

Late November for the red maple, or early spring for the wisteria. The avenue is 2.4 km and lined with pairs of stone figures set in a mature tree corridor, so a long lens across the path compresses the pairs into a repeating pattern. Shoot from a low position at the level of the animals, and arrive early on the November weekends because the colour attracts very heavy crowds.

How do I capture the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum without people in the frame?

Go at opening time or in the last hour before close. The complex is busy in the middle of the day with tour groups, and the stair run is wide enough that any position fills up. The most complete frame is from the upper terrace looking back down the 392 steps, which needs the top of the climb to be empty, so the early morning is the better option than the late afternoon.

Is the Qinhuai River at night worth the crowds?

Yes, and the boat makes the difference. The inner Qinhuai is 4.8 km of water through the old city, and the lit stretch in front of the Confucius Temple has the lantern lines and painted rooflines that people come for. From the Wenmiao Bridge you get a symmetrical frame, but from a boat you get the reflections in the water, which is half the image. A 2 to 4 second exposure smooths the boat movement and keeps the lantern colour.

What is the least known good photo spot in Nanjing?

The Beamless Hall, the Wuliang Hall, in the Linggu Temple valley. Built in 1381 as the Yongle Emperor's sarcophagus chamber, it is a corbelled brick and stone vault with no timber beams, and the only illumination comes through the doorway, giving a symmetric view down the vault. It is on Purple Mountain, a short walk from the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, and almost every visitor passes the gate without going in.

Is the Nanjing City Wall walkable end to end?

The wall is 35.3 km around the old city and is the longest city wall in the world, but the walkable heritage sections are broken into several managed stretches rather than being continuous. Xuanwu Gate has the finest barbican, with a double-arched passage and a vaulted tunnel that once carried boats through the gate wall. The section between Xuanwu Gate and Shence Gate is the most intact for walking, and the night section along the Taiping Rebellion route is separately lit and open later.

Are drone flights possible over the Nanjing City Wall?

Not over the wall or the Ming Xiaoling area, which are nationally protected heritage sites with strict protection rules, and there is controlled airspace in the approach to the airport to the south-east. The Purple Mountain scenic area also has restrictions. The wall walk at 14 m high and the mausoleum terrace give you raised perspectives without any of the regulatory problems.
