Overview: Day Trips from Beijing

Beijing's ring roads hide a set of day-trip destinations that are entirely unlike the Forbidden City and Tiananmen. Within an hour or two you can be standing on Ming brick at the Great Wall, walking the 7 km Spirit Way at the Ming Tombs, or sitting in a 1,600-year-old ginkgo grove at Hongluo Temple. Each one is a genuine half-day or full-day outing rather than a photo stop, and each rewards a car because the last stretch of road is never the point.

Mutianyu Great Wall (慕田峪长城)

Mutianyu is the best-preserved Ming wall in Beijing and, unlike Badaling, it is not mobbed. The section runs 2,250 m along the ridge with 22 watchtowers, built mainly of granite and 7 to 8.5 m high with a top 4 to 5 m wide. Forest cover over 90 percent of the hills means the approach road is green rather than the bare scar you get further west. Three ascents and four descents are offered, including a two-rider chairlift, a four-rider gondola and a single-rider toboggan on a winding metal track down to the valley. The wall predates Badaling, appearing in the Northern Qi in the mid-6th century.

How the day runs: Leave central Beijing at 7:00 to clear the 40 km of expressway before traffic builds. Park at the Mutianyu scenic area roundabout and buy the 15 RMB shuttle ticket that runs between the tour plaza and the foot of the wall. Take the chairlift up at 9:00, walk east toward the 14th watchtower, and push on to the 20th for the least crowded stretch — allow 90 minutes for the full ridge. Come down by toboggan, which is faster and more fun than the steps. Sit out at the base for lunch near the village, then reach Jiankou, the older and rougher ridge a few kilometres west, for 4 pm light if the schedule allows. Leave by 5:30 to beat the outbound traffic on the S11.

70 km from Beijing · about 90 min by car from Beijing · full-day trip · best in Late September to mid-October for clear skies and stable weather; avoid the Chinese New Year and the first week of October holiday when parking fills before 9 am.

  • The Mutianyu express coach from Dongzhimen metro runs a morning departure at 8:30 and returns at 16:00, and it only takes cash.
  • Grippy hiking shoes matter more here than on Badaling, because the restored brick steps between towers are steep and polished by visitors.
  • Toboggan riders must be at least 1.3 m tall and 20 kg or over, so small children have to use the steps instead.

Badaling Great Wall (八达岭长城)

Badaling is the most visited section of the Great Wall anywhere, and the reason is geography: it sits roughly 80 km northwest of the city, close to the Juyong Pass road that has always funnelled traffic out of the plain. The wall here was built in 1504 under the Ming, and the surviving walkable run is 3,741 m. Average height is over 7 m, with sections reaching 14 m, and the base averages 6.5 m wide against a top width of 5 m. Its high point, Beibalou, reaches about 1,015 m above sea level.

How the day runs: Depart at 6:00 am to reach the cable car queue before it becomes an hour long. The ground cable car line runs 700 m from the lower station opposite the Zhan Tianyou memorial hall up to the South Fourth Tower, which saves roughly 45 minutes of climbing. Walk north from there toward the North Eighth Tower for the highest viewpoint and the widest valley view, then turn around rather than pushing the full length. Come back down past the bullet-hole tower for the classic view down into the valley, which is exactly the framing that appears in most Badaling photography. Have lunch at the base plaza, then head back at 2 pm to avoid the Sunday-afternoon crush.

80 km from Beijing · about 110 min by car from Beijing · full-day trip · best in Spring and autumn; the section opens at 6:30 am in summer and 7:30 am in winter, so an early start matters in any season.

  • A separate North Line and South Line of the wall exist on either side of the valley; the North Line is generally quieter.
  • The restored section is quite commercial, with cable cars, a glass platform and a large tourist centre at the base.
  • Going on a weekday rather than a weekend changes the character of the place more than anything else.

Ming Tombs and Dingling (明十三陵)

The Thirteen Tombs occupy a 40 square km basin under Tianshou Mountain in Changping, roughly 50 km from the city centre, and they are the largest and best-preserved group of imperial mausoleums from any Chinese dynasty. Construction began in 1409 when the Yongle Emperor chose the site on feng shui grounds, and the first mausoleum, Changling, was not finished until 1427. The 7 km Spirit Way lined with stone officials and guardian animals is the spine of the complex. Only three tombs are open: Changling, Dingling and Zhaoling, plus Kangling. Dingling is the standout, the underground chamber of the Wanli Emperor's tomb opened in 1957.

How the day runs: Start at the stone archway at the southern end of the Spirit Way, a five-arched pailou built in 1540 and one of the largest surviving Ming stone archways in China. Walk 1.5 km to the Great Red Gate, the ceremonial entrance to the tomb precinct, then continue along the avenue past the marble animals. Reach Dingling around 10:30, allow 90 minutes for the excavated underground palace with its jade burial suit, then climb to the above-ground halls. Cross to Zhaoling in the afternoon, which has the best preserved spirit way of the open mausoleums and the most spacious grounds. Be back in the city by 5 pm.

45 km from Beijing · about 70 min by car from Beijing · half-day trip · best in April for wisteria and peony at the entrance, October for the ginkgo and maple on the Spirit Way, and any winter week for clear air over the valley.

  • The shuttle bus runs along the Spirit Way between the parking area and the tombs, and it saves a long walk in summer heat.
  • Dingling requires a separate entry ticket from the scenic area and a queue that can reach an hour at peak times.
  • The Ming Tombs were listed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2003 together with the Qing Eastern and Western Tombs.

Hongluo Temple (红螺寺)

Hongluo is the oldest Buddhist temple in the Beijing area, founded in 338 during the Eastern Jin by the monk Fotucheng, and it sits at the southern foot of Hongluo Mountain about 4 km north of Huairou town. It is far less visited than the Great Wall sections and rewards a slower look: the grounds cover 4 square km, with more than 700 plant species across 70 tree types, including two ginkgos over a thousand years old called the male and female pair. The male tree has grown one side trunk for every dynasty since the Tang, and now carries ten.

How the day runs: Aim for 9 am arrival, which puts you there before the tour buses from Beijing begin arriving. Enter through the mountain gate and go straight to the Temple of the Dragon King and the Main Hall, then spend the middle of the day in the courtyard garden where the two ancient ginkgos stand. The 500-year-old wisteria trained over a flat-topped pine behind the Main Hall puts out a dense purple canopy in late spring and shades an area of more than 400 square metres. Walk the hillside path above the temple for views over the Ronglu Lake valley before eating lunch at the temple vegetarian restaurant. Return to the city by 4 pm to avoid the weekend traffic back through Huairou.

75 km from Beijing · about 100 min by car from Beijing · half-day trip · best in Mid-to-late November for the ginkgo turning gold; early spring for the temple plum and for fewer buses.

  • The male ginkgo is the one that flowers but does not fruit; the female ginkgo fruits but does not flower.
  • The temple is a working monastery administered by the Huairou District cultural relics bureau rather than the Buddhist association.
  • Pair it with a drive through the Huairou reservoir road for genuinely northern scenery an hour from the capital.

Jinshanling and Simatai Great Wall (金山岭长城)

Jinshanling is the quieter alternative to Mutianyu and the first wall most experienced hikers choose. It runs 10.5 km from Gubeikou in the west to Wangjing Lou in the east, with five passes and 67 watchtowers, and its tallest tower sits at 980 m above sea level. The masonry here is brick-faced with a stone core, and unlike the heavily rebuilt tourist sections, large stretches still show the original Ming repair work. The eastern tower's brick embrasure was used in the CCTV national anthem sequence. Joining it to Simatai, the most precipitous section of the wall at 5.4 km, gives a wild 6 km ridge walk.

How the day runs: Leave at 6:30 am; the drive through the northeastern suburbs and into Hebei takes close to two and a half hours. At Jinshanling park in the west end and take the cable car to save time on the initial climb. Walk east along the ridge, spending the main of the morning in the section around the 15th to 30th tower where the wall undulates over open ridges with long views. Continue to Wangjing Lou by early afternoon, then decide whether to push on toward Simatai, which adds two more hours of ridge, or to turn back. Take the cable car down at the Simatai end if you have walked the whole way. Be on the road home by 4 pm.

130 km from Beijing · about 150 min by car from Beijing · full-day trip · best in October for clear air and autumn colour in the hills; the exposed ridge is uncomfortable in high summer wind.

  • Simatai and Jinshanling are separate ticketed areas with separate parking, so a car that cannot reach Simatai is genuinely locked in.
  • This is the section to attempt if you want an unrestored, physically demanding wall rather than a reconstructed monument.
  • The wall is exposed and there is little shade for most of the ridge, so carry more water than you think you need.

Summer Palace and Kunming Lake (颐和园)

The Summer Palace is the most complete imperial garden surviving from the Qing, laid out across 297 hectares in Haidian northwest of the city, three quarters of it water. The original Qingyi Yuan was begun in 1750 by the Qianlong Emperor and completed in 1764 at a cost of more than 4.8 million silver taels. Kunming Lake is entirely man-made: the Yuan engineer Guo Shoujing dug it in the 1270s as a reservoir and training ground, and its excavated earth became Longevity Hill. The complex is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the 1,287 km South-to-North Water Transfer Project terminates here, making the lake Beijing's main water supply.

How the day runs: Enter through the East Palace Gate at 8:30 and take the boat across Kunming Lake rather than walking the eastern shore, which cuts 40 minutes off the circuit. Get off at the South Lake Island and continue on foot through the long corridor and the Hall of Dispelling Clouds. Climb to the Tower of Buddhist Incense, a 41 m structure on a 20 m stone base that was originally conceived as a nine-storey pagoda modelled on the Yellow Crane Tower before it was stopped at the eighth storey. Work your way down past the Suzhou Street replica, which the Qianlong Emperor had built after a tour of Jiangnan, then finish along the western shore and the Marble Boat. Total walking is about 5 km, and leave by 2 pm.

15 km from Beijing · about 35 min by car from Beijing · half-day trip · best in April for the Kunming Lake bank of flowering crabapple and wisteria, and late November for the reflected pagoda shots across still water.

  • Boat rides and the enclosed shuttle buses inside the grounds cost extra, and they do save a lot of energy in summer.
  • The western bank of the lake is where the evening light is best for photographing the 17-arch bridge and the reflected pagoda.
  • The complex is genuinely walkable at 5 km, so comfortable shoes matter more than fitness.

Driving out of Beijing for the day

Beijing's ring roads are gridlocked between 7:30 and 9 am and again from 4:30 pm, and the northern expressways toward Badaling and Jinshanling are the worst affected. Leaving before 6:30 am is the single biggest factor in whether a day trip feels relaxed or stressful, because the same distance takes 70 minutes at 6 am and two hours at 8 am.

Park-and-ride is the standard solution. Every major attraction outside the ring roads has its own paid car park with a shuttle bus to the entrance, and the shuttle tickets are cheap — 15 RMB at Mutianyu — but they are separate from the admission ticket and worth buying.

For anything more than an hour from the centre, a driver who knows the small county roads is worth more than a faster car. The last few kilometres to Hongluo, the Ming Tombs and the Jinshanling entrance all involve navigation that a phone will get wrong on a screen that will not update.

Planning a day trip from Beijing

Summer Palace and Kunming Lake, Jinshanling and Simatai Great Wall and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Beijing day trip route — Summer Palace and Kunming Lake sits closest at 15 km, roughly 35 minutes by car, while Jinshanling and Simatai Great Wall reaches furthest at 130 km, about 150 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Ming Tombs and Dingling, Hongluo Temple, Jinshanling and Simatai Great Wall each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Beijing itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Great Wall section is best if I only have one day from Beijing?

Mutianyu. At 70 km from the city centre it is closer than Badaling and far quieter, with 22 watchtowers across 2,250 m of well-restored Ming granite, woodland on nearly every approach, and both a gondola and a toboggan for getting up and down. Badaling is the more famous choice and has the higher summit at 1,015 m, but it is the most visited wall in China and the queues reflect that.

How early do I need to leave Beijing for Badaling?

By 6 am to arrive at 8, when the first cable car starts and the numbers on the wall are still manageable. The drive is around 110 minutes on the G6 expressway under free-flowing conditions, but the return leg on a Sunday afternoon can take considerably longer. If you cannot get an early start, Jinshanling at 130 km is a better deal than a late arrival at Badaling.

Is the Ming Tombs worth half a day, or should I give it the full day?

Half a day is right for the open tombs. The 7 km Spirit Way, the Great Red Gate, Changling, Dingling and Zhaoling together fill a comfortable morning and early afternoon, and you can add the Dingling underground palace without rushing. The full-day option only makes sense if you also want to walk the whole basin to the closed tombs at the far end, which adds two hours of relatively quiet walkway.

Can I visit the Great Wall without a car?

Yes, and it is easier than most visitors expect. A tourist coach runs from the Dongzhimen bus parking lot outside Dongzhimen metro station directly to Mutianyu, departing around 8:30 am and returning at 4 pm, for 30 RMB in cash. Badaling is on the Beijing Metro Line 1 to Badaling station with a shuttle bus for the last stretch. Both work for a solo traveller who does not need a car waiting at the top of the ridge.

What season should I pick for a Beijing day trip?

October is the safest answer for the wall sections — the rain has stopped, the summer humidity has gone and the crowds thin after the first week of the holiday. April is better for the softer sights: the Ming Tombs wisteria, the Hongluo plum and the Kunming Lake crabapple all bloom in the same three weeks. Winter is fine for the Forbidden City and the wall in clear weather, but it is cold on the exposed Jinshanling ridge.

Do the day trips work with children or older visitors?

The Ming Tombs, the Summer Palace and the Temple of Heaven area are the easiest, with flat paths, shuttle buses and plenty of places to stop. Mutianyu is manageable because the chairlift and toboggan remove the hardest climbing. Jinshanling and Simatai are the least accessible of the group, with long exposed ridge sections and no internal transport, so they suit fitter walkers rather than small children.
