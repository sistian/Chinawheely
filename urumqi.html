Name Origin

Urumqi (Wulumuqi) means "beautiful pasture" in the Mongolian language. It is the capital of Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region and the farthest major city from any ocean.

Education & Universities

Xinjiang University, Xinjiang Medical University, Xinjiang University of Finance and Economics, and Xinjiang Agricultural University are major institutions.

Getting Around

Urumqi Diwopu International Airport (URC) has domestic and international flights. Urumqi Railway Station and Urumqi South Station are major rail hubs. The city has one metro line (Line 1).

Natural Resources & Environment

Urumqi lies in the northern foothills of the Tianshan Mountains with a semi-arid continental climate. It has mountains, the Urumqi River, and is a gateway to the Tian Shan mountains.

What to eat and what to bring home from Urumqi

Traditional Urumqi teahouses serve loose-leaf tea in covered bowls, and the regional green teas are the usual house pour. Your driver can point you to a teahouse that specialists use.

Urumqi Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Urumqi like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

Heavenly Lake is the sight everyone should build the day around. Ask your driver to arrive early, when the coaches have not arrived yet, and to wait nearby rather than circling the car park.

🥢 Skip the tourist menu

Eat where the queues are shortest: the morning market and the small noodle shops a block back from the main square usually feed locals at a fraction of the tourist-restaurant price.

⏱️ Plan the driving time

Allow extra time between the airport or main station and the old town — the city spreads out, and the distance that looks short on a map can take half an hour in traffic.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Urumqi

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Urumqi

🍵 Tea Houses of Urumqi

Tea culture runs through the day in Urumqi: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
