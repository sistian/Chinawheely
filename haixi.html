Name Origin

Haixi means "west of the lake" — everything from Qinghai Lake's western shore out toward Xinjiang. Locals also call it Qaidam, after the great basin at its heart. The official title carries both Mongolian and Tibetan names, and until 1985 Kazakhs too, before they returned to Xinjiang and the prefecture took its present name.

Education & Universities

Qinghai Qaidam Vocational and Technical College (青海柴达木职业技术学院) is the prefecture's listed institution of higher education.

Getting Around

The Haixi corridor is one of the great land gates between inland China and southern Xinjiang — an ancient Silk Road route ranked alongside the famous Hexi Corridor. The county-level cities (Delingha, Golmud, Mangya) and counties (Ulan, Dulan, Tianjun) string along it, and the old Qinghai-Tibet Highway construction headquarters, the "General's Building" (将军楼), survives as a national heritage monument to the road-builders who opened this route toward Tibet.

Natural Resources & Environment

Haixi is the Qaidam Basin prefecture: Kunlun, Altun and Qilian ranges ring a basin averaging about 3,000 m, its landforms banded outward from mountains through hills and gobi to plains and salt marshes, with crescent dune chains across the south. It holds China's largest salt lake, Qarhan, plus Chaka and Keke salt lakes and a chain of others — Tuosu, Dabusun, Sugan, East and West Taijnai, and the Hohusu twins. The southwestern exclave of Tanggula Town reaches the Yangtze's true source, the Tuotuo River rising at Geladandong (6,621 m). Elevations span 2,675-6,860 m, yet the basin floor at 2,700-3,200 m is one of Qinghai's warmer corners under a dry, windy, cold-desert climate.

Best Time to Visit Haixi

Dry and sunny most of the year; summer is the comfortable window before winter cold closes the high routes.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · 3-5月 — Dry and windy; basin days can be surprisingly mild. (daytime 6.7–17.8°C, nights around -7°C; about 11 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · 6-8月 — The travel season, warm by Qinghai standards on the 2,700-3,200 m basin floor. (daytime 20.7–23.1°C, nights around 8.2°C; about 37 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · 9-10月 — Clear but cooling fast, especially off the basin. (daytime 3.7–18.3°C, nights around -9.4°C; about 8 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · 11-2月 — Bitterly cold in the mountains and dry everywhere; salt lakes photograph starkly but expect ice. (daytime -3.7–0.7°C, nights around -16.8°C; about 3 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Haixi

What to drink in Haixi, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Haixi

No tea custom is recorded for Haixi; on the dry, windy basin floor — where annual winds average 3-4 m/s — the practical rule is simply to drink more water than you think you need.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Haixi

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Haixi

🍵 Tea Houses of Haixi

Tea culture runs through the day in Haixi: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
