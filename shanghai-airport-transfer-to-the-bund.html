The Bund is a waterfront area and protected historical district centred on East Zhongshan Road No.1, running along the west bank of the Huangpu River and facing the towers of Lujiazui across the water. From the 1860s to the 1930s it was the financial centre of the foreign establishment in the treaty-port city, and 52 buildings of Western classical and modern styles still line this stretch of road. Both of Shanghai's airports serve it: Hongqiao from 13 kilometres west, Pudong from roughly 30 kilometres east.

Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG)

Pudong Airport sits about 30 kilometres east of the city centre on a coastal site, and the Bund lies on the far, western bank of the Huangpu River. The run west on the Yingbin Expressway and through the river tunnels covers roughly 42 kilometres, adding the final cross-river stage that a Lujiazui drop-off avoids.

Shanghai Hongqiao International Airport (SHA)

18 km from the Shanghai terminal, about ? minutes by car.

Hongqiao Airport stands 13 kilometres west of downtown Shanghai near the town of Hongqiao, in Changning and Minhang districts. The eastbound run on the Yan'an Elevated Road passes south of the old city before the last blocks to East Zhongshan Road No.1, a shorter trip in kilometres but one that shares the city-centre congestion of the final approach.

Why transfer to the Bund?

The district is a catalogue of styles — Gothic, Baroque, Neoclassical, Romanesque, Art Deco and Renaissance — built by banks and trading houses from Britain, France, the United States, Italy, Russia, Germany, Japan, the Netherlands and Belgium, together with consulates, a newspaper, the Shanghai Club and the Masonic Club. The British and American settlements were combined into the International Settlement in 1863, and Beaux Arts commercial buildings rose around the turn of the 20th century as the Bund became a major financial centre of East Asia.

The waterfront has been rebuilt more than once. Zhongshan Road was widened in the 1990s, a tall flood levee was constructed in the same decade, and the pre-Expo makeover included a year-long restoration of the century-old Waibaidu Bridge at the northern end and the conversion of the former 10-lane roadway into a double-levelled arrangement, after which an elevated bypass built in 1991 was demolished as obsolete. The Bund reopened to the public in 2010.

What to do at the Bund once you arrive

The walk itself is the attraction: the row of 52 buildings on the west side of East Zhongshan Road No.1, with the Customs House clock tower of 1927 at No. 13 among the landmarks, and the elevated promenade — raised about 10 metres above street level behind the flood levee — giving an unbroken view across the river to the Lujiazui skyline. The Waibaidu Bridge closes the circuit at the north end where Suzhou Creek meets the Huangpu.

South of the bridge the street turns towards the old walled city and the former French Bund of the Shanghai French Concession, once a working harbourside of comparable size. The Bund's own ground floors carry some of the city's noted eateries. Metro Line 2 and Line 10 both stop within blocks at East Nanjing Road station, and the ferry terminals at the water's edge run across to Pudong, so the district connects onward without needing a vehicle again.

  • East Nanjing Road station on Metro Lines 2 and 10 sits a few blocks inland from the waterfront
  • The raised promenade sits roughly 10 metres above street level behind the 1990s flood levee
  • Waibaidu Bridge and the Suzhou Creek mouth mark the north end of the walk
  • The river ferry to the Pudong bank boards at the waterfront and costs a few yuan

Arriving at Shanghai's two airports

Pudong handled roughly 85 million passengers in 2025, fifth-busiest in the world and the busiest international gateway of the mainland with 35.25 million international passengers, and it runs two passenger terminals with four parallel runways. Hongqiao, 13 kilometres from downtown, is the domestic and regional workhorse — a base for China Eastern, Shanghai Airlines, Juneyao Air and Spring Airlines, hosting 25 airlines at 63 scheduled destinations. Which airport you land at changes the drive to the Bund by more than 20 kilometres.

From Pudong, the metro's Line 2 serves both terminals but stops around 21:00, the maglev to Longyang Road costs 50 yuan single or 40 yuan with a same-day flight ticket, and a metered taxi to the centre runs about 150 yuan. From Hongqiao, Line 2 runs direct to East Nanjing Road in about half an hour. A pre-booked car from either airport lands you at the kerb on East Zhongshan Road No.1 regardless of the hour, with the driver tracking the flight number for delays.

What the transfer costs in Shanghai Airport Transfer To The Bund

From Pudong the metered taxi benchmark to the city centre is around 150 yuan; the Bund's position across the river keeps it near the lower end of that figure. Private transfers from PVG typically quote in the 250 to 400 yuan band for a sedan, tolls and airport waiting included. From Hongqiao the same trip is shorter — a metered run lands well below the Pudong figure — and private sedan transfers are commonly quoted from about 150 yuan upward.

Vans and seven-seaters suit groups arriving together, and both airports' ranks handle them, though queues at Pudong after international banks of flights can stretch long. Where a booked car earns its margin is the fixed price across a delayed arrival and the drop at your exact address on the Bund side, rather than the rank plus a walk from wherever the taxi is held by the riverside traffic controls.

East Zhongshan Road No.1 is a controlled roadway: have the driver use the hotel forecourts and side-street drop points one block inland, then walk the last block to the promenade, since kerbside stopping on the waterfront road itself is restricted.

Frequently Asked Questions: Booking in Shanghai Airport Transfer To The Bund

Which Shanghai airport is closer to the Bund?

Hongqiao, by a wide margin. It stands 13 kilometres west of downtown Shanghai in Changning and Minhang districts, against Pudong's roughly 30 kilometres east of the centre on the coast. If your flight options include Hongqiao, it is the shorter and cheaper run to the waterfront, typically under 30 minutes without heavy traffic.

How long does the transfer from Pudong Airport to the Bund take?

The road distance is roughly 42 kilometres via the Yingbin Expressway and the cross-river tunnels. Budget 50 to 70 minutes: the expressway section runs quickly, and the slow part is the final approach through the city streets to East Zhongshan Road No.1, particularly in the evening peak when the tunnel portals back up.

Can I take the metro from either airport to the Bund?

Yes. Line 2 serves Pudong's terminals, Hongqiao's terminals and East Nanjing Road station, a few blocks inland from the waterfront, in one ride. From Pudong the service runs until about 21:00; later than that, the maglev to Longyang Road plus a Line 2 connection, or a car, are the remaining options.

What are the landmark buildings to look for on the Bund?

Fifty-two protected buildings of Western classical and modern styles line the road. The Customs House at No. 13, completed in 1927, carries the clock tower that anchors the row; the Waibaidu Bridge at the north end is over a century old; and the former bank and trading house facades represent Gothic, Baroque, Neoclassical, Romanesque, Art Deco and Renaissance styles.

Where is the best place for a driver to drop off at the Bund?

The waterfront road is a controlled carriageway with the elevated promenade behind the flood levee, so direct kerbside stops are restricted. Use a hotel forecourt or a side street one block inland off East Zhongshan Road No.1, then walk east to the raised walkway — the whole row of buildings remains in view the entire way.
