Hongyadong is an 11-storey complex of timber-framed stilt houses (diaojiaolou) built into a cliff on the southern bank of the Jialing River, and it is the reason Chongqing photographs the way it does after dark. The site covers 53,000 square metres of commercial space spread across four streets and eleven levels, and it holds a national 4A scenic rating. Coming from Jiangbei Airport in the evening puts you at the best light of the day without the pressure of finding a car park first.

Chongqing Jiangbei International Airport (CKG)

28 km from the Chongqing terminal, about 45 minutes by car.

Jiangbei opened in 1990 and sits about 19 km north of the city centre in the Liangjiang New Area, with Terminal 3A taking domestic and international traffic. The drive south crosses the Jialing River on one of the bridges and ends in the Yuzhong District, where the hillside complex rises directly above the riverbank next to Jiefangbei.

Why transfer to Hongyadong?

The complex is a modern reconstruction in a traditional style, and the history underneath it is real. The cliff face here was occupied from the ancient Ba state period, and the site takes its name from Hongyang Gate, a city gate dated to the 14th century during the early Ming. The hillside filled with stilt-house housing when waterborne trade made the area busy in the 1940s, and those buildings were pulled down from 2005 onwards by the local government and replaced with new structures in the bayu diaojiaolou idiom. Construction of the new complex began in 2006.

What visitors actually come for is the evening lighting. The stacked timber balconies, hung with lanterns and reflected in the river below, have become the most-reproduced image in the city, and the appearance of the complex has been compared to the bathhouse district in the 2001 animated film Spirited Away. The difference between arriving at four in the afternoon and arriving at seven is the difference between a lit-up riverside and a shopping street, which is the single strongest argument for taking the transfer as an evening drop rather than a daytime one.

  • The complex is 11 storeys high with four commercial streets inside, covering 53,000 square metres
  • It is built into the cliff on the southern bank of the Jialing River in the Jiefangbei commercial district
  • Hongyang Gate, the 14th-century Ming city gate the site is named after, stood on this spot
  • The new complex was built from 2006 after the original stilt houses were demolished from 2005

What to do at Hongyadong once you arrive

The complex is organised vertically. The fourth floor holds the food market, including the spice stalls that Chongqing is famous for, and the upper floors give way to restaurants and bars, with a viewing platform at the top where the Jialing River can be watched. The four named streets are Tiancheng Lane Bayu Style Street, the Hongyadong Feast Food Street, the City Balcony Foreign Style Street and the riverside promenade, and they are stacked one above the other along stepped lanes.

Most people walk down through the middle levels, eat, then climb to the upper viewing platform and come back down to the riverbank at street level, where the buildings are lit from below and the reflections in the water are the point of the whole place. Note that this is a working commercial district as well as a sightseeing site, so the ground floors fill with diners in the evening and the lanes between the stilt houses get crowded. Coming by car and walking in from the Jiefangbei side keeps you out of the worst of it.

  • Scenic area admission is 32 yuan, or 88 yuan combined with the Bayu cultural performance, and ticket sales stop at 17:00
  • Opening hours are 9:00 to 18:00 for the ticketed scenic area, though the riverside streets stay open into the evening
  • Chongqing Rail Transit Line 1 serves Xiaoshizi station, a short walk from Jiefangbei and the complex
  • The Jiang River cableway runs between the two banks and gives a good overhead view of the stilt houses

Arriving at Chongqing Jiangbei International Airport

Jiangbei has three passenger terminals in use, with Terminal 1 closed, Terminal 2A and 2B handling domestic services and the larger Terminal 3A taking both domestic and international traffic. Terminal 3B, a new satellite concourse, and a fourth runway were completed in 2025. Terminal 1 was the original passenger building when the airport opened in 1990, and Terminal 2A and 2B were added around a 2004 expansion. Rail Transit Line 10 runs between Terminal 2 and Terminal 3A, and a free shuttle bus covers the same link for luggage.

Allow twenty minutes for checked bags and immigration, particularly on international arrivals into Terminal 3A, where the walk out is long. Every booked driver meets you in the arrivals hall with a name board and monitors the flight number. The road south from the airport crosses the Jialing River, and that crossing can slow down badly in the evening peak, so leave a margin if you want to be at the complex for the lighting. Local traffic towards Jiefangbei is heavy well into the evening for the same reason.

What the transfer costs

A private car from CKG to Hongyadong is typically priced at 130 to 190 yuan for a sedan, with the quote covering fuel, tolls and the driver's waiting time at the drop-off. A sedan suits one or two people with hold luggage. A van or seven-seat MPV suits a family or a small group and usually comes to 170 to 240 yuan.

Hires of six hours or more are billed at a fixed day rate instead of a per-kilometre meter, and waiting time at the drop-off is normally included for the first two hours, which is enough to cover the climb from the car up to the viewing platform and back down again. A sedan is the usual choice for one or two people, while a van or MPV suits a family or a group and costs more on the base rate rather than on a per-passenger charge. Because the streets inside the complex are pedestrian-only, the vehicle part of the visit is short and the walking part is long.

Ask for the drop-off on the Jiefangbei side rather than the riverside road, since the lanes inside the complex are too narrow for cars to turn around in. For an evening visit, agree a fixed pick-up time at a main road junction near the complex rather than having the driver wait in the pedestrian lanes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is it from Chongqing Jiangbei Airport to Hongyadong?

Jiangbei Airport lies about 19 km north of the Chongqing city centre, and Hongyadong sits on the south bank of the Jialing River in the Yuzhong District, so the full road distance including the river crossing is roughly 28 km. Expect about 45 minutes with clear traffic, and allow longer in the evening peak when the bridges into Yuzong slow down.

Do I need a ticket for Hongyadong and how long is it open?

The ticketed scenic area is 32 yuan, and a combined ticket with the Bayu cultural performance is 88 yuan. Ticketed entry runs 9:00 to 18:00 with sales stopping at 17:00. The riverside streets and the lower commercial levels are open into the evening, and that is when the lighting is worth seeing, so a late afternoon arrival works better than a midday one.

What time should I arrive to see the lights?

Aim for an hour before full dark. The lanterns and the under-lighting come on through the early evening, and the reflections in the Jialing River need another half hour to build. Arriving at four in the afternoon gives you time to eat in the food market on the fourth floor and find a spot on the upper viewing platform before the best light.

Which metro line goes to Hongyadong?

Chongqing Rail Transit Line 1 runs through the middle of the Yuzhong District with Xiaoshizi, Jiaochangkou and Chaotianmen among its stations, and Xiaoshizi puts you a short walk from the Jiefangbei commercial district where the complex stands. Line 1 runs east to west through the city centre and its extension to Bishan is also open, which is useful if you are combining this with a trip out of town.

How long does a visit take and is it worth going up and down more than once?

Two hours is enough to walk down through the commercial levels, eat, reach the top viewing platform and come back to the riverbank. The complex is vertical rather than large, so most of the walking is stairs rather than distance. Staying for the evening lighting is the best use of the time rather than rushing in and out in the middle of the afternoon.
