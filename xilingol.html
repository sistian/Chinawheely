Name Origin

Xilin Gol means "the river of the plateau" in Mongolian, after the Xilin Gol river that crosses the grasslands. The league was the country of the Xiongnu, Rouran and Turkic khaganates, of the Khitan and the Jurchen, and then of the Mongol banners — and it holds the summer capital of the Yuan dynasty at Shangdu, in Zhenglan Banner.

Education & Universities

Higher education is served by Inner Mongolia Normal University for Nationalities, the Xilin Gol branch of Inner Mongolia University for Nationalities and local vocational colleges, with strengths in animal husbandry, veterinary medicine, teacher training and grassland ecology.

Getting Around

Getting around Xilin Gol is by road and rail. The Jining–Tongliao line reaches Xilinhot, with the Xi-Sang, Xi-Ulanqab and Xi-Erenhot lines branching from it, and the Beijing–Tianjin–Baotou expressway runs along the southern edge through the Ulanqab side. Xilinhot airport connects the league with Hohhot and Beijing, and Erenhot has its own airport and the Ceke port on the Mongolian border.

Natural Resources & Environment

The league sits on the central Inner Mongolian Plateau, 203,000 km² of it, with the Daxing'anling foothills in the east, the Ulagai basin in the middle and the Ulanqab plateau in the west. Altitudes run from 800 to 1,800 m, the highest point being Gurugsula Mountain at 1,957 m, and the Hunshandake sandy land — the Little Tengger — crosses the middle of the league for 280 km. The climate is northern temperate continental: wind, dryness and hard cold, with a mean annual temperature of 0 to 3 °C, a January average of -20 °C and about 295 mm of rain.

Food & Shopping in Xilin Gol

What to eat and what to bring home from Xilin Gol

🍽️ Must-Try Xilin Gol Cuisine

Xilin Gol is the food of the Mongolian herders: hand-grabbed lamb (手把肉), whole roasted lamb (烤全羊), salted milk tea, fried milk cakes and the fat-tailed Ujimqin sheep's cheese and dried milk. The mutton is the whole point — it is what the grasslands are for.

🛍️ Shopping in Xilin Gol

The bazaars of Xilinhot and Erenhot sell dried milk, cheese and beef jerky, Mongolian knives, saddles and silver jewellery, and heavy felt and deerskin goods. The horse-culture workshops around Sunit Left Banner are worth a visit.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Salted milk tea (奶茶) is drunk with every meal in the camps, poured from a pot that has been simmering on the stove since morning, and it is refilled constantly. It is sweet, milky and salty at once — do not ask for it without the salt.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

A private car and driver lets you start at the morning market, cross the city for lunch, and finish at an evening teahouse without worrying about parking or the last bus back to your hotel.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Xilin Gol

The living traditions of Xilin Gol — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The league is the homeland of the Mongolian herders, and its heritage is horses and grassland: the Ujimqin horse, the Mongol wrestling and horse-racing festivals of the summer, the long song and the morin khuur, and the white felt tent (yurt) tradition of the camps. Shangdu is the great historical site — the Yuan summer capital, an entire city of palaces and workshops now on the UNESCO tentative list — and the Ceke port has its own small but lively border culture.

Best Time to Visit Xilin Gol

Late June to early September for the green grassland and the festivals; September for the autumn colour; January to February for the ice festival.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — dry and windy with hard frost still on the ground; the grasslands are beige and the roads are easy. (daytime 2.3–20.8°C, nights around -11.6°C; about 12 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the main season, with the grass at its longest, the horse races and wrestling festivals on, and long evenings over the campfire. (daytime 25.9–27.9°C, nights around 12.3°C; about 60 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — the grass turns gold, the sky is enormous and clear, and the livestock are in for the winter. (daytime 0–20.9°C, nights around -12.4°C; about 13 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–February — severely cold, with -20 °C or lower in January, but the grasslands are covered in snow and the ice-and-lantern festival draws visitors to the city. (daytime -12.1–-6.7°C, nights around -23.9°C; about 2 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Xilin Gol

What to drink in Xilin Gol, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Xilin Gol

Salted milk tea (奶茶) is drunk with every meal in the camps, poured from a pot that has been simmering on the stove since morning, and it is refilled constantly. It is sweet, milky and salty at once — do not ask for it without the salt.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Xilin Gol

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Xilin Gol

🍵 Tea Houses of Xilin Gol

Tea culture runs through the day in Xilin Gol: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
