Tiananmen Square is the one place in Beijing where the country's political geography is laid out in the open air. The gate itself was raised in 1417 under the Ming, the square was laid out in 1651 and then enlarged fourfold in the 1950s, and the ground it now covers measures 880 metres north to south by 500 metres east to west, about 440,000 square metres. Coming straight from the airport puts you at the north end near the flagpole, which is where the daily flag-raising ceremony happens.

Beijing Capital International Airport (PEK)

30 km from the Beijing terminal, about 45 minutes by car.

Capital sits about 32 km northeast of the city centre in Shunyi District, with Terminal 3 handling most international traffic. The road run joins the Airport Expressway, crosses into the city on the Fourth Ring Road and drops you on the north side of the square near the flagpole.

Beijing Daxing International Airport (PKX)

45 km from the Beijing terminal, about 60 minutes by car.

Daxing opened in September 2019 on 47 square kilometres of land in the south of the city, its single terminal building the largest of its type anywhere. The drive north up the Daxing Airport Expressway takes roughly an hour in clear traffic and longer at rush hour, finishing on the west side of Tiananmen Square.

Why transfer to Tiananmen Square?

Everything that defines the square is within a few hundred metres of each other. On the west side stands the Great Hall of the People, on the east side the National Museum of China, and at the southern end the 38-metre Monument to the People's Heroes, with the Chairman Mao Memorial Hall just behind it. The Tiananmen gate itself, 34.7 metres tall and the tallest in the Forbidden City, sits on the northern edge above the flagpole where the raising ceremony is held daily at sunrise and lowered at sunset.

The square is also the natural starting point for a longer walk rather than a single stop. Cross Chang'an Avenue to the north and you are at the Forbidden City and the Meridian Gate; head south across the square and the Qianmen arrow tower and Zhengyangmen gate lead into the old commercial quarter beyond. Arriving by car means you can be dropped at the gate at 07:00, walk the full axis before the daytime crowds, and still have the afternoon free for the museums along the western and eastern sides.

  • The square measures 880 m by 500 m, roughly 440,000 square metres, and was enlarged fourfold between November 1958 and August 1959
  • The Tiananmen gate was first raised in 1417 under the Ming dynasty; the present square dates from 1651
  • The Monument to the People's Heroes stands 38 metres high on the southern edge of the square
  • Capital Airport is about 32 km from the square, and Daxing about 45 km south of it

What to do at Tiananmen Square once you arrive

Start at the northern flagpole end. The flag-raising ceremony takes place at sunrise every day and the crowds are heaviest in the first half hour, so arrive early if you want to stand near the rope and get a clear view down the axis. From there the axis runs straight south across the square past the Monument to the People's Heroes, and the 38-metre stone relief on the monument's south face is worth looking at from the kerb rather than from the ticket line. The north-south walk is open ground with almost no shade, so in summer this is another reason to come early.

The National Museum of China on the east side is free to enter with a passport or ID reservation and is closed on Mondays, and it holds the famous bronze chariot and the Four Great Bronzes. The Great Hall of the People on the west side runs guided tours for a small fee that includes the large auditorium and the banquet hall. Both buildings take well over an hour each, so pick one rather than trying to squeeze both into the same afternoon.

  • Line 1 of the Beijing Subway serves Tiananmen West and Tiananmen East stations, and Line 2 has Tiananmen West
  • Security screening at the square's entrances is separate from the museum queues and takes time in the morning
  • The square is crossed on foot end to end in about ten minutes, so wear shoes you are happy to walk in
  • The Forbidden City requires a timed advance reservation and is closed on Mondays, which is a good reason not to plan it on the same day as the National Museum

Arriving at Beijing airport for Tiananmen Square

Capital International has three terminals and handles the large share of Beijing's international traffic, with Terminal 3 the largest passenger terminal building by floor area at the time it opened for the 2008 Olympics. The airport itself opened on 1 March 1958, and Terminal 2 was added in November 1999 while Terminal 1 was closed for rebuilding, which tells you how much the traffic has grown over the years. The run into the centre crosses the ring roads, so allow 45 minutes with clear traffic and 70 minutes or more during the morning peak or when it rains.

Meet your driver inside the arrivals hall rather than at the kerbside, and allow fifteen minutes for checked bags and immigration before you walk out. Your driver holds a printed name board and tracks the inbound flight on both PEK and PKX, which means a delay does not turn into a phone call. From Daxing the terminal is a single large building that only opened in 2019, and the walk from the gate to the pickup area is a long one, so build in another ten minutes on top of the usual allowance.

What the transfer costs

A private car from Capital Airport to Tiananmen Square is typically priced at 150 to 210 yuan for a sedan, and from Daxing 190 to 260 yuan, with the quote built on distance, vehicle class, airport tolls and waiting time. A sedan suits one or two travellers with hold luggage. For a family with suitcases, the seven-seat van tier runs about 210 to 300 yuan from Capital and 250 to 340 yuan from Daxing.

Longer hires are usually billed as a fixed day rate rather than through a per-kilometre meter, and the waiting time at the gate while you are on the square is normally included for the first two hours. Vehicle classes range from a comfortable sedan through to a nine-seat business van for a larger group, and the price difference between them is normally the distance element plus a modest class premium rather than a large multiple.

Ask for the north side of the square near the flagpole as the drop-off point, and give your driver the gate name for the pickup rather than a car park number. If you plan to walk on to the Forbidden City afterwards, say so at booking so the driver can wait at the Meridian Gate instead of circling the square.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is it from Beijing Capital Airport to Tiananmen Square?

Capital International Airport lies about 32 km northeast of central Beijing, and Tiananmen Square is at the centre of the city, so the road distance is roughly 30 km. Expect 45 minutes with clear traffic, closer to an hour and a half during the morning rush hour or on a wet day on the Airport Expressway.

Is the Daxing airport transfer to Tiananmen Square longer than from Capital?

It is. Daxing International Airport opened in September 2019 in the far south of Beijing and sits about 45 km from the square, roughly 15 km further than Capital. The drive usually takes 60 minutes, and because the airport is newer with simpler road links, the variance between a clear run and a congested one is smaller than it is from Capital.

How do I watch the flag-raising ceremony at Tiananmen?

The ceremony happens at the flagpole on the northern end of the square at sunrise every day, with the flag lowered at sunset. Crowds build from well before sunrise in summer, so a car transfer that puts you at the north side of the square by 07:00 gives you a workable position. The square itself is open to the public without a ticket.

Do I need a ticket for Tiananmen Square and how long does the visit take?

The square is free and open, and walking the full 880-metre axis takes about ten minutes. The ticket applies to the buildings around it: the National Museum of China is free with an advance reservation and closed on Mondays, and the Great Hall of the People charges a small fee for its guided tours. Two hours is enough for the square itself, and a half day for the square plus one museum.

Can I combine Tiananmen Square with the Forbidden City in one day?

Yes, they are about a kilometre apart on either side of Chang'an Avenue, and the Forbidden City ticket names a specific entry time with a 20-minute window. The practical order is Forbidden City first in the morning, then out through the Meridian Gate and north across the square, because the palace closes to general entry in the afternoon while the square stays open until the flag comes down.
