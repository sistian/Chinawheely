Popular Day Trips

Overview: Day Trips from Suzhou

Suzhou's classical gardens and canals crowd the old city, so the best excursions are the ones that leave it. Within an hour you reach the canal towns of Tongli and Zhouzhuang, the hunting grounds of Tiger Hill, and the fishing villages of the Taihu shoreline. A longer run takes you across the provincial border to Wuzhen, whose lantern-lit lanes made it the permanent host of the World Internet Conference. Every route here works as a single-day loop from a Suzhou hotel, with a driver handling the highway sections while you walk.

Tiger Hill and the Sword Pond (虎丘)

Tiger Hill, 5 km northwest of the Suzhou old city, is the hill where King Helu of Wu was buried around 496 BC, with the classical record of 100,000 conscripts and elephants hauling soil to raise the mound. The attraction is now the 47.7-metre Yunyan Temple Pagoda, begun in 959 and finished in 961, an octagonal brick tower of seven storeys that leans more than 2 metres off vertical and is locally called China's first leaning pagoda. At its foot lies the Sword Pond, where some 3,000 blades of King Helu's treasure are said to be buried, alongside the two-piece Sword-Testing Rock. The hill itself rises just over 30 metres and covers roughly 20 hectares, and its site was a temple from 327 CE.

How the day runs: Set out from your Suzhou hotel at 08:00 and be at the south gate by 08:40, before the tour groups arrive. Enter on foot and follow the path past the Thousand-Person Stone to the Sword Pond, then continue up the stone steps to the pagoda platform, where the lean is most obvious from the pool below. The Huqiu Sword Pool and the Yunyan Temple ruins sit at the summit. Spend about two hours, then drop back down and drive to Shantang Street, a 3,829.6-metre canal street linking Changmen to the foot of the hill, for lunch and an hour of walking before returning to the city around 14:30.

6 km from Suzhou · about 20 min by car from Suzhou · half-day trip · best in March to May for blossom around the hillside path; any clear winter morning for the leaning pagoda.

  • The pagoda interior staircase is narrow and often closed; view the lean from the viewing platform at the base instead.
  • Enter through the south gate early, because the main path funnels into a single bottleneck after 10:00.
  • Combining the hill with Shantang Street saves a separate vehicle transfer, since the street runs directly to the hill gate.

Tongli Water Town (同里)

Tongli in Wujiang District sits half an hour from central Suzhou and is served directly by Metro Line 4, which keeps it the least traffic-choked of the region's canal towns. The town is threaded by a canal system and carries 55 bridges, three of which -- Taiping, Jili and Changqing -- form the emblem everyone photographs, with the local tradition of walking all three for a marriage, a birth or a birthday. The standout building is the Retreat and Reflection Garden, designed by the artist Yuanlong for Ren Lansheng and built between 1885 and 1887; in 2001 it was inscribed with the other classical Suzhou gardens as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Luoxing Zhou, an island in the lake, carries Buddhist, Taoist and Confucian shrines on the same small footprint.

How the day runs: Depart Suzhou by car at 09:00 and arrive at Tongli's entrance area near 09:40. Park outside the old town and walk in through the archway, taking the canal path straight to the Three Bridges, which are quietest before 10:30. Follow the canal north past the Pearl Tower and the Jiayin Hall, the former home of the early-20th-century actor Liu Yazi, then cross to Luoxing Zhou by boat. Lunch on whitebait and braised pork in one of the riverside restaurants, then take in the Retreat and Reflection Garden, which needs about 90 minutes. Leave by 15:30 for the 40-minute drive back to Suzhou.

25 km from Suzhou · about 40 min by car from Suzhou · full-day trip · best in May and June for lotus on the canal ponds; September and October for the clearest light on the whitewashed walls.

  • Take Metro Line 4 if you want to avoid parking queues; the line terminates at Tongli and takes roughly 35 minutes from central Suzhou.
  • Walk the canal loop anticlockwise to avoid the queue of sightseeing boats at the main bridge.
  • Visit the Retreat and Reflection Garden after 13:30, when the group visitors have moved on to the water towns further east.

Zhouzhuang and the Twin Bridges (周庄)

Zhouzhuang, 30 km southeast of Suzhou inside the administrative area of Kunshan, is rated an AAAAA scenic area and holds a 900-year-old water town fabric: roughly 60 original brick archways and about 100 original courtyards, according to a 2017 Smithsonian account. The symbol is the Double Bridges -- Shide Bridge running east-west with a round arch and Yongan Bridge running north-south with a square arch -- built in the Wanli era between 1573 and 1619 and meeting in the middle like the teeth of a Chinese key. Painter Chen Yifei put the pair on canvas in 1984 and 38 of his canvases were shown at a New York gallery of Occidental Petroleum's chairman Armand Hammer. Fuan Bridge at the east end of the main street dates to 1355.

How the day runs: Leave Suzhou at 08:30 and reach the Zhouzhuang car park at 09:15, before the coach groups. Walk straight to the Double Bridges and shoot them from the banks where the two waterways meet, then cross over and work north through Shenxian Street. Allow an hour for the Shenting House, built in 1742, and the museum cluster on the main street, then take a boat along the outer canals to see the town from the water. Lunch is straightforward here, with river fish and seasonal vegetables along the main street. Turn back around 15:00 for the 45-minute run to Suzhou and arrive before the evening rush.

30 km from Suzhou · about 45 min by car from Suzhou · full-day trip · best in October and November for clear reflection shots off the double bridges; spring mornings for peach blossom along Shenxian Street.

  • The Double Bridges are best framed before 09:30 or after 16:00, when the tour coaches have left the car park.
  • A half-hour boat on the outer canal gives a different angle than the inner lanes and avoids the crowd entirely.
  • Most of the town's buildings are privately owned, so keep to the public lanes and do not enter courtyards without permission.

Wuzhen Across the Provincial Border (乌镇)

Wuzhen lies in Tongxiang, northern Zhejiang, inside the triangle formed by Hangzhou, Suzhou and Shanghai, and was first built in the 7th century during the Tang dynasty. It covers 71.19 square kilometres and has a population of about 60,000, of whom only 12,000 are permanent residents. The old town divides into Xizha in the west and Dongzha in the east, and the layout follows an east-west-east circuit through six districts: traditional workshops, local-style dwelling houses, traditional culture, food and beverage, shops and stores, and water-town customs and life. Since 2014 Wuzhen has been the permanent host of the annual World Internet Conference. The Mao Dun former residence occupied 650 square metres before a 1984 renovation expanded it to 1,731.5 square metres, and it was listed as a Key State Preserved Relic Unit in 1988.

How the day runs: This is a full-day run: leave Suzhou at 07:30 and take the expressway toward Hangzhou, arriving in Wuzhen around 09:00. Buy a combined ticket covering Xizha and Dongzha and start in Xizha, walking the restored workshops before the daytime crowds arrive from the Shanghai direction. Take a shuttle barge across the river to Dongzha for the Mao Dun former residence, opened to the public after the 1984 renovation and renamed the Mao Dun Museum of the City of Tongxiang in 1994, and the Mu Xin Art Museum. Have lunch in the food and beverage district, then browse the shops and stores zone. Leave by 16:00 for the 90-minute return drive to Suzhou.

80 km from Suzhou · about 90 min by car from Suzhou · full-day trip · best in Late March for the plum and wisteria; the annual World Internet Conference week in autumn for lantern displays.

  • Two tickets are sold separately for Xizha and Dongzha; the shuttle boat between them takes roughly ten minutes.
  • The town is large and the sights are spread out, so allow more walking time than a typical water-town visit.
  • Note the difference between day tickets and the after-dark ticket, which includes the evening lantern lighting on the canal.

Mudu Historic Town and the Grand Canal Turn (木渎)

Mudu, on the western edge of Suzhou, lies 12 kilometres from the city centre and only a few kilometres from Lake Tai, and its area of 70.3 square kilometres holds about 80,000 residents across eight subdistricts and ten villages. The town's name is recorded as coming from the Spring and Autumn period, when King Fuchai of Wu used timber sent as tribute by Yue to build the Gusu Terrace and three years of blocked logs caused the channels to silt up. Mudu was established as a town during the Northern Song. Its importance in Chinese visual culture is exceptional: in the Qianlong era the painter Xu Yang's scroll of the prosperous Suzhou, known as the Gusu fanhua tu, devoted half of the entire painting to Mudu, alongside one village, one city and one street.

How the day runs: Drive out from central Suzhou at 09:00 and be in Mudu by 09:25, parking near the Hongyin Shan footpath. Take the hillside walk up through the Ming-era Fan Zhongyan temple and the hillside tomb line, which is the quietest part of the town and a short climb of about 300 steps. Come back down to the Yan Family Garden and the old streets, then eat lunch of local Suzhou-style dishes on the main street. Continue to Lingyan Hill and the Plum Blossom Valley if you have the afternoon, otherwise return toward Suzhou at 13:30. The drive back takes under half an hour, so this pairs easily with a museum visit later in the day.

14 km from Suzhou · about 30 min by car from Suzhou · half-day trip · best in April for the azaleas on the hillside behind town; late autumn for the maple slope above Hongyin Shan.

  • The Fan Zhongyan temple's stele inscriptions are the main reason to climb; the streets below are commercial.
  • Mudu works best as a morning trip, because the hillside paths attract the least traffic before noon.
  • Because it is only 12 km from the city centre, you can return to your hotel to swap vehicles before a second outing.

Dongting Xishan Island on Lake Tai (洞庭西山)

Dongting Xishan, formerly called Baoshan, sits inside Lake Tai in the Jinting area of Suzhou's Wuzhong District and covers 79.8 square kilometres, making it the largest lake island in China. It is linked to the mainland by the Taihu Bridge, described as the longest bridge on an inland lake in China, and its highest point is Piaomiao Peak at 336.6 metres. On the island's Zhonggongshan lies Linwu Cave, listed by Daoist tradition as the ninth of the Ten Great Caves under heaven. The island's villages grow tea on the slopes and fish the lake below, and because the causeway is a road rather than a bridge of a single span, the drive across itself takes around 20 minutes with stops at the lookout bays.

How the day runs: Leave Suzhou at 08:00 and reach the Taihu Bridge toll plaza at 08:50, crossing the causeway with two or three stops at the viewing platforms where the lake opens up on both sides. On the island, drive to the Linwu Cave area near Zhonggongshan in the morning, walk the cave mouth path and the stone steps up toward Piaomiao Peak if you have the legs for the summit. Lunch on lake fish in a village restaurant near the western shore. Spend the afternoon driving the back road along the cliff edge to Pukou harbour, where the fishing boats come in, then leave the island by 16:30 for the hour back to Suzhou.

40 km from Suzhou · about 60 min by car from Suzhou · full-day trip · best in May to June for the lake mist over the causeway; October and November for clear views to the far shore.

  • The island takes far longer to cross than the distance suggests, because the coastal road follows the shoreline rather than cutting through.
  • Book a lakeside restaurant ahead on weekends; the village eateries fill up with Suzhou weekenders by noon.
  • Bring a wind layer even in summer, as lake conditions change the temperature noticeably through the afternoon.

Planning a Suzhou day trip

Traffic decides the plan. Suzhou's expressway ring is fastest between 09:30 and 16:00 and crawls at rush hour, so for the water towns a mid-morning start beats an early one, while the hill and garden trips work best early. Chinese long-distance bus services also run from the Suzhou Railway Station to Tongli, Zhouzhuang and Wuzhen for a fraction of a chartered car, but the departure points move and the frequencies are thin.

Plan the return rather than the departure. Zhouzhuang and Tongli are both within 45 minutes of the city, so a driver can drop one group and loop back for a second outing the same day. Wuzhen and Xishan are all-day commitments of 80 to 90 minutes each way, and both work better with a single overnight rather than a same-day return if your schedule allows. Bring cash for the smaller towns, where some family-run boats and museums still do not take cards.

Planning a day trip from Suzhou

Tiger Hill and the Sword Pond, Wuzhen Across the Provincial Border and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Suzhou day trip route — Tiger Hill and the Sword Pond sits closest at 6 km, roughly 20 minutes by car, while Wuzhen Across the Provincial Border reaches furthest at 80 km, about 90 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Tiger Hill and the Sword Pond works best March to May for blossom around the hillside path; any clear winter morning for the leaning pagoda., while Tongli Water Town suits May and June for lotus on the canal ponds; September and October for the clearest light on the whitewashed walls. — in Suzhou that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

Zhouzhuang and the Twin Bridges, Wuzhen Across the Provincial Border, Mudu Historic Town and the Grand Canal Turn each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Suzhou itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which water town near Suzhou is the most worth visiting?

For a first visit, Zhouzhuang is the strongest single choice. It holds the Double Bridges, roughly 60 original brick archways and about 100 original courtyards, and it is only 30 km from the city, so you get the classic water-town scenery without a long transfer. Tongli is the better option if you want gardens, because the Retreat and Reflection Garden there is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it is directly served by Metro Line 4. Wuzhen is the largest and most polished but sits across the Zhejiang border, about 80 kilometres out.

Can I visit the Humble Administrator's Garden and the Lingering Garden in one day?

Yes, and it is a common pairing because both sit in the Gusu District, roughly 1 km apart. The Humble Administrator's Garden is the larger at 78 mu, or 5.2 hectares, and took 16 years to build between 1513 and 1526; allow two hours. The Lingering Garden, begun in 1593, is organised into four themed sections around a pond and yellow-stone grotto; allow 90 minutes. Enter early, because both fill with tour groups from around 10:00.

What is the best season for a Suzhou day trip?

Spring from March to May and autumn from October to November are the most comfortable. March and April bring plum blossom and wisteria in the gardens, and the lake area around Taihu is misty in May and June. Autumn clears the haze and gives the crisp light that suits the whitewashed canal houses and the granite of Tiger Hill. Winter is quiet and dry but cold, and some waterfront sections close early. Summer brings heavy rain, humidity and the strongest crowds.

Is Tiger Hill worth a visit if I have only half a day?

Yes, it is the most compact of the major Suzhou sights. The hill rises just over 30 metres and covers about 20 hectares, so the whole climb takes about 90 minutes including the Sword Pond, the Sword-Testing Rock and the 47.7-metre Yunyan Temple Pagoda that leans more than 2 metres off vertical. Because the approach road doubles as Shantang Street, a 3,829.6-metre canal street with old stone bridges, you can add a walk along the water without adding travel time.

Do I need a private car for these trips, or are buses enough?

Buses cover Tongli, Zhouzhuang and Wuzhen from the Suzhou Railway Station area and are inexpensive, but they are slower than a car and the timetables are thin at weekends. A private vehicle is worth it for Xishan Island and Mudu, where the sights are spread across a large area with no practical bus service. On a tight schedule, a single chartered car for the day usually costs less than several separate transfers and keeps the group together.

Are the Suzhou gardens crowded enough to skip?

They are busy, but the crowds concentrate on a small window. The Humble Administrator's Garden and the Lingering Garden both admit large numbers of tour groups, and the way around it is to arrive before 09:00 or after 15:00. Pingjiang Road, 1,606 metres long with 51 intersecting alleys and about 3 km of canals inside the historic district, is a better place to see daily Suzhou life than the garden gates, and it is at its calmest before breakfast.

Day Trip Tips

Two things make a day trip out of Suzhou work: agree the pick-up time and the return time before you leave, and keep the last stop within an hour of the city so the drive back stays easy. A private car with a waiting driver handles both — no schedule to keep and no luggage to move between vehicles.
