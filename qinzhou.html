Qinzhou at a Glance

Qinzhou (钦州) — named after the ancient Qinzhou state and the title of the legendary Empress Qinzhou — is a historic coastal city on the Gulf of Tonkin (Beibu Gulf, 北部湾) in southern Guangxi Province. The city faces Vietnam across the sea and has been a maritime trading hub since the Han Dynasty, when it was already connected to the ancient Maritime Silk Road.

The Empress Qinzhou Heritage

Qinzhou is named after Empress Qinzhou (钦州皇后), also known as the Pearl Concubine (珠妃), a legendary figure in local history. According to legend, she was a beautiful woman from the Qinzhou area who became an imperial consort and brought fame to her hometown. The city's name — literally "Revered Prefecture" — and its cultural identity are deeply connected to this legendary figure. The Empress Qinzhou Temple (钦州天后宫) near the old harbour is the main memorial to her.

Tanka People — The Boat Dwellers of Guangxi

Qinzhou is the historic center of the Tanka people (蛋家人/Danjia) — an ethnic group who have lived on boats and stilted houseboats in the coastal waters of southern China for over a thousand years. The Tanka (also called the Dan) are recognized as a distinct ethnic community with their own language, customs, and boat-building traditions. The Qinzhou Tanka communities — particularly around Weizhou Island and along the Maicheng River — offer a window into one of China's most distinctive maritime cultures.

Geography & Coastal Location

Qinzhou is located on the southeastern coast of Guangxi, facing the Beibu Gulf (北部湾) — the gulf that China shares with Vietnam. The city has a deep-water harbor that has been used for maritime trade since the Han Dynasty (2nd century BCE). The city is flanked by the Yunkai Mountains (云开大山) to the north and the sea to the south. The coastline is dotted with tropical islands, mangrove forests, and tidal flats. Nanning (the Guangxi capital) is about 100km to the northwest.

Qinzhou Porcelain — Ming Dynasty Export Ware

Qinzhou was an important center of porcelain production during the Ming and Qing dynasties — producing ceramics specifically for the Southeast Asian and Marine Silk Road export markets. The Qinzhou kiln sites produced distinctive blue-and-white porcelain with designs reflecting both Chinese tradition and Southeast Asian tastes. The Qinzhou Porcelain Museum traces this history and displays artifacts from the maritime trade.

Top Qinzhou Attractions

From Tanka boat villages to tropical islands, explore southern Guangxi's coastal culture with a private driver.

🏝️

🏝️ Weizhou Island (涠洲岛)

China's largest volcanic island · Tropical beach

Weizhou Island — the largest volcanic island in China — is about 36 nautical miles off the Qinzhou coast. Formed by ancient volcanic eruptions, the island has black volcanic rock beaches, coral reefs, a Gothic Catholic church (built by French missionaries in the 19th century), and tropical vegetation. It is one of China's most appealing island destinations, still relatively unspoiled compared to Hainan.

🚣

⛰️ Tanka Boat Village — Maicheng River

Living Tanka culture · Stilted houseboats

The Tanka boat people have lived on the Maicheng River in Qinzhou for generations. The stilted houseboats, colorful fishing vessels, and the daily life of the Tanka community — fish farming, net mending, boat building — offer a fascinating window into a traditional maritime culture. A private boat tour through the Tanka village is one of the most memorable experiences in Qinzhou.

🏺

🏝️ Qinzhou Porcelain Museum

Ming Dynasty export ware · Marine Silk Road

The Qinzhou Porcelain Museum displays the history of porcelain production in Qinzhou from the Tang Dynasty through to the present day, with particular focus on the Ming and Qing export ware produced for the Marine Silk Road trade. The museum has an outstanding collection of blue-and-white porcelain, including pieces with东南亚 motifs produced specifically for export to Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines.

🛕

🛕 Empress Qinzhou Temple (钦州天后宫)

Historic temple · Goddess of the Sea

The Empress Qinzhou Temple (also known as the Tianhou Temple / Mazu Temple) near the old harbour is the spiritual center of Qinzhou's maritime culture. Dedicated to the goddess of the sea (Mazu), the temple dates to the Song Dynasty and has been rebuilt many times. The temple complex includes the main hall, opera stage, and stone carvings documenting the history of Qinzhou's harbor. Popular with local fishermen before voyages.

🌿

🏛️ Daxu Ancient Town (大芦)

Lingnan architecture · Ancient mercantile families

Daxu Ancient Town, about 30km north of Qinzhou, is one of the best-preserved historic towns in Guangxi — a trading port that was at its peak during the Ming and Qing dynasties. The town is famous for its Linngnan (southern Chinese) architectural style — arcade streets, carved wooden brackets, and ancient halls built by wealthy merchant families. The three largest ancestral halls have particularly impressive woodcarving and calligraphy plaques.

🌊

🕳️ Gauledong Mangrove Nature Reserve

Mangrove forest · Ecosystem · Boat tours

The Gauledong (高德港) Mangrove Reserve near the Qinzhou coast protects one of the most extensive mangrove ecosystems on the Beibu Gulf coast. The twisted mangrove roots create an otherworldly landscape, and boat tours through the narrow channels offer close views of the mudflats, fiddler crabs, and birds. A peaceful and ecologically significant destination.

Qinzhou's Best Photo Spots

Capture southern Guangxi's coastal culture from its most evocative angles.

🏝️ Weizhou Island — Black Rock Beach at Sunset

Best at sunset

The volcanic rock beaches of Weizhou Island — with their distinctive black volcanic boulders and warm-toned sand — are particularly dramatic in the evening light. The contrast between the dark rocks, the turquoise water, and the golden sunset makes for striking photography. The rocks at the island's eastern tip are the most dramatic.

⛰️ 🚣 Tanka Boat Village — Morning Light

Best at dawn

The Tanka boat village on the Maicheng River is most atmospheric in the early morning when mist rises from the water, fishing nets are spread out to dry, and the daily routines of the boat people begin. The low morning light on the colorful boat hulls and stilted houses makes for beautiful documentary photography.

🍵 🌿 Mangrove Channels — Aerial View

Best with a drone

The Gauledong Mangrove Reserve is best photographed from above — the complex network of tidal channels, twisted mangrove roots, and small boats threading through the green maze creates an extraordinary abstract pattern when viewed from a drone. If you have a drone, this is one of Qinzhou's most rewarding subjects. Permission may be required.

🛕 Empress Qinzhou Temple — Incense & Ritual

Best during festivals

The Empress Qinzhou Temple is most alive during the Mazu festival (around the spring equinox, March 20–22) when fishermen from across the Beibu Gulf come to pay respects to the goddess of the sea. The temple is filled with incense, opera performances are staged in the courtyard, and the harbor is filled with decorated boats. Photography is welcomed outside of ritual moments.

🏛️ 🏺 Porcelain Museum — Blue & White under Light

Best with a guide

The Qinzhou Porcelain Museum's collection of Ming and Qing export ware is most compelling when viewed with a guide who can explain the distinctive designs — pieces made specifically for Vietnamese, Thai, and Filipino markets, with motifs that blend Chinese and Southeast Asian elements. Photography is permitted throughout the museum.

🏛️ Daxu Ancient Town — Lingnan Architecture

Best in overcast weather

The carved wooden brackets, arcade streets, and ancient halls of Daxu Ancient Town are most beautifully photographed on an overcast day when the soft, even light eliminates harsh shadows and shows the full detail of the woodcarving and stone carving. The ancestral halls are most impressive — particularly the Ziyun Hall and Dezheng Hall.

Food & Shopping in Qinzhou

🍜 Must-Try Qinzhou & Southern Guangxi Cuisine

Qinzhou Fresh Crab Noodles (钦州蟹粉面)

The signature Qinzhou dish — hand-pulled noodles in a rich, sweet crab roe broth, topped with fresh crab meat, coriander, and chili oil. The crab roe gives the broth a rich orange color and an intense umami flavor. Best at the morning wet market noodle stalls near the old harbour, where the crab is extremely fresh.

Tanka Fish Hotpot (蛋家鱼火锅)

The Tanka people are master fish farmers — their stilted fish pens in the Maicheng River produce exceptional freshness. The Tanka fish hotpot — with ultra-fresh fish slices, tofu, seasonal vegetables, and a mild herbal broth — showcases the boat people's intimate knowledge of the sea. Best eaten at a riverside restaurant overlooking the Tanka village.

Fresh Seafood at Qinzhou Port

The Qinzhou fishing port is one of the busiest on the Beibu Gulf. The seafood restaurants along the harbour waterfront serve the catch of the day — mantis shrimp, sea bass, grouper, cuttlefish, and the local "Qinzhou oyster omelette." Buy the seafood at the morning market and have it cooked at one of the harbour restaurants for the freshest and cheapest meal in Qinzhou.

Guangxi Sour Soup Fish (酸汤鱼)

The famous Guangxi sour soup — a fermented tomato and chili broth — cooked with ultra-fresh fish from the Beibu Gulf. The sour and spicy broth is extraordinarily refreshing in the tropical heat. Found at restaurants throughout Qinzhou. The dish originated in the Guizhou province but is done exceptionally well in Guangxi's coastal cities.

Lychee & Longan — Qinzhou Orchards

Qinzhou is one of China's most important lychee and longan growing areas. The orchards north of the city supply markets across China. In season (May–July for lychee, July–September for longan), roadside stalls sell freshly picked fruit at very low prices. The "Qinzhou Premium Lychee" (钦州荔枝) variety is considered among the best in China.

🛍️ Shopping Destinations

Qinzhou Old Harbour & Waterfront

The old harbour waterfront near the Empress Qinzhou Temple is the heart of the city — a working harbor where fishing boats unload the catch, seafood vendors sell the morning's catch, and local restaurants serve the freshest seafood. The best place to experience the living maritime culture of Qinzhou.

Qinzhou Fruit Markets — Lychee & Longan

The fruit markets north of the city are the best place to buy Qinzhou's famous lychee and longan (in season). Prices are significantly lower than in tourist cities. The "Qinzhou No. 1" lychee variety is particularly prized — ask your driver to take you to the right stall.

Qinzhou Night Market Food Stalls

The night market near the city center is the best place to experience Qinzhou's street food culture — crab noodles, oyster omelette, grilled squid, and fresh fruit shakes served from dozens of stalls. Open from around 6 PM until midnight. Loud, colorful, and delicious.

🎁 What to Buy in Qinzhou

Qinzhou Porcelain (钦州陶瓷)

Ming and Qing dynasty Qinzhou porcelain — blue-and-white export ware with Southeast Asian motifs — is a valued collector's category. Modern Qinzhou porcelain continues the tradition with pieces that blend traditional Chinese designs with tropical marine motifs. Available at antique shops near the old harbour and at the Qinzhou Porcelain Museum gift shop.

Dried Seafood from the Beibu Gulf

Dried shrimp, dried squid, dried fish, and dried scallops from the Beibu Gulf are sold at the Qinzhou fishing port markets. These are important ingredients in southern Chinese and Vietnamese cooking. Lightweight and travel-friendly, they make excellent souvenirs. Ask your driver to take you to the wholesale market for the best prices.

Fresh Lychee & Longan (Seasonal)

Fresh lychee (May–July) and longan (July–September) from Qinzhou's orchards — one of China's premier fruit growing areas. These are best consumed fresh during your visit or shipped home. Your driver can take you directly to an orchard for the freshest and best-priced fruit of the season.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts of Qinzhou

Traditional crafts and cultural traditions of southern Guangxi's coastal city.

🚣 Tanka Boat-Building & Fishing Traditions

The Tanka boat-building tradition — constructing the distinctive flat-bottomed, high-bowed fishing boats that have been the Tanka people's home and livelihood for over a thousand years — is one of China's most distinctive maritime crafts. The techniques, tools, and designs are specific to the Tanka and have been passed down through generations. The boat villages on the Maicheng River are the best places to observe this living tradition.

Where to experience: Maicheng River Tanka boat village, Weizhou Island fishing communities.

🏺 Qinzhou Porcelain Making

The tradition of porcelain making in Qinzhou dates to the Tang Dynasty and flourished during the Ming and Qing periods when Qinzhou was an important production center for export ceramics. The distinctive Qinzhou style — blue-and-white with Southeast Asian-influenced motifs — reflects the city's role as a Maritime Silk Road port. The Qinzhou Porcelain Museum and local workshops preserve this tradition.

Where to find: Qinzhou Porcelain Museum, local ceramic workshops.

🎭 Qinzhou Puppet Opera (木偶戏)

Puppet opera — using carved wooden puppets manipulated by operators behind a screen, accompanied by a live traditional music ensemble — is a beloved art form in southern Guangxi. Qinzhou has a distinctive local style of puppet opera, performed in the local Qinzhou dialect with music that blends Cantonese and Guangxi traditions. Performances are staged at the Empress Qinzhou Temple during festivals.

Where to experience: Empress Qinzhou Temple during Mazu Festival (March), cultural events.

🧶 Qinzhou Embroidery & Textile Crafts

Embroidery is a traditional women's craft in the Qinzhou area — with distinctive floral and bird motifs that reflect the tropical coastal environment. The embroidery uses fine silk thread on silk or cotton ground, with designs that incorporate local flowers, fish, and coastal birds. The patterns and techniques differ from the better-known Suzhou or Hunan embroidery traditions.

Where to find: Local craft markets, Qinzhou Cultural Center.

⛵ Mazu Sea Goddess Worship

Mazu — the goddess of the sea, worshipped across coastal China under various names including "Empress Qinzhou" — has been central to the maritime culture of the Beibu Gulf for over a thousand years. The Empress Qinzhou Temple in Qinzhou is one of the oldest Mazu temples in Guangxi. The annual Mazu Festival (around the spring equinox, March 20–22) is the most important event, with temple rituals, opera performances, and boat processions on the harbor.

Where to experience: Empress Qinzhou Temple, especially during Mazu Festival (March).

🍜 Qinzhou Noodle Making

The art of making Qinzhou's signature crab roe noodles — the hand-pulled wheat noodles with their distinctive chewy texture, served in a rich crab roe broth — is a local craft. Some noodle restaurants in Qinzhou still make their noodles by hand daily. The skill of working the dough to the right consistency and pulling it into perfectly uniform strands is taught in the traditional noodle restaurants of the old harbor area.

Where to experience: Old harbour noodle stalls, morning wet market area.

Qinzhou Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Qinzhou like a local.

🚣

Hire a Tanka Boat for the Day

The best way to experience the Tanka boat village is to hire a private Tanka boat for the full day — not just for a short tour. Your driver can arrange this. A private boat with a Tanka captain can take you through the fishing grounds, to secluded islands, and into the mangrove channels far beyond what the standard tourist tour covers. Negotiate directly at the dock for the best price.

🏝️

Stay Overnight on Weizhou Island

A day trip to Weizhou Island is rushed — the ferry crossing takes 70 minutes each way, and there is enough to see to fill two days. Stay one night in one of the island's boutique guesthouses. Wake up to the sound of the sea, explore the island at dawn before the day-trippers arrive, and watch the sunset from the volcanic rock beach.

🦀

Eat Crab Noodles at the Morning Market

The famous Qinzhou crab noodles are best eaten at the morning wet market stalls near the old harbour — where the crab roe is extremely fresh and the noodles are made by hand that morning. The market opens around 6 AM and the crab noodle stalls are busiest between 7 and 9 AM. Follow the locals to find the best stall.

🛕

Visit the Empress Qinzhou Temple at Dawn

The Empress Qinzhou Temple is most atmospheric in the early morning when local worshippers come to burn incense before work — the smell of incense, the low murmurs of prayer, and the harbor views through the temple gates make for a genuinely moving experience. After 9 AM it becomes more touristy. Go early for the authentic experience.

🍈

Pick Fruit Directly from the Orchard

In lychee season (May–July) and longan season (July–September), your driver can take you directly to a Qinzhou orchard where you can pick your own fruit. The orchards north of the city are among the most productive in China. The freshly picked fruit is far superior to what you'll find in markets — and significantly cheaper. Ask your driver to arrange this during your visit.

🌿

Bring a Drone to the Mangrove Reserve

If you have a drone, the Gauledong Mangrove Reserve is one of the most rewarding subjects in southern China — the aerial view of the mangrove channel network is extraordinary. Check local regulations before flying and obtain any required permissions. The reserve management can advise on current rules. The best time to fly is at high tide when the channels are full.

Best Time to Visit Qinzhou

Each season reveals a different face of southern Guangxi's coastal city.

🌸

Spring

Mar–May · 18–28°C

The Mazu Festival (around March 20–22) is the cultural highlight of the year in Qinzhou — the harbor comes alive with temple rituals, opera performances, and boat processions. The weather is warm and pleasant. May brings the start of lychee season. This is one of the best times to visit.

Highlights: Mazu Festival (March), beginning of lychee season (May)

☀️

Summer

Jun–Aug · 26–33°C

The peak season for beach and island activities — Weizhou Island is at its most beautiful, the water is warm enough for swimming, and the marine activities are in full swing. This is also typhoon season — monitor weather forecasts. The lychee harvest (peak June–July) is excellent. Hot and humid but vibrant.

Highlights: Peak lychee season, beach weather, island hopping

🍂

Autumn

Sep–Nov · 20–30°C

The longan fruit season (July–September) overlaps with early autumn. The weather is slightly cooler and less humid than summer. Good for sightseeing, mangrove tours, and visiting the Tanka village. September can still be hot. October and November are pleasant. Hotel prices drop after the summer peak.

Highlights: Longan season (Sep), comfortable weather, fewer tourists

❄️

Winter

Dec–Feb · 10–18°C

Mild and pleasant compared to most of China — the warmest winter destination in southern China after Hainan. Not suitable for swimming or island activities, but excellent for cultural tourism — the Tanka village, porcelain museum, and temple visits are all comfortable in the mild winter. Hotel prices at their lowest.

Highlights: Mild winter weather, cultural tourism, low prices
