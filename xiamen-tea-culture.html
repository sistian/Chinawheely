The tea

Tieguanyin (铁观音)

Tieguanyin is a semi-fermented oolong from Anxi County in southern Fujian, about 60 km from Xiamen, counted among China's ten famous teas. The leaves are rolled into dark, tightly packed pellets, and the same portion can be brewed eight or ten times, with each round sweeter than the last. Modern styles split into light qingxiang and roasted nongxiang types.

How to have it here: In Xiamen it is brewed gongfu-style everywhere: 7-8 grams of leaf in a 100-150 ml pot, a first rinse, then pours of 15-30 seconds. Tea shops on and around Zhongshan Road pour tastings without obligation, and the Anxi tea villages run about 60 km up the expressway for a day trip between the April and October harvests.

Gongfu tea (工夫茶)

Gongfu cha is a method rather than a tea: a small pot, a small cup and short pours repeated many times. It is the standard way tea is served in Fujian and Guangdong, and it is why a good Tieguanyin or pu-erh can run to a dozen rounds. The kit is a clay pot or covered bowl, a strainer, a pitcher and thimble-sized cups.

How to have it here: The full table - pot, strainer, pitcher and three to six cups - is ordinary equipment in Xiamen homes and shops, not a ceremony prop. A session runs from about 20 minutes to an hour; the first pour is rinsed away, and at well-run tables the count of rounds is announced as it climbs.

Gongfu table manners in Xiamen

The host handles the pot and never drinks first: guests' cups are filled in one circling pour - the traditional 'Guangong patrols the city' move keeps every cup equal - before the host takes any. When someone tops you up, tap your index and middle fingers twice on the table. Cups hold 20-30 ml; taking one in a single swallow is expected, not greedy.

The first infusion goes down the drain as a rinse; pours after that run 10-30 seconds, and one portion of leaf serves eight to ten rounds. Asking which round you are on is normal table talk. To leave a session early, finish your cup and set it inverted on the tray - the table's understood signal for enough.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is gongfu tea?

A brewing method, not a tea type: a 100-150 ml pot packed with 7-8 grams of leaf, quick pours of 10-30 seconds, and many rounds - eight to ten from a good Tieguanyin, up to a dozen from dense pu-erh. It is the everyday serving style across Fujian and Guangdong, which is why Xiamen tea tables carry the full small-pot kit.

Is Tieguanyin grown in Xiamen?

No - it comes from Anxi County about 60 km northwest of the city, in the hills of southern Fujian. The county is the documented origin of the cultivar, and its villages welcome visitors during the spring and autumn harvests, roughly April and October. Xiamen is the trading and drinking hub for the leaf rather than the grower.

How long does a gongfu session last?

Between about 20 minutes and an hour, depending on the leaf and the company. Count the first rinse and eight to ten drinkable rounds at 10-30 seconds each, with the pours stretching longer as the leaf gives out. Offices in Xiamen keep the pot refilling through the workday on the same portion.

Can I visit the Tieguanyin growing area from Xiamen?

Yes - Anxi County is roughly 60 km up the expressway, close enough for a half- or full-day trip. Spring picking peaks in April and a second harvest follows around October, so workshops are busiest then. Tea shops around Zhongshan Road in the city itself also pour side-by-side tastings of qingxiang and nongxiang styles.
