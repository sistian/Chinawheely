Name Origin

Aba is the Tibetan name of the prefecture, Ngawa, and the name of the Qiang people who have lived on these slopes for millennia. The area was Qiang and Di territory in ancient times, became part of the Qin and Han empire in the third century BC, and passed through the Tibetan and Qiang kingdoms before the Qing brought the region under direct administration in the eighteenth century.

Education & Universities

Higher education is provided by the Aba campus of Sichuan Minzu University and local vocational colleges, with programmes in teacher training, veterinary science, plateau agriculture, traditional medicine and Qiang and Tibetan cultural studies.

Getting Around

Getting around Aba is by road. The G213 runs the length of the prefecture from Wenchuan through Songpan, Aba town, Ma'erkang and Hongyuan to the Gansu border, the G317 runs south-west towards Lhasa, and the Chengdu–Ma'erkang and Chengdu–Jiuzhaihuang expressways give quick access from the plain. Air and rail both reach the prefecture, but not at the seat: Hongyuan Airport (IATA AHJ) is 128 km from Ma'erkang, Jiuzhai Huanglong Airport (IATA JZH) serves the Jiuzhaigou and Huanglong area, and the railway reaches Songpan on the Qinghai-Tibet line, which opened to Songpan in August 2024.

Natural Resources & Environment

Aba is the transition zone between the eastern Tibetan Plateau and the Hengduan mountains, with average elevations of 3,500–4,000 m. Four Girls Mountain reaches 6,250 m only 59 km from where the Min river leaves the mountains, a drop of 5,470 m. Half the prefecture is plateau and half high gorges, with more than 530 streams draining into the Min, Jialing, Fu, Dadu and Yellow rivers — the Yellow River itself runs 165 km through Aba. The climate is cool and dry with strong sun: a mean annual temperature of 9.3 °C, 705 mm of rain and 1,920 hours of sunshine a year.

Food & Shopping in Aba

What to eat and what to bring home from Aba

🍽️ Must-Try Aba Cuisine

Aba's cooking is Qiang and Tibetan with a Sichuan accent: yak and beef hotpot, butter tea and tsampa in the Tibetan towns, smoked pork and sausage from the Qiang villages, and the wild mushrooms and greens of the high forests.

🛍️ Shopping in Aba

The county towns of Ma'erkang, Songpan and Wenchuan sell Tibetan silver and turquoise, prayer flags and thangka prints, Qiang embroidery and the carved wooden tujia and Qiang furniture, and the dried mushrooms and yak products of the pastures.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

In the Tibetan half of the prefecture, butter tea is churned with yak butter and salt and served in wooden bowls through the whole day; in the Qiang and Han towns, Sichuan-style gaiwan tea is drunk with the meal. Both are worth trying in the same day.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Aba

The living traditions of Aba — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The prefecture is home to two of China's major ethnic cultures. The Qiang villages of the western valleys keep the watchtower villages of Taoping and the Zhuangke chieftain's fortress, along with the sedge dance, the the girl festival and the Qiang new year in the lunar tenth month. The Tibetan side of Aba keeps the monasteries of Tagong, the Kham muqam and the horse festivals, and the tea-horse road posts that linked Sichuan with Tibet. The Jiuzhaigou and Huanglong valleys are also on the UNESCO World Heritage list for their landscapes.

Aba Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Aba like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

Aba is high country: the seat is at 2,600 m and the valleys reach 4,000 m. Take the first day slowly, drink water rather than tea, and avoid alcohol on arrival.

🥢 Skip the tourist menu

The Jiuzhaigou and Huanglong valleys have their own ticketing and shuttle systems, and the autumn colour in late October is worth the queues.

⏱️ Plan the driving time

The <em>one</em> road to the far west of the prefecture — the G213 over the Chuan-Zang border and the tea-horse road post at Ta'ergong — is one of the great drives in western China.

📍 Ask the driver

The Qiang villages are working communities, not museums; ask before photographing people, and always knock before entering a tower house.

Best Time to Visit Aba

June to October, with September and October the best for colour and clear air; the high passes close in winter.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · April–May — the passes reopen, the snow is still deep in the high valleys and the light is very clear. (daytime 16.6–22.2°C, nights around -0.5°C; about 62 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the main season, with green meadows, clear high lakes and the best walking weather in the forests. (daytime 23.6–25.2°C, nights around 9.7°C; about 131 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — the best month of the year, with gold larch in Jiuzhaigou, low cloud and sharp light. (daytime 14.9–22.3°C, nights around -2.7°C; about 64 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–March — very cold, with snow everywhere and some roads closed; the monastery towns are quiet and the food is at its richest. (daytime 10.7–13.7°C, nights around -7.4°C; about 5 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Aba

What to drink in Aba, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Aba

In the Tibetan half of the prefecture, butter tea is churned with yak butter and salt and served in wooden bowls through the whole day; in the Qiang and Han towns, Sichuan-style gaiwan tea is drunk with the meal. Both are worth trying in the same day.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Aba

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Aba

🍵 Tea Houses of Aba

Tea culture runs through the day in Aba: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
