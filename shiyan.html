Shiyan at a Glance

Shiyan (十堰) — "Ten Dams" — is a prefecture-level city in northwestern Hubei Province, on the upper Han River (汉江). Shiyan is best known as the gateway to Wudang Mountain (武当山) — one of China's most sacred Taoist mountains and a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and as China's "Car City (车城)", the home of Dongfeng Motor Corporation (东风汽车), one of China's largest automobile manufacturers. The city is also the site of the Danjiangkou Dam, a key part of China's South-to-North Water Transfer Project.

Wudang Mountain — Sacred Taoist Peak

Shiyan's most important cultural claim is as the gateway to Wudang Mountain (武当山) — one of the most sacred mountains in the Taoist tradition and one of China's most famous "Wushu" (martial arts) centers. Wudang Mountain (approximately 30km from Shiyan city) was the birthplace of Taoist internal martial arts — including Tai Chi (太极拳) and Xing Yi (形意拳) — and the mountain is dotted with ancient Taoist temples, monasteries, and palaces built over a period of more than 1,000 years.

In 1994, the Ancient Buildings on Wudang Mountain (武当山古建筑群) were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The mountain's 13 major ancient palace complexes (道观) and 36 temple subgroups — including the famous Golden Hall (金顶) at the summit, the Nanyan Temple (南岩宫) built into the cliff face, and the Purple Cloud Temple (紫霄宫) — represent the best preserved and most complete example of Taoist architecture in China. The mountain receives millions of visitors annually, many coming specifically to study Wudang martial arts.

Name Origin

The name Shiyan (十堰) — "Ten Dams" — derives from the historical presence of ten ancient dams (堰, yàn) on the slopes of what is now called Fengeshan (封山, "Sealing Mountain") in the city area. These dams were built to manage water flow and support agriculture in the mountainous terrain. The name has been in use since the city was established during the Qin Dynasty (221–207 BCE) as a administrative center. The name reflects the city's long history of water management in a challenging mountain environment.

China's Car City — Dongfeng Motor

Shiyan is known as China's "Car City (车城)" — the home of Dongfeng Motor Corporation (东风汽车集团), one of China's "Big Three" automobile manufacturers and the largest truck manufacturer in China. Dongfeng Motor's history in Shiyan dates to the 1960s–1970s, when the company was established as part of China's Third Line construction program — the massive industrial relocation to the interior for security reasons during the Cold War. Today, Dongfeng's Shiyan operations produce trucks, buses, and specialized vehicles for both civilian and military use.

The automobile industry transformed Shiyan from a small regional center into a major industrial city. The city's identity is deeply connected to automobile manufacturing — many of the city's residents work in the automobile industry or in supporting industries, and the city's cultural institutions reflect its industrial heritage. Dongfeng's trucks are widely used in the Chinese military, and the company has extensive joint ventures with international automobile manufacturers including Nissan, Honda, and Renault.

Geography & Location

Shiyan is located in northwestern Hubei Province, on the upper reaches of the Han River (汉江) — a major tributary of the Yangtze. The city sits in a mountainous area — part of the Qinling–Daba Mountains (秦岭大巴山系) that separate the Sichuan Basin from the central Yangtze plain. The landscape is dramatic — steep forested slopes, deep river valleys, and limestone karst formations. Shiyan is approximately 400km from Wuhan (Hubei's capital) and 350km from Xi'an (Shaanxi's capital), making it a strategic transportation node between central and western China.

Top Shiyan Attractions

From the legendary Taoist temples of Wudang Mountain to the Danjiangkou Reservoir, explore China's Car City and sacred mountain gateway with a private driver.

🏔️

⛰️ Wudang Mountain (武当山)

UNESCO World Heritage · Sacred Taoist mountain · 30 km from Shiyan

The Wudang Mountains (武当山) — approximately 30km from Shiyan — is one of China's most sacred Taoist mountains and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The mountain is famous for its ancient Taoist temple complexes (道观) — including the Golden Hall (金顶) at the summit, the Nanyan Temple (南岩宫) built dramatically into the cliff face, and the Purple Cloud Temple (紫霄宫). Wudang is also the birthplace of Taoist internal martial arts — Tai Chi, Xing Yi, and Baguazhang. The mountain receives millions of visitors annually.

🏛️

🏛️ Golden Hall & Taihe Palace (金顶·太和宫)

Wudang Mountain · Summit temple · 1,612m

The Golden Hall (金顶) and Taihe Palace (太和宫) — at the summit of Wudang Mountain at 1,612 meters — are the most dramatic architectural complex on the mountain. The Golden Hall (a small bronze hall first installed during the Yuan Dynasty, 1271–1368 CE) sits at the very peak, with sweeping views across the mountain range. The surrounding Taihe Palace complex includes multiple halls and is the spiritual center of the Wudang Taoist tradition. The sunrise from the Golden Hall is extraordinary.

⛩️

⛰️ Nanyan Temple — Cliff Face Monastery (南岩宫)

Wudang Mountain · Built into cliff · Song Dynasty

The Nanyan Temple (南岩宫) — one of the most spectacular temple sites in China — is built into the cliff face on Wudang Mountain's southern cliff. The temple complex — dating to the Song Dynasty (960–1279 CE) and rebuilt multiple times — extends along the cliff edge with halls, pavilions, and corridors built directly on the rock face. The most famous feature is the "Dragon's Head Rock" (龙头香) — a narrow stone beam projecting over the cliff with a incense burner at its tip, where the most devoted pilgrims climb out to light incense.

💧

🛶 Danjiangkou Reservoir (丹江口水库)

China's largest reservoir · South-to-North Water Transfer

The Danjiangkou Reservoir (丹江口水库) — on the Han River near Shiyan — is one of China's largest reservoirs and a key component of the South-to-North Water Transfer Project (南水北调工程). The reservoir — with a surface area of over 1,000 km² — was significantly enlarged in the 2000s–2010s to support the water transfer to northern China. The reservoir area has scenic boat trips, lakeside scenery, and the Hanjiang River valley landscape. The Danjiangkou Dam itself is an impressive piece of engineering.

🏭

🕳️ Dongfeng Motor Industrial Tourism Zone

Car manufacturing · Industrial heritage · Factory tours

The Dongfeng Motor Industrial Tourism Zone (东风汽车工业旅游区) in Shiyan offers visitors the opportunity to see China's truck manufacturing operations. The tour includes visits to the production lines where Dongfeng trucks (东风卡车) are assembled, displays of the company's history and product range, and hands-on activities including simulator driving experiences. The tourism zone also has a museum of China's automobile industry development.

🌊

🌊 Hanjiang River Waterfront (汉江沿岸)

Shiyan city · Riverside · Evening views

The Hanjiang River (汉江) — the upper Han River — flows through the center of Shiyan city. The riverside promenade — developed in recent years — is a pleasant area for an evening stroll, with views across the river to the city's mountainside setting. The river was historically an important transportation route connecting Shiyan to Hankou (modern Wuhan) and the broader Yangtze River system. Boat trips on the Hanjiang are available in the city area.

Shiyan's Best Photo Spots

Capture China's Car City and Wudang Mountain gateway from its most evocative angles.

🏔️ Golden Hall — Sunrise over Wudang

Best at sunrise

The Golden Hall (金顶) at the summit of Wudang Mountain — at 1,612 meters, the highest point on the mountain — is most dramatic at sunrise when the first light illuminates the ancient bronze hall and the views extend across the mountain range. Arrive at the summit before dawn via the cable car (or for the adventurous, by the old stone steps) to secure a good position. The scene of the temple emerging from the morning mist with the sunrise behind it is one of China's classic mountain temple images.

⛩️ Nanyan Temple — Dragon Head Rock at Dusk

Best at dusk

The Nanyan Temple (南岩宫) cliff-face complex — with its halls built into the vertical cliff and the famous "Dragon's Head Rock" (龙头香) projecting over the abyss — is most atmospheric in the late afternoon when the light rakes across the cliff face and illuminates the temple's architectural details. The dramatic overhang and the precarious position of the temple against the rock face is extraordinary. The temple complex is at its most dramatic just before closing time.

💧 Danjiangkou Reservoir — Misty Morning

Best at dawn

The Danjiangkou Reservoir (丹江口水库) — China's largest reservoir, with over 1,000 km² of surface area — is most atmospheric in the early morning when mist rises from the water and the surrounding mountains create a dramatic backdrop. A boat trip on the reservoir at dawn — with the first light illuminating the water and the mist — is one of the most serene natural photography experiences in the Shiyan area.

🏭 Dongfeng Factory — Truck Assembly Line

Best with available light

The Dongfeng Motor assembly lines — where China's largest trucks are manufactured — are a compelling subject for industrial photography. The scale of the production — massive truck chassis moving along automated lines, thousands of workers in the assembly process — is impressive. Photography inside the factory requires permission and is typically arranged through the industrial tourism zone booking. The Dongfeng museum also has excellent historical photography displays.

🌊 Hanjiang River — Night Lights

Best at night

The Hanjiang River (汉江) flowing through Shiyan city — illuminated at night with the city's lights reflecting in the dark water — is most beautiful after dark. The riverside promenade is the best viewpoint for capturing the city's reflected lights and the mountain backdrop. The bridge across the Hanjiang — lit up at night — creates a strong compositional element for cityscape photography.

🥋 Wudang Martial Arts — Training in the Mist

Best early morning

The Wudang martial arts schools on the mountain — where students train in Tai Chi and other internal martial arts — are most photogenic early in the morning when training sessions take place against the backdrop of the mountain's ancient temples. The flowing movements of Tai Chi practitioners against the stone courtyards and ancient architectural backgrounds create compelling images. Early morning at the Purple Cloud Temple (紫霄宫) is particularly atmospheric.

Food & Shopping in Shiyan

🍜 Must-Try Shiyan & Hubei Cuisine

Preserved Egg & Tofu Soup (皮蛋豆腐汤)

A classic Hubei comfort dish — preserved eggs (皮蛋) and fresh tofu in a clear, savory broth — is found throughout Shiyan's restaurants and home kitchens. The preserved egg has a distinctive dark, jelly-like texture and a savory, slightly mineral flavor that pairs well with the soft tofu. Found at traditional restaurants throughout the city.

Steamed Mandarin Fish (清蒸鳜鱼)

The Hubei style of steamed fish — using the famous Chinese perch (鳜鱼, guì yú) from the Han River and its tributaries — is a highlight of the regional cuisine. The fish is steamed with ginger, scallion, and soy sauce, then topped with hot oil that "opens" the flavors. The meat is delicate and sweet. The Han River around Shiyan produces excellent freshwater fish, and this is the definitive preparation.

Wudang Mountain Taoist Vegetarian (武当山斋菜)

The Taoist temples on Wudang Mountain serve vegetarian temple cuisine (斋菜, zhāi cài) — a tradition of Buddhist/Taoist vegetarian cooking that uses seasonal mountain vegetables, mushrooms, and tofu to create elaborate meat-free meals. The temple restaurants at the Purple Cloud Temple (紫霄宫) and other temple complexes serve these meals to visitors. A unique culinary experience in a magnificent setting.

Hubei Hot Dry Noodles (武汉热干面)

Wuhan-style hot dry noodles (热干面, rè gān miàn) — wheat noodles tossed in a sesame paste sauce with scallion, radish pickles, and chili oil — is a beloved breakfast throughout Hubei Province, including Shiyan. The noodles have a distinctive chewy texture and the sauce is rich with sesame and peanut flavors. Found at morning markets and noodle shops throughout the city.

Three Gorges Dam Mandarin Fish (三峡鳜鱼)

The freshwater fish from the Three Gorges and upper Yangtze area — particularly the mandarin fish (鳜鱼) — is considered among the finest freshwater fish in China. Shiyan's proximity to the Han River and the broader Yangtze system means excellent access to these premium fish. The Wudang Mountain area's mountain streams also produce excellent small stream fish, prepared in the local spicy stir-fry style.

🛍️ Shopping Destinations

Wudang Mountain Souvenir Market

At the base of Wudang Mountain and along the tourist path, the souvenir markets sell Wudang-related products including miniature temple models, Taoist talismans and amulets, martial arts training equipment (wooden training swords, Tai Chi practice sets), and local agricultural products. The best products are the martial arts equipment and the Taoist cultural items.

Shiyan Riverside Night Market

The Shiyan Riverside Night Market — along the Hanjiang River waterfront — is the best place to experience the city's street food and buy local products in the evening. The market has dozens of food stalls selling local specialties, fresh fruit, and small daily items. Open from around 5 PM until 10 PM.

🎁 What to Buy in Shiyan

Wudang Taoist Talismans & Amulets

Taoist talismans and amulets (平安符, píng ān fú) — small red cloth packets containing sacred Taoist texts and blessed objects — are sold at the Taoist temples on Wudang Mountain. These are an important part of the Taoist tradition and are worn or carried for protection and good fortune. The most authentic ones are those blessed at the Golden Hall or Purple Cloud Temple.

Wudang Tai Chi Training Sets

Wudang Mountain is famous for its martial arts schools, and training equipment — including wooden practice swords (木剑), Tai Chi fans, and traditional martial arts practice clothing — is sold at the martial arts schools and souvenir markets on the mountain. Buy from established martial arts equipment shops for quality items that will last.

Han River Dried Fish & Preserved Products

The Hanjiang River (汉江) produces excellent dried fish products — dried river fish (河鱼干), dried shrimp, and smoked fish — sold at the riverside markets in Shiyan. These are used in Hubei cooking and make excellent souvenirs. The morning markets near the river have the best selection of dried river products.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts of Shiyan

Traditional crafts and cultural traditions of China's Car City and Wudang Mountain gateway.

🥋 Wudang Internal Martial Arts (武当内家拳)

Wudang Mountain is the birthplace of Taoist internal martial arts (内家拳, nèi jiā quán) — including Tai Chi (太极拳), Xing Yi (形意拳), and Baguazhang (八卦掌). These martial arts — which emphasize internal energy (气, qì), mental focus, and flowing movements rather than brute force — developed from the Taoist meditation and breathing practices that were cultivated on the mountain over centuries. Today, Wudang Mountain has dozens of martial arts schools (武术学校) where students from around the world come to train. Daily training sessions at the schools are open to visitors who want to observe or participate.

Where to see: Wudang Mountain martial arts schools, morning training sessions, Wudang Martial Arts Performance.

⛩️ Taoist Temple Rituals (道教科仪)

The Taoist temple rituals on Wudang Mountain — including daily chanting (诵经, sòng jīng), the "Morning Bell and Evening Drum" (晨钟暮鼓, chén zhōng mù gǔ) ceremonies, and the annual Taoist festivals — are living religious practices that have been continuously performed for over 1,000 years. The most important Taoist festivals on Wudang Mountain include the birthday celebrations of the Taoist deities worshipped on the mountain. The rituals combine music, chanting, meditation, and traditional instruments.

Where to see: Golden Hall, Purple Cloud Temple, Nanyan Temple — daily at the temples, festival times on the lunar calendar.

🏗️ Taoist Architecture & Temple Conservation

The ancient Taoist architecture on Wudang Mountain — built over more than 1,000 years and spanning the Song, Yuan, Ming, and Qing Dynasties — represents the best preserved and most complete example of Taoist architecture in China. The knowledge of traditional Taoist temple construction — including the specific timber joinery, roof tile traditions, and painted decoration patterns — is a specialized craft that is still practiced by the craftsmen who maintain and restore the ancient buildings. The UNESCO World Heritage status has brought increased attention to conservation techniques.

Where to see: All ancient buildings on Wudang Mountain, particularly the Golden Hall and Nanyan Temple.

🏭 Dongfeng Automobile Manufacturing Heritage

The automobile manufacturing heritage of Shiyan — the development of the Dongfeng Motor Corporation from its Third Line construction origins in the 1960s to becoming one of China's "Big Three" automobile manufacturers — is documented in the Dongfeng Motor Industrial Tourism Zone and museum. The heritage includes the physical factories, the historical records, the personal stories of the engineers and workers who built the industry, and the cultural impact on the city. The Third Line construction period is an important chapter in China's industrial history.

Where to see: Dongfeng Motor Industrial Tourism Zone, Dongfeng Motor Museum.

🧵 Traditional Hubei Embroidery (鄂绣)

Hubei embroidery (鄂绣) — one of China's regional embroidery traditions, with a distinctive style characterized by bold floral patterns, vibrant colors, and fine needlework — is practiced in workshops throughout the Hubei area including Shiyan. The tradition reflects the agricultural and riverine culture of the Hubei region, with motifs drawn from local flowers, birds, and auspicious symbols. The craft is practiced in cultural centers producing embroidered artworks and decorative items.

Where to find: Specialty handicraft shops, cultural centers.

🌊 Han River Boat-Building & Navigation

The traditional knowledge of boat-building on the Han River (汉江) — wooden river boats designed for the specific conditions of the upper Han River and its tributaries — was an important craft in the Shiyan area before the advent of modern transportation. The river was historically the main transportation route connecting Shiyan to the Yangtze and the broader central China waterway system. While wooden boat-building is no longer actively practiced, the knowledge is documented in local museums and by oral history projects.

Where to learn: Shiyan Museum, Hanjiang River waterfront.

Shiyan Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Shiyan like a local.

🏔️

Go to Wudang Mountain on a Weekday Morning

Wudang Mountain can get very crowded with Chinese tourists — particularly on weekends and public holidays. The best experience is to visit on a weekday, arrive at the park entrance at opening time (around 7:30 AM), and take the first cable car to the summit. You will have the Golden Hall and the mountain's major temples almost to yourself in the early morning, and the sunrise from the summit is extraordinary without crowds blocking the view.

🥋

Take a Tai Chi Class on the Mountain

The martial arts schools on Wudang Mountain offer daily Tai Chi classes for visitors — typically in the morning (7–9 AM) when training sessions are held at the temple courtyards. Even a single one-hour class with a Wudang master — learning the basic movements of Tai Chi against the backdrop of the ancient temples — is a memorable experience. Your driver or hotel can arrange a class at one of the established schools.

🍜

Eat Taoist Vegetarian at the Purple Cloud Temple

The Purple Cloud Temple (紫霄宫) — one of the most important temple complexes on Wudang Mountain — serves Taoist vegetarian meals (斋菜, zhāi cài) to visitors. The meal — a set menu of mountain vegetables, mushrooms, tofu, and other vegetarian dishes prepared in the traditional Taoist temple style — is served in the temple's dining hall. Eating in the temple courtyard, with the ancient buildings around you, is an extraordinary experience.

💧

Go to Danjiangkou Reservoir at Dawn

The Danjiangkou Reservoir (丹江口水库) — China's largest reservoir — is most atmospheric in the early morning when mist rises from the water and the surrounding mountains create a dramatic backdrop. Your driver can take you to the reservoir area for sunrise, then you can take a boat trip as the morning mist clears. The boat trip offers the best views of the reservoir's scale and the surrounding landscape.

🏭

Book the Dongfeng Factory Tour in Advance

The Dongfeng Motor Industrial Tourism Zone requires advance booking — at least one day ahead, and preferably more during peak periods. Your driver or hotel can help arrange the booking. The factory tour is most interesting for visitors interested in industrial processes, Chinese manufacturing, or automobile history. The museum alone (without the factory floor) is also available with simpler booking requirements.

🌊

Walk the Hanjiang River Promenade at Night

Many visitors to Shiyan see the city only as a transit point to Wudang Mountain and miss the city itself. The Hanjiang River waterfront promenade — developed in recent years with modern landscaping and lighting — is a pleasant place for an evening walk after dinner. The illuminated river, the city's mountain backdrop, and the night market along the waterfront make for a relaxed end to a Wudang Mountain day trip.

Best Time to Visit Shiyan

Each season reveals a different face of China's Car City and Wudang Mountain gateway.

🌸

Spring

Mar–May · 10–26°C

Excellent. Spring is one of the best seasons for Shiyan — comfortable temperatures, fresh spring vegetation on Wudang Mountain, and the mountain's ancient temples set against the backdrop of spring blossoms. April and May are particularly recommended — the weather is warm enough for comfortable hiking, the mountain is green and lush, and the crowds are smaller than in summer. Spring is also the start of the peak season for Wudang Mountain martial arts training.

Highlights: Comfortable weather, spring scenery, Wudang martial arts training

☀️

Summer

Jun–Aug · 22–35°C

Hot but active. Summer in Shiyan is hot (often above 30°C in July and August) and humid. Wudang Mountain is cooler than the city (the summit is significantly cooler) and can be a refuge from the summer heat. Summer is peak season for Wudang Mountain, with the longest days and most martial arts schools in full operation. Thunderstorms in the afternoon are common in July and August — plan outdoor sightseeing for the morning.

Highlights: Wudang Mountain refuge from heat, peak martial arts season, long days

🍂

Autumn

Sep–Nov · 10–24°C

Excellent. Autumn is the best season for Shiyan — comfortable temperatures (15–22°C in September and October), clear skies, and the Wudang Mountain autumn foliage turning the mountain's slopes red, orange, and gold. October is particularly recommended as the best all-around month for weather, visibility, and scenery. The Danjiangkou Reservoir area is also at its most beautiful in autumn with the surrounding hills in autumn colors.

Highlights: Best weather, autumn foliage, clear mountain views

❄️

Winter

Dec–Feb · 2–10°C

Cold but atmospheric. Winter temperatures in Shiyan range from 2–10°C, and Wudang Mountain's summit can be cold enough for frost and occasional snow. The mountain is less crowded, the misty atmosphere can be dramatic, and the Golden Hall in winter snow is extraordinary. However, the summit cable car may be affected by high winds and some mountain trails can be icy. The city of Shiyan itself is comfortable in winter.

Highlights: Fewest crowds, winter mountain scenery, atmospheric Wudang
