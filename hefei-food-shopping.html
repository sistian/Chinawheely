Overview: Eating and Shopping in Hefei

Hefei, old name Luzhou, cooks the plain fare of the Huai river plain: roast duck in the northern style, goose braised whole, rice-flour dumplings from the river town of Sanhe, and crayfish by the sack in summer. None of it is court food; nearly all of it is measured in decades-old shopfronts rather than dynasties. Six dishes and four shopping areas follow, with founding dates and counts where on record.

What to eat

Luzhou roast duck (庐州烤鸭)

Hefei's roast duck under its old Luzhou name, distinguished by a honey-sugar glaze and smoking over local wood before the oven. Sliced and eaten with thin pancakes and sauce in the northern manner, or shredded over rice with duck-fat gravy. The namesake restaurant has traded in the old city for decades and pushes a single-digit price band for the duck-over-rice bowl at its counter.

Where: Luzhou Kaoya restaurant's old branch sits on Zhongcaishi Road in the city center; duck-rice counters cluster around Huaihe Road.

Sanhe rice dumplings (三河米饺)

Deep-fried crescents from Sanhe ancient town in Feixi county: wrappers of rice flour cooked to a paste, filled with dried shrimp, tofu and pork, then fried till the shell blisters. One dumpling weighs about 50 grams and orders come in fours or sixes. The town's ferry-port kitchens have sold them for well over a century; they now appear at old-city breakfast stalls too.

Where: Best at the source — Sanhe Ancient Town's shopfronts; old-city stalls around Huaihe Road sell them at breakfast.

Duck-fat flatbread (鸭油烧饼)

Small sesame-topped flatbreads made with rendered duck fat layered into the dough, sold alongside the roast-duck counters they share a bird with. Each biscuit is about palm-sized and baked in batches through the day; a standard purchase is a bag of five to ten. The pairing of duck bowl plus duck-fat biscuit is the documented Hefei lunch formula.

Where: Sold at the Luzhou Kaoya counter on Zhongcaishi Road and bakeries around Huaihe Road pedestrian street.

Wushan tribute goose (吴山贡鹅)

Whole goose salted and braised in a master stock with a dozen-odd spices, from Wushan town in Changfeng county; 'tribute' in the name claims an imperial provenance from the Tang era. Sold chopped by weight at dedicated shops, white-cut style with the braising broth spooned over. A half goose feeds three to four and travels poorly — eat it near the shop.

Where: Wushan-named goose shops operate across the city; the deepest cluster sits along the old airport-side streets of Shushan district.

Crayfish (小龙虾)

Hefei sits inside China's crayfish belt between the Huai and Yangtze basins and runs an annual crayfish festival begun in the early 2000s. Summer streets fill with stalls selling 13-spice and garlic batches by the jin or by the sack, one jin holding roughly 10 to 15 shells. Peak season runs May to August; prices fall as the season deepens.

Where: Night-market rows off Huaihe Road and Lei Street's stalls carry it May through August.

Dried tofu and Luzhou snacks (庐州小吃)

A catch-all counter tradition: dried tofu in five-spice broth, gluten puffs, sweet fermented rice soup and sesame pastries, sold together at old-city snack shops by the piece or small bowl. Counters list 20-plus items under one glass case, most priced at a few yuan each. Serving hours run from breakfast into late afternoon.

Where: Old snack shops around Zhongcaishi Road and the City God Temple market block.

Where to shop

Huaihe Road pedestrian street (淮河路步行街)

The city's central pedestrian street, running about a kilometer through the old Luzhou core with the restored residence of Qing statesman Li Hongzhang near its east end. Department stores anchor both ends and snack lanes cut off the middle; the street was rebuilt as a pedestrian-only mall in the early 2000s. Hours run roughly 10:00 to 22:00, later on summer weekends.

Where: Luyang District, central Hefei, between Suzhou Road and Huancheng North Road.

Sanhe Ancient Town (三河古镇)

A water town at the meeting of three rivers in Feixi county, some 35 kilometers southwest of downtown, rated among China's top scenic towns. Streets of stone-flagged shopfronts along the Xiaonan river sell rice dumplings, rice wine, dried shrimp and bamboo wares; the town's documented trading history runs past the Qing era. A full circuit of streets takes half a day.

Where: Feixi County, about 35 km from downtown; buses run from the west bus stations, or a 50-minute drive.

City God Temple market (城隍庙)

A temple-front small-goods market that has traded since the 1980s, holding hundreds of stalls across connected lanes — clothes, incense, phone parts and festival goods — plus old snack counters around its edges. Renewed in the mid-2010s with a cleaner layout, it keeps daytime hours of roughly 09:00 to 18:00. Bargaining remains normal on non-tagged goods.

Where: Luyang District, off Mengcheng Road north of Huaihe Road's west end.

Lei Street food block (罍街)

A food-and-culture block opened in 2013 south of the old city, named for the lei ritual wine vessel and built in imitation-Anhui style. Several dozen restaurants serve Anhui regional cooking — Hefei duck, Wushan goose, mountain-area black pork — plus beer halls open to 24:00 on weekends. Evening is the working hours; most kitchens close by 22:00 weekdays.

Where: Baohe District, on Ningguo Road south of the old town, a short taxi ride from Huaihe Road.

Eating and shopping in Hefei: practical notes

The old city's eating spine is short and walkable: Huaihe Road's kilometer-long pedestrian street, the City God Temple lanes at its west end, and Zhongcaishi Road's roast-duck counters one block south. The documented Hefei meal is a two-counter affair — duck-over-rice plus duck-fat biscuits from the same shop — while Sanhe rice dumplings appear at breakfast stalls through the morning hours only. Lei Street, a 2013 food block in Baohe district, carries the late-night load once old-city kitchens shut near 21:00.

For a half-day out, take Sanhe Ancient Town: 35 kilometers southwest, stone streets along the river, and the rice dumplings fried at the source rather than reheated in town. Season-wise, summer belongs to crayfish — festival-era stalls sell by the jin from May to August, one jin running 10 to 15 shells — while Wushan goose and Luzhou duck hold steady year-round.

Frequently Asked Questions

What food is Hefei known for?

Luzhou roast duck with its duck-fat biscuits from decades-old Zhongcaishi counters, Sanhe rice dumplings from the water town of the same name, and Wushan tribute goose from Changfeng county. Summer adds crayfish by the jin, backed by a city festival running since the early 2000s. None of it is banquet food — it is counter and stall fare.

Is Sanhe Ancient Town worth the trip out of town?

Yes, for half a day. The town sits 35 kilometers southwest where three rivers meet, with stone-flagged shop streets and a trading history running past the Qing era. Its rice dumplings — shrimp-and-tofu filled, about 50 grams each — are best eaten fried on the spot; buses from the west stations take under an hour.

What is a duck-fat biscuit?

A palm-sized sesame flatbread with rendered duck fat layered through the dough, baked in batches and sold in bags of five to ten. It exists because Hefei's roast-duck counters render the fat and waste nothing. The standing lunch order pairs it with the duck-over-rice bowl from the same counter.

Where do people shop in central Hefei?

Huaihe Road pedestrian street, about a kilometer long with Li Hongzhang's restored residence near its east end, anchors the center; the City God Temple market at its west end has traded small goods since the 1980s. Lei Street south of the old city, opened 2013, is the evening food-and-beer alternative.

When is crayfish season in Hefei?

May to August, with the city's annual crayfish festival dating to the early 2000s. Stalls sell 13-spice and garlic batches by the jin — roughly 10 to 15 shells — with prices falling as the season deepens. Lei Street and the lanes off Huaihe Road carry the densest stall rows.
