The Real Tea Story in Chongqing

Tea is woven into daily life in Chongqing, and the city's tea counters are where that shows up most clearly. The tea on the table comes from the producing areas; what is local is the way it is drunk.

Tea Houses Worth Stopping At in Chongqing

Chongqing Tea houses around Chongqing Art Museum Most of Chongqing's tea trade happens in the streets around Chongqing Art Museum, where the tea shops pour a pot for you at the counter rather than selling by the tin. Ask for a longjing, a shou pu-erh or a jasmine blend and they will let you taste before you buy. Morning tea and the market stalls Tea drinking in Chongqing starts early: the markets open with the first kettle of the day, and the stalls around the old commercial streets are where the city's own blend is put together.

Where the Tea Comes From

The tea served in Chongqing comes from the producing areas around the city — the green teas of the lower Yangtze and the darker fermented teas of the south-west — brought in by the usual supply routes.
