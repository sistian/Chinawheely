The Silk Road's Western Oasis

Hotan (和田) — known historically as Karakax (喀喇昆仑, "Black Kunlun") — sits at the foot of the Kunlun Mountains (昆仑山) on the southern edge of the Taklamakan Desert. The city is an oasis fed by snowmelt rivers descending from the Kunlun range, creating a narrow band of green in the otherwise arid desert. This geographic position made Hotan one of the most important cities on the ancient Silk Road (丝绸之路) — a nexus for trade between China, Central Asia, India, and the Persianate world.

Hotan is famous for three things that have defined its identity for millennia: jade (和田玉), silk (丝绸), and carpets (地毯). The jade from the Kunlun Mountains — white jade (羊脂玉), spinach jade (菠菜玉), and other varieties — is considered the finest jade in China and has been prized by Chinese imperial courts for over 3,000 years. Hotan's silk production dates to the early Han Dynasty, when the Chinese attempt to monopolize silk production led to the legendary tale of silk worms being smuggled to the West.

Name Origin

The historical name Karakax (喀喇昆仑) comes from Mongolian — "Kara" meaning "black" and "Kax/Kun" referring to the Kunlun Mountains. The name reflects the dark, imposing appearance of the Kunlun range as seen from the desert oasis. The modern name Hotan (和田) comes from the ancient kingdom of "Kustana" or "Khotan" — used since at least the 2nd century BCE and reflecting the city's Buddhist heritage. "Khotan" derives from the local Buddhist kingdom name, later Sinicized as 和田.

Jade, Silk & the Desert Trade

The jade trade is the backbone of Hotan's economy and cultural identity. The Yurungkash River (玉龙喀什河, "White Jade River") and the Karakash River (喀拉喀什河, "Black Jade River") — two rivers descending from the Kunlun Mountains through the Hotan area — carry nephrite jade boulders that have been collected for over 3,000 years. Hotan jade (和田玉) — particularly the rare white "mutton fat" jade (羊脂玉) — is the most prized jade in China and commands extraordinary prices. The jade markets in Hotan are a must-visit experience.

Hotan's silk industry is equally legendary. The city is one of the few places in China where traditional silk production continues using ancient techniques — silk weavers using hand-operated looms to produce the famous Hotan silk fabric. The story of how silk worms' eggs were smuggled out of China to Central Asia (the legend involves a Chinese princess who hid them in her headdress) is one of the great tales of technological espionage in history — and it began with Hotan's silk trade.

Geography & Location

Hotan is located in southern Xinjiang, on the southern edge of the Taklamakan Desert where the Kunlun Mountains meet the Tarim Basin. The city lies approximately 1,500km from Ürümqi (Xinjiang's capital) and 500km from Kashgar. The Taklamakan Desert — "You go in, you don't come out" in the local Uyghur language — surrounds Hotan on three sides, with the Kunlun Mountains to the south. The city is reached by the desert highways that cross the Tarim Basin from the north (via Korla and Kuqa) and from the east (via Qiemo).

Top Karakax (Hotan) Attractions

From jade markets to Buddhist cave temples, explore the western Silk Road's most storied oasis with a private driver.

💎

Hotan Jade Markets (和田玉市场)

Jade trading · River pebbles · Jewelry

The jade markets of Hotan are legendary — open-air markets where vendors sell rough jade pebbles, carved artifacts, and finished jewelry. The main jade market near the city center is most active in the morning. Watch vendors bargaining, inspect jade quality with a flashlight, and learn to distinguish authentic Hotan nephrite from imitations. Even if you don't buy, the market is a fascinating window into one of China's oldest luxury trades.

🏺

Hotan Museum (和田博物馆)

Silk Road archaeology · Buddhist artifacts · Jade history

The Hotan Museum has an excellent collection documenting the archaeology and history of the Hotan area — from the ancient Silk Road kingdoms through the Buddhist era to the modern city. Highlights include ancient Buddhist manuscripts discovered in the nearby caves, Silk Road trade goods, Hotan jade artifacts spanning 3,000 years, and textile fragments from the early silk production era. Free entry.

🧶

Hotan Silk Factory & Workshop (和田丝绸厂)

Traditional silk weaving · Hand looms · Demonstration

The Hotan Silk Factory — one of the few remaining traditional silk production facilities in China — demonstrates the complete process of silk production from cocoon to finished fabric. Watch the silk weavers working hand-operated looms to produce the famous Hotan silk — a fabric known for its lightweight, translucent quality. Visitors can purchase authentic Hotan silk products directly from the factory.

🌊

Yurungkash & Karakash Rivers (玉龙喀什河 & 喀拉喀什河)

"White & Black Jade Rivers" · Jade collecting · Desert oases

The two rivers flowing through Hotan — the Yurungkash (玉龙喀什河, "White Jade River") and the Karakash (喀拉喀什河, "Black Jade River") — are the source of Hotan's jade wealth. Both rivers carry nephrite jade boulders from the Kunlun Mountains. A visit to the river banks — particularly the Yurungkash — allows you to see local jade hunters wading in the river looking for pebbles, and the distinctive white and gray gravel beds where jade is found.

⛪

White Jade Lake (白玉河)

Desert oasis · Jade pebbles · Scenic drive

The White Jade River (白玉河) area — a series of pools and channels surrounded by desert on the outskirts of Hotan — is a peaceful spot where locals come to wade in the jade-rich waters, picnic by the river, and escape the city heat. The area is particularly beautiful in spring when the poplar trees lining the river turn green. A good half-day excursion with your driver.

🏠

Hotan Old City & Traditional Bazaars (和田老城)

Traditional Uyghur architecture · Old town · Crafts

The old city of Hotan — with its traditional Uyghur courtyard houses, narrow lanes, and artisan workshops — gives a sense of what the Silk Road oasis city looked like before modern development. The old bazaar area is particularly atmospheric, with spice sellers, hat makers, metalworkers, and traditional carpet dealers operating from small shops. Walking the old city lanes with your driver is a window into centuries of continuous urban life.

🚗 Driving to Hotan — The Desert Highways

Kashgar → Hotan

~500 km · 7–8 hours · Southern Tarim Desert Highway

The desert highway from Kashgar (喀什) to Hotan runs along the southern edge of the Taklamakan Desert, passing through ancient Silk Road waypoints, desert oases, and dramatic mountain-front landscapes. This is one of the most spectacular road journeys in China — the desert on one side, the Kunlun Mountains on the other. Your private car with an experienced driver is the most comfortable way to make this journey.

Korla → Hotan (Northern Route)

~700 km · 10 hours · Northern Tarim Highway

The northern route from Korla (库尔勒) — the main city of the northern Tarim Basin — crosses the full width of the Taklamakan Desert via the 塔克拉玛干沙漠公路 (Taklamakan Desert Highway). This is the world's second largest shifting sand desert, and the desert highway crossing — through towering dunes and past ancient oasis towns — is an extraordinary experience.

Hotan's Best Photo Spots

Capture the western Silk Road's jade city from its most evocative angles.

💎 Jade Market — Morning Trading

Best before 10 AM

The Hotan jade market in the morning — when vendors first lay out their wares and the light is good for examining jade quality — is the most authentic and photogenic time to visit. The interplay of torchlight on jade surfaces, the hand-to-hand bargaining, and the variety of jade on display make for compelling photography. Early morning (8–10 AM) is best before the midday heat and crowds.

🧶 Silk Factory — Loom & Thread

Best with available light

The Hotan Silk Factory — with its rows of hand-operated looms, the translucent silk threads, and the traditional techniques still in use — makes for rich photographic subjects. The best time to photograph the weaving process is mid-morning when the factory is in full operation. The contrast between the old wooden looms and the brilliant white and colored silk they produce is visually striking.

🌊 Yurungkash River — Jade Pebbles in Clear Water

Best in morning light

The Yurungkash River ("White Jade River") in morning light — when the sun catches the white and green jade pebbles visible through the clear mountain water — is one of Hotan's most distinctive landscapes. Local jade hunters wading in the river add human interest to the scene. Early morning is best, when the light is still soft and the river is not yet crowded with visitors.

🏠 Old City — Uyghur Courtyard Architecture

Best in golden hour

The old city of Hotan — with its traditional Uyghur courtyard houses, carved wooden facades, and narrow lanes — is most beautiful in the golden hour before sunset. The warm light on the carved wooden balconies, the laundry hanging in the lanes, and the traditional doorways make for atmospheric urban photography. The old city is still a living residential neighborhood, so be respectful of privacy.

🌅 Taklamakan Desert — Dunes at Sunrise

Best at sunrise

The Taklamakan Desert dunes on the northern edge of Hotan — visible from the city on clear days — are most dramatic at sunrise when the low light creates sharp shadows across the dune crests. A short drive north with your driver in the early morning brings you to the dune edge. The shifting sand patterns, the silence, and the scale of the desert make for extraordinary landscape photography.

🌙 Night Market — Silk Road Food Scene

Best after 6 PM

The Hotan night market — which opens from around 5 PM — is a feast of colors, smells, and activity. The food stalls, the traditional bread ovens, the grilled lamb, the Uyghur bread displays, and the crowds of locals make for excellent documentary photography. The night market is also a great place to try local Hotan and Uyghur cuisine — nan bread, grilled skewers, rice dishes, and local fruits.

Food & Shopping in Hotan

🍜 Must-Try Hotan & Uyghur Cuisine

Hotan Nan Bread (和田馕)

The large, round Uyghur flatbread (馕, nan) baked in a clay oven (馕坑, nan hang) is a staple of the Hotan diet. Hotan's nan is distinctive — often larger than those in other Uyghur cities, with a thin, crispy edge and a soft center. Found at every street corner and market. Eat it fresh from the oven with lamb or just with tea.

Grilled Lamb Skewers (羊肉串)

Lamb skewers —Chunks of lamb marinated in cumin and chili, grilled over charcoal — are the soul of the Hotan night market. The lamb from the Hotan area is from sheep raised on the desert and mountain edges, with a distinctive flavor. Best at the night market stalls where the charcoal grills create smoke and drama.

Hotan Polo (抓饭)

Pilaf (抓饭, polo or pilaf) — slow-cooked rice with lamb, carrots, onions, and cumin — is a Central Asian dish deeply embedded in the Hotan culinary tradition. The Hotan version uses local rice, mountain lamb, and the distinctive sweet carrots grown in the Hotan oasis. Served at traditional restaurants, often at lunchtime.

Uyghur Laghman (拉条子)

Hand-pulled noodle dishes (拉条子, laghman) — served dry or in a broth with vegetables and lamb — are a staple of the Uyghur diet and found at every noodle restaurant in Hotan. The thick, chewy noodles are made fresh daily. Served with chopped green peppers, lamb, and a spicy sauce.

Hotan Grapes & Melons

Hotan's oasis climate produces exceptional fruit — particularly seedless grapes, melons, pomegranates, and figs. The Hotan grape (和田葡萄) is a prized variety known for its sweetness and large, seedless berries. In season (August–October), the fruit markets of Hotan overflow with colorful displays.

🛍️ Shopping Destinations

Hotan Jade Market (和田玉市场)

The jade market is Hotan's most famous shopping destination — an open-air market where vendors sell rough jade pebbles, carved artifacts, jewelry, and decorative objects. The market is largest in the morning. Even if you are not buying, watching the jade trade is a fascinating experience. Your driver can take you to the best market and introduce you to a local expert.

Hotan Night Market (和田夜市)

The Hotan night market near the city center is the heart of the city's food and street life — open from around 5 PM until midnight. Food stalls sell every variety of Uyghur street food, along with spices, dried fruits, and local crafts. A must-visit for experiencing the living culture of the city.

🎁 What to Buy in Hotan

Hotan Jade (和田玉)

Authentic Hotan nephrite jade — particularly the white "mutton fat" variety (羊脂玉), spinach jade (菠菜玉), and ink jade (墨玉) — is the definitive Hotan souvenir. Prices range from a few dollars for small pebbles to thousands for fine carved pieces. Buy from reputable dealers at the jade market. Ask your driver to introduce you to a trusted dealer.

Hotan Silk (和田丝绸)

Hotan silk fabric — produced using traditional hand-loom techniques — is sold at the Hotan Silk Factory and at specialty textile shops. The silk is known for its lightweight, translucent quality and comes in natural colors (white, cream, pale gold) as well as dyed versions. Hotan silk is used for scarves, shirts, and decorative fabric.

Hotan Carpets (和田地毯)

Hand-knotted carpets — using traditional Uyghur patterns, natural dyes, and local wool — are sold throughout Hotan. The carpets range from small prayer rugs to large room-sized pieces. Traditional Hotan carpet patterns feature medallions, floral motifs, and geometric borders in rich reds, blues, and golds. Available at the carpet bazaar and specialty carpet shops.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts of Hotan

Traditional crafts and cultural traditions of the western Silk Road's jade city.

💎 Hotan Jade Carving (和田玉雕)

The art of carving Hotan nephrite jade — transforming rough river pebbles into refined ornaments, figurines, vessels, and jewelry — is a craft that has been practiced in Hotan for over 3,000 years. Hotan jade carvers use traditional cutting, grinding, and polishing techniques to reveal the beauty within each stone. The craft requires years of training and a deep understanding of jade's properties. Workshops in Hotan still practice the traditional carving techniques.

Where to see: Hotan jade market carving workshops, Hotan Museum jade collection.

🧶 Hotan Silk Weaving (和田丝绸编织)

The traditional craft of silk weaving in Hotan — using hand-operated looms to produce fine silk fabric from locally grown silk — dates to the Han Dynasty and is considered one of the origins of China's silk trade. The Hotan Silk Factory continues this tradition, producing silk fabric that is considered among the finest in China. The craft involves cultivating silkworms, reeling silk from cocoons, and weaving on traditional Jacquard looms.

Where to see: Hotan Silk Factory, specialist textile workshops.

�毯 Hotan Carpet Weaving (和田地毯编织)

Hand-knotted carpet weaving — using a traditional loom, hand-spun wool, and natural plant dyes — is a major craft in Hotan. Hotan carpets are known for their dense knotting, rich colors, and traditional Uyghur patterns. The craft is practiced in village workshops throughout the Hotan area, with weavers — traditionally women — working on large looms in their homes. The patterns have been passed down through generations.

Where to find: Hotan carpet bazaar, village workshops, night market.

🎵 Muqam Music & Dance (十二木卡姆)

The Twelve Muqam (十二木卡姆) — a complex suite of music, song, and dance that is the pinnacle of Uyghur performing arts — is performed throughout the Uyghur world, including Hotan. The Muqam combines Central Asian maqam melodies, Uyghur folk music, and poetic singing in the Uyghur language. Hotan has its own local Muqam tradition, distinct from the better-known Kashgar version.

Where to experience: Hotan Cultural Centre, local music venues, festivals.

🍞 Nan Bread Baking (馕坑烤馕)

The craft of baking nan bread (馕) in a traditional clay oven (馕坑, nan hang) is a daily art in every Hotan neighborhood. The nan hang — a large clay cylinder set into the ground — is heated with scrubbed animal dung or brushwood, and the flatbread dough is slapped against the oven walls to bake. The resulting bread — with its characteristic round shape, thin crispy edge, and soft center — is the daily staple of the Hotan table.

Where to see: Nan bakeries throughout the old city, Hotan morning markets.

🎭 Traditional Bazaar Crafts

The traditional Hotan bazaar is home to dozens of specialized craft trades — hat makers, saddle makers, copper smiths, wood carvers, spice merchants — that have been practiced in the same way for centuries. Walking through the old bazaar area with your driver reveals these living crafts in action — a hat maker shaping a Uyghur doppa cap, a copper smith hammering a tray, a saddle maker stitching leather.

Where to see: Hotan old city bazaar, traditional craft workshops.

Hotan Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Hotan like a local.

💎

Go to the Jade Market Before 9 AM

The Hotan jade market is most authentic and least touristy in the early morning — before 9 AM when the main traders are doing their business. After 10 AM, more tourists arrive and the atmosphere changes. If you want to see the real jade trade and have serious conversations with dealers, go early. Bring a small flashlight (essential for examining jade quality) and don't be afraid to bargain.

🧶

Ask Your Driver to Arrange a Home Weaving Visit

The Hotan Silk Factory is the official demonstration, but the most authentic experience is a visit to a family weaving workshop in one of the old city lanes. Your driver likely knows families who still weave on traditional looms in their homes — an intimate experience that shows the complete process from cocoon to finished silk. Ask your driver to arrange this in advance.

🌅

Drive to the Desert Dunes at Sunrise

The Taklamakan Desert dunes north of Hotan are most dramatic at sunrise — the temperature is cool, the light is extraordinary, and you may have the dunes entirely to yourself. Ask your driver to take you to the dune edge about 30 minutes before sunrise. The drive takes about 20 minutes. The silence of the desert at dawn, with dunes stretching to the horizon, is one of Central Asia's great experiences.

🍜

Eat Lamb Skewers at the Night Market

The lamb skewers (羊肉串) at the Hotan night market are exceptional — lamb from sheep raised on the desert and mountain edges, marinated in cumin and chili, grilled over charcoal. The best stalls are the ones with the longest queues of locals. Eat them standing at the stall, with cold nan bread and cold beer. The night market atmosphere — with the cooking smoke, the chatter, and the street life — is unforgettable.

🌊

Wade in the Yurungkash River

The Yurungkash River ("White Jade River") runs through Hotan and locals wade in it to cool off in summer. Ask your driver to take you to a quiet section of the river in the late afternoon. The experience of walking in the same river that has been searched for jade for 3,000 years — with the Kunlun Mountains visible in the background — is uniquely Hotan. Bring water shoes or wear sandals.

🏺

Visit the Hotan Museum First

Before exploring the city, visit the Hotan Museum first. Understanding the 3,000-year history of jade trading, the Silk Road Buddhist era, the ancient manuscripts, and the archaeology of the Hotan oasis will transform how you see the city. The museum is free, air-conditioned, and has English labels. It takes about 1–2 hours and your driver can wait in the car park.

Best Time to Visit Hotan

Each season reveals a different face of the Taklamakan's jade oasis.

🌸

Spring

Mar–May · 15–30°C

Good. Spring brings warm days and cool evenings, with the poplar trees around Hotan turning green in April and May. The desert is at its most comfortable for sightseeing and desert excursions. Spring is also a good time for jade hunting — the rivers are running clear from snowmelt and the jade pebbles are visible in the river beds. April and May are particularly pleasant months.

Highlights: Comfortable desert weather, river jade hunting, green oasis scenery

☀️

Summer

Jun–Aug · 30–42°C

Hot. Summer temperatures in Hotan can exceed 40°C, making daytime sightseeing uncomfortable. However, the long summer days allow for early morning and late evening activities. Summer is peak fruit season — grapes, melons, and pomegranates are at their best. If visiting in summer, plan sightseeing for early morning and evening, with midday rest in air-conditioned spaces.

Highlights: Peak fruit season (grapes, melons), long days, early morning jade market

🍂

Autumn

Sep–Nov · 10–28°C

Excellent. The most comfortable season for visiting Hotan. Temperatures are mild — warm days (20–28°C in September and October), cool evenings. The desert is at its most beautiful, the fruit harvest continues, and the jade rivers run clear. October is particularly good — the summer heat has passed, the oasis is green, and the jade markets are active. The desert sunsets in autumn are extraordinary.

Highlights: Best weather of the year, jade market active, comfortable desert excursions, harvest fruit

❄️

Winter

Dec–Feb · -5 to 8°C

Cold but manageable. Winter temperatures in Hotan are cold but less extreme than in northern Xinjiang — typically ranging from -5°C at night to 8°C during the day. Sunny winter days can be pleasant for sightseeing. The jade market is active year-round. Hotel prices are at their lowest and the city is quiet. The desert has a stark, dramatic beauty in winter, though some desert excursions are not recommended.

Highlights: Lowest hotel prices, jade market active, quiet atmosphere, dramatic winter desert
