Name Origin

Diqing means "the good place" in Tibetan, and the prefecture seat was called Zhongdian in the Ming and Qing dynasties before it was renamed Shangri-La City in 2001, after the fictional valley in the novel. The area was part of the Tibetan Kham region and one of the most important stations on the Tea Horse Road.

Education & Universities

Higher education is provided by Diqing University for Nationalities and local vocational colleges, with programmes in teacher training, Tibetan and Naxi studies, forestry, animal husbandry and tourism management.

Getting Around

Getting around Diqing is by road. The G214 highway and the Shangri-La expressway link the seat with Lijiang in the south and Deqin in the north, the road over the passes reaches Shangri-La from Sichuan, and the Lijiang–Shangri-La railway now runs all the way to the seat itself, its terminus at Shangri-La station on the edge of town. The prefecture's airport connects Shangri-La with Kunming and Chengdu.

Natural Resources & Environment

Diqing is where the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau lifts into the Tibetan Plateau: three mountain ranges — the Meili Snow Mountain, the Yunling range and the Zhongdian range — run west to east, with the Jinsha and Lancang rivers cutting north to south between them. Altitude runs from 1,486 m at the Lancang mouth in Weixi to 6,740 m at Kawagarbo on Meili, which gives the prefecture three distinct ecological belts: valley, mountain and alpine.

Food & Shopping in Diqing

What to eat and what to bring home from Diqing

🍽️ Must-Try Diqing Cuisine

Diqing's food is highland Tibetan and Naxi: yak hotpot, butter tea with tsampa, Tibetan bread and butter, and in Weixi the Naxi dishes of hotpot chicken and pigeon soup. The old town of Shangri-La has the widest choice of restaurants in the prefecture.

🛍️ Shopping in Diqing

Shangri-La's old town sells Tibetan silver and turquoise, prayer wheels, thangka prints, local honey and highland barley products. The Deqin and Weixi county markets sell the butter and cheese of the summer pastures.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Butter tea is the daily drink here, churned with yak butter and salt and served in wooden bowls that are refilled all day. In the Naxi villages sweet tea and local soda are also on the table, and the Naxi sancha tea of Weixi is worth taking home.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Diqing

The living traditions of Diqing — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

Diqing is a meeting point of three cultures: the Tibetan Kham traditions of the monasteries and the Kham Kagyu school, the Naxi Dongba culture of the Baishuitai terraces and the old town, and the Tea Horse Road trading tradition that ran through the prefecture for centuries. The central town gong, Shouguo Temple, the Cizhong church and the Jinsha river rock paintings are listed national heritage sites, and the Six Tea Horse Roads of Diqing are being inscribed as a UNESCO cultural route.

Diqing Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Diqing like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

Best Time to Visit Diqing

October to November for the clearest air and white peaks; May to September for green meadows and clear trails.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · April–May — the roads out of Lijiang open, the barley greens appear and the peaks are still clear. (daytime 9.4–16.8°C, nights around -2.7°C; about 34 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the greenest season, with rain most afternoons and the meadows at their best between showers. (daytime 19.1–19.4°C, nights around 9°C; about 126 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–November — the finest weather of the year, with the air at its clearest and the harvest in the valleys. (daytime 11.3–17.6°C, nights around -5.1°C; about 47 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · December–February — dry, cold and sunny, with snow on the high passes and very few visitors. (daytime 6.9–8.6°C, nights around -9.8°C; about 10 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Diqing

What to drink in Diqing, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Diqing

Butter tea is the daily drink here, churned with yak butter and salt and served in wooden bowls that are refilled all day. In the Naxi villages sweet tea and local soda are also on the table, and the Naxi sancha tea of Weixi is worth taking home.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Diqing

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Diqing

🍵 Tea Houses of Diqing

Tea culture runs through the day in Diqing: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
