Orange Island (橘子洲) is a five-kilometre sandbar sitting in the middle of the Xiang River, and it is the piece of Changsha that every visitor ends up photographing. It formed in 305 AD, it measures 320 metres across at its widest point and only 170 metres at its narrowest, and since 2001 it has been part of the Orange Island scenic area within Yuelu Mountain. Coming by car from Huanghua Airport saves you the awkward bus-and-metro hop across the river.

Changsha Huanghua International Airport (CSX)

24 km from the Changsha terminal, about 40 minutes by car.

Huanghua opened in August 1989 in Changsha County and sits about 25 km from the city centre, with Terminal 2 in operation since July 2011 and Terminal 1 reopened in May 2018. The drive comes in on the airport expressway and then crosses towards the river through Yuelu District, ending on the east side of the Xiang River opposite the island.

Why transfer to Orange Island?

The island's draw is the landscape and the poetry. Mao Zedong swam here and visited repeatedly from his youth through to after the founding of the People's Republic, and in 1925 he wrote the poem Qinyuan Chun, Changsha, opening with the lines about who shall rule this boundless land, which are cut into stone on the western end of the island. A 32-metre bronze sculpture of young Mao Zedong was erected at the head of the island on 20 December 2009, and it has become the single most photographed object in Changsha.

The island is also the best free view of the city itself. From the western tip you look back at the riverfront of Changsha proper, with the coloured pavilions, the orange groves and the arched Xiangjiang First Bridge running away to the east, and the hills of Yuelu behind you on the land side. The scenic area was rated a national 5A site, had the rating briefly withdrawn in 2017 and had it restored in February 2018, which tells you how seriously the landscaping and the sightlines are taken.

  • The island runs about 5 km from north to south, up to 320 m wide at its broadest and 170 m at its narrowest
  • It formed in 305 AD during the Yongxing era of the Jin dynasty and has been a sandbar in the Xiang River ever since
  • The 32-metre young Mao Zedong sculpture at the head of the island was completed in December 2009
  • Orange Island station on Line 2 of the Changsha Metro opened on 29 April 2014 directly south of the Xiangjiang First Bridge

What to do at Orange Island once you arrive

Most drivers set down on the eastern side of the island near the main gate, and the first thing to do is walk west along the tree-lined avenue rather than heading straight for the sculpture. The middle section of the island has the pavilions, the calligraphy stele and the orange groves, and it is a flat, easy 2 km walk from the east gate to the head of the island with the river on your right the whole way. Waterfront monument architecture from the Qing dynasty sits along this stretch, and the island was laid out as part of the Orange Isle scenic area within Yuelu Mountain in 2001.

At the western head you will find the 32-metre young Mao sculpture and the large stone stele carved with the Qinyuan Chun poem. The sculpture is normally busy from mid-morning, so the earlier end of the walk is the better time for a photograph without people in front of it. The scenic area also runs a sightseeing shuttle around the island, priced at 25 yuan per person, which is worth using if you want to cover the far end and get back to your car without retracing the walk.

  • The sightseeing shuttle on the island costs 25 yuan per person and is sold through the Changsha Chengfa WeChat mini program
  • Metro Line 2 stops at Orange Island station, opened on 29 April 2014 on the south side of the Xiangjiang First Bridge
  • The island is flat and exposed, so bring water and sun protection in summer
  • The best light on the sculpture is late afternoon, when the sun comes across the river from behind you

Arriving at Changsha Huanghua International Airport

Huanghua is in Changsha County, about 25 km from the city centre, and it has two terminal buildings. Terminal 2 has 212,000 square metres of floor space and 22 bridge gates, and the older Terminal 1 was closed for renovation from July 2011 until it reopened in May 2018, having first opened its doors in 2000. The airport serves domestic and regional routes, so the walk from the gate to the arrivals hall is short and the process of collecting bags is usually uncomplicated.

Allow twenty minutes to collect checked bags, and note that the road in to Changsha crosses the Xiang River on one of three bridges, so a slow crossing at peak time adds ten minutes or more. Every booked driver waits in the arrivals hall with a name board and monitors your flight, which means a delayed arrival at CSX does not need a phone call to sort out. The airport sits in Changsha County rather than in the city proper, so the last part of the drive is a local road section with more junctions than the expressway.

What the transfer costs

The run from CSX to Orange Island is priced on distance and vehicle class, and the quote covers fuel, tolls and the driver's waiting time. A sedan for one or two travellers with hold luggage is typically in the region of 110 to 160 yuan. The seven-seat tier, which swallows a family plus cases, usually lands at 150 to 210 yuan from Huanghua. Some operators also quote the return leg, which is useful if you are going back to the airport the same evening.

Hires of six hours or more are normally billed as a fixed day rate rather than through a per-kilometre meter, and waiting time at the drop-off point is included for the first hour or two. Two adults normally ride in a sedan; a household with pushchairs and cases fits one van, and because the driver waits rather than looping, you set the walking pace on the island yourself.

Ask your driver to drop you at the island's eastern entrance near the main gate, which is the easiest point to start the walk from and the nearest point back to the road. If you would rather ride the sightseeing shuttle round the island, tell the driver in advance so they can wait at the western end instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is it from Changsha Huanghua Airport to Orange Island?

Huanghua Airport lies in Changsha County about 25 km from the city centre, and Orange Island sits in the middle of the Xiang River in Yuelu District, so the full road distance including the river crossing is roughly 24 km. Expect around 40 minutes with light traffic and closer to an hour during the morning or evening peak.

Do I need to buy a ticket to enter Orange Island?

The main public areas of the island and the head-of-island sculpture are reached through the scenic area without a separate entrance charge for the walk itself, but the sightseeing shuttle that circles the island is a paid service at 25 yuan per person. Tickets for the shuttle are bought through the Changsha Chengfa WeChat mini program, which is worth setting up before you arrive so you are not queuing with a phone screen at the gate.

Which metro line goes to Orange Island?

Line 2 of the Changsha Metro serves Orange Island station, which opened on 29 April 2014 on the south side of the Xiangjiang First Bridge in Yuelu District. The station was built as a civic integrity themed stop and carries displays on clean-government culture, and it puts you on the east side of the river a short walk from the island's main gate.

How long should I spend on Orange Island?

Two hours is comfortable for the eastern gate to the sculpture at the head and back again, with the shuttle taking about 30 minutes off the return if the river is busy. Three hours is better if you also want to walk the riverside path in Yuelu District on the mainland side, where the Orange Isle Youth Art Palace and the Huxinting restaurant sit close to the bridge.

Is Orange Island busy, and when is the best time to go?

It gets crowded from mid-morning onwards, particularly at the young Mao sculpture, which is the first stop most visitors make. Early morning or the last two hours before sunset are much quieter, and sunset over the river with the city skyline behind you is the reason most photographers choose the west end of the island. Avoid weekends and public holidays, when the crowd is at its heaviest.
