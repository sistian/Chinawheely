Overview: Day Trips from Xiamen

Xiamen sits on the western shore of the Taiwan Strait and administers six districts, Huli, Siming, Jimei, Tong'an, Haicang and Xiang'an, covering 1,700.61 square kilometres with a population of 5,163,970 counted in 2020. The Kinmen islands lie under 6 kilometres offshore across Xiamen Bay. Most of the interest for a day trip is concentrated on the island itself: a UNESCO-listed quarter reached by a 20-minute ferry, two temples at the foot of Wulao Peak, a ring road of 43 kilometres around the coast, and the school village built across the bay by the same benefactor who founded the university.

Gulangyu Island (鼓浪屿)

Gulangyu is an island of under 2 square kilometres in the Siming district, with a resident population of about 20,000. It was settled in the late Song and early Yuan periods under the names Yuanshazhou and Wulongyu, renamed in the Ming dynasty, and used by Zheng Chenggong as a military base, where a troop drill platform and stone barbican gate remain on Riguang Rock. Under the 1843 Treaty of Nanjing Xiamen became a treaty port, and in January 1902 consuls of nine countries signed the Charter of the Amoy International Settlement, after which 13 nations set up consulates. The settlement was inscribed on the World Heritage List on 8 July 2017 at the 41st session in Krakow.

How the day runs: The crossing itself takes about 20 minutes and no motor vehicles are permitted on the island beyond electric shuttle carts, so the day is a walking one. Take the morning ferry and be on the island by 09:00 to have Riguang Rock, a boulder more than 40 metres high rising to 92.68 metres at the top of Longtou Mountain, before the coach parties. Walk the international settlement streets, where the 13 former consular buildings and the mission houses stand close together, then continue to Shuzhuang Garden, whose construction by Lin Erjia took more than ten years and which is organised in two sections, Bushan on the land side and Canghai on the sea side. Take the afternoon ferry back.

3 km from Xiamen · about 20 min by car from Xiamen · full-day trip · best in October to April, when the sea crossing is calm and the masonry buildings are at their clearest.

  • Riguang Rock at 92.68 metres is the island's highest point and the first thing worth doing on arrival, since the climb empties out as the day goes on.
  • Motor vehicles are not allowed on the island apart from electric carts, so the outer buildings and the naval dock area need the walking route or the shuttle.
  • Shuzhuang Garden took more than ten years to build and is laid out as a land garden, Bushan, and a sea garden, Canghai, which are walked in opposite directions.

Nanputuo Temple (南普陀寺)

Nanputuo stands below Wulao Peak on the south-east side of Xiamen, facing the bay, and is the second largest temple in the city after the Fantian monastery in Tong'an. It was founded in the late Tang as Sizhou Temple, rebuilt in the Northern Song as Wujin Rock, abolished under the Yuan and rebuilt in the Ming as Puzhao Temple at its present site. After the early Qing wars it was restored and enlarged in 1684 by the naval commander Shi Lang, who added the Great Compassion Hall, and the name records its position south of the Putuo shrine of Guanyin. In 1924 it became a ten-direction monastery, and the Minnan Buddhist College opened the following year.

How the day runs: Allow two hours for the temple and the surrounding grounds, and pair it with the university across the road. The complex is arranged along an axis of halls running uphill toward Wulao Peak, with the Great Compassion Hall and the surrounding halls of the monastic quarters forming the core, and the halls of the college behind. The five hundred Arhats cast in bronze in the principal hall are the piece usually sought, and the vegetarian restaurant at the entrance is the standard place to eat before or after the walk. Finish at the foot of the five peaks, where the stone inscriptions line the approach path, and return to the city by 12:30.

5 km from Xiamen · about 15 min by car from Xiamen · half-day trip · best in All year; the temple is busyest in the first lunar month and at the end of the year.

  • The name records a direction, south of Putuo, because the shrine worships the same Guanyin, and the building sequence was rebuilt twice after periods of abandonment.
  • The 1684 rebuilding under Shi Lang added the Great Compassion Hall, which is the architectural centre of the present complex.
  • The Minnan Buddhist College opened in 1925 and sits within the temple grounds, so the academic buildings are part of the visit rather than a separate site.

Xiamen University and the Early Buildings (厦门大学)

Xiamen University was founded on 6 April 1921 by the overseas Chinese businessman Chen Jiageng, the first university in modern Chinese history founded by returned emigrants. It opened with two divisions, a normal school and a commercial school, and a first intake of 120 preparatory students with fewer than twenty teachers and 98 in attendance. It added engineering and journalism departments in July 1922 and enrolled at seven places, bringing 152 new students including the first women and making it the country's first co-educational university. It became a national university in July 1937, and in 2006 the State Council listed its early buildings with Jimei School Village as the sixth batch of national key cultural relics.

How the day runs: Give the campus two hours and go on foot from Nanputuo, which is the easiest approach. The early group of buildings, the Jiannan and Qunxian halls and the group along the South River, are the part covered by the 2006 listing, and they face the sea with the Wu Tao and Zhoushan islands beyond. The main building, built in 1954 by the architect Chen Ji in a Chinese revival style with a pyramidal roof and a 45-metre main tower, anchors the campus. Walk the South River frontage and the cliff path above the beach, which returns to the ferry terminal and to Nanputuo in one loop.

6 km from Xiamen · about 20 min by car from Xiamen · half-day trip · best in March to June and September to November; the campus is quietest outside the admission and graduation seasons.

  • The first intake was 120 preparatory students with fewer than twenty teachers, and the 1922 enrolment across seven places was what introduced co-education, so the campus layout still reflects a small school rather than a modern university.
  • The 2006 listing covers the early buildings of the university together with Jimei School Village, which means the two sites are administratively one protected group.
  • The main building was completed in 1954 in a Chinese revival style with a 45-metre tower and is the campus's clearest landmark from the coast road.

Jimei School Village (集美学村)

Jimei School Village lies in the Jimei district across Xiamen Bay, about 30 minutes by car from the centre of the island. Chen Jiageng founded the school there in 1913 and gave it a wider brief than a single college, adding a kindergarten, a hospital, a library and an education extension department under the collective name of Jimei School. He personally planned the site and commissioned the architects who designed it, with the resulting Jiageng architecture deliberately combining Chinese and Western forms. The village and the early buildings of Xiamen University were listed by the State Council in 2006 as the sixth batch of national key cultural relics, one of the largest entries of that group.

How the day runs: The village is a walk rather than a drive, since the buildings are grouped along the axis between the main gate and the water. Start at the entrance gate and work down the central axis, taking in the school buildings and the halls of the 1950s and 1960s on the same grid, then continue to the waterfront and the pool and the dragon wall. The Aoyuan memorial garden is on the sea side at the end of the axis and was built by the students in memory of Chen Jiageng, whose statue stands in it. Allow two hours for the village and the garden together.

25 km from Xiamen · about 35 min by car from Xiamen · half-day trip · best in All year, with the academic calendar setting the rhythm of the streets.

  • The village was planned as a self-contained educational town from 1913, with a kindergarten, hospital and library listed alongside the schools themselves.
  • The buildings are registered under a single protection entry that includes the early buildings of Xiamen University across the bay.
  • The Aoyuan garden at the sea end of the axis was built by the students of the school and holds the bronze statue of Chen Jiageng.

Huandao Road and the Coastal Promenade (环岛路)

Huandao Road is the ring road around Xiamen island, 43 kilometres long as county road 401, crossing the Siming and Huli districts. Construction began in 1991 in three phases at a cost of close to 3.8 billion yuan, and the road is four or six lanes wide throughout. It includes the Yanwu Bridge, the Ring Road South and East, the Ring Road North and Lujiang Avenue. The 13-kilometre stretch from the university White City to Xiang'an was named Donghuan Wanghai in the city's 1997 list of new twenty scenic sites, and faces the Kinmen, Dadan and Erdan islands. The Yanwu Bridge, opened on 6 September 2003, is 2.2 kilometres long, with its deck only 5.5 metres above sea level.

How the day runs: Drive the southern arc in the morning, when the road is at its busiest, and park at the viewpoints rather than trying to complete the whole 43 kilometres. The stretch from the university to Xiang'an is the named scenic section and takes about 40 minutes at a stop-start pace, with the beach platforms, the sports centre and the observation points along it. The Yanwu Bridge at the south-west corner joins the university end to the Lujiang Avenue, and the low deck means the harbour and the ferries are directly below the road. Return along the east side through the mangrove-lined stretch past the airport, which is quieter than the tourist arc.

12 km from Xiamen · about 25 min by car from Xiamen · half-day trip · best in All year, with autumn and winter the clearest for the view across to the offshore islands.

  • The road is 43 kilometres and four to six lanes wide, so distances along it are longer than the map suggests and the southern section is slow in peak hours.
  • The Yanwu Bridge deck sits 5.5 metres above sea level, which is why the harbour view from it is so close.
  • The named scenic section is only 13 kilometres of the ring road, running from the university White City to Xiang'an and facing the Kinmen islands.

Hulishan Fort and the Xiamen Museum (胡里山炮台与厦门市博物馆)

Hulishan Fort on the south-east tip of the island was built in the Self-Strengthening Movement, from March 1894 to October 1896, a span of two years and eight months. It is a half-bastioned, half-curtilage structure combining European and Ming-Qing styles, covering more than 70,000 square metres. Its 280-millimetre coastal gun, cast by the German Krupp works in 1894, is the largest surviving nineteenth-century gun of its type. It was listed among the fourth batch of national key cultural relics in 1996 and entered the Guinness records in 2000. The Xiamen Museum, opened in 1983 and moved to its present building in 2006, became a national first-grade museum in 2024.

How the day runs: The fort is the better half-day, and the museum is a useful rainy alternative. At Hulishan, the surviving positions are the fortification itself, the barracks behind it and the rear hill, linked by covered passages and with a moat, an ammunition store and observation posts. The famous gun stands in its original emplacement, and the range covers the outer channel where the older Baishi and Panshi batteries sit 4,500 and 5,000 metres away to the west. Allow ninety minutes. The museum has seven exhibition halls over two floors of a 52,300-square-metre steel-frame building, with 9,981 square metres of display area and more than 70,000 items in the collection.

9 km from Xiamen · about 25 min by car from Xiamen · half-day trip · best in All year; the fort and the museum are both outdoors or semi-outdoors in a mild coastal climate.

  • The 280-millimetre Krupp gun was cast in 1894 and is the largest surviving nineteenth-century piece of its type, which is the specific reason for the site's ranking.
  • The fort covers more than 70,000 square metres, and the barracks and rear hill sections are separate from the fortification, so a full visit takes longer than the guns alone.
  • The Xiamen Museum was rated a national first-grade museum in 2024, having been a second-grade museum from 2018, and its seven halls are spread over two floors of a 52,300-square-metre building.

Planning day trips from Xiamen

The ferry is the fact that organises this page. Gulangyu is 3 kilometres across the water and takes about 20 minutes, but the island has almost no motor traffic, so a day there is measured in walking hours rather than in kilometres driven. The opposite is true on the mainland: Huandao Road runs 43 kilometres around the island, Jimei is 25 kilometres away across the bay, and the forts and museums on the island itself are 9 kilometres out. Plan one of each per day rather than trying to combine the island and the ring road, since the ring road's southern section is congested through the middle of the day.

Two practical points cover most of the timing. The university and the temple face each other across a single road, so both sit comfortably in one half-day with a walk between them, while Jimei and the university share a protection listing and are best seen on separate days if you want to read the early buildings properly. The second is the calendar: the school village and the campus both run on the academic year, and the museum's seven halls are laid out over two floors of a 52,300-square-metre building, which needs more time than the building's size suggests.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the ferry to Gulangyu take?

About 20 minutes, which is short enough that the crossing is not a significant part of the day, and it is the reason the island works as a full-day trip rather than an evening one. The island covers under 2 square kilometres and holds about 20,000 residents, and motor vehicles are not permitted on it apart from electric carts. Because of that restriction, the practical figure for the day is walking distance rather than the 3 kilometres of open water, and Riguang Rock at 92.68 metres is worth doing first while the island is still quiet.

What makes Gulangyu a World Heritage site?

The Gulangyu International Historic Settlement was inscribed on 8 July 2017 at the 41st session of the World Heritage Committee in Krakow. The basis is the built record of an international settlement: after the 1843 Treaty of Nanjing opened Xiamen as a treaty port, consuls of nine countries signed the Charter of the Amoy International Settlement in January 1902, and 13 nations established consulates in turn. The mission houses and consular buildings stand close together, and 19th-century domestic architecture sits beside them on the same streets.

Can the university and the temple be done in one morning?

Yes, and they are the natural pairing. Nanputuo stands below Wulao Peak and Xiamen University is directly across the road, with the cliff path above the beach linking the two ends of the walk. Give the temple two hours, including the Great Compassion Hall and the halls of the Minnan Buddhist College opened in 1925, and the campus two hours for the early buildings listed in 2006 together with the 1954 main building with its 45-metre tower. Both are half-day sites about 5 to 6 kilometres from the centre.

How far is Jimei School Village and what is there?

About 25 kilometres, or roughly 30 minutes by car across the bay from the centre of the island. Chen Jiageng founded the school there in 1913 and expanded it into a small town with a kindergarten, hospital, library and education extension department, planning the site himself and commissioning the architects who produced the Chinese-Western Jiageng style. The village and the early buildings of Xiamen University were listed by the State Council in 2006 as the sixth batch of national key cultural relics, and the Aoyuan memorial garden stands at the sea end of the central axis.

Is Huandao Road worth driving in full?

Not in one sitting. The road is 43 kilometres, four or six lanes wide, and was built from 1991 in three phases at a cost of close to 3.8 billion yuan. The part worth the time is the named 13-kilometre stretch from the university White City to Xiang'an, which faces the Kinmen, Dadan and Erdan islands and was named Donghuan Wanghai in 1997. The Yanwu Bridge at the south-west corner is 2.2 kilometres long with a deck 5.5 metres above the water, and the eastern side past the airport is the quieter return.

Which site has the most distinctive single object?

Two candidates. Hulishan Fort holds a 280-millimetre Krupp coastal gun cast in 1894, the largest surviving nineteenth-century gun of its type, and the fort itself covers more than 70,000 square metres; it was listed among the fourth batch of national key cultural relics in 1996 and entered the Guinness records in 2000. The alternative is the bronze Arhats at Nanputuo, in a temple rebuilt and enlarged in 1684 and renamed then for its position south of the Putuo mountain shrine.
