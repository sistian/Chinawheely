Pu'er Local Secrets

The things a driver who works here every day would point out.

🌿 Old tea forests and plantations

Where Pu'er tea comes from

Pu'er takes its name from the tea that has been made in these southwestern Yunnan mountains for centuries. The Jingmai old tea forest, a UNESCO-listed cultural landscape, is the most famous of the old groves, and the planted slopes around the city are where the modern production happens.

🏞️ A mountain city

Forests, rivers and Dai villages

The city sits in a valley of the Ailao Mountains, with forest, rivers and Dai ethnic villages around it. A day outside the city is spent on the road between tea gardens, not in the city centre.

🍄 What to eat here

Yunnan mountain cooking

Pu'er tea of course, then Yunnan rice noodles, wild mushrooms, bamboo rice and Dai dishes. The mushroom season in the summer is the one to plan a day around.

☕ In the tea houses

A city built around tea

Pu'er is one of the few Chinese cities where the tea trade is the town's main business. Shops cup tea for you at the counter, and the compressed tea cakes are sold by weight rather than in tins.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Pu'er

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Pu'er

🍵 Tea Houses of Pu'er

Tea culture runs through the day in Pu'er: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
