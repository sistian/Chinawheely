Name Origin

Qiandongnan means "the Miao and Dong country of the east of Guizhou", and the prefecture was created in 1956 out of the old Zhenyuan and Dushan prefectures, with the seat at Kaili. It sits on the Miao Ling mountains, where the Yangtze and Pearl river systems divide, and it is the most purely minority region in Guizhou: Miao and Dong people make up more than 70% of the population.

Education & Universities

Higher education is served by Kaili University and local vocational colleges, with a distinctive focus on Miao and Dong language and culture, ethnology, teacher training, medicine, ecology and the crafts of the two minorities.

Getting Around

Getting around Qiandongnan is by road and rail. The Shanghai–Kunming railway and the high-speed line from Guiyang pass through Kaili, and the G60 and G65 expressways link the prefecture to Guiyang, Hunan and Guangxi. The villages themselves are reached by provincial and county roads, many of them mountain roads — allow more time than the map suggests.

Natural Resources & Environment

The prefecture is 30,282 km² of folded mountain running from the Miao Ling range down to the hills of Guangxi, with the highest point at the 2,178.8 m Huangyang summit of Leigong Mountain and the lowest at 137 m on the Liping river at the provincial border. It is one of the wettest parts of Guizhou, with 2,900 rivers and an annual runoff of 19.2 billion cubic metres; north of the Miao Ling the rivers run to the Yangtze, south of it to the Pearl. The climate is humid subtropical with a distinct vertical layering, annual means of 14.6 to 18.5 °C and 1,010 to 1,368 mm of rain.

Food & Shopping in Qiandongnan

What to eat and what to bring home from Qiandongnan

🍽️ Must-Try Qiandongnan Cuisine

Qiandongnan's food is defined by the sour soup (酸汤) — a fermented rice-and-tomato broth that is the base of almost every dish, with fish, chicken or cured pork in it, always sharp with green chili. Miao glutinous rice cakes and the Dong rice wine complete the table.

🛍️ Shopping in Qiandongnan

The markets of Kaili and the village bazaars sell Miao and Dong silver headdresses and silverwork, indigo-dyed cloth, embroidered collars and cuffs, bamboo hats, and the batik and embroidery work of the villages. Buy direct from the makers where you can.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Tea in the villages is the local green tea of the Miao Ling slopes, grown and roasted at the village level and drunk strong and simple in small cups. It is also what goes into the local oil tea with fried rice cakes, which is the version travellers meet in the Miao villages of Leishan.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

A private car and driver lets you start at the morning market, cross the city for lunch, and finish at an evening teahouse without worrying about parking or the last bus back to your hotel.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Qiandongnan

The living traditions of Qiandongnan — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

This is one of the richest ethnographic regions in China. The Miao culture of the singing-dancing groups, the silver ornaments, the indigo dyeing and the embroidery of the Miao collars; the Dong drum towers, covered bridges and kam (the great multi-voice song of the young men); the Guizhou Dongjing folk opera; and the Sisters' Meal festival, when the villages fill with young people courting in the mud. The Dong Kam singing of the drum towers was inscribed by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage, and the whole prefecture is a craft landscape of embroidery, batik and silver.

Best Time to Visit Qiandongnan

March to May for the spring festivals and the plum blossom, and September to November for the clearest, most comfortable weather.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — the <em>Sisters' Meal</em> festival in the third lunar month, the <em>Lusheng</em> festival in the tenth, and the Miao fields in blossom. (daytime 15.5–25.5°C, nights around 7.8°C; about 116 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — warm, wet and green, with the heaviest rain of the year and the best river and waterfall conditions. (daytime 28–30.5°C, nights around 20.2°C; about 172 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–November — the clearest and driest weather, with the harvest and the rice terraces turning gold. (daytime 16.8–27.3°C, nights around 9°C; about 72 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · December–February — cold and damp in the mountains, with the miasma and the empty streets; the quietest time, and the best for the craft work. (daytime 8.4–11°C, nights around 2.4°C; about 33 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Qiandongnan

What to drink in Qiandongnan, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Qiandongnan

Tea in the villages is the local green tea of the Miao Ling slopes, grown and roasted at the village level and drunk strong and simple in small cups. It is also what goes into the local oil tea with fried rice cakes, which is the version travellers meet in the Miao villages of Leishan.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Qiandongnan

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Qiandongnan

🍵 Tea Houses of Qiandongnan

Tea culture runs through the day in Qiandongnan: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
