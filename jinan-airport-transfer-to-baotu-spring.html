Baotu Spring is three jets of water coming up through the floor of a stone pool, and it has never stopped properly running since September 2003. Getting there from Yaoqiang Airport takes about 50 minutes, because the airport sits 33 km north-east of the city and the spring itself is buried inside the old walled town on the south-west corner. What makes the trip worth the drive is the setting: a Qing dynasty pavilion, a stone stele, and seventy other named springs within walking distance in a city whose identity is built on water coming out of the ground.

Jinan Yaoqiang International Airport (TNA)

36 km from the Jinan terminal, about 50 minutes by car.

Yaoqiang sits about 33 km northeast of the city centre in Yaoqiang Subdistrict. The run comes in on the Jinan Ring and the Jingjintang Expressway, then drops into the old city on Gongqingtuan Road for the short final approach to the spring pool on Luoyuan Street.

Why transfer to Baotu Spring?

The water is genuinely remarkable. All of Jinan's springs are fed by an Ordovician karst aquifer that recharges over 1,500 sq km of mountain south of the city, and Baotu is the most famous of roughly 70 named springs in the downtown area. Three outlets push water up from an underwater limestone shelf, peaking at about 1.6 cubic metres a second, and the jets have been recorded as high as 26 metres. The temperature holds at 18 degrees Celsius all year, which is why the pool looks equally alive in January and in July.

The cultural weight is as heavy as the water. The spring is mentioned in the Spring and Autumn Annals, one of the Five Classics, and the Qing emperor Qianlong named it the Number One Spring under Heaven when he toured the south. Li Daoyuan's Commentary on the Waterways Classic described it as a spring gushing up like a wheel. It is also fragile: over-extraction stopped it flowing for 926 days between March 1999 and September 2001, and the city now publishes the underground water level daily and issues colour-coded warnings when it drops.

The park around the pool is small and dense. Guanlan Pavilion, the First Spring stele and the spring pool itself sit within a few minutes' walk of each other, and Shuyu Spring with its Li Qingzhao memorial hall is just across the water. Wanzhu Garden, a bamboo garden, is on the western side. It is the kind of site that rewards a slow hour rather than a scheduled stop, and the surrounding old lanes have been rebuilt in Qing style with shops selling spring water, sugar and lotus-root starch.

  • Flowed for 926 days only between 1999 and 2001, when extraction exceeded the aquifer's recharge

What to do at Baotu Spring once you arrive

The pool is best viewed from the east and north sides, where you get the jets, Guanlan Pavilion and the First Spring stele in one frame; the pavilion itself is a poor vantage point for the thing it is named after. The water runs off into a channel that joins the old city moat, and the section of moat beside the park is where the locals gather. If the ground-level square is busy, the raised walkway at the northern end gives a cleaner view of the three outlets.

The wider park is worth an extra half hour. Shuyu Spring across the water carries a Li Qingzhao memorial hall in the small courtyard behind it, and Wanzhu Garden on the west side is planted almost entirely with bamboo, which sounds eccentric and works. Daming Lake is a fifteen-minute walk north if you have the time, and the old city streets between the spring and Daming Lake are where Jinan eats and shops.

  • Adult admission to the park is around 40 yuan, with reduced rates for students and children
  • The nearest metro access is Line 2 at Luo Kou, with a walk from there to the south gate
  • Go early in the morning; the water is clearest and the crowd is thinnest before 09:30
  • The park sits inside the old city, so expect narrow approach streets and few coach parking bays

Arriving at Jinan Yaoqiang International Airport

Yaoqiang is a large airport in the north-east of the city, about 33 km from the centre, opened in 1992 and repeatedly expanded since, with a terminal that handles around 12 million passengers a year. It is on the Jinan Ring and the Beijing-Shanghai and Qingdao-Yinchuan expressways, which makes the approach straightforward but also means the last kilometres are spent on urban roads with traffic lights. It was China's busiest airport by passengers in 2025, and flights to Los Angeles have operated seasonally from here since 2016.

Frequently Asked Questions: Booking in Jinan Airport Transfer To Baotu Spring

How far is Baotu Spring from Jinan Yaoqiang airport?

Yaoqiang Airport lies about 33 km north-east of Jinan city centre and the spring is inside the old walled town on the south-west side, so the road distance is about 36 km. The drive takes 45 to 50 minutes, and a little longer at the evening peak when the ring road into the city slows.

Is Baotu Spring currently flowing?

Yes. The spring has flowed continuously since it came back up in September 2003, after a 926-day shutdown caused by excessive groundwater extraction for industrial and municipal use. The city publishes the underground water level for Baotu and Black Tiger Spring daily and issues warnings when the level falls below set thresholds, so the situation is actively monitored.

How much does it cost to enter Baotu Spring Park?

The adult ticket is around 40 yuan, with half rates for students and children and concessions for senior citizens. The spring pool, the Guanlan Pavilion, the Shuyu Spring memorial hall and Wanzhu Garden all sit inside the same paid park area, so one ticket covers the whole visit rather than each site separately.

How long should I stay at Baotu Spring?

An hour and a half is enough for the pool, Guanlan Pavilion, the First Spring stele, Shuyu Spring and Wanzhu Garden at a normal pace. Add another hour if you want to walk north to Daming Lake or eat in the old town lanes, and the full afternoon works well if you arrived from the airport in the morning.

Is the spring easy to reach without a car?

Broadly, yes, though not directly. Line 2 of the Jinan Metro has Luo Kou station near the south-west corner of the old city, from which the spring is a short walk, and other central stations are within a comfortable stroll. The catch is that the park sits inside narrow old-city streets where coach parking is limited, which is exactly where a private car earns its keep.
