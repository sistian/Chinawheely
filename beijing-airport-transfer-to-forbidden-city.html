The Forbidden City is the largest surviving wooden palace complex in the world and it sits at the exact centre of Beijing, which makes it the hardest site to reach independently and the easiest to reach with a car. The complex covers 72 hectares and holds 8,886 rooms inside 980 buildings. Construction started in 1406 under the Yongle Emperor, and the buildings you walk through today are almost all the Ming originals rather than Qing reconstructions.

Beijing Capital International Airport (PEK)

30 km from the Beijing terminal, about 45 minutes by car.

Capital sits 32 km northeast of the city centre in Shunyi. The Airport Expressway (S12) feeds the Fourth Ring Road, then the run continues on the East Second Ring and drops you at the kerb beside the Meridian Gate on Jingshan Front Street, a few minutes from the Tiananmen Gate complex.

Beijing Daxing International Airport (PKX)

45 km from the Beijing terminal, about 60 minutes by car.

Daxing is 46 km south of central Beijing and its terminal is 700,000 square metres on 47 square kilometres of land. The quickest road runs north on the Daxing Airport Expressway to the South Fifth Ring, then up the West Second Ring and across towards the Forbidden City.

Why transfer to the Forbidden City?

The numbers behind the palace are what make it worth the drive. It covers 72 hectares, a little larger than the whole of Disneyland Paris, and counts 980 buildings with 8,886 rooms. Construction began in 1406 after the Yongle Emperor moved the capital north from Nanjing, and the layout follows a strict north-south axis: the Meridian Gate on the south, the Hall of Supreme Harmony at the centre, and the Gate of Divine Might and the northern garden beyond.

Almost everything visible in the courtyards is the original Ming fabric, which is rare for a complex this heavily rebuilt. The Hall of Supreme Harmony sits on a three-tier white marble terrace, and behind it the Imperial Garden is dense with old cypress and paulownia. Since 1925 the buildings have housed the Palace Museum, with nearly a million objects in the collection, so the visit covers architecture, court ritual and painting in the same ticket.

The advantage of coming by car is timing. Entry is only through the Meridian Gate, the palace runs on a one-way south-to-north flow, and the whole thing is graded on a very steep curve: the courtyards are quiet at opening, the axis is packed by mid-morning, and the side halls thin out after lunch.

  • The palace covers 72 hectares and holds 980 buildings with 8,886 rooms
  • Construction began in 1406 under the Yongle Emperor and the core buildings are original Ming work
  • Admission is 60 yuan from 1 April to 31 October and 40 yuan from 1 November to 31 March
  • Opening time is 08:30, last entry 15:30, and the gates close at 16:30
  • The Meridian Gate on the south is the only entrance; you leave through the Gate of Divine Might or the East Glorious Gate

What to do at the Forbidden City once you arrive

Your driver should set down on Jingshan Front Street, a short walk north of the Meridian Gate, which is the single visitor entrance for every ticket sold. Visitors arriving from Tiananmen Square cover about 400 metres along the ceremonial avenue through the Gate of Heavenly Peace and the Upright Gate. Security screening happens at the Meridian Gate, and inside the walls the flow is strictly one-way from south to north.

Start with the Three Great Halls on the central axis: the Hall of Supreme Harmony, the Hall of Central Harmony, and the Hall of Preserving Harmony. From the Meridian Gate the walk along the axis takes roughly forty minutes, and the Empress Garden's north gate opens onto the Gate of Divine Might, where you can exit. If you have two hours, skip the western halls and spend the time in the Imperial Garden and the northern courtyards instead.

  • Reservations open seven days ahead at 20:00 and are tied to the passport or ID used, so book before you fly rather than on the day
  • The Palace Museum WeChat mini-program sells tickets for visitors with WeChat Pay; other passport holders can reserve by email instead
  • The Treasure Gallery inside costs an extra 10 yuan and needs its own timed reservation
  • The museum closes on Mondays except public holidays, so plan the Forbidden City for a Tuesday to Friday

Arriving at Beijing airport for the Forbidden City

Capital International handles the large share of Beijing's international traffic and has three terminals, with Terminal 3 taking the long-haul and transfer-heavy traffic. The distance to the palace is around 30 km, and with traffic on the Airport Expressway and the ring roads the drive commonly takes 45 to 60 minutes. Daxing is a longer run at 45 km and about an hour, but the airport is newer and the road links are simpler, so peak-hour delays are shorter.

Meet your driver in the arrivals hall rather than at the kerbside, and allow fifteen minutes to collect checked bags and clear immigration before you walk out. Every driver in the booking will meet you with a name board and monitor the flight number, so a delay at PEK or PKX does not need a phone call.

What the transfer costs

A private car from PEK to the Forbidden City typically runs 150 to 220 yuan for a sedan, and from Daxing 180 to 260 yuan, with the price built on distance, vehicle class, airport tolls and waiting time. A sedan suits one or two travellers with hold luggage. A family of four to six fits the van class, priced around 220 to 320 yuan from Capital and 260 to 360 yuan from Daxing.

Longer car hires are usually billed as a fixed day rate rather than a per-kilometre meter, and the waiting time at the gate while you are inside the palace is normally included for the first two hours.

Give your driver the Meridian Gate as the drop-off point rather than the Tiananmen Square car park, which is far larger and slower to get out of. If you also want the Forbidden City and Tiananmen Square on the same day, say so at booking so the driver can plan the one-way walking flow between the two.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is it from Beijing Capital Airport to the Forbidden City?

Capital International Airport sits 32 km northeast of downtown Beijing, and the Forbidden City is right in the middle of the old city, so the road distance is about 30 km. Expect 45 minutes on a clear run and closer to 70 minutes during the morning rush or when the ring roads are loaded.

How much does it cost to enter the Forbidden City?

The main admission ticket is 60 yuan per person from 1 April to 31 October and 40 yuan in the low season from 1 November to 31 March. Students with a school letter pay 20 yuan, and the Treasure Gallery costs an additional 10 yuan. Annual passes are also sold, but they require advance registration.

Do I need to book the Forbidden City ticket before I fly?

Yes. The museum does not sell same-day tickets and releases reservations seven days ahead at 20:00 Beijing time. Bookings are tied to the passport or identity document you present, and one document buys one ticket per day. Bring the original document with you, because you cannot swap the name after booking.

Which gate do I enter and leave through?

All visitor tickets are for the Meridian Gate on the south side, facing Tiananmen Square. Inside, the circulation is one-way from south to north, and you leave through the Gate of Divine Might in the north or the East Glorious Gate on the east side. Plan your exit so your driver can collect you at the matching gate.

How long does the visit take and is it worth a whole day?

Two to three hours covers the central axis, the courtyards and the Imperial Garden properly. Half a day is the practical minimum if you are arriving on a flight, and a full day lets you add the Treasure Gallery, the Clock and Watch Gallery, or Jingshan Park immediately north of the palace wall.
