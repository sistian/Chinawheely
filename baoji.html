Name Origin of Baoji

Baoji's name is linked to a legend of a stone rooster (bao ji) that brought good fortune. It was historically known as Chencang and was an important military and transport hub on the Silk Road.

Baoji Education & Universities

Baoji University of Arts and Sciences is the main comprehensive university, along with several vocational colleges.

Getting Around Baoji

Baoji Airport (BJG) offers limited domestic flights. Baoji Railway Station, Baoji South Station, and Baoji West Station are major rail hubs on the Longhai and Baoji–Chengdu railways. Buses and taxis cover the city.

Baoji Natural Resources & Environment

Baoji sits at the western end of the Guanzhong Plain where it meets the Qinling Mountains. The Wei River flows through the region, and the area has rich agricultural and mineral resources.

Buddhist Heritage

One of China's most sacred Buddhist sites, housing a finger bone relic of Buddha. The new golden pagoda and ancient ruins make this a profound visit.

Great Wall Heritage

Baoji marks the western end of the Wei River Great Wall. Explore preserved watchtowers and fortifications at this historic site.

Ancient Bronzes

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Baoji

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Baoji

🍵 Tea Houses of Baoji

Tea culture runs through the day in Baoji: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
