Overview: Eating and Shopping in Xiamen

Xiamen cooks from the water and from the diaspora. Oysters are fried in sweet potato starch, a worm from the mudflat sets into its own jelly, and the sauce in the city's best-known noodle bowl is a transliteration of the Malay word for satay. This page sets out six foods and four areas to walk and buy, with dates, dimensions and counts drawn from the record.

What to eat

Shacha soup noodles (沙茶面)

Xiamen's local noodle dish: wheat noodles blanched in a boiling pot and topped with pork liver, kidney, duck gizzard, intestine, fresh squid or dried tofu, then flooded with a shacha broth kept rolling in a large cauldron. The seasoning descends from the Malay satay sauce. The dish was named among the first Chinese famous snacks in December 1997.

Where: Four shops — Wutang, Sili, Dazhong and the 1980 Shaorouzong — were named the four great shacha noodle counters when the dish was listed among the first Chinese famous snacks in December 1997.

Shacha sauce (沙茶酱)

The paste that gives the noodle bowl its colour, descended from the peanut satay sauce of Southeast Asia and rebuilt in China from peanuts, sesame, fish, dried shrimp and garlic, then boiled down with oil and salt. The sauce is made in 4 documented regional styles: Nanyang, thick and sweet-spicy with much peanut; Chaoshan, finely grained and salty-sweet; Fujian, shrimp-dominant and leaning hot; Taiwan, coarse-grained and peanut-free.

Where: Sold in jars across the old town and used in shacha hotpot, shacha beef and shacha duck; it is also eaten as a dip with raw egg, spring onion, garlic and chilli.

Oyster omelette (海蛎煎)

Fresh oysters bound in starch and egg and fried, eaten with a sour-spicy sauce. The Xiamen method differs from the Taiwanese one: a thin layer of dry sweet potato starch is sprinkled over the oysters in the pan, and garlic shoots are tossed in at the end. The dish is traced to Fujian, and to the 1594 drought that followed the arrival of sweet potato from Manila.

Where: Stalls and small shops around the old town, on Zhongshan Road and in the lanes behind it, and along the seaward side of the island.

Tusun dong, worm jelly (土笋冻)

A grey-white, translucent jelly set from the sea worm known as sipunculid, dug from coastal mud flats, cleaned of sand and boiled hard for two or three minutes, then left to cool and set in small bowls. It is served with garlic, vinegar and soy sauce. When 8 American warships visited Xiamen for seven days in October 1908, the dish went out at the Qing court banquet.

Where: Speciality counters on the island's old streets, where the best raw material is still taken from the mudflats of Yundang Harbour towards Haicang.

Ginger duck (姜母鸭)

A duck stewed with old ginger and medicinal herbs, more broth than braise and closer to a sesame-oil chicken in method. Its origin is argued: it may descend from the salted duck of Quanzhou, and the Tong'an district was transferred to Xiamen only in 1958. The Taiwanese version added sesame oil, rice wine and a herbal formula in the 1980s, and from 1993 that flavour worked back on Xiamen.

Where: Old shops on Zhongshan Road and in the market streets behind it, and in the restaurants of the Jimei district.

Taro bun (芋包)

A steamed parcel with a taro wrapper folded around a filling, the wrapper's slippery, elastic texture being the point of the dish and the filling fragrant and crisp. It is a Minnan and northern Fujian preparation, made in Xiamen, Sanming and neighbouring counties for the Spring Festival, and comes in 3 regional variants including the Xiamen bun, the Yong'an bun and the Anxi bun.

Where: Cooked at home for the New Year in southern Fujian households, and sold from steamers in the old-town lanes during the festival weeks.

Where to shop

Zhongshan Road (中山路)

The main shopping street of Xiamen island, about 1 kilometre long and 15 metres wide, running from Lujiang Road in the west to the junction with Xinhua Road in the east, built in 1925 to link the Wanshi Hills with Gulangyu. The western section was started in June 1928 and finished in 1929, originally 893 metres long and 15.2 metres wide. The street is lined throughout with qilou arcades.

Where: Southwest Xiamen island, in Siming District, running east from the Lujiang waterfront.

Gulangyu Island (鼓浪屿)

A car-free island of 1.88 square kilometres off the west side of Xiamen, with a recorded population of 12,509 in 2023 and more than 10 million visitors a year. It was opened as an international settlement under land regulations approved in May 1902, and 13 countries established consulates there. Inscribed by UNESCO in July 2017, it is a national scenic spot and a 5A-rated attraction.

Where: Off the southwest coast of Xiamen island, reached by ferry from the Dongdu cruise terminal.

Huandao Road (环岛路)

The coastal ring road around Xiamen island, 43 kilometres long and carried as county road 401, built in three phases from 1991 at a total cost of nearly 3.8 billion yuan. It is four to six lanes wide and includes the Yanwu Bridge and the Lujiang Road facing Gulangyu, the section past the Hulishan Fort known as Huandao South Road, which passes Zengcuo'an and Huangcuo.

Where: Circling Xiamen island across Siming and Huli districts, with the seaward stretches on the south and east sides.

Jimei School Village (集美学村)

A school village across the water in the Jimei district, about 30 minutes by road from the centre of Xiamen island, founded in 1913 when Tan Kah Kee returned to his home town to establish the Jimei schools. The foundation included a kindergarten, a hospital, a library and a department for the promotion of education. It is grouped with Gulangyu among the city's principal sights.

Where: Jimei District, on the mainland shore north of Xiamen island.

Eating and shopping in Xiamen: practical notes

The shacha noodle shops keep different hours from the rest of the city, and many of the older counters are morning and midday businesses rather than dinner rooms. Order the toppings yourself rather than taking a fixed set: the broth is the same in every bowl, and the choice of liver, kidney, gizzard, squid or dried tofu is what makes the dish yours. Tusun dong is a cold dish, so it is worth looking for a counter that keeps it set in the chilled case.

For shopping, Zhongshan Road is the one to walk end to end, and its arcades give shelter from the sun and the rain in a way an open street cannot. Gulangyu rewards a slower pace: the lanes are pedestrian-only, the colonial consulates and villas are now shops and guesthouses, and the goods on sale are the island's own rather than the mainland's. The mainland trip to Jimei is a half-day undertaking and suits a morning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Xiamen's best-known noodle dish?

Shacha soup noodles: wheat noodles blanched and topped with offal, seafood or tofu, then flooded with a broth made from the shacha sauce descended from Malay satay. The dish was recognised among the first group of Chinese famous snacks in December 1997, and four shops were named its four great counters.

How is Xiamen's oyster omelette different from the Taiwanese version?

The Xiamen method sprinkles dry sweet potato starch over the oysters in the pan and lets the oysters' own water form the batter, adding garlic shoots at the end; the Taiwanese method adds the starch later as a slurry together with egg and vegetables. The local standard is that the finished cake must not stick into one solid mass.

Is tusun dong a sweet dish?

No. It is a savoury jelly set from a marine worm dug from tidal mud flats, boiled for two or three minutes and cooled until firm, then eaten with garlic, vinegar and soy sauce. It is counted as a Minnan delicacy and was served at the Qing court's banquet for the visiting American warships in October 1908.

Which shopping street should I spend the most time on?

Zhongshan Road, about 1 kilometre and 15 metres wide, lined with qilou arcade buildings along its whole length and protected as a historic district since 2006. It was voted the first of the city's thirty landmark places in a 2008 poll by a local evening newspaper.

What can I buy on Gulangyu that I cannot buy on the mainland?

The island's own goods, in lanes that are closed to cars and bicycles apart from small electric buggies. Its 1.88 square kilometres hold a recorded population of 12,509, more than 10 million visitors a year, and a cluster of former consulates turned into shops and guesthouses, including the Shuzhuang Garden estate built in 1913 over 20,000 square metres.
