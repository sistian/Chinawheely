Overview: Eating and Shopping in Zhengzhou

Zhengzhou is a rail city that grew into a food city: the old commercial streets around Erqi Square still pull the day's crowds, and the bowls on the counters are built around Henan staples. This page sets out seven dishes with traceable histories and four shopping streets with measured lengths, from the lamb broth of huimian to the 600-metre pedestrian run of Dehua Street.

What to eat

Henan braised noodles (烩面)

Henan's signature bowl is built around the broth: fresh lamb is soaked, simmered with whole spices until the meat falls apart, and hand-pulled dough is dropped into the same pot. The dish originates in Fangcheng County, Nanyang, and is eaten in 3 cities: Zhengzhou, Kaifeng and Luoyang. Bowls carry mutton, tofu, daylily buds, wood ear and glass noodles, with coriander, chilli oil and sweet garlic on the side.

Where: Noodle shops crowd the side streets off Dehua Street and the Erqi Square area; station-side counters near Zhengzhou Railway Station also serve it through the day.

Barrel chicken (桶子鸡)

A chicken preserved in brine rather than fried, named for the barrel-shaped pot it is packed in. Only old laying hens with a high fat layer are used, soaked whole with spices and pressed down to draw out the flavour. The technique is traced to the middle of the Ming dynasty, with some accounts placing it as early as the Northern Song. Kaifeng's Ma Yuxing shop dates itself to 1853.

Where: Delicatessen counters and the poultry stalls in the Erqi Square and Dehua Street food lanes, where a whole bird is cut to order in front of the buyer.

Daokou roast chicken (道口烧鸡)

From Daokou town in Hua County, northern Henan, this roast chicken ranks among the country's four famous railway-line chickens, beside Dezhou, Fuli and Goupangzi birds. Production began in 1661; in 1787 founder Zhang Bing was taught the eight-spice-and-old-broth method by a former imperial chef. The family published the recipe in 1955, and the bird is pulled apart by hand.

Where: Served at specialist roast-chicken counters and in Henan restaurants across the city, including branches in Jinshui District and around Erqi Square.

Grilled lamb skewers (烤羊肉串)

Lamb threaded on iron or wooden sticks and seared over charcoal. Northern seasoning stays spare — salt, cumin and chilli powder — so the meat keeps its own taste, while southern stalls brush on a sauce instead. The style reached the mainland through Xinjiang's kebab tradition and spread nationwide from the 1980s. Common cuts include plain lamb, tendon, lamb heart and grilled flatbread.

Where: Street-front grills line the evening pavements of Erqi Square, Dehua Street and the Jinshui District business streets, where stalls run past midnight in summer.

Peppery hu la tang (胡辣汤)

Henan's breakfast staple is a thick soup of beef or lamb, gluten, tofu skin, white pepper and chilli, thickened until it barely pours. Its origin is unrecorded; folk accounts trace it to a Song-dynasty palace broth, and the arrival of pepper in China sets an upper limit of 618, the year the Tang began. The town of Xiaoyao in Xihua county is the best-known production centre.

Where: Breakfast counters in the old city and the railway-station area, traditionally paired with crisp youtiao or flat buns and eaten before mid-morning.

Soup dumplings (灌汤包)

Dumplings with a cube of jelly sealed inside the filling, which melts into broth under the steamer. The form dates to the Northern Song capital of Bianjing, where they were sold by one of the city's 72 licensed restaurants. Kaifeng's First Floor dumpling house, founded in 1922, moved production to small bamboo baskets served in the steamer. Zhengzhou counters serve the same style with vinegar and ginger.

Where: Old-established dumpling houses around Erqi Square and the Dehua Street arcades, most of them open from early morning for the breakfast trade.

Clay-pot stewed chicken (黄焖鸡)

Chicken thigh, green pepper and shiitake simmered in a clay or stone pot until the sauce turns glossy and the meat slides off the bone. The dish is traced to Jinan in Shandong in 1927, and reached national prominence through the Yang Mingyu chain founded by Yang Xiaolu, now spread across more than ten thousand stores in China and abroad. In Zhengzhou it is ordered as a one-pot rice meal.

Where: Chain counters and independent kitchens throughout the city, from the Erqi Square area to the Jinshui District office blocks, usually sold with rice.

Where to shop

Dehua Street (德化街)

Zhengzhou's oldest shopping street runs 600 metres from Erqi Square down to Datong Road, 20 metres wide and paved in granite. It began as Huairen Street in 1905 and was renamed in 1916 after traders objected to the sound of the old name. Declared a pedestrian street on 28 December 2002, it was awarded the national designation Famous Commercial Street of China in 2006.

Where: Erqi District, running south from Erqi Square along the old city centre axis.

Erqi Square and memorial tower (二七广场)

The plaza where six streets converge holds the city's best-known landmark, a 63-metre reinforced concrete tower of 14 storeys, each side finished with green glazed tiles and six bell faces of 2.7 metres. The site was named Erqi Square in 1951 around a 21-metre wooden tower that collapsed in the summer of 1971. The concrete tower opened on 1 October 1971.

Where: Central Zhengzhou, at the meeting point of Renmin Road, Xidajie, Dehua Street, Zhengxing Street, Jiefang Road and Erqi Road.

Dennis department stores (丹尼斯百货)

Part of Taiwan's Tong Yu group and one of two Taiwanese department-store pilots selected by the State Council, Dennis opened its first Zhengzhou store on 16 November 1997. The group now runs more than 150 outlets across Henan, with hypermarkets in 12 prefecture-level cities. The David City complex at Erqi and Taikang roads opened in May 2015, and a seven-building park links its blocks by footbridges.

Where: Stores across Erqi and Jinshui Districts, with the largest cluster around Erqi Square and the Zhengdong New District business ring.

Zhengzhou Shang City ruins (郑州商城遗址)

Excavated from 1950 and listed as a national key cultural relic in 1961, the Shang capital's rectangular wall runs 6,960 metres and has 11 gaps, some of them probably gates. Palace foundations lie in the north-east corner, and two bronze hoards found outside the south wall included the Duling rectangular ding. The site occupies ground now enclosed by the modern city centre.

Where: Jinshui District, beside Dongdajie and the Zijing Mountain park area, a short walk north of the shopping streets.

Eating and shopping in Zhengzhou: practical notes

Eat breakfast early and eat it standing: hu la tang counters open around 05:30, and the lamb-broth huimian shops fill from mid-morning as the first trains unload. Dehua Street's 600 metres cover the classic combination in one walk — dumplings and soup at the northern end near Erqi Square, roast chicken and pepper soup further south. The bar-side stalls around the square keep grilling until well past midnight.

For shopping, treat Erqi Square as the anchor and choose one market outside it: Dennis carries the department-store range, while the Shang City ruins are a documented stop for the archaeology. Shop from late morning to early evening on weekdays, since weekend afternoon footfall on Dehua Street is heavy, and remember the granite paving makes the 600-metre run longer than it looks on a hot day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What food is Zhengzhou best known for?

Henan braised noodles, or huimian: hand-pulled dough dropped into lamb broth with daylily, wood ear and glass noodles. The dish originates in Fangcheng County, Nanyang, and Zhengzhou is its most famous home. The city also treats pepper soup, barrel chicken and Daokou roast chicken as breakfast and banquet staples of its own.

Which Zhengzhou street is the main shopping street?

Dehua Street, a 600-metre, 20-metre-wide pedestrian run in Erqi District between Erqi Square and Datong Road. It started as Huairen Street in 1905, was renamed in 1916, became a pedestrian street on 28 December 2002, and won the national Famous Commercial Street of China title in 2006.

Where should I see Erqi Square?

The 63-metre memorial tower of 14 storeys stands at the centre of the square, built between July and September 1971 and opened on 1 October. It replaced a 21-metre wooden hexagonal tower that stood there from 1953 until it collapsed in the summer of 1971. Six streets converge on the plaza.

Is hu la tang spicy?

It is peppery rather than fiery: a thick soup of beef or lamb, gluten, tofu skin, white pepper and chilli, thickened until it barely pours. It is Henan breakfast food and its origin is unrecorded, with folk accounts pointing to a Song-dynasty palace broth and a Tang-dynasty limit set by pepper's arrival in China.

Can I buy local products to take home?

Yes. Dennis department stores operate more than 150 outlets across Henan with hypermarkets in 12 prefecture-level cities, and the group runs its own food label through a Zhengzhou-based production company, so packaged Henan goods are sold alongside clothing, jewellery and household goods.
