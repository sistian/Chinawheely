Overview: Food & Shopping in Shenzhen

What to eat in Shenzhen and where the shopping actually happens.

The 3 dishes below are the ones Shenzhen is known for, each of them listed in the city's own encyclopaedia entry. Stopping at a market or a food street is easier with a car and a driver who knows where the stalls are, because the good ones are rarely on the main road.

Overview: Eating and Shopping in Shenzhen

Shenzhen's food map was written by migrants, and it keeps their receipts: a satay-derived sauce arriving through the diaspora, rice-roll machinery borrowed from Cantonese tea houses, a beef hotpot that insists on killing the animal the same day, and a braised pork-knuckle rice that became a national delivery category. Four districts carry the retail side of the story, from an electronics street of 1.45 square kilometres to a 1979 industrial zone covering 10.76 square kilometres.

What to eat

Satay-style broth noodle soup (沙茶面)

A noodle soup built on a fried, ground sauce derived from Southeast Asian satay paste, adapted in Fujian and Chaoshan cooking with peanut, shrimp, garlic and dried fish. The Xiamen version scalded noodles in a basket and dressed them with liver, kidney, squid and dried tofu, and was recognised among the first batch of Chinese famous snacks in December 1997, with 4 Xiamen shops holding the 'four absolutes' title.

Where: Served in Chaoshan and southern Fujian noodle kitchens across the Pearl River Delta, including Shenzhen, where the same sauce is spooned over rice noodles and beef offal.

Rice noodle rolls (肠粉)

Rolled rice noodles made from rice slurry and wheat starch, steamed into translucent sheets and rolled around diced beef, pork, sliced fish or shrimp, then cut and finished with dried shrimp and spring onion. 3 techniques are distinguished: sheets pulled over a cloth, hand-pulled sheets, and stone-ground slurry poured into drawers. Versions without filling are standard at Cantonese porridge houses.

Where: Common at Guangdong and Hong Kong style tea houses and congee shops in Shenzhen, where the filled versions are eaten as breakfast with soy milk.

Chaoshan beef hotpot (潮汕牛肉火锅)

A clear-broth hotpot from Chaoshan, developed out of a 'beef stove' that cooked meat over charcoal in satay-flavoured stock; the satay became the dipping sauce. The broth is now beef bone and ginger or plain water, and cattle are slaughtered on the day of eating. Hanging dragon needs 8 seconds in the pot, brisket fat 5 minutes, and only 35 percent of the animal suits direct cooking.

Where: Chaoshan-style hotpot rooms in Shenzhen's Futian and Nanshan dining districts, where the remaining 65 percent of the carcass arrives as hand-pounded beef balls.

Poached chicken with ginger scallion (白切鸡)

Chicken cooked without aromatics, plunged into water at about 90 degrees Celsius and lifted and lowered several times so the cavity heats evenly, then cooled under running water to set the skin and hung to dry. The usual dip is ginger, spring onion and oil. Hakka hosts serve head, tail, feet and wings together as the four gold points, left uneaten by the guest as respect.

Where: Found in Cantonese restaurants and roast-meat shops throughout Shenzhen, where it is ordered as a cold dish at the start of a banquet or with rice.

Double-skin milk custard (双皮奶)

A milk custard that forms 2 skins, said to have been invented in the 1890s by an elderly woman in Daliang, Shunde. Water buffalo milk is boiled, poured into a bowl and left to form a skin; the milk is poured off and returned over the skin to steam 15 minutes. The house names to watch are Yishun in Macau, Nanxin in Guangzhou, and Renxin in Shunde.

Where: Sold at Shunde-style sweet shops and dessert counters in Shenzhen, where water buffalo rather than cow milk is the marker of the authentic version.

Oyster pancake (蚝烙)

Oysters bound with starch and egg and fried, served with a sour-spicy sauce. One account traces it to Fujian in the 16th century, when sweet potato was brought back from Manila and a 1594 drought made the tubers and local oysters a staple; by the mid-17th century the dish had spread along the southern coast into Southeast Asia. Fujian and Kinmen fry sweet potato flour with the oysters.

Where: Best versions in Shenzhen come from the Shajing area of Bao'an District, whose oysters are sold grilled with garlic or raw with lemon along the oyster quarter there.

Braised pork knuckle rice (隆江猪脚饭)

Rice topped with a knuckle braised in brine and cut into 4 sections, the head ring, the broken wheel, the four gold points and the trotter tail, with pickled greens, rolled pork, braised egg and dried tofu on the side. It originates in Longjiang town, Huilai county, where porters ate cheap and filling knuckle rice. Delivery data counted more than 70 million orders in 2021.

Where: Sold at counters across Shenzhen and the rest of the Pearl River Delta, where the standard order is one knuckle rice with a small dish of greens.

Where to shop

Huaqiangbei electronics district (华强北)

The commercial heart of the world's electronics trade, a district running about 930 metres north to south and 1,560 metres east to west over roughly 1.45 square kilometres in Futian District, holding around 38,000 businesses as of 2020. It was designated a national 'safe shopping street' in 2000, published the Huaqiangbei price index from October 2007, and was pedestrianised from February 2013, with the walkable stretch opening in January 2017.

Where: Futian District, between Shennan Road and Hongli Road, around SEG Plaza and the Huaqiang Road metro station.

Chung Ying Street border market (中英街)

A short street on the Hong Kong boundary at Shatoujiao, jointly administered by Hong Kong and Shenzhen, with a boundary stone standing in the middle of the carriageway. It formed after the 1898 Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory and the survey of 16 to 18 March 1899, when the riverbed still served as the line. A warning day has been marked each 18 March since 2002.

Where: Yantian District, at the Shatoujiao border crossing between Shenzhen and Hong Kong's Sha Tau Kok.

Nantou Ancient City (南头古城)

A walled town of about 70,000 square metres in Nanshan District that served in turn as the seat of Dongguan County and the Dongguan garrison town, at one point administering parts of modern Hong Kong, Shenzhen, Dongguan, Zhongshan and Zhuhai. The surviving fortification dates from the 27th year of the Hongwu era, 1394, with a yellow-clay base on an irregular rectangle 680 metres by 500 metres long.

Where: Nanshan District, north of Shennan Avenue at the junction with Nanxin Road, beside Zhongshan Park.

Shekou Industrial Zone (蛇口工业区)

A 10.76-square-kilometre zone on the Shekou peninsula, approved by the State Council on 31 January 1979 and built by the China Merchants Group under Yuan Geng, and now the anchor of the Qianhai free-trade area. Ground was broken on 8 July 1979 for the mainland's first export processing zone, and the district now holds more than 1,000 enterprises. The Shenzhen Bay Highway Bridge opened on 1 July 2007.

Where: Nanshan District, at the southern tip of the district facing Yuen Long across Shenzhen Bay.

Eating and shopping in Shenzhen: practical notes

Build the day around districts rather than around meals, because in Shenzhen the distance is the deciding factor: Bao'an's Shajing oyster quarter in the far north-west, the Futian electronics district in the centre and the Shekou zone at the southern tip are a long way apart, and the metro lines 1 and 2 connect them in a single pass. Where a dish is documented, the timing matters as much as the address: Chaoshan hotpot rooms operate in the evening, and the Shajing stalls work through the afternoon, so an oyster lunch and a beef hotpot dinner in two different districts fits the city's geography rather than fighting it.

Two rules keep the shopping honest. In Huaqiangbei, compare prices between stalls and start the negotiation at about half the quoted figure, since bargaining is expected and the trade includes counterfeit goods as well as genuine stock; the area was designated a safe-shopping street in 2000 and the SEG Plaza tower at its centre is the tallest steel-pipe concrete structure in the world. On Chung Ying Street, remember that both sides require permits: Hong Kong residents need a closed-area pass and mainland residents need a border special-zone pass, and the 2020 inspection hall on the Shenzhen side added 20 smart channels to ease the queues.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the essential dishes to eat in Shenzhen?

The Chaoshan beef hotpot is the city's most demanding order: yellow cattle slaughtered the same day, a clear broth of beef bone and ginger, and cuts timed individually, with hanging dragon blanched for 8 seconds and brisket fat for 5 minutes, and about 35 percent of the carcass suitable for the pot. Alongside it, Chaoshan-style satay noodle soup, Cantonese poached chicken with ginger and scallion, and Shunde double-skin milk custard are the three that carry a documented regional history into the city.

Is Huaqiangbei still worth visiting for shopping?

It remains the largest electronics market in the world by trade volume, spread over about 1.45 square kilometres with roughly 38,000 businesses registered in 2020, and it holds the SEG Plaza tower, the world's tallest steel-pipe concrete structure. The street was pedestrianised from February 2013 and opened as a walk zone in January 2017, it has published the Huaqiangbei price index since October 2007, and it was named China's first electronics street in 2008. Compare prices between stalls and bargain from about half the asking price.

Which historical sites in Shenzhen can be walked?

Nantou Ancient City covers about 70,000 square metres and keeps the remains of the Ming garrison town built in 1394, with an irregular base 680 metres long and 500 metres wide and a south gate carrying a stone plaque with two seal-script characters. It has been a provincial protected relic since July 2002. The Shekou Industrial Zone, approved on 31 January 1979 and broken ground on 8 July that year, is the other walkable site, and the first export processing zone in the mainland.

Where can I eat oysters in Shenzhen?

Shajing in the far north-west of Bao'an District is the city's oyster quarter, and the oysters are generally served grilled with garlic or raw with lemon. The dish itself, the oyster pancake, is documented as a Fujian specialty that spread along the southern coast and into Southeast Asia by the middle of the seventeenth century, and its Fujian versions fry sweet potato flour directly with the oysters.

How long does it take to get between Shenzhen's shopping districts?

The three main retail areas sit far apart: Huaqiangbei is in central Futian, Nantou Ancient City is in southern Nanshan near Shennan Avenue, and the Shekou zone occupies the southern tip of the peninsula facing Hong Kong. The Chung Ying Street border market is a separate trip north-east in Yantian. Metro lines 1 and 2 link the Futian and Nanshan ends, and a border permit is needed for the Chung Ying Street crossing on both sides.
