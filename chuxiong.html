Name Origin

The name Chuxiong was created in the Ming dynasty: in 1382 the seat was renamed from Weichu (威楚) to Chuxiong, taking the first character of Chuxiong (楚雄府) and the second of Nanxiong (南雄府). Before that the area was the seat of the Cuan (爨) clan, whose ancient walled city and culture are still visible in the prefecture's museums and villages.

Education & Universities

Higher education is provided by Chuxiong Normal University, Chuxiong Vocational and Technical College and Yunnan University of Finance and Economics' Chuxiong campus, with a distinctive Yi-language and teacher-training focus.

Getting Around

Getting around Chuxiong is by road and rail. The Guangda railway (广大铁路) links the prefecture seat to Kunming, and the G56 Hangzhou–Ruili expressway connects the county towns of Lufeng, Lufeng county seat, Yuanmou, Wuding, Lunan, Nanxiong, Zheng'an and Nangua. Chuxiong Airport has scheduled flights to Kunming.

Natural Resources & Environment

The prefecture is a highland of 104 basins ringed by the Wumeng range in the east, the Ailao range in the south-west and the Baicaoling in the north-west, with the Jinsha and Yuan rivers cutting through. Mean temperatures run from 14.8 to 21.9 °C, and 800–1,000 mm of rain falls mostly between July and October. It is one of China's richest mining areas — copper, iron, arsenic, rock salt, mirabilite and gypsum — and its wild medicinal plants and edible fungi are famous throughout the country.

Food & Shopping in Chuxiong

What to eat and what to bring home from Chuxiong

🍽️ Must-Try Chuxiong Cuisine

Chuxiong is famous above all for its wild edible fungi (野生菌) — matsutake, boletes and porcini gathered in the forests from July to September — and for Yi cookery: fire-roasted pork, buckwheat cakes, millet porridge and home-pressed oil. The prefecture is also one of China's best sources of medicinal plants.

🛍️ Shopping in Chuxiong

Buy dried wild mushrooms, matsutake and Yi embroidery direct from the county-town markets; Chuxiong city and Lufeng also have good shops for Dongba-style craft, bark cloth and ceramics.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Tea is the everyday drink across the prefecture, and in the Yi villages it is served in the old way: a bowl shared round the fire while the host pours. In the Muslim county towns you will also find the local tradition of sweet rose tea and powdered doucha.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Chuxiong

The living traditions of Chuxiong — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The Yi Ten-Month Solar Calendar (十月太阳历) — the only surviving calendar of its kind — and the Torch Festival (火把节) are the best known of the Yi traditions here, alongside the Cuan culture of the 7th century and the Yi village architecture of the Ailao mountains. Craft traditions include black-dye batik, embroidery, weaving and the making of wooden farm tools.

Best Time to Visit Chuxiong

October to April for mild weather and clear mountain light; July to September for the wild mushroom season, with the rainy weather that comes with it.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — dry, warm and clear, and the best weeks for the red earth forest and the old towns. (daytime 22.4–26.4°C, nights around 7°C; about 34 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the rains arrive and the wild mushrooms follow; green countryside but the highest chance of afternoon storms. (daytime 25.5–26.2°C, nights around 17.1°C; about 166 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–November — the best all-round season: clear skies, harvest festivals and mushroom drying season. (daytime 18.8–24.1°C, nights around 7.4°C; about 77 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · December–February — dry and mild in the basins, cold in the highlands, and the quietest time at the festivals. (daytime 16.4–18.9°C, nights around 1.7°C; about 11 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Chuxiong

What to drink in Chuxiong, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Chuxiong

Tea is the everyday drink across the prefecture, and in the Yi villages it is served in the old way: a bowl shared round the fire while the host pours. In the Muslim county towns you will also find the local tradition of sweet rose tea and powdered doucha.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Chuxiong

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Chuxiong

🍵 Tea Houses of Chuxiong

Tea culture runs through the day in Chuxiong: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
