Overview: Eating and Shopping in Hangzhou

Hangzhou's dishes carry dates the way other cities carry slogans: vinegared fish tied to Song-dynasty legends, dongpo pork first named in Ming texts after the poet who devised it, and pian'erchuan noodles credited to a shop founded in 1867. This page lists five foods with sourced histories and three shopping streets measured in meters and shop counts.

What to eat

West Lake vinegared fish (西湖醋鱼)

The most-ordered fish dish among Hangzhou visitors: whole river fish in a sweet-sour vinegar glaze, associated by legend with a Song-era sister-in-law who cooked it for her departing brother-in-law — hence the alternate name 'shao-sao chuan-zhen'. Preparation is exacting and accounts differ on flavor between restaurants, so results vary by kitchen. The reference version is served at the Gushan-side restaurant by West Lake.

Where: Louwailou at Solitary Hill on West Lake is the documented benchmark; most lakeside restaurants in the Shangcheng and Xihu districts list a version.

Dongpo pork (东坡肉)

Cubes of half-fat, half-lean pork roughly two inches on a side, slow-braised with soy sauce, rock sugar and Shaoxing wine, and credited to the poet Su Dongpo's improvement of an older method. A near-identical recipe appears in the Yuan-dynasty 'Yunlintang' dietary manual, the name itself first appears in Ming writings, and sea trade carried the dish to Ryukyu and Japan, where kakuni and raftē descend from it.

Where: Standard on Hangzhou menus; the lakeside restaurant cluster near Gushan and the old-brand houses on Renhe Road serve it by the cube or the pot.

Longjing shrimp (龙井虾仁)

A Hangzhou signature pairing river shrimp with Longjing tea leaves, the tea contributing fragrance to the shrimp's freshness in a single quick stir-fry. The dish is built on the city's own tea, grown in the hills west of the lake, and is listed among the representative dishes of Zhejiang cuisine. Served in small portions suited to a banquet sequence rather than a solo meal.

Where: Louwailou at Solitary Hill is the named exponent; Longjing-tea-themed restaurants in the Meijiawu and Longjing village area also plate it.

Pian'erchuan noodles (片儿川)

A soup noodle created by the Kuiyuan Guan noodle house, with a topping of pickled potherb mustard, bamboo-shoot slices and lean pork — the pairing of pickle and shoot supplies the bowl's stated freshness. Alkaline 'chao' noodles are boiled to eighty percent, then finished in the topping's broth. The dish appeared nationally in May 2015 on the second season of 'A Bite of China'.

Where: Kuiyuan Guan, founded 1867, runs its flagship at 154 Jiefang Road and two further branches; pian'erchuan is also common at noodle counters citywide.

Dingsheng cake (定胜糕)

A Jiangnan steamed rice cake of coarse glutinous and coarse japonica rice, made in varieties including lard and sweet-bean-paste, first called 'dingsun cake' for its tenon-like shape before dialect homophony turned the name to 'certain victory'. Locals steam it for birthdays, house-building and moves; Southern Song records tell of cakes stamped 'dingsheng' baked for Han Shizhong's army.

Where: Snack stalls along Hefang Street and the Southern Song Imperial Street sell it fresh through the day; cake shops in the older western lanes bake to order.

Where to shop

Hefang Street (河坊街)

The old commercial strip at the foot of Wu Hill, running east from Jiangcheng Road to Nanshan Road, with its heritage section between Zhonghe Middle Road and Yan'an South Road. Together with Middle Zhongshan Road it formed the 'Four Corners', the city's chief trading block from Song times into the early Republic. Roughly one hundred old-brand and specialty shops now line it inside a national 4A-rated historic area, trading mainly 09:00–21:00.

Where: Shangcheng District at Wu Hill's foot; the west end sits a short walk from West Lake's Liulang Wenying shore.

Southern Song Imperial Street (南宋御街)

The pedestrianized stretch of Middle Zhongshan Road from West Lake Avenue south to Hefang Street, laid out on an imperial processional road that dated to the Wuyue kingdom and was rebuilt and extended under the Southern Song, when commoners were barred from it — hence 'forbidden street'. Historical sources count about 150 named shops along it in seven trades, from daily goods and restaurants to medicine, silver and entertainment. Republican-era renaming made the whole axis Zhongshan Road.

Where: Shangcheng District, immediately north of Hefang Street; Metro Line 1's Ding'an Road station serves the middle of the street.

Wulin Road (武林路)

A 1,650-meter street from Qingchun Road north to Stadium Road, recorded in the Southern Song as one of the city's three principal north-south roads, renamed Changzheng Road in 1956 and restored to Wulin Road in 1981. In the late 1980s it ranked in the country's first batch of commercial streets alongside Shanghai's Huaihai Road and Chengdu's Chunxi Road, and it has since specialized in women's fashion retail.

Where: Gongshu District, west side of the old city; near Wulin Square and its metro interchange.

Eating and shopping in Hangzhou: practical notes

Group the restaurant list by neighborhood: Gushan by West Lake holds the benchmark vinegared fish and Longjing shrimp kitchens; 154 Jiefang Road keeps Kuiyuan Guan's 1867 flagship for pian'erchuan, with two branches covering the west and new-town sides; Renhe Road's 1913-founded Zhiweiguan, a 7,000-square-meter flagship seating over 2,000, works as a fallback for the full snack set. Dongpo pork travels well across these kitchens, unlike the fish, which genuinely varies by house.

Shopping splits along the same axis: Hefang Street's hundred-plus old-brand shops — Hu Qingyutang pharmacy, Zhang Xiaoquan scissors, Wanlong ham — cover souvenirs between 09:00 and 21:00, and the Imperial Street section north of it adds the Southern Song's documented 150-shop commercial spine. Wulin Road, 1,650 meters and a 1980s first-batch commercial street, is the modern-fashion alternative, best visited in the afternoon.

Frequently Asked Questions

What food is Hangzhou most famous for?

West Lake vinegared fish, dongpo pork and Longjing shrimp form the banquet trio, followed by pian'erchuan noodles from Kuiyuan Guan, founded 1867, and dingsheng cake, whose 'certain victory' stamp dates to cakes baked for Han Shizhong's Southern Song army. Dongpo pork has the clearest paper trail, named in Ming texts and exported to Japan and Ryukyu centuries ago.

Where should I eat West Lake vinegared fish?

Louwailou at Solitary Hill is the documented reference kitchen. Because the dish is technically demanding, quality varies noticeably between restaurants — treat any lakeside menu listing as a separate audition rather than a fixed standard. Ordering it at the same house where you take Longjing shrimp keeps one kitchen accountable for both.

What is pian'erchuan and where did it come from?

A soup noodle topped with pickled mustard, bamboo-shoot slices and lean pork, first made by Kuiyuan Guan, a noodle house founded in 1867 by an Anhui native and later run by Ningbo owners. The dish reached a national audience on the second season of 'A Bite of China' in May 2015. The Jiefang Road flagship remains the point of reference.

Which street is better for souvenirs, Hefang or Wulin Road?

Hefang Street, inside a national 4A historic area with about one hundred old-brand shops — Hu Qingyutang, Zhang Xiaoquan, Wanlong — is the souvenir street, open roughly 09:00–21:00. Wulin Road, 1,650 meters long and a 1980s first-batch commercial street, specializes in women's fashion retail. They serve different purposes and lie fifteen minutes apart by metro.

Can I walk Hefang Street and the Imperial Street together?

Yes — the Imperial Street is Middle Zhongshan Road running north from Hefang Street to West Lake Avenue, so the two form one continuous heritage axis. The Southern Song 'forbidden street' carried about 150 documented shops in seven trades; today's pedestrian treatment covers the same line. Two hours cover both comfortably at browsing pace.
