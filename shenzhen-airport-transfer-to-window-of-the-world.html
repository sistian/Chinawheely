Window of the World is a Shenzhen theme park holding about 130 reproductions of world landmarks, opened on 18 June 1994 after construction began in July 1991. Its Global Square carries a one-third-scale Eiffel Tower replica standing 108 metres tall, and the park received national 5A-level attraction status in 2007. More than 3.25 million visitors a year pass through, and from Bao'an Airport the trip is a single eastward run across the city's western districts.

The run east passes through Bao'an and Nanshan districts on the Guangshen corridor; the last section approaches the park from the Qianhai side, and evening flows toward the Futui corridor slow the final kilometres.

Why transfer to Window of the World?

The park was built so that mainland visitors could see world landmarks at a time when overseas travel was out of reach for most, following the concept of Splendid China, its sister park of domestic replicas elsewhere in Shenzhen. The replica list spans continents and eras: Angkor Wat, Christ the Redeemer, the pyramids of Giza, the Sydney Opera House and the Taj Mahal, plus a Manhattan skyline and the Niagara Falls, where the Grand Canyon and Niagara water rides were added to what had begun as a static attraction.

The park has kept adding since. An indoor skiing venue representing the Alps opened in July 2000 as China's first large-scale indoor ski slope, and the dinosaur-themed Jurassic Water World followed in June 2024. The 5A designation from 2007 puts it in the top official grade of Chinese tourist attractions, and the 3.25 million annual visitors make it one of the city's most reliable draws, located on Metro Line 1 with the park's name on the station.

What to do at Window of the World once you arrive

The Global Square is the centre of the layout, and the 108-metre Eiffel Tower replica at its heart is climbable, with viewing levels that survey the whole replica field — the practical way to plan which of the 130 landmarks to walk to. The replicas cluster by continent, so the pyramids of Giza, Angkor Wat and the Taj Mahal sit in one arc while the Sydney Opera House and Manhattan skyline hold another.

The newer additions sit deeper in the park. The Alps indoor ski venue, open since July 2000, rents equipment on site and holds a snow slope inside a city that averages summer heat for most of the year; the Niagara Falls and Grand Canyon water rides adjoin it. Jurassic Water World, opened June 2024, carries the dinosaur theme into a full water park with a separate entrance and ticket, so visitors combining it with the main replicas should budget a full day rather than an afternoon.

  • The Eiffel Tower replica's viewing platform gives the overview of all 130 landmarks before you commit to a route
  • The indoor ski venue and the water park are ticketed separately from the main park
  • Window of the World station on Metro Line 1 serves the entrance directly
  • Allow at least half a day for the replica field alone, more with the ski slope or Jurassic Water World

Arriving at Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport

Bao'an handled 52.7 million passengers in 2023 by Civil Aviation Administration figures, ranking among China's busiest. The airport occupies 10.8 square kilometres with a first runway of 3,400 metres and 53 apron parking spaces; a second runway opened in 2011, the Fuksas-designed Terminal 3 in 2013, and a third runway entered service on 29 November 2025, with a satellite hall of 22 million annual capacity connected to the main terminal.

Rail from the airport runs on Metro Line 11, the express line with 120 km/h operation, transferring at Qianhai-side stations toward Line 1 for Window of the World. The Shenzhen Metro opened on 28 December 2004 with Line 1 services running to Shijie Zhi Chuang — now Window of the World — as one of its original termini. A private pickup with flight tracking spares a late arrival the transfer with luggage, particularly after the evening banks of flights land together.

What the transfer costs

The airport sits at the far western edge of Shenzhen and the park lies mid-city, so the metered fare is a genuine cross-town run; Shenzhen taxis charge on the meter with a fuel surcharge, and the Bao'an-to-Nanshan trip lands in a middling band between the short Futui-centre hops and the long eastern-district runs. Private sedan transfers are typically quoted in the 120 to 220 yuan range depending on vehicle class and hour, covering tolls and arrivals-hall waiting.

Groups and families more often take the seven-seat class, which the park's plaza entrance handles easily — the drop-off loop sits directly opposite the Global Square approach. Compare against Line 11 plus the Line 1 connection: cheap and quick at off-peak times, but with two changes of platform and stairs, which is where luggage decides the argument for the car.

Have the driver aim for the park's plaza entrance off Shennan Avenue rather than the metro station kerb, since the station side fills with queueing taxis while the plaza loop clears quickly at opening and closing hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

When did Window of the World open?

The park opened on 18 June 1994. Construction had begun in July 1991, and the Eiffel Tower replica section was completed by 1992 before the rest of the site followed. It was designated a national 5A-level tourist attraction in 2007 and now receives more than 3.25 million visitors annually.

How do I get from Bao'an Airport to Window of the World?

Metro Line 11 runs from the airport into the city at up to 120 km/h; transfer toward Line 1, which stops at Window of the World station directly at the park entrance. A car covers the cross-town distance in one seat — the park sits east of the airport in the Nanshan area, with the drive passing through Bao'an on the Guangshen corridor.

How tall is the Eiffel Tower replica at Window of the World?

The replica at the Global Square is built at one-third scale and stands 108 metres — 354 feet — tall. It is the vertical landmark of the park and its viewing levels double as the orientation point for the roughly 130 other reproductions spread across the site.

Is there skiing at Window of the World?

Yes. The indoor venue themed on the Alps opened in July 2000 and was China's first large-scale indoor skiing facility. Equipment rental is available on site, and the venue is ticketed separately from the main park entry, so check combined passes if you plan to ski and tour the replicas on the same day.

How much time should I allow for a visit?

Half a day covers the main replica field from the Global Square outward. Add the Alps ski venue, the Niagara Falls and Grand Canyon water rides, or the Jurassic Water World water park opened in June 2024, and the visit stretches to a full day — the water park in particular operates as a separate-gated attraction.
