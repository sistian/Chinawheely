Overview: Day Trips from Dalian

Dalian is built on two peninsulas, and a day trip here is usually a decision about which one. To the south the coast road runs 45 kilometres to Lüshun, the old naval town at the tip of the Liaodong peninsula with its ice-free harbour and its war museums. To the north the same coast reaches the resort strip at Jinshitan, where the sea cliffs are called a stone forest and the whole district is a national geopark. Between them the city itself has a working harbour that has been in use since 1898, a tram system that has run without interruption since 1909, and a black hill with a Goguryeo wall on it.

Lüshun and the Southern Tip of the Peninsula (旅顺)

Lüshun district covers 506 square kilometres at the southern end of the Liaodong peninsula, with the Yellow Sea to the east and the Bohai to the west, 45 kilometres from central Dalian, and it carries three national designations at once: a national scenic area, a national nature reserve and a national forest park. The harbour of Lüshun is the ice-free port on the strait between the two peninsulas, which is why it was contested from the late Qing onwards, and the area was successively held by Qing China, Russia, Japan and the Soviet Union. In the war of 1894 to 1895 the Japanese took the port, and the port was rebuilt afterwards under foreign investment and modern planning, which is where the street architecture of the town comes from. The new Lüshun port has become the shipping channel between the Liaodong and Shandong peninsulas.

How the day runs: Leave Dalian at 08:00 for the 45-kilometre run and arrive in Lüshun by 09:15. Take the coast road in rather than the highway, since the viewpoints along it are the point of the drive. Spend the middle of the day on the war history sites and the old town, and finish with the harbour and the naval museum area, which look best in the late afternoon light. Leave at 16:00 for the return, which is slower than the outward run, and be back in the city around 17:30.

45 km from Dalian · about 75 min by car from Dalian · full-day trip · best in May to October for the coastal road; the museums are open all year and are the better answer in winter.

  • The museum sites are the strongest part of the visit and they are indoors, so the trip works in every season.
  • The coast road has numerous viewpoints; the run back in the late afternoon light is worth more than the run out.
  • Lüshun is a working port town as well as a memorial one, and the two sit side by side, which the photographs show.

Jinshitan and the Sea Cliffs (金石滩)

The Jinshitan national tourism resort covers 62 square kilometres of land and 58 square kilometres of sea in the north of the Liaodong peninsula, and holds the status of a national key scenic area, a national tourism resort, a national 5A site and a national geopark; CNN included it among the forty most beautiful sights in China. The landform is what makes it: marine erosional cliffs and sedimentary rock formed over hundreds of millions of years, a formation known locally as the stone forest on the sea, which gave the district its name after it had been called Manjatan. Development began in 1984 under the county party secretary Bo Xilai, the site became a county-level nature reserve in March 1986 and a municipal one in November 1987, and in August 1988 the State Council approved it as the second batch of national key scenic areas. Its sections include the Golden Coast, the Jinshi Garden, the coastal geopark, a wax museum and the Discovery Kingdom theme park.

How the day runs: Drive out at 08:30 for the 50 kilometres and reach the resort by 09:40. The cliffs and the coastal geopark are the subject and work best early, before the beach fills; allow two hours with the walking paths and the viewpoints. Lunch on the Golden Coast, then pick one of the indoor attractions for the hottest part of the day, either the wax museum or the human anatomy museum, both of which are beside the Jinshitan metro station. Leave by 16:00 to be back in the city before dark.

50 km from Dalian · about 70 min by car from Dalian · full-day trip · best in June to September for the beach and the boat trips; May and October for the cliff colours without the crowds.

  • The geopark paths are the reason to come; the beach is interchangeable with any other and the cliffs are not.
  • The indoor attractions sit together on the north side of the metro station, which makes the hot hours easy to fill.
  • The stone forest is best seen at low tide, so check the tide table before choosing the hour.

Laohutan and the Polar Aquarium (老虎滩)

The park began as a walking garden in 1959 on the eastern shore and grew into a municipal seaside park; in 1980 a floating pier was built in the bay to the east and sea excursions began. The neighbouring Xiuyue Villa park was laid out in 1984 over 539.4 hectares, adjoining the original garden on one side and the Fujiazhuang park on the other, and the two were joined in 1989. The site was renamed Dalian Hutang Paradise in 1991 and opened in May 1992, and on 8 August 2002 it became the Dalian Laohutan Ocean Park, developed with foreign investment into what was then the largest polar and marine animal aquarium in the world. It is now a national 5A scenic area, and the sea excursion from the old pier remains the part of it that is outdoors.

How the day runs: Allow a half-day and go in the morning, when the animal buildings are quietest. The polar aquarium is the core, and the sea lion and the performance halls follow it. If the weather is good, add the pier and the boat trip, which run from the eastern side of the park; if it is not, the aquarium alone fills the time. The Xiuyue Villa gardens on the western side are a short drive and make a quieter second half of the day.

10 km from Dalian · about 30 min by car from Dalian · half-day trip · best in All year; it is the standard wet-weather and hot-weather answer within the city.

  • The polar hall and the boat pier are on opposite sides of the park, so allow fifteen minutes between them.
  • The aquarium was the world's largest polar facility when it opened in 2002, which is the historical framing worth knowing before you visit.
  • Winter is the season for the coastal walk around the park as well as the tanks.

Dahei Mountain and the Goguryeo Wall (大黑山)

Dahei Shan, also called the Dahe Shan or the Great Monk Mountain, is the highest peak of the southern Liaodong peninsula at 663 metres, 25 kilometres from the centre of Dalian and 5 kilometres east of Jinzhou, and it is named for the black rock the mountain is made of. On the summit stands the city of Pisha, a defensive wall of the Goguryeo kingdom that runs for 5 kilometres at a height of 3 to 5 metres, which makes it one of the more substantial early fortifications on the peninsula. Around it are the Guanyin Pavilion, the Chaoyang Temple, the Xiangshui Temple and the Tang King Hall, and the climb takes about an hour and a half on the stepped path.

How the day runs: Drive out at 09:00 and be at the trailhead by 09:45, because the mountain is at its best in the first hours of light. The stepped path to the summit takes about 90 minutes, with the old wall section at the top, and the view takes in the whole southern peninsula and the sea on both sides. Descend by 13:00, have lunch in Jinzhou town, and be back in the city by 15:00. This is the shortest drive of the six and the only one that counts as a walk rather than a visit.

25 km from Dalian · about 45 min by car from Dalian · half-day trip · best in April to June for the blossom and the clear views; September and October for the colours on the granite.

  • The wall is the subject, and it sits right at the summit; the summit itself is only a short section beyond the car park.
  • The path is stepped and uneven, so the return descent is slower than the climb.
  • The view covers two seas, which is unusual for a 663-metre hill this close to a city.

The Harbour and the Waterfront (大连港与滨海路)

Dalian harbour was built in 1898, at the end of the Qing, and it is a natural ice-free port on the strait between the Liaodong and Shandong peninsulas. It works now with seven specialist terminals and 48 berths to a maximum depth of 17.5 metres, 82 modern specialist berths of which 57 take vessels of 10,000 tonnes and over, 149 kilometres of port rail, and harbour waters of more than 310 square kilometres. Cargo throughput rose from 32 million tonnes in 1980 to more than 320 million tonnes in 2010, and the port now trades with more than 160 countries through over 300 ports, with 81 container lines. The waterfront along the south shore is where the city meets that working infrastructure, and it is the shortest trip of the six.

How the day runs: This works as a walk rather than an excursion. Start at the viewing terrace above the harbour in the middle of the afternoon, look down over the container quays and the old shore, then walk the waterfront south to the old pier and the fishing boats, which come in late in the afternoon. Combine it with the tram on Zhongshan Road for the street architecture of the old concession. Two hours is enough for the harbour itself, three with the tram.

4 km from Dalian · about 20 min by car from Dalian · half-day trip · best in All year; the winter light over the harbour is as good as the summer evening.

  • The working quays and the old stone shore are two hundred metres apart and photograph very differently; walk between them.
  • The fishing boats come in through the afternoon, which is the hour to shoot them.
  • The harbour is the reason for the city's position, and the 1898 date explains most of the street plan behind the waterfront.

The Trams and Zhongshan Road (大连有轨电车与中山路)

Dalian has one of the few tram systems in China that has never been interrupted, and it began on 25 September 1909, when the South Manchuria Railway transport department opened a trial line from the electric amusement park, the former zoo and today's centre park, along the Taisho street, today's Zhongshan and Renmin roads, to the great pier, today's harbour bridge, in a single run of 2.45 kilometres. The first fleet was 30 type 11 cars with bodies built by Preston in the United States, chassis by Montagu Gibson in Britain and electrical equipment by AEG in Germany, timber double-deck cars rated for 72 seated and 145 loaded, nicknamed the great American wooden cage. By 1945 there were 11 lines, three operating depots and two workshops. Most of the network was cut in the 1970s as the city grew, and by the end of the century only lines 201, 202 and 203 survived, alongside the modern cars built in the 1990s and 2000s.

How the day runs: Ride line 201 or 202 rather than photographing them from a platform, because the point is the wooden car interior and the sound of the rails. Board at the centre park end of the original 1909 route and ride to the harbour bridge, which is the first line in its entirety and takes about fifteen minutes. Allow an hour for the ride and the stops, and then walk Zhongshan Road for the concession-era blocks, or go out to the end of line 203 for the outer terminus and the overhead wires. In winter the cars are heated and the windows fog, which changes what a photographer can do inside.

3 km from Dalian · about 15 min by car from Dalian · half-day trip · best in All year; the surviving 1930s cars run in all seasons.

  • The 1930s cars are the ones to shoot; the modern fleet is a different vehicle and should not be confused with it.
  • The original 2.45-kilometre route between the zoo and the pier is still recognisable, which makes it the best single ride.
  • Interior light is best on the platform end of the car, where the doors open and the sun comes in across the boards.

Planning day trips from Dalian

Distance here means something different from inland cities, because the coast road runs both ways from the centre and the two directions do not connect. Lüshun is 45 kilometres south and Jinshitan 50 kilometres north, and a day that tries to do both ends up spending four hours in a car. The sensible rule is to pick a direction in the morning and stay in it, and to use the two close trips, Laohutan and the harbour, for the days when the weather or the schedule is against a longer run.

The second point is that Dalian's seasons are maritime rather than continental. The same latitude as Beijing carries winters with the port open and the trams running, which is why the museum and tram trips are not merely a wet-weather substitute here but a genuine part of the year. The summer crowding at the beach resorts is a fact of the calendar, and the coastal cliffs and the working harbour are at their best in the shoulder months when the resorts are empty.

Planning a day trip from Dalian

The Trams and Zhongshan Road, Jinshitan and the Sea Cliffs and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Dalian day trip route — The Trams and Zhongshan Road sits closest at 3 km, roughly 15 minutes by car, while Jinshitan and the Sea Cliffs reaches furthest at 50 km, about 70 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Lüshun and the Southern Tip of the Peninsula works best May to October for the coastal road; the museums are open all year and are the better answer in winter., while Jinshitan and the Sea Cliffs suits June to September for the beach and the boat trips; May and October for the cliff colours without the crowds. — in Dalian that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

Laohutan and the Polar Aquarium, Dahei Mountain and the Goguryeo Wall, The Harbour and the Waterfront each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Dalian itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which day trip is the essential one?

Lüshun, 45 kilometres south. The district covers 506 square kilometres at the southern tip of the peninsula with a national scenic area, nature reserve and forest park designation, and it holds the ice-free harbour that has been contested since the late Qing. It was built up after 1895 under foreign investment and modern planning, which is where the colonial street plan and the memorial museums both come from, and it works as a full day with a museum in the middle of it.

What makes Jinshitan different from an ordinary beach resort?

The geology. The marine erosional cliffs and sedimentary rock of the area, formed over hundreds of millions of years and known as the stone forest on the sea, gave the district its name. It holds national key scenic area, national resort and national geopark status, was approved as the second batch of national key scenic areas in August 1988, and CNN put it among the forty most beautiful sights in China. The coastal geopark paths are worth more than the beach.

Is there anything to do inside in Dalian?

Two things worth the name. The Laohutan Ocean Park began as a walking garden in 1959, added a floating pier and sea excursions in 1980, joined the Xiuyue Villa park in 1989 and became the polar and marine animal aquarium in August 2002, when it was the largest of its kind in the world. And the Hunan Provincial Museum of the city, the Dalian Museum and the modern art museum sit within the centre, with the harbour waterfront and the fishing boats outside.

Why is the Dalian tram worth a trip?

Because it has never stopped. The first trial line opened on 25 September 1909 from the zoo to the pier, 2.45 kilometres long, with 30 timber double-deck cars built by Preston in the United States, mounted on Montagu Gibson chassis with AEG electrical equipment, rated for 72 seated and nicknamed the great American wooden cage. By 1945 there were 11 lines. Most of the network was cut in the 1970s, and lines 201, 202 and 203 plus the 1930s cars are what survived.

How old is the harbour?

The port was built in 1898, at the end of the Qing, and it works as a natural ice-free harbour on the strait between the two peninsulas. It now has 48 berths to a maximum depth of 17.5 metres, 82 modern specialist berths including 57 for vessels of 10,000 tonnes and over, and 149 kilometres of port rail, with throughput rising from 32 million tonnes in 1980 to more than 320 million tonnes in 2010.

Can you walk up a hill from the city?

Dahei Shan is 25 kilometres out and 663 metres high, the highest peak of the southern Liaodong peninsula, and it has the remains of the Goguryeo defensive city of Pisha on the summit, five kilometres of wall at three to five metres high. The stepped path takes about 90 minutes, and the summit looks over the sea on two sides, which is why it is the one walk rather than one visit on this list.
