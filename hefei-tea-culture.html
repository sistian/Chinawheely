Overview: Tea Culture in Hefei

Hefei is the provincial capital of Anhui and the market through which the province's best green teas are bought and sold. Two of the country's ten most famous teas come from the mountains to the west of the city: Lu'an Guapian from the Lu'an area and Huangshan Maofeng from around the Huangshan massif, both about 150 kilometres away, with Huoshan Huangya from the neighbouring Dabie Mountains a short distance further west.

The tea

Lu'an Guapian (六安瓜片)

Lu'an Guapian is a leaf tea from Lu'an in western Anhui. Unlike other green teas it is made from single leaves rather than buds, pressed flat and cut into melon-seed-shaped slices. It counts among the ten most famous teas of China, its exact date of creation remains disputed, and its making technique is now listed as national intangible cultural heritage.

How to have it here: The slices are brewed whole in a glass or porcelain pot at 80 to 85 degrees Celsius, without a rinse, and the liquor is pale yellow-green. In Hefei shops the tea is graded by picking date and by leaf size, and the early spring lot of single leaves from the Jinzhai and Huoshan hills carries the highest price in the year's list.

Huangshan Maofeng (黄山毛峰)

Huangshan Maofeng is a green tea from the slopes around Huangshan in Anhui, with downy tips, a slightly chestnut aroma and a taste that carries the mountain's coolness. In 1955 the China Tea Company rated it one of the country's ten most famous teas, the Ministry of Commerce awarded it the name of famous tea in 1982, it took a certificate of honour in 1983, and in 1986 the Ministry of Foreign Affairs designated it a gift tea.

How to have it here: The leaf is fine, slightly curved and heavily tipped with white hairs, and it opens in the cup like a small cloud. The best plots lie in the Huangshan scenic area and the neighbouring villages of Tangkou, Chongchuan, Gangcun, Fangcun, Yangcun and Changtan, with Taohua Peak, Yungu Temple, Ciguang Pavilion and Diaoqiao Nunnery generally named as the pickings of highest quality.

Huoshan Huangya (霍山黄芽)

Huoshan Huangya is a yellow tea, produced mainly at Dongliuhe village in Mozitan Town of Huoshan County and at Dahuaping, Manshuihe and Jiugongshan in Shangtushi. It is older than the Tang dynasty; Li Zhao's Guoshi Bu of that period listed Huoshan Huangya of Shouzhou among fourteen tribute teas, and Ming writer Wang Xiangya gave the judgement that the best of the Shouzhou Huoshan Huangya is superb. It is also a Chinese geographical indication product and is counted among the three yellow teas of Anhui.

How to have it here: The leaf is closely rolled in the shape of a sparrow's tongue, golden in colour with white down showing, and the brew is a clear yellow-green with a mellow chestnut aroma. Hefei buyers take it from the county's 13 towns and 3 townships, where the growing area lies in the northern foothills of the Dabie range, a county of 2,043 square kilometres with about 290,000 residents.

Serving Anhui tea in Hefei

Brewing rules here follow the leaf rather than the tea house. Lu'an Guapian is made of single flattened leaves with no buds and no stems, so it is never rinsed: a first short infusion would strip what little is left of the leaf. Water at 80 to 85 degrees is used rather than a full boil, the leaves are allowed to open in the pot, and the same liquor can be drunk through several rounds because the cut leaf gives up its substance slowly and evenly.

In a tasting room the order of operations is fixed. The host measures the leaf, warms the vessel, pours for every guest before pouring for themselves, and refills without asking. The cup is tipped slightly to be drained rather than emptied on the table, and the pot lid is lifted to signal that hot water is wanted. A cup left covered while the tea goes cold is taken away and replaced without comment, since in Anhui the second infusion is treated as the better one.

Storage matters as much as brewing for the teas of this region. The cakes and bricks that arrive from Yunnan and the boxed leaf that comes from Lu'an are kept in the dry, cool rooms that line the back of a Hefei tea shop, away from the street, where a constant temperature protects the white down on the tips. Huangshan Maofeng is packed in tins, Lu'an Guapian in sealed tins of 250 grams, and the dating and origin of a lot is stated on the label in the same way a vintage year would be printed on a wine.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Lu'an from Hefei and how long does the journey take?

Lu'an lies about 150 kilometres west of Hefei, on the western edge of Anhui, and the whole prefecture covers 15,451 square kilometres with about 4.4 million residents. It borders Henan province to the north, which is also the province that supplies most of the buyers at Hefei's tea trade.

What makes Lu'an Guapian different from every other green tea?

It is the only major Chinese green tea made without buds or stems. Single leaves are opened, pressed flat by hand and cut into slices shaped like melon seeds, which gives both the name and the flat, blade-like appearance. The technique has been listed as national intangible cultural heritage, and the exact year the tea was first created is still argued over.

How old is the history of Lu'an tea?

The area began sending tea to the imperial court in the early Ming dynasty. The Cha Jian of that period records of the Lu'an tea that it was of fine quality and best taken as medicine, but that it was badly fired and could not release its fragrance. At that time Songluo tea from Xiuning in Huizhou was at the height of its fame, and Lu'an developed its own Songluo tea, which is generally treated as the earliest form of Guapian.

Which teas of the Huoshan county are recognised?

Two. Lu'an Guapian is the green tea made in the county, and in 2024 the prefecture produced 47,500 tonnes of tea in all, of which 16,000 tonnes was Lu'an Guapian. The second is Huoshan Huangya, a yellow tea of sparrow-tongue shape, older than the Tang dynasty and named among the fourteen tribute teas in Li Zhao's Guoshi Bu.

When was Huangshan Maofeng given its official rankings?

In 1955 the China Tea Company rated it among the ten most famous teas of China, in 1982 the Ministry of Commerce awarded it the title of famous tea, in 1983 it received a certificate of honour from the Ministry of Foreign Trade, and in 1986 the Ministry of Foreign Affairs fixed it as a gift tea for foreign guests.

Which Huangshan villages give the best picking?

The growing area covers the Huangshan scenic area and the belt of villages along its edge, including Tangkou, Chongchuan, Gangcun, Fangcun, Yangcun and Changtan. Within that belt the pickings named most often as the finest come from Taohua Peak, Yungu Temple, Ciguang Pavilion and Diaoqiao Nunnery, and from the villages of Gangcun and Chongchuan.
