Name Origin of Ma'anshan

Ma'anshan means "saddle mountain" in Chinese, named for the mountain shaped like a horse saddle. It is an industrial city in eastern Anhui near Nanjing.

Ma'anshan Education & Universities

Anhui University of Technology (Ma'anshan campus) and Ma'anshan Normal University are the main institutions.

Getting Around Ma'anshan

Ma'anshan has no commercial airport; Nanjing Lukou Airport is the nearest. Ma'anshan Railway Station connects to Nanjing and Shanghai on the Nanjing–Anqing intercity railway. Buses and taxis serve the city.

Ma'anshan Natural Resources & Environment

Ma'anshan lies on the Yangtze River with a humid subtropical climate. It is famous for steel production and nearby Caishiji scenic area.

Ma'anshan Local Secrets

The things a driver who works here every day would point out.

🏞️ Caishiji on the Yangtze

The Yangtze riverside

Caishiji is the cliff on the Yangtze where Li Bai spent his last years and where his tomb stands today. The water below is the reason the city sits where it does — Ma'anshan grew as a river port and steel town on the north bank.

🏭 An industrial city on the Yangtze

Steel and river trade

Ma'anshan is one of China's major steel centres, and the Yangtze still carries the raw materials in. The contrast between the industrial waterfront and the old hill paths above the city is the thing worth seeing.

🍜 What to eat here

Yangtze river food

Ma'anshan grilled fish and Yangtze River shrimp are the local plates, alongside Anhui-style stinky mandarin fish and simple wheat noodles. The fish is the reason to sit down here at all.

🍂 Autumn and winter

Seasonal highlights

Autumn is the comfortable season on the riverfront, and the city's Yushan District parks are at their best in late October. Winter is cold and damp off the river, so plan the outdoor stops for midday and leave the long drives to the warmer part of the day.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Ma'anshan

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Ma'anshan

🍵 Tea Houses of Ma'anshan

Tea culture runs through the day in Ma'anshan: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
