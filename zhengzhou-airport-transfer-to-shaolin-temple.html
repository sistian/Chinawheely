Shaolin Temple sits in the folds of Mount Song, about 90 km from Zhengzhou Xinzheng Airport, and the journey is part of the day rather than a gap in it. Why transfer to Shaolin Temple?

The temple was founded in 495 AD for the Northern Wei emperor Xiaowen, and the tradition of martial practice attached to it goes back to the Bodhidharma story itself. Later a Northern Wei emperor granted the monastery the name Shaolin, meaning Shaolin Forest, and it was rebuilt and renamed repeatedly through the Tang, where the Indian monk Batu and the Chan master Bodhidharma are both traditionally associated with it. The Emperor Taizong of Tang is recorded as dispatching envoys to the temple and recognising it as a centre of the new Chan school.

The complex is large and specific. The Pagoda Forest holds the stupa of every eminent monk who died at Shaolin, more than 200 of them, in rows graded by rank, and it is the quietest and most affecting corner of the site. The Hall of the Heavenly Kings keeps the clay arhats, a set of 500 sculpted figures each with a distinct face, carved over decades by monks and local craftsmen. The martial arts demonstrations and the performance halls are separate from the historic core and are where most modern visitors spend their time.

The setting is part of the argument. Shaolin lies in the Dengfeng mountains below Mount Song, with the Pagoda Forest on a hillside terrace and the Songshan peaks behind, and it forms one element of the UNESCO World Heritage property known as the Historic Monuments of Dengfeng in the Centre of Heaven and Earth, which covers nine sites across the county including the observatory and the ancient pagoda forests. Arriving by road gives you the approach through the valley and the turn into the hills, which is part of the experience.

What to do at Shaolin Temple once you arrive

Get to the Pagoda Forest early. It is a walled hillside of small brick and stone stupas, each the tomb of one Chan master, and the whole point of the place is that they are individual. The oldest and most senior are at the highest point. Going before 09:30 gives you the quiet and the low sun raking across the brickwork, and it is also when the tour groups have not yet arrived from Zhengzhou and Luoyang.

Inside the monastery, follow the axis through the Mountain Gate, the Heavenly Kings Hall with its 500 arhats, the Main Hall where the historical Buddha platform stands, and the Thousand-Buddha Hall with its vast brick relief. The martial arts and performance area sits further down the valley, with regular kung fu and Shaolin dance shows through the day and the Zen performance theatre further out. If you only have time for one, choose the Pagoda Forest over the shows; the shows are excellent but they are entertainment, while the stupas are the monastery.

  • The Shaolin Temple ticket is about 80 yuan, with separate charges for the performance area and the cable car
  • Opening hours are generally 07:00 to 18:00 in the main season, and the temple closes part of the day for monks
  • The Dengfeng public bus runs from Zhengzhou and Luoyang, but a car from the airport is far quicker
  • Wear shoes for the Pagoda Forest paths and bring water, since the canteens inside are limited

Arriving at Zhengzhou Xinzheng International Airport

Xinzheng is a large airport 37 km south-east of downtown Zhengzhou, opened in 1997 to replace the old Dongjiao field, which had been built in 1956 with a runway too short and too close to the city. It is the biggest airport in Henan and one of the busiest in central China, handling close to 29 million passengers in 2025 and a heavy share of cargo traffic. From here the city is a straightforward 40-minute run, but Dengfeng is in the opposite direction, west across the Zhengzhou ring road and then north into the hills.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to drive from Zhengzhou airport to Shaolin Temple?

Xinzheng Airport is 37 km south-east of Zhengzhou and the temple is in Dengfeng in the hills west of the city, giving a total road distance of about 90 km. Expect 100 minutes of driving, and closer to two hours at weekends when the Dengshan expressway fills with coaches and private cars heading for the same destination.

How much is the Shaolin Temple ticket?

Admission to the historic temple area is around 80 yuan, and it is commonly bought online in advance through the ticket platforms rather than at the gate. Separate tickets apply for the martial arts performance area and the Shaolin Temple cable car, and the Pagoda Forest sits inside the main ticket area. Students with valid identification usually pay a reduced rate.

Is a day trip from Zhengzhou airport realistic?

It is long but entirely doable. Leaving around 07:30 puts you at the temple gate by 09:00, which gives you five or six hours on site before the return run of a hundred minutes. If you land later in the morning, an overnight in Dengfeng or Luoyang is the more relaxed option, and it also lets you start before the coach groups.

What is the Pagoda Forest and why is it worth the time?

It is a hillside cemetery containing the stupas of more than 200 eminent monks who died at Shaolin, each built as a small brick or stone tower and graded by the rank the monk held. The setting, with the Songshan ridges behind and the older pagodas higher up the slope, is quieter than anywhere else in the complex and is where the monastic history is most tangible.

Can I combine Shaolin with other Dengfeng sites?

Yes, since the Historic Monuments of Dengfeng in the Centre of Heaven and Earth covers nine separate locations in the county. The Songshan Observatory, the Zhougong Sundial and the Shaoshi Temple are all within reach of Dengfeng town. Most visitors see one or two of them on the way to or from the monastery rather than trying to cover the whole property in a single day.
