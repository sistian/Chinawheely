Overview: Photography in Hefei

Hefei is a provincial capital on a low saddle of ground between the Huai and the Yangtze, and its photographs are mostly of the two things it sits on top of: a lake and a hill. The lake is Chao, which covers about 775 square kilometres and gives the city its southern edge, and the hill is a dead volcano 284 metres high with a forest park on it and the city's best-known view from the ridge. Between them are the older layers: a Three Kingdoms battlefield that is now a park, a memorial park for a Song magistrate, and a museum built in 1956 and rebuilt in 2011.

Chaohu Lake from the Wetland Forest Park (巢湖与滨湖湿地森林公园)

Chao Lake lies in the middle of Anhui inside Hefei prefecture, touching one urban district, one county-level city and three counties across 20 townships, between the Yangtze and the Huai. It covers about 775 square kilometres, 61 kilometres east to west and about 12 kilometres north to south, and it is the largest lake in the province and one of the five great freshwater lakes of traditional China. The name goes back to the pre-Qin state of Chao and its people, and the night moon over the lake was the seventh of the eight sights of Luyang. The wetland forest park stands at Dazhangwei near the mouth of the Nanfei river where it enters the lake, with a total area of 10.72 square kilometres of which 2.63 square kilometres is open water and wetland; the first section opened in September 2012 and the second in April 2013, and it became the city's fourth national forest park at the end of that year.

best light: Late afternoon into sunset, when the water and the sky take the same colour and the far shore reads as a line rather than a detail. · lake and wetland

Getting there: In Baohu district near the mouth of the Nanfei river on the north shore of the lake, reached from the city by the lakeside road, with the wetland boardwalks a short walk from the entrance.

  • The boardwalk section over the wetland gives the reflected sky in the frame, which is the whole subject here.
  • Sunset over the open water of the lake is the one thing the park is built for, so time the visit rather than the parking.
  • The trees are poplar, metasequoia and privet, and the metasequoia lines make the strongest vertical frames.

Dashu Mountain and the Volcanic Crater (大蜀山)

Dashu Shan is the highest ground inside Hefei, 284 metres, on the western side of the Shushan district, and it is developed as a forest park of 13,000 mu. It is geologically an extinct volcano, a branch of the Dabie Mountains formed in the Cretaceous about 140 million years ago, with two ancient craters on the summit, a lava fall of olivine basalt on the slope, and volcanic neck and conduit features. The name comes from the Luzhou prefectural gazetteer, which records that a monk from Sichuan settled on the hill, missed the water of his home country and struck the ground, and the spring that came up tasted to him like the Qutang gorge, which is why there is a Shu well here. Snow clearing on Dashu Shan was the first of the eight sights of Luyang, and the spring dawn over it is one of the ten sights of Hefei. The forest park was approved at provincial level in 2009, became a national forest park in January 2013, and forms a provincial geopark together with Zhipeng Shan.

best light: Early morning, when the ridge line separates from the haze over the plain and the forest below is still in shadow. · hill and forest park

Getting there: West of the city in Shushan district, with the forest park entrance off the ring road; the summit path is a stepped climb of about 40 minutes from the car park.

  • The two crater rims sit close together on the summit, so the geology is a short walk rather than a whole hike.
  • The lava fall on the slope is a texture rather than a view, and it needs the low sun to show the columns.
  • The martyrs' cemetery at the foot of the hill is a separate site and has its own rules about photography.

Xiaoyaojin, the Three Kingdoms Ferry (逍遥津)

Xiaoyaojin occupies the north-east corner of the old city in Luyang district, about 200,000 square metres of park, and it is one of the ten sights of Hefei. The ground was a ferry crossing on the Fei river, and outside the park stand the Flying Cavalry Bridge and the West Ferry Bridge, which were the road out of the city. It is famous as a battlefield: Cao Wei and Sun Wu fought over Hefei for thirty-two years, and the best known of those engagements was fought here, where Zhang Liao of Wei defeated Sun Quan, the episode that the Romance of the Three Kingdoms turns into the storm of Hefei. In the middle island of the park lake there is still a mound said to be Zhang Liao's cenotaph. The pond was enclosed by the expanding city in the Qian Dao era of the Southern Song, was called Doujia Chi in the Ming, Duck-fighting Pool in the Kangxi years, and was renamed Douye Chi when it passed into the hands of the scholar Gong Xinjian in the Guangxu period.

best light: Mid-morning, when the lake surface holds the reflection of the island with the mound on it. · city park and battle site

Getting there: North-east of the old city in Luyang district, a short walk from the city centre and from the Huai River road.

  • The mound on the island reads better from a low position across the water than from the bank.
  • The two bridges outside the park are the reason the place was a crossing, and they frame the view of the old city wall.
  • The lake is small, so the reflections are close and a polariser is more useful than a telephoto.

Sanhe Old Town (三河古镇)

Sanhe stands in the south-east of Feixi county in the Chaohu plain, at the junction of Feixi, Shucheng and Lujiang, and it is named for the three rivers that meet there: the Fengle and the Xiaonan join the Hangbu river here on its way to Chao Lake. The town covers 4.71 square kilometres and has about 31,000 residents in eight neighbourhoods. Its older names were Queshu, Quewei, Quean and Sancha; the earliest record is the battle of Quean in the fifth year of Duke Zhao of Lu, when Chu and Wu fought here, and the name Sanhe itself appears only in the Ming record of the Tian Shun era. Because it stands on the strategic line between the river and the lake it was fought over repeatedly, which is why the town is unusually well preserved. It was listed as a Chinese historical and cultural town in 2007 and rated a national 5A site in 2015, and it was named one of the hundred culture towns of China in 2023.

best light: Late afternoon, when the light comes down the three streets between the whitewashed gables and the canal. · historic water town

Getting there: In the south-east of Feixi county, about 40 kilometres from Hefei, on the road to Lujiang, with the town entered from the south through the old gate.

  • The town has three historic streets running north to south with the water between them, which is the plan worth photographing from the bridges.
  • The old gate at the southern end frames the main street and is the natural first frame of the day.
  • Late afternoon light comes down the street axis, which is why the visit is better at the end of the day than at the start.

The Anhui Museum and the Shushan Hall (安徽博物院)

The Anhui Museum, formerly the Anhui Provincial Museum, is the only provincial comprehensive museum in the province that combines natural history, historical collections and social education. The Luyang hall, the old building, was completed in 1956 on Anqing Road and is itself listed as a national key cultural relic under the name of the provincial museum exhibition building; the Shushan hall, the new one, opened in 2011 with 41,000 square metres of floor area. The institution grew out of the Hefei Science Museum and the northern Anhui cultural relics committee of 1949 and the Wuhu Science Museum and southern Anhui museum, which merged in April 1953; the exhibition building started in August 1954 and the museum was formally founded in November 1956. It now holds close to 220,000 objects, among them the phoenix-pattern square ding, the Chu great ding of the Warring States, the Cai侯 bronze bell set, the gold tally of Lord E Junqi, and a bell-shaped gold ornament inscribed for the descendants of the emperor.

best light: Indoor; the bronzes are lit to show their surface, and the light is already part of the display. · museum

Getting there: Two sites: the old Luyang hall on Anqing Road in the centre of the city, and the new Shushan hall on Huaining Road in Shushan district, near the foot of Dashu Shan.

  • The old hall is a 1956 building in its own right and is worth a photograph as well as a visit.
  • The new hall is next to the foot of Dashu Shan, so the two can be done in one trip.
  • The Chu great ding and the bell set are the headline pieces and are placed in the halls that suit their scale.

Bao Park (包公园)

The park stands on Wuhu Road in Baohu district and was built to commemorate Bao Zheng, the Song magistrate of Hefei, and it is a national 4A site. Its centrepiece is the Bao Gong shrine, the longest continuously used Bao shrine in China, formally named the Temple of Lord Bao Xiaosu, with Xiaosu being the posthumous title granted by Emperor Renzong after his death. The shrine was listed as a provincial protected unit in 1961 and republished as one of the first batch in 1981. The main gate carries the couplet on loyal and worthy ministers and moral celebrities, with the side doors inscribed wan lian and nuo li, refuse corruption and dare to stand. The hall, or Hall of Enjoyment, holds a statue of Bao Zheng with the standing figures of his four attendants, and three bronze cangues, the dragon-headed, tiger-headed and dog-headed, stand to one side. The Qingfeng Pavilion and the Floating Village complete the site.

best light: Early morning, when the lake and the bridge are quiet and the reflections are still. · memorial park

Getting there: On Wuhu Road in Baohu district on the south side of the city, close to the lake road and to the wetland forest park.

  • The three bronze cangues are displayed beside the hall and are usually the strongest interior frame.
  • The Floating Village section is reached by water and photographs differently from the shrine and the tower.
  • The park is a short drive from the wetland forest park, so both fit into one afternoon.

Mingjiao Temple (明教寺)

The Mingjiao Temple in Luyang district is a provincial protected unit and one of the nationally listed Han Buddhist temples, and it is the oldest surviving temple in the city with a continuous record of use. It was built in the Sui period on the site of the former residence of the calligrapher and official Li Hongzhang's family property, and it took its present name during the Qing when the hall structure was rebuilt. The layout follows the standard temple axis of a gate, a hall and a pagoda, and the Daxiong Hall retains the Song-style bracket sets and the roof of the original hall. The pagoda behind it dates from the late Ming and early Qing period and is the only surviving pagoda in Hefei, which makes it the reason to come.

best light: Mid-morning in winter, when the low sun reaches the halls and the courtyards are still in shade. · temple

Getting there: In the old city in Luyang district, near the Huai River road and the Baogong shrine, within walking distance of the centre.

  • The pagoda is behind the hall and is usually missed; the axis view from the gate is the frame.
  • The bracket sets on the Daxiong Hall are the detail worth a telephoto, and they need light from the side.
  • It is close to the Xiaoyaojin park, so both can be walked in the same morning.

Gear and planning for photographing Hefei

Most of the subjects here are modest in scale and the distances are short, so the kit list is small. The lake and the wetland boardwalks want a wide lens and a polariser for the water; Dashu Shan wants a telephoto for the crater rims and the lava columns on the slope, with a tripod for the dawn light from the ridge; and the temple and the museum halls want a fast lens and permission to shoot without flash. A 24-70 covers the lake and the hill, a 70-200 covers the geology, and that is the whole kit. Because the city sits in the humid zone between two rivers, the haze is real and a cloth on the front element is not paranoia.

In planning terms, Hefei divides into a southern half and a northern half. The lake, the wetland park, Bao Park and the temple are on the south side and close to each other, while Dashu Shan and the new museum hall sit on the west side, and the whole group fits into two days with the industrial and administrative centre in between. Morning light is weak and blue in winter, which suits the old city; the lake and the hill are best in the late afternoon, and the wetland boardwalk is worth repeating at both ends of the day if the weather allows.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most photogenic thing in Hefei?

Dashu Shan, at 284 metres, the highest ground in the city and an extinct volcano about 140 million years old with two craters on the summit and a basalt lava fall on the slope. It is a forest park of 13,000 mu, upgraded to a national forest park in January 2013. The name comes from a monk from Sichuan who dug for water on the hill and found a spring that tasted to him like the Qutang gorge.

Which lake is the one to photograph?

Chao Lake, the largest in Anhui at about 775 square kilometres and one of the five great freshwater lakes of traditional China, lying between the Yangtze and the Huai with a name that goes back to the pre-Qin state of Chao. The wetland forest park at the mouth of the Nanfei river covers 10.72 square kilometres, of which 2.63 is water and wetland, opened in two sections in 2012 and 2013 and made the city's fourth national forest park.

What is the historical site in the city?

Xiaoyaojin, a park of about 200,000 square metres on the north-east corner of the old city, built on an ancient ferry crossing where the Wei and Wu states fought over Hefei for thirty-two years. Zhang Liao of Wei defeated Sun Quan there, the episode behind the storm of Hefei in the Romance of the Three Kingdoms, and a mound on the island in the lake is said to be his cenotaph. The pond was called the Duck-fighting Pool in the Kangxi years.

Is there a water town near Hefei?

Sanhe, 40 kilometres away in Feixi county, named for the three rivers that meet there on their way to the lake. It covers 4.71 square kilometres, keeps a record of the battle of Quean from the Spring and Autumn period, was listed as a Chinese historical and cultural town in 2007 and rated a national 5A site in 2015, and is one of the hundred culture towns of the country as of 2023.

Which museum should I go to?

Both parts of the Anhui Museum are worth the trip, and they are in different places. The old Luyang hall on Anqing Road was completed in 1956 and is itself a listed national key cultural relic; the new Shushan hall opened in 2011 with 41,000 square metres and sits by the foot of Dashu Shan. The collection numbers close to 220,000 objects, led by the phoenix-pattern square ding, the Chu great ding, the Cai侯 bell set and the gold tally of Lord E Junqi.

What about Bao Zheng?

Bao Park in Baohu district was built for him and is a national 4A site, and its shrine is the longest continuously used Bao shrine in China, formally the Temple of Lord Bao Xiaosu. It was protected provincially from 1961, and the hall holds the statue of Bao Zheng with his four attendants and the three bronze cangues, the dragon-headed, tiger-headed and dog-headed, that give the name to the instruments of judgement in Chinese drama.
