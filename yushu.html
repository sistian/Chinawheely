Name Origin

Yushu is the Chinese form of the Tibetan Yul-shul (ཡུལ་ཤུལ). Its capital Gyêgu grew up as Jyekundo, the old Tibetan trade mart of this corner of Kham — Yushu belongs culturally to Kham (eastern Tibet), not Lhasa's central Tibet, which still shows in its monasteries, dialect and horsemanship.

Education & Universities

Yushu is served by local colleges, vocational schools and teacher-training institutions rather than by large research universities.

Getting Around

Batang Airport (opened 2009) and National Highway 214 serve the eastern, populated half of the prefecture; since 2017 the G0613 Xining-Lijiang Expressway has linked Gyêgu toward Hainan Prefecture and Xining, making the drive from Xining a realistic day. The far west — hundreds of kilometres from the eastern core and almost empty of people — is crossed only by National Highway 109 and the Qinghai-Tibet Railway.

Natural Resources & Environment

The source prefecture: the Yangtze, Yellow and Mekong rivers all rise here — the "Three Rivers Source" (Sanjiangyuan) — with the Tongtian (upper Yangtze, Drichu in Tibetan) and the Zaqu (upper Mekong) draining a land of 287 lakes covering 3,531 km². Average elevation tops 4,200 m, from the 3,335 m Jinsha River floor up to 6,860 m Bukadaban; over 70% of the land is permafrost ice-edge terrain. Kunlun, Tanggula, Bayan Har and the Hoh Xil ranges frame the prefecture, much of it inside the Sanjiangyuan National Nature Reserve protecting the river headwaters.

Food & Shopping in Yushu

What to eat and what to bring home from Yushu

🍽️ Must-Try Yushu Cuisine

Yushu feeds the province: it is Qinghai's main livestock base, producing roughly a quarter of its animal products, while the valley farms grow wheat, millet and black highland barley. The everyday table is herding meat and dairy plus barley staples.

🛍️ Shopping in Yushu

No market district is recorded; the practical base is Gyêgu — Jyekundo, the old trade mart of Kham, whose bazaar tradition is the town's own origin story.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

No specific tea custom is spelt out in the record; the Khampa herding diet is dairy-and-barley centred, and hot drinks from the family's own milk are the everyday norm.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Yushu

The living traditions of Yushu — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

Seven national key protected sites, among them the Gyana Mani stone pile, the Zangniang stupa with Sangzhou Monastery, Gesar's Thirty Generals' stupas at Dana Monastery, and the Vairocana grotto with Lebagou's cliff carvings. The living tradition is immense: before 1958 the prefecture held 195 monasteries with over 25,000 monks and nuns — mostly Kagyu, with strong Sakya and Nyingma followings — and Khampa horse culture still headlines public festivals with races such as the Gesar King Cup.

Best Time to Visit Yushu

The short, rainy, cool-to-warm summer (June-September) is the season; winters are long and hard.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · 3-5月 — Late thaw; still winter-like across the 4,200 m average elevation. (daytime 8.8–16°C, nights around -6.1°C; about 27 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · 6-8月 — Short, rainy and cool to warm; most of the year's rain falls June-September. Gyêgu's annual mean is 3.22 °C. (daytime 18.7–20.7°C, nights around 6°C; about 95 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · 9-10月 — Clear and cooling fast; valleys golden, passes frosting. (daytime 7.3–17.7°C, nights around -8.9°C; about 36 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · 11-2月 — Long and cold — Qumarlêb averages -2.13 °C over the year; expect snowbound routes. (daytime 2.7–4.8°C, nights around -14.2°C; about 3 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Yushu

What to drink in Yushu, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Yushu

No specific tea custom is spelt out in the record; the Khampa herding diet is dairy-and-barley centred, and hot drinks from the family's own milk are the everyday norm.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Yushu

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Yushu

🍵 Tea Houses of Yushu

Tea culture runs through the day in Yushu: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
