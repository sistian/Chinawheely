Lujiazui is the financial district on the peninsula formed by a bend in the Huangpu River, directly across the water from the Bund. Development began after 1992, when the area was designated a special investment zone, and in 2005 the State Council confirmed its 31.78-square-kilometre zone as the only finance and trade zone among mainland China's 185 state-level development zones. Landing at Pudong puts you roughly 32 kilometres from this skyline, in the same district, across the river tunnels.

Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG)

Pudong Airport occupies a 40-square-kilometre coastal site about 30 kilometres east of the city centre, in the same Pudong New District as Lujiazui itself. The westbound run follows the Yingbin Expressway and the S20 outer ring before the roads dive through the river tunnels that link the airport corridor with the Lujiazui peninsula.

Why transfer to Lujiazui?

The district holds the world's first trio of adjacent supertall skyscrapers. The Jin Mao Tower opened in 1999, the Shanghai World Financial Center reached 492 metres, and the Shanghai Tower is a 128-storey, 632-metre megatall that ranked among the world's tallest buildings from its 2015 completion until Merdeka 118 passed it in 2021. The Oriental Pearl Radio & Television Tower, finished in 1995, still anchors the skyline and set the pattern for what followed.

Lujiazui is also the practical base for many visitors: the area carries five five-star hotels with about 2,443 rooms, plus shopping centres and a convention centre on the waterfront. Metro Line 2 runs directly beneath the district and connects it with the airport corridor, the old city and Hongqiao in the west. For a first arrival in Shanghai, the peninsula puts the skyline, the river crossings and the Bund ferries within walking distance of each other.

  • 31.78-square-kilometre finance and trade zone, designated in 1992 and reaffirmed by the State Council in 2005
  • Shanghai Tower: 128 storeys and 632 metres, completed in 2015
  • Three adjacent supertalls — Jin Mao Tower, 492-metre Shanghai World Financial Center, Shanghai Tower
  • Oriental Pearl Tower completed in 1995 as the district's inaugural landmark
  • Five five-star hotels with roughly 2,443 rooms on the peninsula

What to do in Lujiazui once you arrive

The three supertalls each sell observation access at different heights, so the district works as a single vertical itinerary. The Oriental Pearl Tower, the 1995 landmark, was the pioneer of paid viewing platforms here; entry to the tower and its attractions runs about 100 yuan with add-ons taking the total to roughly 220 yuan at the top levels. The Shanghai Tower's deck sits near the crown of its 632 metres, and the Jin Mao Tower's 88th-floor hall sits below that again, so pick one or two rather than all three.

Between the towers, the Riverside Promenade runs along the peninsula's western edge, facing the Bund across the Huangpu River, and the ferry terminals below connect to the Puxi bank for a crossing that costs a few yuan. Metro Line 2's Lujiazui station sits under the superblock of towers, and the Line 14 and Line 2 interchange at Lujiazui puts the rest of the city within reach without a car once you have arrived.

  • Lujiazui station on Metro Line 2 and Line 14 sits directly beneath the tower cluster
  • Deck tickets are sold per tower; the Oriental Pearl tops out near 220 yuan with all levels included
  • The Bund ferry leaves from the peninsula's west side and crosses the Huangpu in minutes
  • Treat the observation decks and the promenade as one afternoon; the distances between them are short

Arriving at Shanghai Pudong International Airport

Pudong is mainland China's busiest international gateway, with 35.25 million international passengers in a total that reached roughly 85 million in 2025, the fifth-busiest airport in the world. It handled 3.78 million tonnes of cargo in 2024, second only to one other airport worldwide. Two passenger terminals and four parallel runways are in operation, with a third terminal, two more runways and the S1 and S2 satellite halls under construction to lift annual capacity from 60 million to 80 million passengers.

Ground transport to Lujiazui splits three ways. Metro Line 2 links both terminals with the district directly, with services running until about 21:00; the maglev runs to Longyang Road, where you change to Line 2, at 50 yuan single or 40 yuan with a same-day flight ticket; and a metered taxi to the city centre runs around 150 yuan. A booked car removes the transfer at Longyang Road and the walk between the metro exits and your hotel lobby, which matters most with luggage or a late landing.

What the transfer costs

Metered taxi fares from PVG to the city centre are commonly quoted at around 150 yuan, and Lujiazui sits on the near side of that run once the river tunnels are cleared. A private transfer is typically priced above the meter — figure a sedan in the 250 to 400 yuan range depending on vehicle class and hour — with the quote covering the expressway tolls, fuel and the driver's waiting time in the arrivals hall.

For groups, seven-seat vehicles are the usual choice from Pudong, since the district's hotel driveways handle vans without issue. Night arrivals add a surcharge on the metered alternative, and the metro's 21:00 cut-off leaves the maglev plus Line 2 change as the last public option; a pre-booked car is the only option that holds its price and its pickup across a delayed flight.

Ask for the drop-off inside your hotel's driveway or at the Lujiazui station exit on Century Avenue, rather than kerbside on the ring roads, because the peninsula's outer lanes are tunnels and ramps with no stopping space.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Lujiazui from Pudong Airport?

The airport sits about 30 kilometres east of the city centre on a 40-square-kilometre coastal site, and Lujiazui lies on the near side of downtown, across the Huangpu River from the Bund. The road distance is roughly 32 kilometres via the Yingbin Expressway and the S20 ring, and the run takes about 45 to 60 minutes depending on traffic in the tunnel approaches.

Is the maglev a better option than a car?

The maglev connects Pudong's terminals with Longyang Road, where you transfer to Metro Line 2 for Lujiazui. Tickets are 50 yuan single or 40 yuan with a same-day flight ticket, and 80 yuan return. It is fast and reliable, but it ends at Longyang Road, so with hold luggage or a family group the through-car option avoids the transfer entirely.

Which metro line connects Pudong Airport and Lujiazui?

Metro Line 2 runs from the airport terminals through Century Avenue and Lujiazui station beneath the tower cluster, continuing west under the river to the Bund-side stations and on to Hongqiao. Services run until about 21:00 from the airport end, so late arrivals need the maglev connection or a car.

How much do the observation decks in Lujiazui cost?

Each tower prices its own deck. At the Oriental Pearl Tower, entry covering the tower and its attractions starts around 100 yuan, with higher levels taking the total near 220 yuan. The Shanghai Tower and Jin Mao Tower sell separate tickets; check current tariffs before you visit, as levels and prices are adjusted periodically.

Where should the driver drop us off in Lujiazui?

The peninsula's hotel towers — several of the district's five-star properties among them — all have internal driveways, so a hotel drop is the simplest. Otherwise use the Lujiazui station exits on Century Avenue, which open directly under the superblock between the Oriental Pearl Tower and the supertalls, and avoid the tunnel portals on the ring roads where stopping is not permitted.
