Overview: Day Trips from Hefei

Hefei is a provincial capital on the Jianghuai plain, a low basin between the Yangtze and the Huai, and the day trips from it divide cleanly by the roads that leave it. South and south-west lead into the mountains, where the yellow granite of Huangshan, the Buddhist slopes of Jiuhua and the Danxia-like peaks of Tianzhu rise within four hours of the city. North-west runs the line to Chuzhou and the Ouyang Xiu country of Langya Mountain, an hour and a half out. West is the short run to the old river town of Sanhe, 40 kilometres away. Only two of the six need most of a working day, and the rest are comfortably done with an evening still free in the city.

Huangshan and the Granite Peaks (黄山)

Huangshan stands in southern Anhui in the traditional Huizhou region, with the range running 40 kilometres from north to south and 30 kilometres from east to west. The mountains cover 1,200 square kilometres and the core scenic area 160.6 square kilometres, and they are built of granite. The highest point is the Lotus Peak at 1,864.8 metres. The mountain was called Yi Shan in antiquity and was renamed Huang Shan in 747, on the tradition that the Yellow Emperor had refined elixir there. It was listed among the first batch of national key scenic areas in 1982, inscribed as a mixed cultural and natural World Heritage Site in December 1990 and made a Global Geopark in 2004.

How the day runs: Leave Hefei before 06:00 for the 300-kilometre run, which reaches the northern transfer station near 09:30. The mountain works on a one-way system: take the cable car to the upper terrace, walk the ridge past the Welcome Guest Pine and the Jade Screen Peak, and continue toward the Lotus Peak if the sky is clear. Descend by a different route rather than retracing the walk, which keeps the day moving instead of doubling the distance. The summit area is exposed and takes two hours even at a steady pace. Start the descent by 13:30 and be back at the transfer station for the drive home, which arrives in Hefei around 19:00.

300 km from Hefei · about 210 min by car from Hefei · full-day trip · best in May to June and September to October for cloud seas over the sea of clouds; the paths hold ice and snow through the winter and the pines are bare.

  • The mountain operates with separate ascent and descent routes, so the map handed out at the entrance matters more than the number of peaks you plan to reach.
  • The sea of clouds forms on the northern side after a cold front, and the classic view from the Bright Terrace is most reliable in the early morning after rain.
  • Two days is the usual recommendation for the full ridge, and a single day means choosing between the eastern approach and the western one rather than trying both.

Mount Jiuhua and its Monastery Terraces (九华山)

Mount Jiuhua stands in Qingyang County within Chizhou and is one of the four great Buddhist mountains of China, the place of the bodhisattva Ksitigarbha. The mountain was called Lingyang Shan in Han times and was renamed in a couplet by the Tang poet Li Bai, in which the nine peaks are compared to lotuses in bloom. In 719 the Korean prince Kim Qiaojue crossed the sea and practised on the mountain for 75 years, and the monks held that he was an incarnation of Ksitigarbha, which established the mountain as his seat. The mountain now holds 99 temples, of which 93 are open to visitors, along with 15 preserved bodies in the meditative state.

How the day runs: Set out at 06:30 and allow two and a half hours for the 170-kilometre run to the mountain, arriving near the transfer centre in time for the shuttle bus up to the temple terrace. The main cluster of monasteries sits between roughly 300 and 600 metres, and the walk from the lower gate through the Huacheng Temple and the Jiuhua Body Temple to the Tiantai Temple is a steep two-hour climb. Take the sightseeing cable car up the ridge for the return instead of retracing the steps. A full circuit takes five to six hours, so leave the upper terrace by mid-afternoon for the drive back, which reaches Hefei around 19:30.

170 km from Hefei · about 150 min by car from Hefei · full-day trip · best in April to May and September to October for clear air over the ridges; the summit can be cold and windy from November onward.

  • The main monasteries are arranged on one long stepped street rather than in a single precinct, and the ascents between them are steeper than the distances suggest.
  • The embalmed figures are displayed in halls that keep their own opening hours and limit numbers, so check the schedule at the transfer centre before walking up.
  • The cable car and the shuttle bus on the mountain are separate systems, and the return cable car closes earlier than the last bus down.

Mount Tianzhu and the Sanzu Temple (天柱山)

Mount Tianzhu, also called Wanshan and formerly Huoshan, rises about 9 kilometres northwest of the town of Qianshan in the Anqing area, and its main peak, Tianzhu Peak, stands at 1,489.8 metres. The range is the eastern continuation of the Dabie Mountains and holds the largest ultrahigh-pressure metamorphic belt inside China, which produces its characteristic granite landform. The scenic area covers 333 square kilometres, of which 82.46 form the main section, and it was among the first batch of national key scenic areas and national forest parks, and was rated a 5A attraction in 2011. The mountain counts 45 peaks, 25 caves, 86 strange stones and 18 waterfalls, with a forest cover of 97 per cent. Han emperor Wu in 106 BC transferred the southern sacrifice to this mountain.

How the day runs: Leave at 07:00 and allow two hours to Qianshan, where the mountain shuttle takes you to the trailhead below the main cliff. The classic route climbs the Feilai Peak to the Tianzhu Peak platform, a sequence of granite steps and ledges that takes about three hours with the exposed sections taken slowly. The walking is easier than it looks from the map, but the surfaces are polished and the queue at the summit platform forms in the middle of the day. Come down by the Mysterious Valley path, which is longer and passes the hundred-step cloud ladder. Total time on the mountain is about six hours, and the drive back to Hefei takes another two.

140 km from Hefei · about 120 min by car from Hefei · full-day trip · best in April to June and September to November; the granite gorges hold mist through the summer.

  • The route through Mysterious Valley is a genuine addition rather than a shortcut, and it is the quieter half of the mountain in the afternoon.
  • The thousand-year-old cliff inscriptions cluster around Shiniu Gudong and Shangu Liuchuan at the mountain foot rather than on the walking routes.
  • The name Wanshan, or Ten Thousand Mountains, and the alternative name Huoshan both appear on older maps and signage, so check the Chinese name before booking a route.

The Sanhe Ancient Town and the Battle Site (三河古镇)

Sanhe lies in the southeast of Feixi County, at the junction of Feixi, Shucheng and Lujiang, where the Fengle and Xiaonan rivers run into the Hangbu river on its way to Chaohu. The town takes its name from those three channels, and earlier names were Queshu, Quewei, Que'an and Sancha. It covers 4.71 square kilometres and had a population of 31,000. Its history is military as much as commercial: the Spring and Autumn record describes the army of Chu defeating Wu at Queshu, and in the late Qing the Taiping forces won the Battle of Sanhe against the Hunan army there. The town was listed among the Chinese historic and cultural towns in May 2007 and was rated a 5A attraction in October 2015.

How the day runs: Leave the city centre at 09:00 and reach the town in 45 minutes, which leaves the whole morning and early afternoon for the old streets. The historic core is compact enough to walk, with the Liu Tongxing Longzhuang merchant compound, the Yang Zhenning former residence and the Helu garden as the principal sites, all of which charge separately from the free entry to the town itself. The Battle of Sanhe memorial and the ancient dwelling houses were listed together as the fifth batch of Anhui provincial cultural relic protection units in 2004. Allow three hours for the town, then return by 15:00 and be in Hefei with the late afternoon still free.

40 km from Hefei · about 45 min by car from Hefei · half-day trip · best in March to May and September to November; the river lanes are shaded in summer and the stone bridges are slippery after rain.

  • Entry to the town itself is free but the individual attractions inside it charge separately, and the merchant compound is the largest of them.
  • The three rivers meet at the town and the lane network is built along the banks, so a route that follows the water is shorter than one that crosses the blocks.
  • The Battle of Sanhe site and the old residences were protected together as one provincial listing, and they stand a short walk apart near the river.

Mount Langya and the Zuiweng Pavilion (琅琊山)

Mount Langya stands on the southwest edge of Chuzhou, and its principal summit, Xiaofeng Shan, reaches 321 metres. The mountain is a national forest park, a national key scenic area and a national 5A attraction, one of the five principal scenic areas of Anhui. The name comes from the Eastern Jin founding emperor Sima Rui, who held the title Prince of Langya and took refuge here. The Zuiweng Pavilion, built in the Northern Song and recorded in Ouyang Xiu's essay of the same name, is counted as the first of the four famous pavilions in China. Around the pavilion, more than 400 places of cliff and stele inscription survive, of which 7 are Tang dynasty cliff inscriptions.

How the day runs: Leave at 08:00 and reach the mountain in 90 minutes, which leaves the afternoon free for the main circuit. The route runs from the Zuiweng Pavilion up past the Nine Bend Stream to the South Gate and the summit, about 5 kilometres with 300 metres of ascent, and takes two and a half hours. The Zuiweng Pavilion and its surrounding inscription cliffs deserve an hour on their own, and the Ancient Plum Pavilion is on the way up rather than a separate stop. The Fengle Pavilion is on the far side of the ridge and adds another forty minutes. Descending in the late afternoon puts you back at the foot around 17:00 and in Hefei by 18:30.

110 km from Hefei · about 90 min by car from Hefei · full-day trip · best in April to May for the plum blossom and September to November for the clear air; the summit is cold in the winter.

  • The Ouyang Xiu plum is centuries old and the tree's condition varies by year, so a photograph of it is not guaranteed by any particular season.
  • The Fengle Pavilion entrance is tied to the adjacent Nanjing site, and access arrangements for it have changed, so check locally before planning that side of the ridge.
  • The Langya Ancient Path is a separate marked route rather than part of the main circuit, and it needs most of a day in its own right.

Xidi and Hongcun in the Wannan Villages (西递与宏村)

Xidi and Hongcun lie in the county of Yi in southern Anhui and were inscribed together as the Ancient Villages in Southern Anhui in November 2000, the first time residential buildings were entered on the World Heritage list. Xidi was founded during the Yuanyou era of the Song dynasty in the 11th century and took its name from a courier station west of the village. It retains 124 old houses and 3 ancestral halls, and its Hu Wenguang prefect arch was built in 1578. Hongcun was founded in 1131 during the Shaoxing era of the Southern Song and relaid out in the Yongle period of the Ming, with a water channel nearly one metre wide feeding a crescent pond and a southern lake.

How the day runs: Leave Hefei at 06:00 for the 290-kilometre run to the county seat of Yi, which is the base for both villages, and allow most of the day for one of them. Hongcun is the more compact, with a single entrance through the water channel and a layout that reads from the south lake, so two hours covers the village. Xidi is a longer street plan along a stream with the arch at its head, and takes nearer to three. Both are best walked on foot, and both charge separately at the gate. Choose one village rather than two, since the transfer between them and the gate queues will eat the afternoon, and turn back for Hefei by 15:00 to arrive around 19:00.

290 km from Hefei · about 220 min by car from Hefei · full-day trip · best in Late March to early April for the rapeseed blossom and October to November for the autumn colour in the village lanes.

  • The two villages are roughly 20 kilometres apart in different directions from the county seat, so they cannot be combined comfortably in one day with the drive.
  • Hongcun's water channel runs inside the village walls and the house cluster reads best from the southern lake rather than from the main street.
  • The Chengzhi Hall is the largest single building group in Hongcun, and it is larger than several of the attractions you would otherwise visit separately.

Planning day trips from Hefei

The two long runs, Huangshan at 300 kilometres and the Wannan villages at 290, are the only entries that use most of a working day, and both are scheduled around the same constraint: a departure before 06:00 and a return after dark. Everything else sits inside three hours of the city, with Jiuhua at 170 kilometres, Tianzhu at 140 and Langya at 110. Sanhe is the shortest at 40 kilometres and works as a half-day outing, with the town itself free to enter and the individual sites inside it charging separately.

The mountain trips also differ in the way they consume a day. Huangshan operates on a one-way system with cable cars at both ends, so a single visit is a matter of choosing a route rather than a matter of walking distance. Jiuhua is a stepped street of monasteries climbed on foot in one direction and descended by cable car in the other. Tianzhu mixes a granite ridge with a longer valley path and has no single cable car covering the whole mountain, which makes it the most flexible of the three and the easiest to shorten when the weather closes in.

Planning a day trip from Hefei

The Sanhe Ancient Town and the Battle Site, Huangshan and the Granite Peaks and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Hefei day trip route — The Sanhe Ancient Town and the Battle Site sits closest at 40 km, roughly 45 minutes by car, while Huangshan and the Granite Peaks reaches furthest at 300 km, about 210 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Huangshan and the Granite Peaks works best May to June and September to October for cloud seas over the sea of clouds; the paths hold ice and snow through the winter and the pines are bare., while Mount Jiuhua and its Monastery Terraces suits April to May and September to October for clear air over the ridges; the summit can be cold and windy from November onward. — in Hefei that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

Mount Tianzhu and the Sanzu Temple, The Sanhe Ancient Town and the Battle Site, Mount Langya and the Zuiweng Pavilion each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Hefei itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Hefei day trip is worth the longest drive?

Huangshan, at 300 kilometres and about three and a half hours each way. The range covers 1,200 square kilometres with a core scenic area of 160.6 square kilometres, and the main ridge needs two days to walk properly, so the single-day version is a selection of one route rather than the whole mountain. The Wannan villages are a comparable distance but a far easier day, since both villages are walkable and the driving is broken by a stop in the county seat of Yi.

Is Mount Jiuhua manageable in a single day?

Yes, with an early start and one-way planning. The mountain holds 99 monasteries, of which 93 are open, and they sit along one long stepped street between roughly 300 and 600 metres. The climb from the lower gate through the Huacheng and Jiuhua Body temples to Tiantai takes about two hours, and the return is quicker by the ridge cable car. A full circuit runs to five or six hours, so leave the upper terrace by mid-afternoon.

How many days do Huangshan and the Wannan villages need together?

They do not combine well in one trip. Huangshan fills a day on its own and the Wannan villages are another 290-kilometre drive in a different direction, at the far south-west of the province. The two villages sit in the same county as each other and 20 kilometres apart, which is why the pair is usually described together, but the drive from Hefei serves one of them, not both, and picking one keeps the return journey reasonable.

What makes Tianzhu different from Huangshan for a single-day visit?

The route structure. Tianzhu has no cable car covering the whole mountain, so you choose between the upper granite ridge to the 1,489.8-metre summit platform and the longer valley path through Mysterious Valley, and you can use the cable car for one leg only. The mountain also has an unusually dense set of named features for its size, with 45 peaks, 25 caves, 86 strange stones and 18 waterfalls inside a 333-square-kilometre area, and the cliff inscriptions sit at the mountain foot.

Is Sanhe Ancient Town a half-day or a full-day trip?

Half a day is enough. It is 40 kilometres from the city centre and 45 minutes by car, and the historic core covers 4.71 square kilometres and can be walked. The principal sites are the Liu Tongxing Longzhuang compound, the Yang Zhenning former residence and the Helu garden, each of which charges separately, and the Battle of Sanhe memorial stands close to the old residences. Leaving by 15:00 keeps the drive short and the evening free.

Which trip suits a winter visit from Hefei?

Langya Mountain and the Wannan villages are the reasonable ones. Langya is at 321 metres and the paths are manageable, with the plum tree planted by Ouyang Xiu as the seasonal draw in late winter. The Wannan villages are short walks between buildings, so cold weather matters less there than on an exposed ridge. Huangshan is the one to avoid in winter, since the upper paths hold ice and the pines are bare until spring.
