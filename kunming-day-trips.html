Overview: Day Trips from Kunming

Kunming sits at 1,886 metres on the shore of a lake that is the sixth largest freshwater body in China, and most of what is worth a day's journey lies within two hours of the city. To the east is a karst stone forest that is both a national scenic area and a World Heritage site, and to the south a lake three million four hundred thousand years old holding a twentieth of the country's lake freshwater. West, over the lake, a range of peaks runs for 35 kilometres along the shore. Closer in, a restored market town and an open-air museum of twelve minority villages are both inside the ring road.

The Stone Forest (石林)

The Stone Forest, called Lunan Stone Forest or Yunnan Stone Forest, is a karst formation in Shilin Yi Autonomous County, 120 kilometres from Kunming across an area of more than 400,000 mu. It was approved on 8 November 1982 as one of the first batch of national key scenic areas, at that time under the legal name Lunan Stone Forest Scenic Area, and the protection regulations took effect in 1991. It became a national geopark in March 2001, one of the first UNESCO Global Geoparks in February 2004, and a World Natural Heritage site on 27 June 2007 as part of the South China Karst. The site is made up of the large and small stone forests, also known as Liziqing, the Naigu stone forest, Zhiyun Cave, Long Lake and the Great Waterfall. In Yi legend it is the home of Ashima, and it was named in 1931 by the Yunnan governor Long Yun, whose calligraphy was destroyed during the Cultural Revolution and later rebuilt by collecting characters from the Cuan Baozi stele.

How the day runs: Leave Kunming at 07:30 for the 120-kilometre run east, which reaches the gate around 09:30. Take the circular route through the large stone forest first, when the coach groups have not arrived, then walk to the smaller forest and the viewing platform beyond it. The main circuit takes two hours on foot and the karst is best read in raking light, so an early entry is worth more than a fast one. Lunch at the site, then continue to the Long Lake section or the Naigu stone forest, which is quieter and has fewer people in the middle of the day. Leave by 16:00 and be back in Kunming by 18:30.

120 km from Kunming · about 120 min by car from Kunming · full-day trip · best in October to March for clear, dry light; the Yi Fire Festival falls on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth lunar month.

  • The karst reads best in side light, so the morning and the last hour of the afternoon show the towers as separate forms instead of a flat wall of rock.
  • The site is large and the paths between sections are long; the internal shuttle is worth using between the large and small stone forests.
  • The Fire Festival in late June is the busiest time of the year and the best time to see the site used rather than visited.

Fuxian Lake and Chengjiang (抚仙湖)

Fuxian Lake lies between Jiangchuan, Huaning and Chengjiang in Yuxi prefecture, and its name, used since the Ming dynasty, replaced the earlier names of Dachi, Qingyu Xiyue and Luojia. It stands at 1,721 metres, covers 211 square kilometres, and is the third largest lake in Yunnan after Dianchi and Erhai. Its total storage of 20.6 billion cubic metres is the largest in the province and 9.16 per cent of the freshwater stored in Chinese lakes, with a maximum depth of 155 metres that makes it the third deepest in the country after the Tianchi on Changbai Mountain and Kanas Lake. The water reaches drinking standard. The lake basin is about 3.4 million years old, when Fuxian, Xingyun and Qilu were a single lake, and continued uplift of the surrounding mountains deepened it into what it is now. It belongs to the Pearl river system and drains through the Haikou river into the Nanpan river.

How the day runs: Drive to Chengjiang on the eastern shore, about 90 kilometres from Kunming and 100 minutes on the highway, arriving at the lakeside by 10:00. The eastern shore road is the scenic one, with the cliff walls close to the water, and it is best walked rather than driven. Take the shoreline walk at Chengjiang town, then the boat section to the underwater stone forest where the clear water makes the shore visible. Lunch on the lake, and leave around 15:00 for the return, which is usually the slow part of the day.

90 km from Kunming · about 100 min by car from Kunming · full-day trip · best in October to May; the summer monsoon turns the water opaque and the road over the eastern shore difficult.

  • The clarity of the water is the reason to come, and it is at its best in the dry season when the lake level is low.
  • The eastern shore road is narrow in places and the buses are slow, so allow more time for the return leg than for the outward run.
  • The lake belongs to the Pearl river system while Kunming's own lake drains to the Yangtze, which is why the two lakes behave differently in level.

The Western Hills above Dianchi (西山与滇池)

The Western Hills, formerly called Bijishan, are the group of peaks along the western shore of Dianchi, running 35 kilometres from Bijiguan in the north to Haikou in the south, 15 kilometres from the city. The range is also called the Reclining Buddha Mountain or the Sleeping Beauty Mountain for the shape of its profile, and its highest point, Luohan Peak, reaches 2,511 metres. It is a national scenic area and a 4A rated site, with the Longmen cliff section, Huating Temple, Taihua Temple, the tomb of the composer Nie Er and a small stone forest among its features. Below it, Dianchi is the largest plateau freshwater lake in Yunnan and the sixth largest freshwater lake in China, standing at 1,886 metres with a stomach-shaped outline, fed by 29 rivers of which the Panlong river is the largest, and drained by the Haikou river, the Tanglangchuan. A silted embankment at Haigeng divides the lake into the Caohai and the outer lake, and since the Xiyuan tunnel was completed in 1996 the two are no longer connected.

How the day runs: Start at 08:30 and take the road up to the Longmen section, which is reached by a tunnel cut into the cliff and is the most dramatic part of the hill. Walk the Longmen path and the Dragon Gate, then continue to Huating Temple and the Nie Er tomb, about 90 minutes in total. Come down by the cableway or the road, and finish the half-day on the lake side: Haigeng dike along the western shore, with the wetland area and the view back across the water to the city. Return to the centre by 13:30, which leaves the afternoon for the city itself.

15 km from Kunming · about 40 min by car from Kunming · half-day trip · best in All year; the lake haze that settles in the dry season is what gives the range its depth in the afternoons.

  • The lake and the city are best seen looking east from the western shore, not the other way round, because the shoreline and the skyline separate better.
  • The road up the hill has many bends; the return leg is the slow part of the morning.
  • The lake is shallow and silted at the northern end, so the wide water views come from the southern and western sides.

Yunnan Nationalities Village (云南民族村)

The village stands on the shore of Dianchi 8 kilometres south of Kunming, covering about 1,300 mu, and it is built as a working window on the culture of the province rather than as a museum of objects. Twelve village settlements are open, covering the Bai, Dai, Yi, Wa, Naxi, Tibetan, Lahu, Jino, Blang, De'ang, Hani and Jingpo peoples, together with a Mosuo household. The buildings are set at different scales and orientations so that each settlement reads as a place rather than a display, and guides in minority dress explain the marriage, funeral and festival customs of each group. Festival events are staged inside the village as well, including the Bai March Fair, the Yi Torch Festival, the Dai Water Splashing Festival, the Jingpo Munao Festival and the Lisu Blade Pole Festival.

How the day runs: Allow three hours rather than one, because the villages are spread across the site and the walk between them is part of it. Arrive at opening time, start with the Bai and Yi settlements near the entrance and work towards the Tibetan and Naxi houses at the back, where the terrain rises. Take the guided sessions for the customs rather than only the architecture. Finish with the lakeside edge of the site, which looks across the water to the Western Hills, and combine it with the Haigeng dike drive in the same half-day.

8 km from Kunming · about 25 min by car from Kunming · half-day trip · best in Year round; the village stages the major minority festivals through the year, and the Bai March Fair and the Dai Water Splashing are the best attended.

  • The staged festival performances carry more information than the buildings alone, so time one of them into the visit.
  • The site is flat and open, so the light is harsher than in the city; the morning is the better part of the day.
  • The village and the western shore of the lake are close enough to be done in one outing with a car between them.

Jiuxiang Caves (九乡溶洞)

The Jiuxiang cave group lies in Yiliang county in the Jiuxiang Yi and Hui township, distributed along the Maitian river, a tributary of the Nanpan river. The caves are developed in siliceous limestone of the Dengying formation of the Sinian system, and the intermittent uplift of the crust since the Quaternary has given the system a layered structure: the upper levels have no groundwater and carry heavy chemical deposits, while the lower levels hold groundwater, and where cave roofs have collapsed they have left canyons and natural bridges. More than 20 caves are known, including the Three-Legged Cave, the Immortal Cave, the White Elephant Cave and the Bat Cave, spread over roughly 20 square kilometres. Part of the site was opened to visitors in 1989, the area was listed as a third-batch national scenic area in 1994, and it was approved as the Jiuxiang canyon and cave national geopark in August 2009.

How the day runs: Leave Kunming at 08:00 for the 80-kilometre run to Yiliang and arrive at the site around 09:40. The walking route runs through several caves in sequence, including a canyon section between them, and takes two hours; wear shoes with grip, because the floors are wet and the steps are irregular. Lunch in Yiliang, then continue to the open-air sections in the afternoon and return to Kunming by 17:30.

80 km from Kunming · about 100 min by car from Kunming · full-day trip · best in All year; the caves are at a constant temperature and the plateau summer heat is the reason to go underground.

  • The canyon between the caves is part of the route and is exposed, so the walk is longer than the cave distance suggests.
  • Temperatures inside stay constant, so a light layer is needed even on a hot plateau afternoon.
  • The upper levels have the heavier mineral formations and the better light, so spend the first hour on them.

Guandu Old Town (官渡古镇)

Guandu stands on the north shore of Dianchi, on the lower reaches of the Baoxiang river, about 10 kilometres from the city centre, and is one of the national characteristic scenic towns. It is regarded as one of the birthplaces of Dian culture: the site was originally a small rise on the eastern shore of the lake called Wo Dong, with people living there 3,500 years ago, and it became a ferry crossing when the Nanzhao king Fengjiayi set up the landing, with officials and scholars passing and pausing to write poetry, which gave the place its name. The town has more than a thousand years of history and retains the local architectural fabric of old Kunming. A two-year restoration and reconstruction ran from April 2001 to April 2003 at a cost of about 120 million yuan. The site is organised in named sets: eight views including the ancient ferry fishing lights and the spiral peaks, five hills, six temples, seven pavilions, eight temples of the local cults, two palaces and two rivers.

How the day runs: Go in the morning, when the market is running, and allow two hours. The old streets are narrow and the temple complexes are spread along the river, so walk rather than drive between them, with the repaired golden tower and the Miaozhan temple as the anchor points. Combine it with the Yunnan Nationalities Village or the Haigeng dike on the same side of the city, both of which are within a short drive of the northern end of the lake.

10 km from Kunming · about 25 min by car from Kunming · half-day trip · best in All year; the market activity is strongest in the morning.

  • The named groups of halls are scattered; pick two or three groups to see properly rather than trying to cover all of them.
  • The restoration is recent, so the streets are clean and the scale is uniform, which makes it work best as a short walk rather than a full day.
  • The morning market is the reason to come early rather than for the architecture.

Planning day trips from Kunming

Kunming's elevation of 1,886 metres is the fact that organises these trips. The city itself is mild, the air is thin and clear for most of the year, and the plateau takes a two-hour drive to leave behind. Two of the six destinations, the Stone Forest and the Western Hills, are inside or on the edge of that plateau, while Fuxian Lake and Jiuxiang are lower and warmer. A driver booking for a full day is worth it for any of the three that involve a long highway section, and the highway itself is the least interesting part of the day, so it is better done at either end than in the middle.

The lake is the other organising element. Dianchi, Chengjiang and Fuxian all belong to the same province but not to the same river system: Dianchi drains to the Yangtze through the Tanglangchuan, while Fuxian belongs to the Pearl river and holds more fresh water than every other lake in the country combined. Both are within two hours, and the two visits make a natural pair for anyone who has more than one day in the region.

Planning a day trip from Kunming

Yunnan Nationalities Village, The Stone Forest and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Kunming day trip route — Yunnan Nationalities Village sits closest at 8 km, roughly 25 minutes by car, while The Stone Forest reaches furthest at 120 km, about 120 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

The Stone Forest works best October to March for clear, dry light; the Yi Fire Festival falls on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth lunar month., while Fuxian Lake and Chengjiang suits October to May; the summer monsoon turns the water opaque and the road over the eastern shore difficult. — in Kunming that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

The Western Hills above Dianchi, Yunnan Nationalities Village, Jiuxiang Caves each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Kunming itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which day trip is the essential one?

The Stone Forest, 120 kilometres east and approved as one of the first batch of national key scenic areas on 8 November 1982, later a national geopark in 2001, a UNESCO Global Geopark in 2004 and a World Natural Heritage site in 2007 as part of the South China Karst. It covers more than 400,000 mu, is a textbook karst landscape, and is the home of Ashima in Yi legend. The Western Hills are closer at 15 kilometres and the closest thing to the city itself, but they are a hillside walk rather than a journey.

Is Fuxian Lake worth the drive?

It holds 20.6 billion cubic metres, which is the largest volume of any lake in Yunnan and 9.16 per cent of the freshwater in Chinese lakes, and at 155 metres it is the third deepest in the country. The basin is about 3.4 million years old, when it was joined with Xingyun and Qilu into a single lake, and the water reaches drinking standard. The east shore road, with the cliffs close to the water, is the part worth stopping for, and it is about 90 kilometres from Kunming.

How long do the Western Hills take?

A half-day. The range runs 35 kilometres along the western shore of the lake, 15 kilometres from the city, and reaches 2,511 metres at Luohan Peak. The road climbs to the Longmen section, cut into the cliff, and from there the walk continues to Huating Temple, Taihua Temple and the tomb of the composer Nie Er, taking about 90 minutes. Finishing on the lake side at Haigeng gives the view back across the water to the city skyline.

What can be done in half a day close to the city?

Three things. The Yunnan Nationalities Village is 8 kilometres south on the shore of Dianchi, covering 1,300 mu with twelve village settlements and a Mosuo household, and it stages the Bai, Yi, Dai, Jingpo and Lisu festivals. Guandu Old Town is 10 kilometres from the centre, on the north shore of the lake, and has a market running in the mornings. The two are close enough to combine with the Haigeng dike in a single outing.

Can day trips reach Shangri-La?

Not properly. Shangri-La, formerly Zhongdian, is a county-level city of 11,418 square kilometres and 186,400 people in the far north-west of the province at the meeting point of Yunnan, Sichuan and Tibet, renamed in 2001 and made a city in 2014, with a provincial historical town status from 2002. It is a two-day journey at the very least, which puts it outside a single-day plan from Kunming, and it is better treated as the start of a longer route.

What is the best month for these trips?

October to March for the Stone Forest and Fuxian Lake, because 85 per cent of the plateau's rainfall falls between May and October and the dry months give the clearest air and the calmest water. The caves at Jiuxiang are at constant temperature and are a good wet-season answer, and the town at Guandu and the Nationalities Village are open year round.
