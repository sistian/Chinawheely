Overview: Eating and Shopping in Harbin

Harbin's menu was written by two immigrant kitchens and one rural farming calendar. Russian and Lithuanian sausage makers, Manchu and Xibe households who preserved blood through winter, and the beer plant founded in 1900 all shaped what the city eats. This page covers six foods and four streets or squares, with founding dates, measured lengths and shop counts taken from the record.

What to eat

Guo Bao Rou, sweet-and-sour pork (锅包肉)

Pork loin slices coated in starch, fried golden, then tossed in a sweet-sour sauce, its old name guo bao rou meaning pot-exploded pork. Chef Zheng Xingwen created it as an adaptation of a Beijing fried pork dish, made sour-sweet with fruit to suit his Russian wife. He worked out a quick tossed sauce and a thicker tomato version, the latter devised in 1911 and now common in Liaoning.

Where: Restaurants across Harbin, with the classic version served in the historic restaurants along Central Street in Daoli District.

Harbin red sausage (哈尔滨红肠)

A dry, wrinkled, smoke-cured sausage of roughly 80 percent lean pork, about 10 percent diced fat, up to 7 percent starch and under 3 percent salt, with garlic and pepper as the traditional seasonings. The Russian merchant Ivan Churin founded a trading house in 1900, and in 1909 a Lithuanian employee opened the Churin sausage works, producing a sausage whose red colour gave it its Chinese name.

Where: Sold throughout Harbin, with the historic Churin brand on Central Street and Daoli district the reference point.

Slaughter-day pork and sauerkraut pot (杀猪菜)

The winter feast of a household that has just killed its pig, built on sour cabbage, pork belly and blood sausage, all simmered in a copper pot over live charcoal. The blood sausage is made by scrubbing pig small intestine with salt, tying one end, then forcing in fresh blood with salt and Sichuan pepper and boiling it hard. Harbin's mean annual temperature is 5.2 degrees Celsius.

Where: Northeast-style restaurants and farmhouse kitchens across Harbin and the wider Heilongjiang countryside, served in the cold months.

Harbin beer (哈尔滨啤酒)

China's oldest surviving beer brand, traceable to 1900 when the Polish-born merchant Jan Wroblewski opened the country's first brewery in the city. The plant was renamed in 1908, again in 1932, and after 1956 several older breweries merged under the Harbin name. In 1959 the factory made its first 250 tonnes of beer using corn instead of rice, a national first, and the group now runs 13 breweries.

Where: Brewery taps and bottle shops across the city, and the brewery museum in the southern industrial district, opened in September 2014.

Kvass rye drink (格瓦斯)

A low-alcohol fermented drink made from wheat, rye or barley bread, sweet and sour with a little carbonation, usually no more than 1.2 percent alcohol and sometimes none at all, which is why children drink it. It is flavoured with fruit, berries or herbs such as strawberry and mint, and it also forms the base of the cold Russian soup okroshka. It first appears in a chronicle of 989.

Where: Supermarkets in Harbin and across Northeast China, where the drink arrived with Russian settlement in the late nineteenth century.

Heilongjiang regional cuisine (龙江菜)

The provincial cuisine of Heilongjiang, built on 4 traditions, Shandong cooking, northeast cooking, Korean cooking and Russian western food, and organised around fresh ingredients and restorative cooking. Its named banquets include the Mirror Lake fish banquet and the Xingkai Lake whitefish banquet, and its everyday repertoire counts pork with vermicelli, chicken with mushrooms, braised bones and the slaughter-day pot among the household dishes.

Where: Regional restaurants throughout the province, with the fullest selection in Harbin, the capital.

Where to shop

Central Street (中央大街)

Harbin's flagship pedestrian avenue, 1,450 metres long and 21.34 metres wide, paved with square granite blocks known locally as bread stones. It was built in 1898 as Kitayskaya Street, renamed Central Street in July 1928 and opened to pedestrians on 1 June 1997 as the first commercial pedestrian street on the Chinese mainland. The avenue carries 71 European buildings in Renaissance, Baroque, eclectic and modern styles.

Where: Daoli District, running north from Xinyang Square to the flood-control memorial tower on the Songhua River bank.

Sophia Cathedral and the architecture museum (圣索菲亚教堂)

The largest Orthodox church in the Far East, begun on 17 March 1907 as a field chapel for the Russian Fourth East Siberian Infantry Division and completed in timber that August with money from the Jewish tea merchant Chistyakov. Rebuilt in brick by 1932, it measures about 42 metres by 28 metres, was listed as a national key monument in 1996, and reopened as an architecture museum in 1997.

Where: Zhaolin Subdistrict, Daoli District, beside Central Street, with a 7,000-square-metre plaza cleared in 1997.

Gogol Street (果戈里大街)

A road 2,642 metres long and 16 to 29 metres wide through the middle of Nangang District, named for the Russian writer Nikolai Gogol. It was opened in 1901 in two sections called New Business Street and Gogol Street, renamed in 1925 and again in 1958 as the Struggle Road, and only reverted to its Gogol Street name in 2003. It is now a long run of restaurants and shops.

Where: Nangang District, running from Wenchang Street in the southwest to Yueya Street in the northeast.

Madieer Hotel and Central Mall (马迭尔宾馆与中央商城)

At number 89 stands the Madieer Hotel, built in 1906 and opened in 1913 as the city's most luxurious hotel, designed in the Art Nouveau style and now a national key monument and a Chinese time-honoured brand. Across the street the Central Mall opened on 26 November 1994, and at number 112 the Huamei restaurant, founded in 1925 as the Mars Western restaurant, serves Russian food on three floors.

Where: Central Street in Daoli District, at the junction with the Youhao Square side street.

Eating and shopping in Harbin: practical notes

Central Street is best measured on foot. The avenue runs 1,450 metres from Xinyang Square to the flood-control memorial tower, with 71 protected European buildings along it, and it was the first commercial pedestrian street opened on the Chinese mainland, in June 1997. The Sophia Cathedral square lies immediately beside it: in 1997, 14,000 square metres of neighbouring buildings were demolished to clear the site and 7,000 square metres of plaza were laid out around the church.

Winter changes what is on the table rather than where to eat it. The city's average annual temperature is 5.2 degrees Celsius, with long severe winters and short cool summers, and the province's household dishes are built for that climate, including the slaughter-day pork and sauerkraut pot simmered in a copper pot over live charcoal. The brewery, founded in 1900 and now running 13 plants with roughly two thirds of the local market, keeps its museum on the site of the relocated plant.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the signature dish of Harbin?

Guo Bao Rou is the city's best-known dish, created by the chef Zheng Xingwen as a sour-sweet adaptation of a Beijing fried pork dish, made to suit his Russian wife's taste. He developed two methods, a quick tossed sauce of sugar, vinegar, salt and sesame oil, and a thicker tomato-based version that spread into Liaoning cooking. The dish is now recognised well beyond the northeast, including in South Korea.

How did red sausage reach Harbin?

The Russian merchant Ivan Churin set up a trading house in Harbin in 1900 as the Chinese Eastern Railway brought foreign workers into the city. In 1909 a Lithuanian employee opened the Churin sausage works on the commercial street and produced Lithuanian-style sausage, called litovskaya kolbasa. Because its colour was red it became known as red sausage in Chinese, and the recipe runs about 80 percent lean pork with under 3 percent salt.

How long is Harbin's Central Street and what is on it?

The street runs 1,450 metres from Xinyang Square north to the flood-control memorial tower on the Songhua River, paved with square granite blocks and 21.34 metres wide. It was built in 1898 as Kitayskaya Street, renamed in 1928 and made pedestrian in June 1997. There are 71 European buildings along it, including the Madieer Hotel of 1913 and the Hua Mei restaurant of 1925.

Can I visit the Sophia Cathedral?

It no longer functions as a church and now houses the Harbin Architecture Art Museum. The foundation stone was laid on 17 March 1907 and the timber church was finished that August; the present brick building was completed in 1932, measuring roughly 42 by 28 metres. It was designated a national key monument in 1996, the surrounding plaza was cleared and enlarged in 1997, and the site is rated a 4A tourist area.

What is kvass and why is it sold in Harbin?

Kvass is a fermented bread drink from Eastern Europe, made from wheat, rye or barley bread, sour and sweet with a little carbonation and no more than 1.2 percent alcohol, so children drink it too. It first appears in a Russian chronicle of 989 and reached Northeast China with Russian settlement in the late nineteenth century. It is now stocked in supermarkets in Harbin, and also forms the base of okroshka cold soup.
