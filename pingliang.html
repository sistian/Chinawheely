Name Origin

The name "Pingliang" (平凉) means "Peaceful Coolness" (ping = peaceful/flat, liang = cool) - referring to the highland climate at the eastern edge of the Loess Plateau. Historically known as Gonghe (耿赉) and later Anxi (安西) during the Tang Dynasty, Pingliang has been an important military garrison and Silk Road trading post for over 1,600 years as the gateway between the Central Plains and the western frontier.

Culture & Heritage

Pingliang sits at the intersection of Buddhist, Taoist, and Hui Muslim cultural traditions. The city is best known for Kongtong Temple (崆峒山) - one of the Twelve Sacred Taoist Mountains of China (道教十二洞天) and a center of Taoist martial arts and philosophy. The surrounding area has ancient Buddhist cave temples and a significant Hui Muslim community who have lived here for centuries as traders and craftsmen on the Silk Road.

Transportation

Pingliang does not have its own airport. The nearest major airport is Xi'an Xianyang International Airport (XIY), about 300 km east - approximately 3.5-4 hours by road. Lanzhou Zhongchuan Airport (LHW) is about 350 km west. Pingliang is connected by rail on the Xi'an-Zhongning Railway corridor, with the new Xi'an-Pingliang high-speed railway (西平铁路) providing fast access to Xi'an (2.5 hours) and connections to the national HSR network.

Geography & Environment

Pingliang is located in eastern Gansu Province, on the southern edge of the Loess Plateau where it meets the Qinling Mountains. The landscape is a mix of loess hills, river valleys, and highland meadow. The city sits at an altitude of 1,400-1,800 meters - cooler than the surrounding plains. The climate is temperate semi-arid - dry, sunny, with cold winters and mild summers. The Kongtong Mountain area has better vegetation and forest cover than the surrounding loess terrain.

Top Pingliang Attractions

Sacred Taoist mountains, Silk Road heritage, and the ancient gateway between east and west China.

Kongtong Temple (崆峒山)

Twelve Sacred Taoist Mountains - Martial Arts & Philosophy

Kongtong Temple (崆峒山) is one of the Twelve Sacred Taoist Mountains of China (道教名山), rising dramatically above the Loess Plateau at 2,100 meters. The temple complex dates to the Western Wei Dynasty (535 CE). Kongtong is famous as the legendary training ground of the Yellow Emperor and the origin of Taoist martial arts (崆峒派武术). The mountain has five peaks, 48 temples, and spectacular views over the loess landscape.

Huian Temple (徽县寺)

Ancient Buddhist Cave Temples - Northern Wei

The Huian Temple (徽县寺) cave complex, located near Pingliang, contains Buddhist statues and murals dating from the Northern Wei Dynasty (386-534 CE). The caves are carved into a cliff face and contain some of the oldest Buddhist art in Gansu Province. Less visited than the Maijishan Grottoes but equally significant for those interested in early Buddhist art.

Wenxing Ge (文星阁)

Historic Scholar Tower - Qing Dynasty

Wenxing Ge (文星阁) is Pingliang historic scholar tower, built during the Qing Dynasty as a tribute to the god of literature and a wish for academic success. The octagonal tower rises 5 stories and offers views over the old city. The surrounding area has traditional courtyard houses and narrow lanes worth exploring.

Jiuqing Palace (九清宫)

Taoist Temple Complex - Mountain Top

The Jiuqing Palace (九清宫) is the main Taoist temple complex at the summit of Kongtong Mountain. The palace contains the oldest buildings on the mountain and is the center of Taoist religious activity. The temple has a rich collection of Taoist texts, ritual objects, and ancient inscriptions. Important Taoist festivals are held here annually.

Mount Kongtong Scenic Area

Forest & Mountain Views - Best in Autumn

The Kongtong Mountain scenic area has forest coverage unusual for the Loess Plateau - a result of the mountain unique microclimate and elevation. The forested slopes create a cool, green island in the surrounding brown landscape. Autumn (September-October) is the best time to visit when the forest turns golden and the visibility is clearest.

Yongjing County Yellow River Area

Li River Valley - Ancient City & Caves

The area around Yongjing County in southern Pingliang has ancient cave sites, traditional Hui Muslim villages, and a scenic stretch of the Yellow River tributary valleys. The area is less developed for tourism but has authentic rural Gansu character. Your driver can arrange a visit to the area combining cave exploration with village lunch.

High-speed rail hub

The newer high-speed station serving the Xi'an-Pingliang HSR line. Primarily handles high-speed trains to and from Xi'an. Located in the new district, convenient for hotels in the southern part of the city.

Best Photo Spots in Pingliang

Sacred Taoist peaks, loess landscapes, and ancient cave temples at the edge of the western frontier.

Kongtong Mountain at Sunrise

Best before 8 AM

The five peaks of Kongtong Mountain rising above the Loess Plateau are most dramatic at sunrise, when the first light catches the temple rooftops and the forested peaks emerge from morning mist. Your driver can take you to the mountain the evening before so you can hike to the best viewpoint before dawn.

Jiuqing Palace from Below

Best with telephoto lens

The main Taoist palace at the summit of Kongtong Mountain is most dramatically photographed from below, looking up at the temple complex perched on the ridge. The temple buildings appear to grow out of the rock - a striking composition. Wide-angle and telephoto both work well.

Loess Plateau from Kongtong Summit

Best on clear autumn days

From the summit of Kongtong Mountain, on a clear autumn day, you can see the Loess Plateau stretching to the horizon - a vast brown landscape with the city of Pingliang below. The contrast between the forested mountain and the barren plateau is striking. The best visibility is in autumn and winter.

Huian Temple Caves

Best in afternoon light

The cave entrance at Huian Temple catches the afternoon sun, illuminating the ancient Buddhist statues inside. The quality of light on the carved faces and the surrounding cliff face is best in the late afternoon. Bring a tripod for long exposures of the cave interior.

Wenxing Ge in Old City

Best at golden hour

The octagonal Wenxing Ge scholar tower is most photogenic in the late afternoon when the golden light illuminates the brick structure. The surrounding old city lanes and courtyard walls make atmospheric backgrounds. Best photographed from street level looking up.

Autumn Forest on Kongtong Mountain

Best in October

The forested slopes of Kongtong Mountain turn golden and red in October - unusual vegetation for the Loess Plateau. The autumn colors against the brown loess hills create striking landscape photographs. The mountain is least crowded in October and the weather is ideal.

Food & Shopping in Pingliang

Must-Try Gansu & Pingliang Cuisine

Pingliang Hand-Pulled Noodles (平凉拉面)

A Gansu-style hand-pulled lamian noodle, similar to Lanzhou lamian but with a local character - often served with lamb broth and a spicier chili oil. The noodle shops in Pingliang old city open early in the morning and draw queues of locals. Your driver can take you to the most popular morning spot.

Lamb Paomo (羊肉泡馍)

The classic northwestern lamb broth with torn flatbread - a specialty of the Hui Muslim community. In Pingliang, the lamb paomo at the Muslim restaurants in the old city is particularly good. The broth is rich and warming - ideal for the highland climate.

Loess Plateau Mutton Hotpot

A hearty winter dish - thin slices of local mutton cooked in a spiced broth with potatoes, cabbage, and hand-pulled noodles. The meat from the loess plateau sheep has a distinct flavor from the highland grazing. Best at traditional restaurants in the old city during winter months.

Hui Muslim Flatbread (馕)

Large flatbreads (馕, nang) baked in a clay oven - a staple of the Hui Muslim community. Fresh from the oven they are crisp and fragrant. Available at Muslim bakeries throughout Pingliang. The best version is slightly charred around the edges.

Kongtong Mountain Wild Vegetables

The forested slopes of Kongtong Mountain produce wild vegetables not found elsewhere - mountain ferns, wild mushrooms, and mountain greens served at the restaurants near the temple. A unique culinary experience tied to the mountain environment.

Shopping Destinations

Pingliang Old City Market

The old city market near Wenxing Ge is the best place for local products, dried goods, and daily necessities. The market has a traditional feel with vendors selling fresh produce, spices, and local food products. Open daily in the morning.

Hui Muslim Craft Shops

The Hui Muslim quarter near the old city has shops selling traditional metalwork - engraved brass trays, tea sets, and decorative items. The craft combines Islamic geometric patterns with Chinese motifs - a distinctive regional art form.

What to Buy in Pingliang

Kongtong Mountain Herbs

The highland environment of Kongtong Mountain produces herbs used in traditional Chinese medicine. 黄芪 (huangqi, astragalus) and 党参 (dangshen, codonopsis) from the mountain area are available at local TCM pharmacies. The high altitude and clean air produce high-quality herbs.

Gansu Loess Plateau Honey

The loess plateau around Pingliang produces distinctive flavored honey - particularly from wildflowers and locust trees. The mineral-rich soil of the loess plateau gives the honey a unique flavor profile. Available at the old city market and specialty food shops.

Hui Muslim Brass Crafts

Engraved brass items - tea trays, cups, and decorative objects - made by Hui Muslim craftsmen. The geometric Islamic patterns combined with Chinese motifs make these distinctive souvenirs. Available at the craft shops in the old city Muslim quarter.

Medical History & TCM Heritage of Pingliang

Taoist mountain healing traditions, highland herbal medicine, and the ancient gateway between east and west health cultures.

Taoist Mountain Health Culture

Kongtong Mountain has been a center of Taoist health and longevity practice for over 1,500 years. As one of the Twelve Sacred Taoist Mountains (道教十二洞天), the mountain is associated with Taoist breathing exercises (导引术), meditation, and herbal medicine. Taoist monks on Kongtong Mountain traditionally cultivated medicinal herbs and practiced internal alchemy (内丹术) for health and longevity.

The mountain temples still maintain herbal medicine traditions.

Highland Herbal Medicine

The highland environment of the Loess Plateau around Pingtong produces distinctive medicinal herbs. 黄芪 (huangqi, astragalus root) grown at altitude is considered higher quality - used for immune support and energy. 党参 (dangshen, codonopsis) from the Kongtong Mountain area is used as a gentler alternative to ginseng. 枸杞 (goji berries) from the region are sun-dried and available at local markets.

Available at Pingliang TCM pharmacies and the old city market.

Hui Muslim Healing Traditions

The Hui Muslim community in Pingliang maintains distinctive dietary and health traditions. Halal dietary rules (禁忌) - no pork, no alcohol - combined with a diet rich in lamb, flatbread, and wild vegetables create a distinctive health pattern. The community also uses honey (蜂蜜) therapeutically - for sore throats, wound healing, and general wellness. Local honey from the loess plateau is considered particularly therapeutic.

Available at the old city market and Muslim craft and food shops.

Loess Plateau Climate & Respiratory Health

The dry, sunny climate of the Loess Plateau at 1,400-1,800m altitude has benefits for respiratory health. The low humidity is beneficial for asthma and bronchitis sufferers. The high altitude and clean air - particularly on Kongtong Mountain - create conditions associated with longevity in traditional Chinese medicine. Morning walks on Kongtong Mountain are recommended for respiratory wellness.

Kongtong Mountain is best visited in the morning for health and hiking.

Pingliang Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Pingliang like a local.

Book XIY Airport Transfer 24 Hours Ahead

Pingliang has no airport. The 300 km drive from Xi'an Xianyang Airport (XIY) takes 3.5-4 hours. Booking 24 hours ahead ensures your driver is ready for the long-distance pickup. The drive is through scenic loess plateau terrain - request scenic route stops if time permits.

Start Kongtong Mountain at Dawn

Kongtong Mountain is most atmospheric in the early morning when mist rises from the forested slopes. Your driver can pick you up at your hotel at 6 AM so you arrive at the mountain by 7 AM, before the tour groups. The hike to the summit takes 2-3 hours - start early to have time to explore at the top.

Eat Lamb Paomo in the Muslim Quarter

The best lamb paomo in Pingliang is at the traditional Hui Muslim restaurants in the old city Muslim quarter. Look for restaurants with large clay pots and queues of locals. The broth is made fresh each morning - the best bowls are gone by early afternoon.

Visit in October for Autumn Colors

The best time to visit Pingliang and Kongtong Mountain is October when the forest on the mountain turns golden and red. The visibility is clearest in autumn and the weather is ideal for hiking. Hotel prices are moderate outside of Chinese holidays.

Bring Layers for Kongtong Mountain

Kongtong Mountain is 2,100 meters high - significantly cooler than the city below. Even in summer, the summit can be cold and windy. Wear layers and bring a light jacket. The mountain also has sudden weather changes - check the forecast before your visit.

Combine Kongtong with Huian Temple

Both Kongtong Mountain and Huian Temple are within easy driving distance of Pingliang. A full-day private car can take you to both - start with Kongtong Mountain in the morning, then drive to Huian Temple in the afternoon. The cave temples are most pleasant in the afternoon light.

Best Time to Visit Pingliang

Temperate semi-arid highland climate - cold dry winters and mild summers at the edge of the Loess Plateau.

Spring

Mar-May - 8-22C

A good shoulder season for visiting Pingliang. The loess landscape begins to green and the weather is pleasant for outdoor sightseeing. April and May can be windy with dust storms from the Gobi. Good time for the Huian Temple caves and city tours.

Highlights: Pleasant weather, green landscapes, fewer crowds

Summer

Jun-Aug - 18-28C

Warm and pleasant - the most comfortable season for Kongtong Mountain hiking. The mountain forest is green and the temperature at the summit is refreshing. Afternoon thunderstorms are common but usually short-lived. Peak season for outdoor activities.

Highlights: Best for hiking Kongtong Mountain, green forests, mountain retreats

Autumn

Sep-Nov - 6-20C

The best overall season to visit Pingliang. The Kongtong Mountain forest turns golden and red in October - a spectacular transformation for the Loess Plateau. Clear skies and comfortable temperatures. The best time for photography. Hotel prices moderate outside of National Day holiday.

Highlights: Autumn colors on Kongtong Mountain, best photography weather

Winter

Dec-Feb - -8 to 5C

Cold and dry with occasional snow on Kongtong Mountain. The loess landscape has a stark, dramatic beauty. This is hot pot season - the lamb paomo and mutton hotpot are at their best. Fewer tourists and lowest hotel prices. The mountain temples have a special atmosphere in winter snow.

Highlights: Stark loess landscapes, hot pot season, fewest tourists
