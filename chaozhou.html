Chaozhou at a Glance

Chaozhou (潮州) — "Tide Prefecture" — is one of China's most culturally distinctive cities and the ancestral homeland of the Teochew (潮汕) people. With over 1,600 years of recorded history, Chaozhou has one of the best-preserved ancient city walls in Guangdong, Tang Dynasty Buddhist temples, and a living traditional culture that is recognized by UNESCO. It is also widely considered the street food capital of China — a claim supported by the extraordinary depth and quality of its culinary traditions.

Teochew Cultural Homeland

Chaozhou is the ancestral home of the Teochew people (潮汕人) — one of China's most distinctive cultural groups, famous for their language (潮汕话/Teochew dialect, a distinct branch of Chinese), cuisine, diaspora communities, and entrepreneurial spirit. Teochew people form major overseas Chinese communities across Southeast Asia, North America, and Europe. Chaozhou itself, along with the neighboring city of Shantou, forms the Chaoshan (潮汕) region — a cultural unit recognized for its distinctive traditions.

The Teochew cultural tradition places a strong emphasis on education, ancestor worship, clan solidarity, and culinary refinement. The Chaozhou people believe that the quality of food and hospitality directly reflects moral character — which explains the extraordinary sophistication of Teochew cuisine.

Ancient City Wall & City Gates

Chaozhou's ancient city wall (潮州古城墙) — built during the Song Dynasty and rebuilt in the Ming — is one of the best-preserved in Guangdong. The wall runs for approximately 2km around the old city center, with four original city gates still standing: Xiancheng Gate (仙城/湘子桥头), Dongzheng Gate (东门), and others. The most famous section runs along the Han River (韩江), where the wall is at its most dramatic.

Geography & Location

Chaozhou is located in eastern Guangdong Province, on the south bank of the Han River (韩江) — the great river that flows through the city before emptying into the South China Sea. The city is surrounded by low mountains and is close to the coastal area. It is about 300km northeast of Guangzhou and 200km northeast of Shenzhen. The Chaoshan region (潮汕) includes Chaozhou, Shantou, and Jieyang — a coastal cultural region with a shared dialect and culinary tradition.

UNESCO Cultural Heritage

Chaozhou's traditional culture — including the Chaozhou opera (潮剧), Teochew music (潮汕音乐), Chaozhou Kung Fu tea ceremony (工夫茶), and traditional Teochew architecture — has been formally recognized by UNESCO as part of China's intangible cultural heritage. The city's traditional streetscapes, clan halls, and temples form one of the most intact historic urban environments in southern China.

Food & Shopping in Chaozhou

🍜 Must-Try Chaozhou & Teochew Cuisine

Chaozhou Beef Hotpot (牛肉火锅)

Chaozhou's signature dish — thinly sliced premium beef (and beef offal) cooked in a bubbling mild medicinal herb broth at the table. The beef is cut to paper thinness and cooks in seconds. Dip in the signature Teochew沙茶酱 (shacha sauce). The best beef hotpot restaurants in Chaozhou are concentrated in the area near the old city gate — ask your driver for the recommendation.

Teochew Kung Fu Tea & Phoenix Dancong Oolong (凤凰单丛)

The ultimate Teochew culinary experience — the elaborate Kung Fu Tea ceremony (工夫茶) using Phoenix Mountain Dancong oolong tea (凤凰单丛), grown in the hills near Chaozhou. The tea has extraordinary complexity — floral, fruity, and toasty notes depending on the variety. Served in tiny porcelain cups at traditional tea houses on Paifang Street.

Chaozhou Soy Sauce Duck (卤鸭)

The Teochew way with duck — slow-braised in a dark, aromatic soy sauce braise with star anise, cinnamon, and other spices until the meat is tender and the skin is mahogany-colored. Served cold, sliced, with the braising sauce as a dip. Found at traditional Teochew restaurants throughout Chaozhou. The best version is at the old restaurant near Kaiyuan Temple.

Teochew Fresh Rice Noodles (粿条)

Fresh rice noodles (粿条) — smooth, wide, white rice noodles — served in a clear beef broth with thin beef slices, beef balls, and cilantro. The signature breakfast dish of Chaozhou, found at every wet market and street corner in the morning. Follow locals to the most crowded stall — it will be the best.

Gongping Century-Old Dessert Shop (宫畔甜品)

The famous old dessert shop near the Paifang Street — operating for over a century — serving traditional Teochew sweet soups (糖水) and desserts. Try the 莲子百合 (lotus seed and lily bulb sweet soup), the 豆花 (silken tofu dessert with ginger syrup), and the 甜蛋 (sweet preserved egg). Open from early morning to late evening.

🛍️ Shopping Destinations

Paifang Street (牌坊街)

The historic Archway Street is the heart of Chaozhou's shopping and food culture — lined with traditional teahouses, antique shops, silk and embroidery stores, and Teochew food specialty shops. Look for the traditional Teochew-style embroidered items, the locally-made shacha sauce, and the local Phoenix Dancong tea.

Chaozhou Morning Wet Market

The morning wet market near the old city is one of the most fascinating markets in southern China — hanging salted ducks, arrays of dried seafood (dried shrimp, dried scallops, dried fish), fermented tofu, fresh Teochew rice noodles, and seasonal produce. Your driver can take you for a morning visit and help with translations.

🎁 What to Buy in Chaozhou

Phoenix Dancong Tea (凤凰单丛)

Phoenix Mountain Dancong oolong tea — grown in the hills behind Chaozhou — is one of China's most prized teas, with extraordinary floral and fruity complexity. Buy at the specialty tea shops on Paifang Street or at the Phoenix Mountain tea villages. Look for the "岭头单丛" (Lingtou Dancong) and "凤凰水仙" (Phoenix Narcissus) varieties. Your driver can take you to a reputable dealer.

Teochew Shacha Sauce (沙茶酱)

The signature condiment of Teochew cuisine — a rich, savory, slightly sweet sauce made from fermented soybeans, shrimp, garlic, and spices. Used as a dip for beef hotpot and as a cooking sauce for stir-fries. Available at the food stalls and specialty food shops on Paifang Street. A unique and travel-friendly souvenir.

Teochew Antiques & Ceramics

Chaozhou has a strong tradition of antique dealing — the city was historically wealthy and the Teochew diaspora has long collected antiques from their homeland. Paifang Street and the surrounding old city lanes have numerous antique shops selling traditional ceramics, carved wood, and Teochew crafts. Browse carefully and bargain hard — your driver can advise on quality.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts of Chaozhou

Traditional crafts and cultural traditions of China's Teochew cultural capital.

🍵 Teochew Kung Fu Tea Ceremony (工夫茶)

Recognized by UNESCO as part of China's intangible cultural heritage. The Teochew Kung Fu Tea ceremony is one of the most elaborate and refined tea ceremonies in the world — involving a special clay teapot, tiny porcelain cups, and a precise sequence of brewing, pouring, and serving procedures. The ceremony takes 10–15 minutes and is accompanied by traditional music. Tea houses on Paifang Street offer live demonstrations.

Where to experience: Traditional tea houses on Paifang Street, Chaozhou Tea Culture Center.

🎭 Chaozhou Opera (潮剧)

Recognized by UNESCO as part of China's intangible cultural heritage. Chaozhou opera — performed in the Teochew dialect with elaborate costumes, distinctive musical accompaniment (using the traditional nyin (弦) instrument), and a unique vocal style — is one of China's oldest and most distinctive regional opera forms. Performances at the Chaozhou People's Theatre are open to visitors.

Where to experience: Chaozhou People's Theatre, Teochew Opera Museum.

🪡 Teochew Embroidery (潮绣)

Teochew embroidery — a distinct regional tradition within the broader Chinese embroidery art — is characterized by intricate three-dimensional effects, bold use of gold and silver thread, and designs that feature dragons, phoenixes, and floral motifs. The embroidery tradition is particularly associated with the Teochew wedding customs — elaborate embroidered items are essential for traditional Teochew weddings.

Where to find: Embroidery workshops near the old city, Paifang Street craft shops.

🏠 Teochew Clan Hall (祠堂) Architecture

The Teochew clan hall (祠堂) — ancestral halls built by Teochew clan organizations — are among the most elaborate examples of traditional Chinese architecture. Featuring painted and carved wooden brackets, stone carvings, elaborate roof ridge ornaments, and courtyard layouts reflecting Teochew cosmological beliefs, these halls are both religious spaces and architectural masterpieces. Many of Chaozhou's original clan halls still stand along Paifang Street and the surrounding lanes.

Where to see: Paifang Street, old city lanes near Kaiyuan Temple.

🥢 Teochew Culinary Traditions

The Teochew culinary tradition — recognized as a UNESCO intangible cultural heritage element — encompasses a complex philosophy of flavors, cooking techniques, and food culture. The principle of "salty and fragrant" (咸香), the use of fermented and preserved ingredients, the mastery of the hotpot and steaming techniques, and the elaborate tea and dessert culture together constitute a complete culinary system unique to the Teochew people.

Where to experience: Traditional restaurants throughout Chaozhou, Paifang Street food stalls, morning wet market.

🎋 Traditional Teochew Woodcarving

Teochew woodcarving — used extensively in the decoration of clan halls, temples, and traditional houses — is one of the finest woodcarving traditions in China. Carved in deep relief with Buddhist figures, dragon and phoenix motifs, and narrative scenes, the woodcarving is characterized by extraordinary detail and technical mastery. The carved wooden brackets in the Kaiyuan Temple and the clan halls of Paifang Street are outstanding examples.

Where to see: Kaiyuan Temple, Teochew clan halls on Paifang Street.

Chaozhou Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Chaozhou like a local.

🌅

Start at the Morning Wet Market

The morning wet market near the Paifang Street entrance is at its most vivid before 8 AM — the light is soft, the vendors are active, and you can experience the real rhythm of Chaozhou daily life. After the market, have breakfast at the rice noodle stall inside the market, then walk to Kaiyuan Temple before the tourist groups arrive. This is the authentic Chaozhou morning.

🍵

Spend an Hour at the Kung Fu Tea House

Don't rush through Paifang Street. Find one of the old Kung Fu Tea houses and spend an hour — order the ceremony demonstration, try several varieties of Phoenix Dancong oolong, and watch the street life through the window. The tea masters at these tea houses are happy to explain the ceremony and the tea. This is the most authentic cultural experience Chaozhou has to offer.

🦆

Eat Beef Hotpot at the Source

The best Chaozhou beef hotpot is at the restaurants near the old city gate — run by families who have been slicing and serving beef in the same way for generations. Ask your driver to take you to the one with the most locals. The quality of the beef — and the way it's sliced — is extraordinary. Don't dip in anything but the shacha sauce.

🌉

Walk the City Wall at Sunset

The walk along the Chaozhou city wall from Dongzheng Gate east to Xiangzi Bridge at sunset is one of the most beautiful urban walks in southern China. The view of the Han River in the evening light, with the bridge's illuminated pavilions coming on one by one as the sun sets, is unforgettable. Combine with the Han River night cruise for the full experience.

🍜

Eat Rice Noodles for Breakfast

The rice noodle (粿条) stall inside the morning wet market — where the noodles are made fresh that morning and served in clear beef broth — is one of the great cheap breakfast experiences in China. Follow the locals, point at what you want, and eat standing at the small table. The beef is sliced to order and the broth is extraordinary.

🛕

Visit Kaiyuan Temple Before 8:30 AM

Kaiyuan Temple is one of the oldest Buddhist temples in Guangdong, but it is often crowded with tour groups by mid-morning. Arrive before 8:30 AM when the temple is quiet, the morning light is beautiful, and you can experience the temple as a place of worship rather than a tourist site. Monks are often visible at morning prayers at this hour.
