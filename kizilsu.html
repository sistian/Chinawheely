Name Origin

Kizilsu takes its name from the Kizilsu river, which in Turkic means "red water". The autonomous prefecture was established in 1954 for the Kyrgyz people of the region and is China's only Kyrgyz autonomous prefecture.

Education & Universities

Higher education is provided by Kizilsu Normal University and the Kizilsu Vocational and Technical College, which focus on teacher training, agriculture and veterinary work in a region that is overwhelmingly pastoral.

Getting Around

Getting around Kizilsu is by road. The southern Xinjiang rail line passes through Atushi and links the prefecture to Kashgar to the west and to Korla to the east, and long-distance coaches cover the county seats of Artux, Yarkand, Jiashi, Bachu, Wuqia and Tashkurgan. The prefecture has no passenger airport of its own, so most visitors arrive by rail or road from Kashgar or Aksu.

Natural Resources & Environment

Kizilsu is a high mountain region where the Pamir plateau meets the Tian Shan. Mount Muztagh Ata (慕士塔格峰, 7,546 m) and Mount Kongur (公格尔峰, 7,719 m) stand over the region, and the valley floors hold the high-altitude lakes of Bulun-kol and Karakul, together with the glacier-fed rivers that make the oases of Atushi and Artux possible.

Food & Shopping in Kizilsu

What to eat and what to bring home from Kizilsu

🍽️ Must-Try Kizilsu Cuisine

Kizilsu's table is Central Asian: pit-roast meat (缸子肉), hand-pulled noodles, round flatbread baked in a clay oven, and the apricots, melons and grapes of the Yarkand oasis. Among the Kyrgyz, boorsok — meat from the pit oven — and fermented mare's milk are the two things you will be invited to try first.

🛍️ Shopping in Kizilsu

Yarkand's bazaar is the region's main stop for dried apricots, raisins, walnuts, honey and Kashgar silk, while the Kyrgyz villages around Artux sell hand-woven felt kalpak hats, shapan coats and woven carpets. The border town of Tashkurgan sells jade and Pamir wool products.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Tea is drunk Kyrgyz-style across the prefecture: strong black or green tea poured into a shared bowl, diluted with milk, and passed round the yurt. Alongside it, shubat (soured mare's milk) and qymyz (fermented camel milk) are served to guests as a sign of hospitality.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

A private car and driver lets you start at the morning market, cross the city for lunch, and finish at an evening teahouse without worrying about parking or the last bus back to your hotel.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Kizilsu

The living traditions of Kizilsu — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The Kyrgyz Kyz kuu — the horse chase in which the young man catches the girl's kerchief — and kokpar are still played at local festivals, and the craft of making white felt kalpak hats, shapan coats and kyrgyz uru wall hangings survives in the villages of Artux and Wuqia.

Best Time to Visit Kizilsu

May to October for the high mountain roads and the lakes; September and October for clear air and the fruit harvest.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — the Pamir roads reopen as the snow clears, wildflowers appear in the valleys, and the high lakes thaw. (daytime 14.4–27.6°C, nights around 3.8°C; about 9 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the main season, with settled weather over the plateau, yurt camps and the eagle-hunting festivals. (daytime 31.5–33.5°C, nights around 19.1°C; about 13 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — the clearest air of the year on the Pamir Highway, apricot and grape harvest, and golden light on the high passes. (daytime 10.8–27.7°C, nights around -0.2°C; about 6 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–February — dry and bitterly cold, with snow closing the high passes to all but 4x4 vehicles; the oasis towns are quiet. (daytime 0.3–6°C, nights around -8.8°C; about 3 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Kizilsu

What to drink in Kizilsu, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Kizilsu

Tea is drunk Kyrgyz-style across the prefecture: strong black or green tea poured into a shared bowl, diluted with milk, and passed round the yurt. Alongside it, shubat (soured mare's milk) and qymyz (fermented camel milk) are served to guests as a sign of hospitality.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Kizilsu

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Kizilsu

🍵 Tea Houses of Kizilsu

Tea culture runs through the day in Kizilsu: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
