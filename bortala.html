Name Origin

Bortala comes from the Mongolian phrase for "brown grassland" — börö (brown) and tal (plain), a reference to the dry steppe between the Junggar mountains and Lake Sayram. The autonomous prefecture was established in 1954 for the Mongol population of the area.

Education & Universities

Higher education is provided by Bortala Vocational and Technical College and local teacher-training programmes, with a strong vocational focus on animal husbandry, veterinary science and food processing.

Getting Around

Getting around Bortala is by road. Bole, the prefectural seat, lies 524 km east of Urumqi, 219 km west of Yining and 76 km from the Kazakhstan border port of Alashankou; the Bole branch railway ends in the prefecture seat and the G30 expressway runs through it. Bole Alashankou Airport (IATA BPL) lies 18 km east of the seat, so short-haul flights from Urumqi are the quickest way in, with the national rail line and the G30 expressway serving the rest of the corridor.

Natural Resources & Environment

Bortala is a land of extremes. The lowest point in the prefecture is the floor of Lake Ebinur at 189 m, the driest salt lake in Xinjiang at around 400 km²; the highest is the 4,569 m peak of the Alatau range. Between them lie the Sayram Lake basin at 2,074 m, the steppe and gravel fans of the Dzungarian basin, and the mountain pastures that carry the region's herds in summer.

Food & Shopping in Bortala

What to eat and what to bring home from Bortala

🍽️ Must-Try Bortala Cuisine

Bortala's cooking is Mongol and Kazakh rather than Han: boiled mutton eaten with the hands (手抓肉), steamed mutton dumplings (蒸饺), buuz, and dairy from the herds — sour milk, dried curds and fermented mare's milk. The lakes and pastures also bring fresh fish, wild game and the summer milk cheese the region is known for.

🛍️ Shopping in Bortala

Bole's market sells dried milk products, smoked meat, felt kalpak hats, batuluu embroidered bags and the region's cheese. Alashankou is the place for cross-border goods and for dried fish from the lake.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Tea here is boiled with milk and salt and served from a shared bowl — chay — with shubat or qymyz alongside. In the Kazakh camps of Sayram Lake the bowl is passed in a fixed order, and the host always drinks last.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Bortala

The living traditions of Bortala — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

Mongol herding culture is intact here: the Naadam festival in summer brings wrestling, horse racing and kokpar in the county seats, while the craft of making kalpak hats, degdel felt carpets and shapan coats survives in the villages. Sayram Lake's Kazakh camps keep the Askiya horse games alive in summer.

Best Time to Visit Bortala

June to September for the high pastures and Sayram Lake; July and August for the Naadam festivals and the summer camps.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — the steppe greens up, the Sayram Lake road clears of snow and the herders begin moving towards the mountains. (daytime 5.2–25.1°C, nights around -4.8°C; about 20 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the main season: yurt camps on the lake, Naadam and Askiya festivals, and long daylight on the Alatau pastures. (daytime 30–31.7°C, nights around 14.9°C; about 26 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — golden steppe, clear air over the lake and the last of the warm-weather herding before the frosts. (daytime 3.1–23.9°C, nights around -5°C; about 14 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–February — severe cold, snow and wind; the mountain roads close and the towns turn quiet, though the winter horse racing still draws crowds. (daytime -9.6–-5.4°C, nights around -20.5°C; about 7 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Bortala

What to drink in Bortala, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Bortala

Tea here is boiled with milk and salt and served from a shared bowl — chay — with shubat or qymyz alongside. In the Kazakh camps of Sayram Lake the bowl is passed in a fixed order, and the host always drinks last.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Bortala

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Bortala

🍵 Tea Houses of Bortala

Tea culture runs through the day in Bortala: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
