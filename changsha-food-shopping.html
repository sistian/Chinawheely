Overview: Eating and Shopping in Changsha

Changsha cooks with a documented two-thousand-year record. The Qin-era bamboo slips from Mawangdui list dozens of soup preparations, the Spring and Autumn poem Chu Ci names smoke and grilled meats, and the modern kitchen still favours knife work, fresh-chopped chilli and heavy use of pickled vegetables. This page covers six foods and four places to shop, each with a founding year, a street length or a shop count attached.

What to eat

Huo Gong Dian snack house (火宫殿)

The best-known snack complex in Changsha's old city, occupying the protected core of the Xiaoximen historic district. Its origin is religious rather than commercial: a fire-god temple was completed in 1747 on Pozi Street, and the temple festival honouring Zhu Rong was listed in 2008 among the second batch of national intangible heritage. The house became the official reference for Changsha stinky tofu.

Where: Pozi Street, Tianxin District, inside the Xiaoximen historic conservation area, roughly 600 metres west of Huangxing Road.

Changsha stinky tofu (长沙臭豆腐)

Fermented tofu sold in two skin colours, yellow and black, with the black variety dominating the Changsha market. The dish is always dressed with chopped chilli, giving the plate a decided heat. Production runs through a brine culture of rice-wash water and old water spinach, aged about 6 months at room temperature in the classic method, with commercial strains cutting the fermentation to roughly 1 month.

Where: Stalls across Pozi Street and the Huangxing South Road pedestrian street; the Huo Gong Dian counter is the acknowledged official example.

Chopped chilli fish head (剁椒鱼头)

A head of large fish steamed under a mound of Hunan chopped chilli, counted as the flagship plate of one of China's 8 major cuisines. The version from Xiangtan is generally held to be the best of the regional styles. A repeated origin story credits the mathematician Huang Zongxian, who stayed with a farming family in the Qing period and had his own cook reproduce the dish at home.

Where: Hunan restaurants across Changsha, most densely around Wuyi Square and the riverside strips in Yuelu District.

Changsha rice noodles (长沙米粉)

Rice noodles made in 2 local forms, fried and in broth, both dressed with a large quantity of chopped chilli. Within Hunan the noodle width changes from town to town, and the noodles around Yueyang are described as broader and softer than those served in Changsha. Changde in the north of the province is the better-known home of beef rice noodles, a style frequently cited alongside the Changsha versions.

Where: Street-side noodle shops across the city, with the highest concentration in the old town around Pozi Street, Nanmenkou and the Wuyi Square area.

Cha Yan Yue Se milk-tea shop (茶颜悦色)

A Changsha tea-drink chain founded in 2013 by Lu Liang, run as a direct-operated brand rather than a franchise. Its stated principle is Chinese tea prepared by Western methods, and its cups carry classical paintings while its product names use literary Chinese. The menu is split into a cream-topped line and a foam-topped line, and branches are confined to Hunan plus Hubei, Jiangsu, Chongqing, Shenzhen, Guangzhou and Hefei.

Where: Shops throughout Changsha, with dense coverage on Huangxing South Road, Pozi Street and the Wuyi Square underground retail area.

Hunan cuisine (湘菜)

One of China's 8 major regional cuisines, with a written history of more than 2,000 years. The Spring and Autumn poem Chu Hun lists roast, grilled, simmered, fried and steamed methods, and Western Han bamboo slips excavated at Mawangdui record 7 categories of soup. Despite its reputation, only about one fifth of classical Hunan dishes cook with chilli, and its knife work has 10 base shapes.

Where: Restaurants across the province, with the densest concentration of Hunan kitchens inside Changsha, the provincial capital.

Where to shop

Pozi Street (坡子街)

A roughly 1,000-metre snack and commercial street running from Huangxing South Road west to the Xiangjiang Avenue in Tianxin District. The street was already the core of the settlement in the Warring States period and was burned in the 1938 Wenyi fire; in 1971 the upper and lower sections were merged with Xiaoximen Zhengjie and Xiaozheng Matou into one street. A late-1990s rebuild put up Ming and Qing imitation architecture.

Where: Tianxin District, running west from Huangxing South Road to the riverside Xiangjiang Avenue.

Huangxing South Road pedestrian street (黄兴南路步行商业街)

The city's principal shopping street, about 830 metres long and roughly 24 metres wide, running from Simenkou down to Nanmenkou. It covers close to 10,000 square metres of plaza and around 250,000 square metres of retail floor area. The road was laid out in the 1930s and renamed for the republican figure Huang Xing in 1934, and it was converted to a pedestrian zone in June 2001.

Where: Tianxin District, on the southern section of Huangxing Road between Simenkou and Nanmenkou.

Xiaoximen historic district (小西门历史文化街区)

A conservation area on the eastern bank of the Xiangjiang whose protected core is the Huo Gong Dian snack house. Its street network runs along 6 named lanes, Pozi Street, Hongjiajing, Fanxi Alley, Xueyuan Street, Xialijia Po and Sanwang Street, a set that still follows the city's oldest urban geometry. Reconstruction of the area has drawn local complaint over the demolition of pre-war buildings.

Where: Tianxin District, immediately west of the middle section of the Huangxing South Road pedestrian street.

Orange Isle (橘子洲)

A long sandbar in the middle of the Xiang River in Yuelu District, about 5 kilometres north to south, 320 metres across at its widest and 170 metres at its narrowest. The isle is recorded as forming in 305 CE and was known for southern mandarins in the Tang period. It joined the Yuelu Mountain scenic area in 2001, lost its five-star rating in 2016 and regained it in 2017.

Where: Yuelu District, in the Xiang River opposite the city centre, with a sightseeing train circling the island from 07:00.

Eating and shopping in Changsha: practical notes

Treat Pozi Street and the Huangxing South Road pedestrian street as one continuous walk rather than two destinations. The pedestrian street runs 830 metres from Simenkou to Nanmenkou and meets Pozi Street at its southern mouth, so the snack counters of Pozi Street can be combined with the mall floors of Huangxing in a single afternoon. The Huogongdian temple festival falls on the twenty-third day of the sixth lunar month and was inscribed on the national intangible heritage list in 2008; the streets around it run late into the evening on that date.

Crossing to Orange Isle needs its own plan. The sandbar is 5 kilometres long, so the sightseeing train that loops the island from 07:00 is the sensible way to cover ground rather than walking end to end. Metro Line 2 opened in April 2014 with a station on the island, and Line 1 and Line 2 cross at Wuyi Square, where a 30-metre flowerbed marked the original 1952 street island and the metro interchange now stands.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Changsha best known for eating?

Changsha is the provincial capital of Hunan and the centre of Hunan cuisine, a school documented for more than two thousand years. Its most cited dishes are chopped chilli fish head, which the Xiangtan version is said to lead, and stinky tofu, served in two skin colours with chopped red chilli. Rice noodles appear fried or in broth, and the city also runs the well-known Cha Yan Yue Se tea chain founded in 2013.

Where should I eat stinky tofu in Changsha?

The Huo Gong Dian counter on Pozi Street is the acknowledged official example of the Changsha style, which uses mostly black-skinned tofu and always chopped chilli. The stall sits at the protected core of the Xiaoximen historic district, the same site where a fire-god temple was completed in 1747. Vendor stalls also line the Huangxing South Road pedestrian street and the surrounding old-town lanes.

Is the fire and chilli in Hunan food all the same?

No. Sources on Hunan cooking note that only around one fifth of classical dishes are prepared with chilli, and that some use it purely for fragrance rather than heat. The style is also defined by strict ingredient grading, where a two-inch vegetable shoot counts as a vegetable heart and anything under one inch a vegetable stem, and by knife work with ten base shapes including dice, shreds, mince and flowers.

How long do I need for Pozi Street and Huangxing Road?

Pozi Street runs about 1,000 metres from Huangxing South Road to the Xiangjiang Avenue, and the pedestrian section of Huangxing Road is about 830 metres long and 24 metres wide with roughly 250,000 square metres of retail space. Walking both end to end takes under an hour, so leaving half a day covers the snacks, the malls and the ten bronze Old Changsha sculptures on the pedestrian street.

Is Orange Isle worth the trip from downtown Changsha?

It is a river island 5 kilometres long and up to 320 metres wide, part of the Yuelu Mountain scenic area since 2001. It lost its five-star scenic designation in August 2016 over safety and service standards and regained it in December 2017 after an expert review. Metro Line 2 has served an island station since April 2014, and a sightseeing train circles the sandbar daily from 07:00.
