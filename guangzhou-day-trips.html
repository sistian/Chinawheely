Guangzhou sits in the middle of the Pearl River Delta, and the geography is generous: within ninety minutes you can be on a dead volcano, in a hot spring valley, or in a forested hill park that the city itself calls its southern lung. These are not token excursions. Xiqiao Mountain has been the religious and scholarly centre of south China for fifteen centuries, Conghua's hot springs have been drawing people since before the Song dynasty, and the Dafu Mountain park is large enough to lose a whole morning in.

Xiqiao Mountain (西樵山)

Xiqiao is a dead volcano 40 to 50 million years old rising abruptly out of the flat Pearl River Delta plain, 68 km from Guangzhou and about an hour and a half by car across the Foshan side of the delta. It covers 14 square kilometres with 72 peaks, the highest being the Dacheng at 346 m, and the geology gives it 232 springs and 28 waterfalls, among them the Yunya Feipu that was one of the eight famous scenes of Guangzhou in the Qing. The mountain was also a Neolithic quarry of some importance, producing the double-shouldered stone tools that archaeologists now call the Xiqiao culture, and it later hosted the Southern Academy of Neo-Confucianism, where Zhu Xi and his followers taught from the late Ming. Today a 61.9 m statue of Guanyin has been erected on the summit and a 50 RMB ticket covers the whole scenic area.

How the day runs: Leave at 7:30 am and take the Nansha or Guangfo expressway west to the Xiqiao exit, which sits almost at the foot of the mountain. Buy the ticket at the visitor centre and take the sightseeing bus up rather than driving further, since the internal road is steep and narrow. Start with the Bailian Spring and the nearby climbing area, then work up towards the summit of the Dacheng peak for the view across the whole delta, which is the reason to climb on a clear day. Come back down through the Nine Dragons Grotto area and eat at the vegetarian temple restaurant near the gate, a long-standing feature of the mountain. Be back in Guangzhou by 4 pm, well before the evening traffic on the return leg.

68 km from Guangzhou · about 90 min by car from Guangzhou · full-day trip · best in October to March, when the waterfalls have some water and the temperatures are bearable; summer heat makes the exposed climbs punishing.

  • The Yunya Feipu waterfall runs hardest between April and September, so a waterfall-focused visit suits summer even if the climb does not.
  • The scenic area bus is included in the 50 RMB ticket and saves a long walk up the switchbacks.
  • Xiqiao combines easily with a stop in Foshan city, which is only a further 30 minutes and has its own ancestral temple.

Conghua Hot Springs (从化温泉)

The Conghua hot springs occupy a 14.5 square kilometre valley at the northern end of the Guangzhou–Conghua fault line, with the Liuxi River running straight through the middle of them. The water sits in fissures in Yanshan-period granite and emerges along both banks of the river and across the valley floor at between 30 and 76 degrees Celsius, carrying radon, sodium, potassium, calcium and other dissolved minerals. It has been used therapeutically for more than a thousand years and is officially credited with helping arthritis, skin conditions, high blood pressure and neuralgia. The provincial government maintains the Guangdong Hot Spring Guesthouse on the river bank, and there is a second, newer bathing area at the town of Wenquan further up the valley.

How the day runs: Set off at 8 am and follow the Guangqing expressway north to the Conghua exit, about 100 minutes with light traffic. The main bathing complexes sit along the Liuxi River in Wenquan Town, and the older ones have open-air pools fed directly from the springs, which is the point rather than a heated recreation pool. Go in the morning while the valley is still cool, spend two hours in and around the water, then walk the riverside path upstream where the temperature rises and the mineral content changes. Lunch in Wenquan town on wonton noodles and river fish. If you have time, continue 20 minutes to the Liuxi Forest Park, where the river runs through a gorge lined with bamboo. Return by 4 pm to avoid the Sunday evening rush back into the city.

80 km from Guangzhou · about 100 min by car from Guangzhou · full-day trip · best in October to April; the water is a comfortable 40 to 45 degrees Celsius in winter and can exceed 60 in summer.

  • The open-air pools at the old bathing area are the authentic mineral water; the large resort pools nearer town are reheated and less interesting.
  • The Guangzhou Metro Line 14 now reaches Conghua town, so the hot springs are also accessible without a car, though the drive is quicker.
  • Bring a towel and a change of clothes, and check the water temperature on arrival before committing to the hottest pools.

Dafu Mountain Forest Park (大夫山森林公园)

Dafu Mountain is the southern lung of Guangzhou, 9,000 mu in total of which 7,110 mu is forest, mostly planted, straddling the Panyu District boundary between the Shatou and Zhongcun subdistricts. There are nine hills, the tallest being the Dawugang at 226.6 m, the highest point in Panyu, and the park is threaded with artificial lakes and gullies that were dug out as reservoirs and swimming ponds. It is also a genuine archaeological site: halfway up the main peak there is a Southern Song dynasty tomb of Li Juchuan, preserved with its stone flagpole still carved with the names of the family's successive imperial examination successes. Over 300 species of ornamental tree and 50 kinds of flowering plant have been introduced.

How the day runs: This is a short and easy drive, 25 km south of the centre through Panyu, which makes it the most practical of the excursions. Arrive at the Shatou entrance around 9 am and take the hiking route up to the beacon tower ruin on the main peak, about 40 minutes of steady climbing. The viewpoint at the halfway level looks back across the whole southern Guangzhou plain toward the Pearl River, which is a good wide shot. Descend through the Ju Xiu Lake area to the Lotus Lake and the lakeside pavilions, and have lunch at the park's restaurants. Be back in the city by 2 pm, which still leaves the afternoon free for the Chen Clan Ancestral Hall or Shamian Island in the city centre.

25 km from Guangzhou · about 50 min by car from Guangzhou · half-day trip · best in March to May for the flower plantings, and October to December for clear air over the ridge; late spring is showery.

  • The park has several entrances; Shatou Street on the south side is the easiest to reach from the city and has the main parking.
  • The Southern Song tomb halfway up the main peak is usually unlocked only at certain times, so ask at the gate.
  • This pairs naturally with the South China Botanical Garden on the eastern side of the city for a full-day greenery route.

Baiyun Mountain (白云山)

Baiyun Mountain, the 'first scenic wonder of the City of Rams', occupies 28 square kilometres in the north of Guangzhou and is separated into five scenic areas: Moxing Ridge, the highest at 382 m, Yuntai Garden, Zhujiang Bay, Luhuitou Park and the Mingchun Valley. The name comes from the way clouds often blanket the main peak. It has been the city's mountain escape since the Ming and Qing, when wealthy Guangzhou families had villas here, and the Moxing Ridge summit now carries a radio and television tower. The ridge was also a key defensive position in the Second Sino-Japanese War. At night the whole northern city spreads below the summit, which is one of the reasons the mountain is popular in the evening.

How the day runs: This is close enough to be a proper half-day rather than an expedition, and it doubles as an evening outing. Take the Sanyuanqiao exit and drive up through the Yuntai Garden entrance area in the morning, before the buses and school groups arrive. Work up towards Moxing Ridge, which is a 20-minute climb from the Yuntai cable car or a 50-minute walk from the foothill. There is a chairlift and a cableway from Yuntai Garden up to the ridge. Have an early lunch in Yuntai Garden, which has the botanically interesting Zhujiang Bay rose garden and the Mingchun Valley scenic area with its springs and restaurants. If you time it for late afternoon, stay for sunset and the city lights, then drive down the mountain road as the switchback lighting comes on.

12 km from Guangzhou · about 30 min by car from Guangzhou · half-day trip · best in Year-round, but the summit is spectacular only in clear conditions; the city lights below are the draw in any season.

  • The road up to the mountain summit closes to private cars at certain times on weekends and holidays, so arrive early or park at the foot and use the shuttle buses.
  • Yuntai Garden's Zhujiang Bay rose garden is at its best in April and October.
  • Bring something for an evening picnic, since the mountaintop lawns are the local custom after dark.

Liwan Lake and the Xiguan Waterways (荔湾湖公园)

Liwan Lake Park was built in 1958 by municipal mayor Zhu Guang, who organised residents of the district to convert the old Lychee Bay and Pantang waterlands into four artificial lakes as flood relief for the low-lying Xiguan district. It covers 27 hectares, 62 percent of it water, and the four lakes are Xiaocui, Yucui, Ruyi and Wuxiu. The site is historically significant far beyond the park itself: Lychee Bay appears in records from around 200 BC and hosted Tang and Song imperial gardens, the Ming Tingxue Peng, and in the Qing the Haishan Xianyuan, which counted as one of the great gardens of south China. Today the restored Lychee Bay and Enning Road area around it is where the city eats late at night.

How the day runs: This is the shortest drive of the group and works as a morning or an evening rather than a full day. Arrive at the Pantang entrance around 9 am and walk the lakeside path past the Jinxiang Pavilion and the Yellow River sculpture in the middle of the lake. The rowing boats and water bikes on the four lakes are the local morning activity. Walk on to the Xiguan arcade streets around Enning Road and Shamian for the old arcade architecture, and eat late-morning dim sum in one of the old teahouses on Longjin West Road. If you time it for evening instead, return to the Lychee Bay and Enning Road food streets, which fill from around 6 pm with the city's best-known night market.

6 km from Guangzhou · about 20 min by car from Guangzhou · half-day trip · best in October to March, when the banyan and willow trees along the lakes hold their colour; the Lychee Bay evening food scene runs all year.

  • Enning Road's arcade street is the oldest surviving Xiguan commercial street and is best walked at dawn before the shutters go up.
  • The lake paths are flat, lit and popular with local walkers, which makes them an easy and safe morning walk with children.
  • Combine with Shamian Island across the water, which is a 15-minute walk from the north end of the park.

Driving around the Pearl River Delta

Guangzhou's expressways are fast and toll-gated, but the delta's urban sprawl means that the last 20 km of any trip can take as long as the first 60. Conghua and Xiqiao both need the full allowance: 100 minutes and 90 minutes respectively is realistic on a weekday morning, and considerably longer on a Sunday when the whole delta is trying to get back into the city.

Refuelling is cheap and plentiful along the expressways, but fill the tank before leaving for Conghua or Xiqiao, because the service areas on the northern routes are sparse and you will be out past the last one for a while.

Guangzhou drives on the right, and the metro reaches most of the inner sights, so a car earns its keep for the mountain and hot spring trips rather than for the city centre. Parking at Baiyun Mountain and Dafu Mountain is cheap and plentiful; downtown parking at the Chen Clan Ancestral Hall and Shamian is expensive and slow.

Planning a day trip from Guangzhou

Liwan Lake and the Xiguan Waterways, Conghua Hot Springs and the 3 stops in between set the two ends of this Guangzhou day trip route — Liwan Lake and the Xiguan Waterways sits closest at 6 km, roughly 20 minutes by car, while Conghua Hot Springs reaches furthest at 80 km, about 100 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Xiqiao Mountain works best October to March, when the waterfalls have some water and the temperatures are bearable; summer heat makes the exposed climbs punishing., while Conghua Hot Springs suits October to April; the water is a comfortable 40 to 45 degrees Celsius in winter and can exceed 60 in summer. — in Guangzhou that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

Dafu Mountain Forest Park, Baiyun Mountain, Liwan Lake and the Xiguan Waterways each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Guangzhou itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Guangzhou day trip is best for a family with children?

Dafu Mountain Forest Park. It is only 25 km from the centre, the terrain is gentle with paths around nine hills and several lakes, and it works well with a short morning visit rather than a full day. Baiyun Mountain is the other good option for families, since the Yuntai Garden area has a cableway and chairlift that take the walking out of it, and it is only 12 km from downtown.

Is Xiqiao Mountain a real hike or just a scenic park?

It is a genuine short hike with a summit and real height gain. The Dacheng peak is 346 m and the whole massif has 72 peaks across 14 square kilometres, with 232 springs and 28 waterfalls. The internal sightseeing bus takes you most of the way up, so the walk on top is manageable for most people in an hour or two. The 50 RMB entrance ticket covers the whole scenic area and the bus.

How long does the drive to Conghua Hot Springs really take?

Around 100 minutes on a weekday morning over the Guangqing expressway, and closer to two hours on a weekend afternoon return. The springs are 80 km north in a valley at the end of the Guangzhou–Conghua fault line, with the Liuxi River running through them, and the water ranges from 30 to 76 degrees Celsius depending on which pool you find.

Do I need to book the hot springs in advance?

Not for the public bathing areas in Wenquan Town, which you can simply walk into. Some of the larger resort complexes and the provincial government guesthouse do require booking, and at Chinese New Year they fill up weeks ahead. If you want a private or semi-private pool, book several days in advance and specify whether you want the open-air river-fed pools or a heated indoor one.

What is the best evening plan in Guangzhou after a half-day trip?

The Xiguan food streets. Enning Road and Lychee Bay fill from around 6 pm with the old arcade architecture as the backdrop, and the nearby Shamian Island has the best evening walk in the city, with the neo-classical buildings lit along the river. The Shamian Food Street next to the White Swan Hotel is the busiest of them, and it is a ten-minute walk from the western end of the park.

Is Baiyun Mountain worth climbing for a view, or is the cableway enough?

The cableway takes you most of the way to Moxing Ridge at 382 m, the highest point and the source of the mountain's name, and from the summit platform the view over the northern city is genuinely good on a clear day. If you have walked up from the Yuntai Garden entrance the summit feels earned, but the summit itself is a small platform and the 50-minute climb is mostly through forest.
