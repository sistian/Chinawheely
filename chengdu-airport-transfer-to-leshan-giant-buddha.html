The Leshan Giant Buddha is 71 metres tall and was carved out of a red sandstone cliff between 712 and 804 AD, at the point where the Min, Qingyi and Dadu rivers meet. It is the largest and tallest stone Buddha statue in the world, and it sits inside a scenic area of 17.88 square kilometres. Leshan is about 120 km from Chengdu by road, so a private car is the practical way to combine the two in a single day.

Chengdu Shuangliu International Airport (CTU)

140 km from the Chengdu terminal, about 125 minutes by car.

Shuangliu opened in 1957 and sits about 16 km southwest of central Chengdu. The run to Leshan goes out on the Airport Expressway, joins the G0512 Chengdu-Leshan Expressway and then follows the Min River south, dropping you at the Jiuting visitor centre beside the river below the Buddha.

Chengdu Tianfu International Airport (TFU)

155 km from the Chengdu terminal, about 140 minutes by car.

Tianfu opened in June 2021 to the southeast of Chengdu with three runways and two terminals, and international and cargo services moved there from Shuangliu. It is the longer of the two runs to Leshan, because the expressway junction sits north of the airport.

Why transfer to Leshan Giant Buddha?

The numbers on the statue itself are what people remember. The Buddha is 71 metres from the top of the head to the base of the lotus, with shoulders 24 metres wide, 5.6-metre eyebrows, 8.3-metre fingers and a head of 1,021 spiralled curls measuring 14.7 metres high and 10 metres wide. His smallest toenail is large enough for a seated person, and his instep is wide enough to hold about a hundred people. A drainage system carved into the body at the time of construction is still in working order.

The setting is as much a part of the monument as the carving. The Buddha was cut into the face of Qifeng Peak at the confluence of the Min, Qingyi and Dadu rivers, which is why the walking route that most visitors use is the old plank road down the cliff from the Lingyun Temple complex, with the river directly below. The Mahao Cliff Tombs, which show Han dynasty burial practice and the living habits of the people who carved the statue, sit inside the same managed area and are included in the admission ticket.

  • The statue is 71 metres tall, with 24-metre shoulders, and was cut into the cliff between 712 and 804 AD
  • The scenic area covers 17.88 square kilometres, including Wuyou Temple and the Mahao Cliff Tombs
  • Admission is 90 yuan per person, and the sightseeing boat on the riverside is 70 yuan
  • Leshan stands about 120 km from Chengdu, linked by the G0512 Chengdu-Leshan Expressway

What to do at the Leshan Giant Buddha once you arrive

Most drivers set down at the visitor centre on the east side of the river, and the standard route runs up to the Lingyun Temple complex, through the temple and out onto the cliff path, then down the Nine-Turn Stairway to the riverbank below. That is the full circuit and it takes three to four hours on foot, most of it on steps carved into the rock face with the water 20 metres below. Wear shoes with grip and take water, because there is little shade on the exposed cliff path.

The alternative is the boat. Sightseeing boats leave the wharf below the Buddha at 70 yuan per person and take you out onto the Min River for a view back up at the statue and the cliff face, which is the perspective most photographs are taken from. Each boat carries 35 passengers, so expect a short queue at peak times. If you want the full experience, do the walk down and take the boat back rather than paying for both directions.

  • The ticket is 90 yuan and includes Wuyou Temple and the Mahao Cliff Tombs as well as the statue
  • The boat costs 70 yuan and seats 35 passengers per trip, so allow time for the queue at the wharf
  • The Nine-Turn Stairway is steep and stepped, and the walk from the visitor centre takes three to four hours
  • Early morning light on the cliff face from the river is the classic view, so arrive early if photography is the point

Arriving at Chengdu airport for the Leshan Giant Buddha

Shuangliu handles the bulk of Chengdu's domestic traffic and sits only about 16 km from downtown, which makes it the shorter of the two departure points for Leshan. It has been the city's main airport since civil aviation services moved there from Guanghan in 1957. Tianfu, which opened in June 2021 with three runways and two terminals, takes the long-haul and international services and lies southeast of the city, adding roughly 25 to 30 minutes to the run. Both are modern terminals with plenty of space, so collecting bags is quick.

Allow fifteen minutes for checked luggage and immigration before you meet your driver in the arrivals hall. The drive itself is motorway for most of the way, but the last stretch into Leshan crosses the river and follows the tourist roads, which narrow in the afternoon. Share your flight number at booking and the driver will adjust for delays. Carry water and a light layer for the boat, which sits in open air on the river, and leave the driver's contact details with someone in your group.

What the transfer costs

A long-haul transfer from Shuangliu to Leshan is typically quoted at 700 to 900 yuan for a sedan and 900 to 1,100 yuan for a van or seven-seat MPV, with the price covering fuel, tolls on the Chengdu-Leshan Expressway and the driver's time waiting at the visitor centre. A single traveller can halve the cost by sharing, and most operators also price the return journey to Chengdu at the same rate.

Day hires are billed at a fixed rate rather than through a per-kilometre meter, and waiting time at the visitor centre is normally included for the first two hours while you walk down to the river. A sedan is fine for two or three people, but note that the luggage has to fit in the boot while the passenger space stays comfortable on a two-hour motorway run, and a van or MPV is the better choice for a family group travelling with several large cases.

Give the driver the Jiuting visitor centre as the drop-off point rather than the riverside wharf, since the wharf is only reachable on foot and vehicle access there is restricted. If you plan to return the same day, agree a fixed pick-up time instead of having the driver wait, and leave yourself at least three hours for the walk down and the boat back.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is it from Chengdu airport to the Leshan Giant Buddha?

Leshan lies about 120 km from Chengdu, and the G0512 Chengdu-Leshan Expressway connects the two directly. From Shuangliu Airport the road distance is roughly 140 km, taking about two hours, and from the newer Tianfu Airport it is closer to 155 km and two and a half hours. The motorway is fast; the slower part is the final approach into Leshan.

How much does it cost to enter the Leshan Giant Buddha scenic area?

Admission is 90 yuan per person, and that ticket covers the Giant Buddha itself along with Wuyou Temple and the Mahao Cliff Tombs. The sightseeing boat on the river is a separate charge of 70 yuan per person, and the boat carries 35 passengers per trip, so a short queue is common in the middle of the day.

How long does it take to walk down to the Buddha?

The full circuit from the Jiuting visitor centre up to the Lingyun Temple and down the Nine-Turn Stairway to the river takes three to four hours on foot, because almost the whole descent is stepped. The path is carved into the cliff with the river about 20 metres below in places. Taking the boat back from the wharf cuts the return to a few minutes.

Is there a train from Chengdu to Leshan instead?

There are several rail options, including the Chengdu-Mianyang-Leshan intercity railway and the Chengdu-Guiyang high-speed railway, both of which serve Leshan. A car transfer is still worth considering if you want to be at the cliff path early, because the morning light on the rock face is the best hour for the view from the river and the first trains arrive well after sunrise.

Can I visit Mount Emei in the same trip?

Mount Emei is the other half of this World Heritage site and stands about 40 km from Leshan, but it needs a full day of its own for the cable car and the summit walk. Doing both from a Chengdu airport transfer in one outing would mean skipping either the Giant Buddha or the mountain. Pick the Buddha for the half-day and add Mount Emei on a separate trip if you have the time.
