Overview: Photography in Dalian

Dalian photographs in two registers. There is the port city of 1898 and 1899, with its star-shaped square, its ten radiating streets of Russian, Japanese and Renaissance buildings, its timber trams and its working harbour, and there is the maritime city of today, with the largest public square in the world on reclaimed salt flats and a shore road running 45 kilometres to a port that has been fought over since the Qing. Between them, on a 663-metre dead volcano with a Goguryeo wall on top of it, is the one place in the city where the geology is older than everything else.

Xinghai Square (星海广场)

Xinghai Square in Shahekou district is the largest city square in the world and the largest public square in Asia, covering 1.76 million square metres. The ground was the abandoned salt works of Xinghai Bay, and the reclamation began on 16 July 1993, using construction waste to fill 114 hectares and to develop 62 more. The works were completed on 30 June 1997, when the centre carried the largest white marble nine-dragon huabiao in China, put up to mark the handover of Hong Kong. The central area is 45,000 square metres, with an inner ring of 199.9 metres diameter and an outer ring of 239.9, and the square lies between the convention centre to the north and Xinghai Bay to the south, 500 metres from the central point of Central Avenue. The musical fountain, the century city sculpture and the relief of a thousand real footprints are all on the ground.

best light: Sunset, when the fountain is running and the sea beyond the square takes the last light. · reclaimed city square

Getting there: In Shahekou district on the south side of the city between Central Avenue and the bay, with metro line 1 at the square's own station.

  • The 199.9 and 239.9 metre rings are the scale of the place, and a low viewpoint across the grass shows them.
  • The footprint relief needs a close frame rather than a wide one, and the fountain needs the sunset behind it.
  • The square is enormous, so the interesting photographs come from the edges, not the middle.

Zhongshan Square and its Ten Streets (中山广场)

The square in Zhongshan district is a circle 213 metres across with ten streets radiating from it, and it was built by Russia in 1899 and named Nikolaevskaya Square after the first mayor, Sakharov. Under the Japanese occupation it became the Great Square, and after 1945 it was renamed for Sun Yat-sen. The plan copies the star-shaped places of Paris, and the ten streets keep their Japanese-era names in translation: Harima, Satsuma, East Park, Yamagata, Shikishima, Oku, Oyama, Suruga, Nishi and Echigo, which are now Yan'an, Jiefang, Luxun, Renmin, Qi Yi, Minsheng, Shanghai, Minkang, Zhongshan and Yuguang roads. Between 1908 and 1935 ten landmark buildings were put up around it in classical, Gothic and Renaissance styles, and the group was listed as the Dalian Zhongshan Square modern building complex in 2001.

best light: Late afternoon, when the ten radiating streets each catch the sun at a different angle. · star-shaped civic square

Getting there: In Zhongshan district in the eastern part of the city, the centre of the radiating street plan and a short walk from the harbour.

  • Stand in the middle and use a wide lens; the radiating streets are the composition, not any single building.
  • The late afternoon sun lights the east-facing streets first, so walk the plan rather than circling it.
  • The ten buildings are numbered and documented; picking two and shooting them together shows the plan better than shooting all ten.

The Timber Trams (大连有轨电车)

Dalian runs one of the few tram systems in China that has never been interrupted, and it opened on 25 September 1909 as a trial line of 2.45 kilometres from the electric amusement park, the former zoo, along Taisho street to the great pier. The first fleet was 30 type 11 cars with bodies built by Preston in the United States, chassis by Montagu Gibson in Britain and electrical equipment by AEG in Germany, timber double-deck cars rated for 72 seated and 145 loaded, nicknamed the great American wooden cage. By 1945 the network ran 11 lines with three depots and two workshops, and most of it was cut in the 1970s as the city grew, leaving lines 201, 202 and 203 and the original cars from the 1930s.

best light: Any light, and the hour that matters is when the doors open at a stop. · street and rolling stock

Getting there: Lines 201, 202 and 203 run through the centre, with the original 1909 route between the zoo end and the pier end still recognisable.

  • Ride rather than watch from a platform; the interior of the 1930s cars is the subject.
  • The 1930s cars are wooden and dark, so the light inside is all from the doors and the roof lights.
  • The overhead wiring and the poles are part of the street frame and are usually cropped out by mistake.

The Harbour and the Old Pier (大连港)

Dalian harbour was built in 1898 at the end of the Qing on a natural ice-free position at the strait between the Liaodong and Shandong peninsulas, and that position is the whole of the city's later history. It works now with seven specialist terminals and 48 berths to a maximum depth of 17.5 metres, 82 modern specialist berths of which 57 take vessels of 10,000 tonnes and over, 149 kilometres of port rail, and harbour waters of more than 310 square kilometres. Throughput rose from 32 million tonnes in 1980 to more than 320 million tonnes in 2010, and the port trades with more than 160 countries through over 300 ports, with 81 container lines. Behind the quays there is the old stone shore and the fishing fleet, which is the part that is not a container terminal.

best light: The fishing boats come in through the afternoon, so the late light and the working water are the same thing. · working port

Getting there: On the south shore of the peninsula, with the old pier and the harbour viewing terrace in the middle of the working waterfront.

  • The working quays and the old stone shore are two hundred metres apart and photograph very differently.
  • Shooting from the terrace above the container yards gives the scale; shooting from the pier gives the texture.
  • The 1898 date explains most of the street plan behind the waterfront, which is worth a frame in itself.

Laohutan and the Xiuyue Gardens (老虎滩与秀月山庄)

The park began in 1959 as a walking garden on the eastern shore and became a municipal seaside park; in 1980 a floating pier was built in the bay to the east and sea excursions started. The adjoining Xiuyue Villa park was laid out in 1984 over 539.4 hectares between the original garden and the Fujiazhuang park, and the two were joined in 1989. The site was renamed Dalian Hutang Paradise in 1991, opened in May 1992, and became the Dalian Laohutan Ocean Park on 8 August 2002 with the polar and marine animal aquarium, at that time the largest of its kind in the world. It is now a national 5A scenic area, and the sea excursion from the old pier is still the outdoor half of it.

best light: Overcast is fine here; the subject is the structure and the water, not the sun. · seaside park and aquarium

Getting there: On the eastern shore of the city, with the pier and the boat landing inside the park and the Xiuyue gardens on the far side of it.

  • The pier and the aquarium are on opposite sides of the park, so allow fifteen minutes between them.
  • The 1984 garden side is the quieter half and has the older planting.
  • In winter the coastal walk around the park works as well as the tanks.

Lüshun Harbour and the Baiyu Tower (旅顺港与白玉山塔)

The port of Lüshun, also called Port Arthur, is an ice-free harbour at the foot of Baiyu Mountain, built by the Qing government for the Beiyang Fleet from the 1880s. The official responsible, Yuan Baoling, called it the first hazard of the Beiyang, and the dock approved in 1880 was completed in September 1890; that dock survives and was listed in 2013 as the seventh batch of national key cultural relics under the name of the old Lüshun dock site. The Baiyu Mountain Tower was built in 1907 by the Japanese army after the Russo-Japanese War to commemorate 22,723 of its dead, raised with 250,000 yen subscribed in Japan and with granite, cement, timber and steel shipped from there. It was proposed for demolition several times after 1949 and survived, and it is now a patriotic education base listed as a Dalian municipal protected unit since 1985.

best light: Late afternoon, with the tower side-lit against the water and the harbour behind it. · naval harbour and memorial tower

Getting there: In Lüshun district at the southern tip of the peninsula, 45 kilometres from the centre, with the tower on the southern slope of Baiyu Mountain above the harbour.

  • The tower was designed as a memorial and photographs like one; a low angle against the sky is the frame.
  • The harbour below it is where the Russian base stood during the war of 1904, which is what the view is really showing.
  • The old dock site is a separate stop, and it is the piece of industrial archaeology worth the drive.

The Stone Forest on the Sea (金石滩)

The Jinshitan national tourism resort in the north of the peninsula covers 62 square kilometres of land and 58 of sea, and holds the status of a national key scenic area, a national tourism resort, a national 5A site and a national geopark; CNN put it among the forty most beautiful sights in China. The landform is the point: marine erosional cliffs and sedimentary rock formed over hundreds of millions of years, a formation called the stone forest on the sea, which gave the district its name after it had been known as Manjatan. It was made a county-level nature reserve in March 1986 and a municipal one in November 1987, and the State Council approved it as the second batch of national key scenic areas in August 1988. Its sections include the Golden Coast, the Jinshi Garden, the coastal geopark, a wax museum, the anatomy museum and the Discovery Kingdom theme park.

best light: Low tide, and the last two hours of daylight on the cliffs. · marine erosional cliffs

Getting there: 50 kilometres north-east of the centre in Jinzhou district, on the metro extension and by road, with the coastal geopark paths along the cliff line.

  • The geopark paths are the reason to come; the beach is interchangeable with any other.
  • Low tide exposes the bedding the cliffs are cut from, so check the tide table before choosing the hour.
  • The cliffs are a long subject; a telephoto compresses them into layers that a wide lens cannot.

Gear and planning for photographing Dalian

The kit divides cleanly between the city and the coast. In the city the work is street-scale, with the ten radiating streets from Zhongshan Square and the frames inside the timber trams, so a 35mm or 50mm and a fast lens for the tram interiors will do most of the work. On the coast and at the stone forest the subject is distance, so a 70-200 is the tool, and a polarizer earns its keep on the wet rock and on the harbour water. For the harbour itself a tripod is useful at blue hour, and for the timber trams the same tripod is worth carrying because the doors and the roof lights are the only light inside. Dalian is maritime and windy, and a hood is not optional on the coast.

Plan with the coast in mind, because the two directions do not connect. The old port, the square and the trams are inside the city and can be done in a day on foot. The star square, the trams and the harbour fit together into one route of a few kilometres, which is the standard first morning. Everything else, the pier at Laohutan to the east and Lüshun and the stone forest to the north and the south, is a drive of between 45 and 50 kilometres and should be a whole day of its own. The shoulder months, when the resorts are empty and the light is still good, suit the coast; the summer crowds belong to the beach and to nothing else.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the largest square in Dalian?

Xinghai Square, which is the largest city square in the world and the largest public square in Asia at 1.76 million square metres. It was built on the abandoned salt works of Xinghai Bay, with the reclamation starting on 16 July 1993 and the works completed on 30 June 1997, when the centre carried the largest white marble nine-dragon huabiao in China for the handover of Hong Kong. The inner ring is 199.9 metres across and the outer 239.9.

What is the square in the middle of the city?

Zhongshan Square in Zhongshan district, a circle 213 metres across with ten streets radiating from it, modelled on the star-shaped places of Paris. Russia built it in 1899 and named it Nikolaevskaya Square, Japan renamed it the Great Square, and after 1945 it was named for Sun Yat-sen. Ten landmark buildings in classical, Gothic and Renaissance styles were built around it between 1908 and 1935, and the group was listed in 2001.

Why are the trams worth photographing?

Because they have never stopped. The first trial line opened on 25 September 1909 from the zoo to the pier, 2.45 kilometres long, with 30 timber double-deck cars built by Preston in the United States on Montagu Gibson chassis with AEG electrical equipment, rated for 72 seated and nicknamed the great American wooden cage. By 1945 there were 11 lines. Most of the network was cut in the 1970s and lines 201, 202 and 203 with the 1930s cars are what survived.

What is at Lüshun worth the drive?

The harbour, the old dock and the tower above it. The port was built by the Qing for the Beiyang Fleet from the 1880s, the dock approved in 1880 was finished in September 1890 and is listed as a national key cultural relic, and the Baiyu Mountain Tower was built by the Japanese army in 1907 to commemorate 22,723 dead, with 250,000 yen subscribed in Japan and the materials shipped there.

Is the stone forest on the sea really worth going to?

The geology is what makes it. Jinshitan covers 62 square kilometres of land and 58 of sea, and the marine erosional cliffs and sedimentary rock formed over hundreds of millions of years in what is locally called the stone forest on the sea, which gave the district its name. It is a national key scenic area, a national resort, a national 5A site and a national geopark, and CNN put it among the forty most beautiful sights in China.

Can you walk up a hill for a view?

Dahei Shan is 25 kilometres from the centre and 663 metres high, the highest peak of the southern Liaodong peninsula and an extinct volcano about 140 million years old with two craters on the summit. On the top stands the city of Pisha, a Goguryeo defensive wall five kilometres long at three to five metres high. The stepped path takes about 90 minutes and the summit looks over the sea on two sides.
