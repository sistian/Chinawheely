No other site in China justifies a long transfer like the Great Wall does, because the closest good sections sit far outside the city. Mutianyu is 70 km northeast of central Beijing in Huairou District, while Badaling lies north-west in Yanqing, so this is a genuine half-day road trip rather than a city transfer. What you arrive at is a Ming-era granite rampart, 7 to 8.5 metres high, with 22 watchtowers packed into a 2,250-metre restored stretch.

Beijing Capital International Airport (PEK)

80 km from the Beijing terminal, about 90 minutes by car.

Mutianyu lies about 70 km northeast of central Beijing, so the run from Capital goes east on the Airport Expressway and the Fourth Ring, then north up the G11 Beijing-Chengdu Expressway through Huairou and out along the Huairou-Mutianyu road to the car park at the foot of the wall.

Beijing Daxing International Airport (PKX)

110 km from the Beijing terminal, about 105 minutes by car.

Daxing sits 46 km south of the centre, so the trip to the wall is longer than from Capital. The route runs north to the ring roads and then follows the same G11 corridor through Huairou, with the last stretch on the two-lane mountain road that climbs to the Mutianyu ticket office.

Why transfer to the Great Wall?

The Mutianyu section was first built in the mid-6th century during the Northern Qi, which makes it older than the Badaling stretch most visitors picture, and it was rebuilt in granite under the Ming general Xu Da. The wall stands 7 to 8.5 metres high with a walkway 4 to 5 metres wide, and 22 watchtowers are strung along the restored 2,250-metre run that most visitors climb. Almost every tower here is a solid brick block rather than the hollow shell found at Badaling.

Scale explains the effort. The 2012 national survey mapped 10,051 surviving wall sections, 1,764 ramparts and trenches, 29,510 individual buildings and 2,211 forts and passes, with the wall and trenches together spanning 21,196 kilometres, and up to 25,000 watchtowers are thought to have been built along the whole system. Badaling alone drew close to 10 million visitors in 2018, which is why a daily cap of 65,000 was imposed from 2019.

Choosing the right section matters as much as the trip itself. Badaling is the most famous stretch and the first one opened to the public, with a cable car and a railway from Beijing, but it is busy and heavily restored. Mutianyu, with its forest setting, rebuilt watchtowers and two cableway lines, feels like the wall most people imagine, and it is the shorter run from the airport.

  • Mutianyu sits about 70 km northeast of central Beijing and dates from the Northern Qi period, with a major Ming rebuild
  • The wall stands 7 to 8.5 metres high and the walkway is 4 to 5 metres wide
  • The restored Mutianyu run is 2,250 metres long and holds 22 watchtowers
  • The full surveyed Great Wall covers 21,196 km of wall and trenches with 29,510 individual buildings
  • Badaling capped daily visitors at 65,000 from 2019 after nearly 10 million visits in 2018

What to do at the Great Wall once you arrive

Buy the entry ticket and decide on transport up the hill before you start walking. Mutianyu has a chairlift and a toboggan running up to the wall at the north tower, plus a shuttle bus between the visitor centre and the climb. The classic route is to ride up, walk west along the walkway towards Tower 13 and the furthest restored section, then return towards the steepest stairway near the entrance.

Give the climb two hours. From the ticket office the first set of steps rises sharply to the first watchtower, and the masonry gets rougher and more original the further west you go. The wall continues past the last restored tower into an unrestored branch that runs along the ridge, which is closed to visitors but visible from the walkway.

  • Adult admission at Mutianyu is 45 yuan, with reduced rates of 25 yuan for children and students
  • The cable car and toboggan return ticket costs 120 yuan for adults, 100 yuan one way
  • A shuttle bus runs between the ticket plaza and the foot of the steps for 15 yuan
  • Go early: the wall faces north-east, so morning light is best and the crowds arrive after 10:00

Arriving at Beijing airport for the Great Wall

Allow a full morning or afternoon rather than squeezing the wall into a short layover. From Capital International the drive to Mutianyu covers roughly 80 km and takes about an hour and a half in normal traffic, longer on a wet weekend when the mountain road backs up. Daxing is a further 30 km south of the centre, so allow nearly two hours from there.

Meet the driver inside the terminal, collect any checked luggage before you walk out, and tell the driver which cable car you plan to use so the return meeting point is clear. The car parks at the scenic area fill up by mid-morning in autumn and during the May holiday, and finding a space yourself adds 20 to 30 minutes that the transfer absorbs for you.

What the transfer costs

A private car from Capital International to Mutianyu is usually quoted at 300 to 420 yuan for a sedan, and 380 to 520 yuan from Daxing, with tolls on the expressway and the airport road included in the figure. A van or MPV for a family runs 380 to 550 yuan from Capital. Most operators price a wall run as a fixed day rate covering roughly eight hours of vehicle time, so the waiting time while you are on the wall is included.

If you plan to combine the wall with the Forbidden City or Tiananmen Square, a full-day hire is usually cheaper than two separate one-way transfers, and the driver can hold the car in the car park while you walk the section.

Give the driver the scenic area name in Chinese, 慕田峪长城, rather than only the English, because the Mutianyu and Badaling entrances are often called the same thing on signs and local maps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Great Wall section should I go to from Beijing airport?

Mutianyu is the shorter run, about 70 km from the centre, and it has denser watchtowers, forest views and two cableway lines. Badaling lies further north-west, is more heavily restored and much busier, with a daily visitor cap of 65,000. For a first visit with a driver, Mutianyu usually gives a better walk for less time in the car.

How much does it cost to enter Mutianyu and to ride the cable car?

Adult admission is 45 yuan, with children and students paying 25 yuan. The cable car and toboggan return ticket is 120 yuan for adults and 100 yuan one way, and the shuttle bus between the visitor plaza and the foot of the steps costs 15 yuan each way.

How long does the drive from Beijing Capital Airport to Mutianyu take?

The road distance is roughly 80 km and the trip normally takes 90 minutes, running east from the airport and then north on the G11 expressway through Huairou. On a weekend, a holiday or in bad weather the last mountain stretch slows down, so allow up to two hours and plan a later start.

How long should I allow for the climb itself?

Two hours is a sensible minimum for the restored 2,250-metre section with 22 watchtowers. If you want to walk the full out-and-back from the ticket office with a chairlift ride on one side only, allow three hours, and wear shoes with grip because the steps are uneven stone.

Is the wall crowded and are there limits on visitors?

Badaling received close to 10 million visitors in 2018 and has capped daily numbers at 65,000 since 2019, which still means long queues in peak season. Mutianyu is smaller but also busy at weekends. Going early in the morning or in mid-week makes a noticeable difference to how much of the wall you get to yourself.
