Overview: Photography in Fuzhou

Fuzhou is a river city of hills and old stone, and the subjects within it are unusually close together. The three hills of the old town carry a white pagoda, a black pagoda and a Buddhist site, and between them the ward lanes of the Ming and Qing survive in a block that is the largest of its kind in the centre of any Chinese city. On the river there is the tower that marks the anchorage and the yard where the first Chinese naval school was run in the 1860s. The photographs are made at walking distance from each other, in a city that keeps its hills visible through the modern blocks.

The Three Lanes and Seven Alleys (三坊七巷)

The district in Gulou district is the largest surviving historic quarter in the centre of a Chinese city, built around Nanhou Street running north to south, with the three lanes on the west, Yijin, Wenru and Guanglu, and the seven on the east, Yangqiao, Langguan, Ta, Huang, Anmin, Gong and Jibi. It is called a living fossil of the ward system of the Song and a museum of Ming and Qing architecture, and the houses in it have belonged to a long list of names, including Lin Zexu, Shen Baozhen, Yan Fu, Lin Shu, Bing Xin, Zheng Xiaoxu, Sa Zhenbing, Lin Jemin and Lin Changmin, which is why half of modern Chinese history is said to have happened inside it. It has been on the national key cultural relic list since the early 2000s, was in the first batch of Chinese historical and cultural streets, was placed on the world heritage tentative list and is a national 5A site.

best light: Early morning in the lanes, or late afternoon when the light comes down the axis of Nanhou Street between the whitewashed gables. · historic lanes

Getting there: In the heart of Gulou district, with Nanhou Street as its spine, a short walk from the museum on Houtou Street and from the river.

  • The lanes are narrow and the gables are white, so the frame is vertical: one lane, one archway, one line of eaves.
  • The side lanes are quieter than Nanhou Street and keep their residential character.
  • Evening light reaches the eastern facades late, which is the best hour for the whitewashed walls.

Gushan, the Spring Temple and the Cliff Inscriptions (鼓山)

Gushan stands in Jinan district, with Guling in the north, the Min river to the south, Damoxi to the east and the plain of Fuzhou to the west, running about 6 kilometres north to south and 4 kilometres east to west at a general height of 700 to 800 metres, with the highest point, Dafengding, at 919.1 metres. It is named for a rock at the summit shaped like a drum and for the legend that the rock sounds like a drum in wind and rain. The Yongquan Temple was founded in 783, and the range carries eighteen named scenes, a Guanyin Pavilion, two Northern Song ceramic pagodas in front of the temple, and more than 300 ancient cliff inscriptions, including calligraphy by Cai Xiang and Zhu Xi, which makes the rock inscriptions the most concentrated group of them in Fuzhou. The Gushan cliff inscriptions are listed as a national key cultural relic.

best light: Clear mornings, where the haze on the Min river separates the far bank from the range. · mountain, temple and inscriptions

Getting there: In Jinan district on the east bank of the Min river, with a road and then a stepped path up the mountain to the temple and the inscription gallery.

  • The two ceramic pagodas stand side by side in front of the temple and are the strongest single frame on the mountain.
  • The inscription gallery is lit irregularly by the rock overhang, which suits a slow shutter rather than a fast lens.
  • The view back down the Min river over the plain is the reason for the climb as much as the temple is.

Yushan and the White Pagoda (于山)

Yushan, also called Jiuxian Shan or Jiuri Shan, is a hill in Gulou district 52.2 metres at its highest point, the smallest of the three hills of the old town and the one that carries the city's founding legend. It is said that a prince of the Eastern Kingdom of Yue fled to Fuzhou at the end of the Han and hid on this hill, and the peak is still called the Prince's Terrace. The White Pagoda on the summit was first built in 905, collapsed in 1534 and was rebuilt in 1548 at 41 metres, and the Black Pagoda opposite it dates from 799 and was rebuilt in 936. A major restoration completed in 2019 followed the principle of revealing the hill and showing the pagoda, and uncovered a section of the Ming city wall together with a flight of 96 steps that had been buried, so the hill now reads as a wall with a town behind it rather than as a lump of vegetation.

best light: Late afternoon, when the whitewashed tower separates from the dark trees behind it. · hill and pagoda

Getting there: In the centre of Gulou district, within a short walk of the Three Lanes and Seven Alleys, reached by the steps that were uncovered in the 2019 restoration.

  • The uncovered wall steps are the reason to go now; they were hidden until the 2019 works.
  • The white pagoda is seen from the garden on the other side of the hill as often as from its own terrace.
  • The hill is small enough to be photographed with a long lens from the street below, which is the widest possible frame.

Yantaishan, the Treaty Port Hill (烟台山)

Yantaishan stands on Nantai island in Cangshan district, 41.6 metres high, on the south bank of the Min river, and it has been called Teng Shan, Cangqian Shan and Tianning Shan. It took its present name from the beacon tower put up at the summit in the late Yuan, for the ships that used the river. After the opening of Fuzhou as a treaty port it became the foreign hill: several countries set up consulates on it, along with churches, schools and hospitals, and the mixed architecture of those years is still standing among the lanes. The hill park was laid out and opened in 1964 and was restored in the 2010s, and it has been a national 3A site since 2021. The writer Ye Shengtao described it in the essay Ke Yu, and the lanes are the reason the place reads as an essay subject.

best light: Afternoon, when the light rakes across the pitched roofs and the brick walls of the consulates. · hill of foreign houses and churches

Getting there: On Nantai island in Cangshan district, on the south bank of the Min river, reached from the old town across the river or by the road along the southern bank.

  • The consulates and churches are the subject, and they are best photographed from the lanes rather than from the waterfront.
  • The name comes from the beacon tower, which is gone, so the hill itself has to carry the frame.
  • The lower park is a planted slope; the upper lanes with the old houses are the part worth the climb.

The Fuzhou Museum (福建博物院)

The museum is on Houtou Street in Gulou district and holds nearly 170,000 objects and natural specimens, of which more than 30,000 are classed as precious. Its own history starts with the Fujian Provincial Science Museum, founded on 23 October 1933, which was the first institution of its kind in the province and one of the earliest provincial science museums in the country, and it later became the Fujian Museum before the present building. The collection is weighted towards the archaeology of the province and to the maritime and overseas material that Fuzhou's port produced, which is the link to the rest of the photographs in this city: everything here is connected to the river that made the place.

best light: Indoor; the object cases are lit for the objects, which is the only light that matters here. · museum

Getting there: On Houtou Street in Gulou district, next to the Three Lanes and Seven Alleys, with the river and the Fuzhou station both close.

  • The Fujian shipbuilding and trade material is the section that connects to the Mawei and Luoxing Tower frames.
  • The building itself is worth a frame from the river side rather than only the cases inside.
  • The museum is an easy stop between the old lanes and the hill, all within a few hundred metres.

Mawei Shipyard and the Luoxing Tower (马尾船政与罗星塔)

The Fuzhou Navy Yard was established in 1867 and was one of the first major experiments with Western technology in China: a shipyard and an arsenal were built under French guidance, a naval school was opened and an academy was set up at the yard offering courses in English, French, engineering and navigation. It produced a generation of Western-trained officers, among them the scholar-reformer Yan Fu, born 1854. The yard was founded to recover from the defeat of 1856 to 1860, and government interest faded by the mid-1870s. On 23 August 1884 the French Far East Fleet destroyed the Fujian Fleet in Mawei harbour. In 1938 the harbour command school, barracks, shipyard and hospital at Mawei were bombed in turn, the gunboats Fu Ning, Chen Ning and Suming were sunk in the estuary, and Fuzhou fell. The Luoxing Tower stands in Mawei district, built in the Song dynasty and once called the Tower of China, marking the anchorage that the modern port still uses.

best light: Late afternoon, when the tower is side-lit against the water and the shipyard walls are in warm light. · naval heritage

Getting there: In Mawei district at the mouth of the Min river, on the Fuzhou to Mawei cargo railway and the coastal road, with the Luoxing Tower at the end of the peninsula.

  • The Luoxing Tower is best from the water side, where the anchorage and the tower separate cleanly.
  • The 1938 bomb damage is documented in the surviving buildings and the shipyard walls, which photograph better than any reconstructed view.
  • The rail line from Fuzhou station to Mawei runs along the same corridor and is worth including in the sequence.

The Min River and Its Two Channels (闽江)

The Min river runs 577 kilometres with a drainage basin of 60,992 square kilometres, which is half the area of Fujian, and it has three sources. At Nantai island near Huai'an it divides, the northern channel taking the name of the White Dragon river and the southern the Black Dragon, and the two rejoin at the Luoxing Tower before separating again near Tingjiang around Langqi island into the channel of the Long Gate and the channel of the Plum Blossom, which is where it reaches the Taiwan strait. The river is what Fuzhou is built on and around: the old town grew around the two artificial lakes and the canal system laid out here in 282 during the Jin dynasty, and the city's commercial and military history is the history of the water below.

best light: Blue hour, when the bridges and the vessel lights come on and the river holds the sky. · river

Getting there: Along the riverfront on both banks of the old town, with the bridges as the vantage points, and the wider estuary reachable by the coastal road to Mawei.

  • The bridges are the standard frame, and the blue hour with the vessel lights is better than the daylight view.
  • Upstream, where the river splits around Nantai island, the two channels read as separate landscapes.
  • The river is 30 metres deep at the mouth and carries deep-draft traffic, so the working river is part of the frame.

Gear and planning for photographing Fuzhou

Almost everything on this list is inside the old town and on the hills around it, so the walkable distances are short and the light is the variable, not the logistics. A 35mm lens covers the lanes, the pagoda hill and the riverfront; a short telephoto is needed for the cliff inscriptions at Gushan and for the consulates on Yantaishan, where the detail is in the upper storeys; and a tripod is worth carrying for the hill at dawn and for the blue hour on the river. The heavy tripod is not worth it, because most of the work is handheld at street level. Fuzhou is in the wet subtropics, so a cloth on the front element and a dry bag for the body are more useful than another lens.

Plan the old town on foot and the two hills separately. The Three Lanes and Seven Alleys, the White Pagoda hill and the museum are within a few hundred metres of each other and take a morning; the riverfront takes an hour at blue hour; and Gushan and Yantaishan are separate excursions, one inland on the east bank and one across the water. The city is low and subtropical with a long growing season, so the light is soft and the greens are dense, which means the hard sunlight of a northern summer is not the problem here.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most important historic quarter in Fuzhou?

The Three Lanes and Seven Alleys in Gulou district, the largest surviving historic quarter in the centre of any Chinese city. It is organised around Nanhou Street with three lanes to the west and seven to the east, it is called a living fossil of the ward system of the Song and a museum of Ming and Qing architecture, and the residents have included Lin Zexu, Shen Baozhen, Yan Fu, Bing Xin, Sa Zhenbing and Lin Jemin. It is a national 5A site and on the world heritage tentative list.

Which mountain has the oldest inscriptions?

Gushan, in Jinan district, where the Yongquan Temple was founded in 783 and more than 300 ancient cliff inscriptions survive, including calligraphy by Cai Xiang and Zhu Xi. The range runs 6 kilometres north to south and 4 kilometres east to west with Dafengding at 919.1 metres, and the inscriptions are listed as a national key cultural relic in their own right. The most photographed object on the mountain is actually a pair of Northern Song ceramic pagodas in front of the temple.

What happened at Mawei?

The Fuzhou Navy Yard was established there in 1867 as one of the first major Western technology experiments in China, with a shipyard, an arsenal and a naval school under French guidance, and it trained the scholar-reformer Yan Fu among others. On 23 August 1884 the French Far East Fleet destroyed the Fujian Fleet in Mawei harbour, and in 1938 the harbour installations were bombed in turn and the gunboats Fu Ning, Chen Ning and Suming sunk in the estuary. The Luoxing Tower from the Song dynasty still marks the anchorage.

Why is the White Pagoda famous?

It was first built in 905 on Yushan, the smallest of the three hills of the old town, collapsed in 1534 and was rebuilt in 1548 at 41 metres, with the Black Pagoda opposite it dating from 799 and rebuilt in 936. A restoration completed in 2019 uncovered a section of the Ming city wall and 96 buried steps, so the hill now reads as a wall with a town behind it.

Is there a museum in Fuzhou?

The Fuzhou Museum on Houtou Street, with nearly 170,000 objects and natural specimens of which more than 30,000 are classed as precious, and a lineage that starts with the Fujian Provincial Science Museum founded in 1933. Its material is weighted towards the archaeology of the province and to the maritime trade of a port city, which connects directly to the Mawei and Luoxing Tower material.

What about the river itself?

The Min river runs 577 kilometres with a basin of 60,992 square kilometres, half the area of Fujian, and has three sources. It splits at Nantai island into the White Dragon and Black Dragon channels, rejoins at the Luoxing Tower and separates again around Langqi island into the Long Gate and Plum Blossom channels on its way to the Taiwan strait. The old town grew around two artificial lakes and a canal system dug here in 282 during the Jin dynasty.
