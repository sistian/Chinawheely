Overview: Day Trips from Xi'an

The city sits on the southern edge of the Loess Plateau with the Qinling range rising behind it and the Yellow River running 200 kilometres to the east, and the day-trip options follow those three lines. West and south are mountains: Huashan at 120 kilometres, Famen Temple in the Wei valley, the Taoist terrace at Louguantai and Taibai Mountain on the divide. North and east are the river country, ending at the Hukou waterfall where the Yellow River leaves the gorge. Every route below works as a single-day run from an Xi'an hotel with a driver, and every one of them pays better on the strength of a schedule than on the strength of the destination.

Mount Hua, the Western Peak (华山)

Mount Hua is the western of the Five Great Mountains and stands in Huayin, 120 kilometres from central Xi'an at the meeting point of the Yellow River, the Qinling and the Wei valley. The rock face rises almost vertically, which is why it carries the reputation for being the steepest of the five, and why the standard routes are named for the difficulty they pose: the Thousand-Foot Ladder, the Hundred-Foot Gorge, the Canglong Ridge, the Eagle Flips and the Plank Road. The summit area has five peaks, with the South Peak at 2,154.9 metres, the West Peak at 2,082 metres and the North Peak at 1,614.7 metres. It is also a Daoist sacred mountain, the fourth of the thirty-six grotto-heavens, and was listed among the first batch of national key scenic areas in 1982, covering about 150 square kilometres.

How the day runs: Leave Xi'an at 06:30 for the 120-kilometre run to Huayin, and be at the ticket gate by 08:45. Take the cable car up to the North Peak area, walk the Canglong Ridge and the South Peak section, and allow two hours for the summit ridge before descending through the West Peak and the Plank Road. Bring water and eat before you start rather than on the mountain, since the available stalls are all above the treeline. Start the return at 14:00, which puts you back in Xi'an around 17:00 and leaves the rest of the evening free.

120 km from Xi'an · about 120 min by car from Xi'an · full-day trip · best in May to June and September to October for clear visibility; the paths are icy in winter and close to the summit in heavy snow.

  • The chain sections on the Thousand-Foot Ladder and the Plank Road require both hands; gloves make the climb noticeably easier.
  • The summit walk between the South and West peaks is exposed and takes longer than its map distance suggests, so start the descent before 13:30.
  • Geological survey work in 1984 recorded the range as still rising, so the paths are periodically rerouted; the version of the map on site is the current one.

Huaqing Palace and Mount Li (骊山与华清宫)

Mount Li stands 30 kilometres east of central Xi'an in the Lintong district and, together with the hot springs at its foot, has been the destination of emperors for two millennia. The Shiji records that King You of Zhou was killed by the Rong at the foot of this hill, and the first Qin emperor's mausoleum lies on its northern slope. Tang emperors built a palace over the spring, first called the Hot Spring Palace in 644 under Taizong, with the architect Yan Lide supervising, and renamed Huaqing Palace in 747 under Xuanzong. The excavated site covers about 1,300 mu and includes eight bathing pools for emperor and consorts, together with halls for polo, archery and the dancing horses. The palace was listed as a national key cultural relic in 1996. On the hillside above are the beacon tower, the Old Mother's Temple, the Old Lord Temple and the Remonstrance Pavilion, where Chiang Kai-shek was found after the Xi'an Incident of 1936.

How the day runs: Drive out at 08:30 and reach the site at 09:15. Start with the excavated palace and the Tang bathing pools, which take about ninety minutes including the on-site museum, then drive around to the foot of the mountain and take the cable car or the walking path up to the Remonstrance Pavilion and the Old Mother's Temple. The drive back takes 45 minutes and you are in the old city by 14:00, which leaves the afternoon for the Terracotta Army a further 25 kilometres east at Lintong.

30 km from Xi'an · about 45 min by car from Xi'an · half-day trip · best in All year for the palace site; the mountain paths are pleasant from April to November.

  • The bathing pool ruins are outdoors; in summer the shade around the covered walkways is the only comfort in the middle of the day.
  • The mountain section is a genuine climb of about two hours on the stone steps rather than a stroll, so it pairs better with an early start than with a late afternoon.
  • The Terracotta Army museum at Lintong is 25 kilometres further east and opens on a different rhythm, so combining the two works better than two separate outings.

Famen Temple and the Tang Treasure (法门寺)

Famen Temple stands 10 kilometres north of Fufeng county in Baoji and is known as the ancestor of the pagoda temples of the Guanzhong region, with some accounts placing its foundation in the Eastern Han and others tracing it to the Indian emperor Ashoka, who divided the Buddha's relics and built 84,000 stupas, nineteen of them in China. The temple was built for the relic, not the other way round. In 1987 the underground chamber was opened by accident during drainage work, and the discovery of the Buddha's finger bone relic, together with the eight-treasure casket and a small bronze pagoda and thousands of other Tang objects, made it one of the most significant archaeological finds in China. Eight Tang emperors between them welcomed the relic six times and escorted it twice. The complex was expanded under the Western Wei in 555, destroyed during the Northern Zhou suppression of Buddhism, rebuilt under the Sui and renamed in 583, and opened for viewing of the relic in 602.

How the day runs: Set out from Xi'an at 07:00 for the 120-kilometre run west to Fufeng, allowing for the expressway. Reach the temple complex at 09:30 and give the old pagoda and the underground chamber museum two hours; the museum hall where the artefacts are displayed is the reason to come. Lunch in Fufeng or on the return road, then stop at the site of the Tang buildings uncovered during the excavation. Leave at 14:30 and be back in Xi'an around 17:30.

120 km from Xi'an · about 150 min by car from Xi'an · full-day trip · best in April to May and September to October; the valley is hot in July and August.

  • The temple has been a working religious site as well as a museum, so dress for the site rather than for the exhibit hall and keep voices low in the pagoda.
  • The eight-treasure casket and the bronze pagoda are the headline pieces; the written records on display explain why the finger bone matters, which is the harder part of the visit to skip.
  • The museum and the pagoda are a few minutes' walk apart but the site is large, so allow more time than the map suggests before the return drive.

Taibai Mountain and the Qinling Divide (太白山与秦岭)

The Qinling range runs 1,600 kilometres from Lintao in Gansu to the mountains of Henan, is 200 to 300 kilometres wide, and divides the Guanzhong plain from southern Shaanxi. It is the northern boundary of the subtropical zone and the watershed between the Yangtze and the Yellow River, with the climate north of it temperate and dry and the climate south of it mild and wet. Its highest point is the Boutiantai summit of Taibai Mountain at 3,771.2 metres. The range is also asymmetric: the northern slope is short, steep and cut by deep fault valleys, historically described as the most dangerous ground in the nine provinces, while the southern slope is long and gentle. The northern slope carries warm temperate mixed forest and deciduous oak.

How the day runs: This is a long day and should be planned as one. Leave Xi'an at 05:30 and drive to the Taibai county road head at 09:00, from where the cable car climbs to the upper mountain. Walk the plateau section, which is a flat, high-alpine route rather than a scramble, and be back at the cable car by 14:00 for the descent. Return to Xi'an at 17:30. The alternative, if the weather closes in, is the lower Qinling approach near Huyi, which reaches the same northern slope without the altitude.

170 km from Xi'an · about 210 min by car from Xi'an · full-day trip · best in June to October, with clear views on the morning after a cold front; the summit is under snow until late spring.

  • The weather changes fast above 2,500 metres; carry a windproof layer even in summer, because the plateau is exposed on every side.
  • Cable car operations are weather-dependent and the last descent is well before dusk, so build the return around the published closing time rather than around dinner.
  • The Qinling-Huaihe divide is the country's own north-south line, so the summit walk is also the clearest way to see why the plants, the rainfall and the architecture on either side differ.

Louguantai, the Terrace of the Laozi (楼观台)

Louguantai lies in Louguan town in Zhouzhi county, 70 kilometres from Xi'an and 15 kilometres from the county seat, on the northern foot of the Zhongnan Mountains, and it is where Laozi is said to have explained the Dao De Jing to Yin Xi, the keeper of the Hangu Pass. The terrace that carries the story is called the Terrace for Expounding the Classic, and the site gave rise to the Louguan tradition of Daoism and is described as the earliest important Daoist sacred place in China. The foundation goes back to the Western Zhou, when Yin Xi built a thatched tower to watch the sky. Emperor Taizu of the Tang renamed it Zongsheng Palace in 624 and enlarged it, and under Xuanzong it was the largest of the royal Daoist temples. What survives is the Laozi shrine of about 9,000 square metres, built across the Ming, Qing and modern periods. The State Council listed it among the national key Daoist temples in 1983, and it was the first batch of provincial protected monuments in Shaanxi in 1956.

How the day runs: Leave Xi'an at 09:00 for the 70-kilometre run west and arrive at the gate around 10:20. The site covers about 5 square kilometres, with the core terrace sitting on a ridge at 580 metres above the plain and the view running north across the Wei valley. Walk the terrace and the cypress avenue, take in the Zongsheng Palace ruins, and allow two hours. Lunch in Zhouzhi county, which is 15 kilometres on, then return to Xi'an by 13:30, which leaves time for the Guanzhong Folk Art Museum or the Small Wild Goose Pagoda on the same afternoon.

70 km from Xi'an · about 80 min by car from Xi'an · half-day trip · best in April to June for the blossom on the Zhongnan foothills; September for clear views across the Wei valley.

  • The Su Shi verse written here about the view over the Qin river plain is on the terrace; the wording is short and the site is quiet enough to read it in the open.
  • The cypress avenue and the terrace are exposed with little shade, so the morning is the better time even in summer.
  • Pairing the site with the county town works because Zhouzhi is only 15 kilometres further on and the drive back to Xi'an is the same road.

Hukou Waterfall on the Yellow River (壶口瀑布)

Hukou is where the Yellow River leaves the Jin-Shaan gorge, and it is the largest waterfall on the river. The falls are 30 metres wide and about 50 metres deep, with a maximum cascade face of 30,000 square metres, formed between the southern end of the Lüliang range on the Shanxi side, facing Jixian in Linfen, and Huanglong Shan on the Shaanxi side, facing Yichuan, 25 kilometres southwest of the Jixian county seat. The classical explanation is in the Shangshu Yu Gong, which compares the whirlpool to the mouth of a jar, and the two local wonders are the boats that are said to travel on the dry bed and the mist that rises off the water. The Hukou Waterfall National Geopark was formally opened on 5 September 2006. The river here runs hot in summer and cold in winter, with the local climate averaging 9.9 degrees Celsius across the year and the winter lasting from 17 October to 4 April.

How the day runs: This is the longest run of the six and it needs an early start. Leave Xi'an at 05:00 for the 300-kilometre drive to Yichuan and across the provincial boundary to the Shanxi side, which is the viewing side with the better view of the cascade. Allow four and a half hours on the road including the border crossings and the ticket transfer, reach the falls at 11:00 and spend two hours on the platforms and the canyon paths. Eat before the drive back and leave by 14:30, which puts you in Xi'an at 19:00. Booking the vehicle for a two-day hire removes the fatigue from the return.

300 km from Xi'an · about 270 min by car from Xi'an · full-day trip · best in Late September to mid-November for the clearest water; the winter ice spectacle is a separate draw and the summer flow is brown and loud.

  • The Shanxi side and the Shaanxi side are separate entrances with separate tickets and separate access roads, so confirm which side your vehicle is permitted to reach before the day.
  • Rock spray reaches the viewing platforms, so waterproof footwear matters more than a camera bag here.
  • The falls have their own micro-climate and the platforms are exposed; the walk between the entrances is not the same distance as the map figure.

Planning day trips from Xi'an

Distance is the misleading variable in this region. Mount Hua is 120 kilometres away and Famen Temple is about the same, but Huashan is on the expressway with a cable car at the top, while Famen is a museum visit with no walking at all. Hukou is 300 kilometres and takes most of a working day; Taibai Mountain is nearer at 170 but includes a cable car, an exposed plateau and a hard schedule. Fix the departure time first and choose the destination to match the hours you have, not the other way round.

Two practicalities cover most of the ground. Xi'an sits on the edge of the Loess Plateau, so the drives south and west climb into thinner air and cooler weather than the city, and a single layer of extra clothing is often enough even on a hot day. And because the terrain around the Wei valley produces most of Shaanxi's specialist food, an excursion and a meal belong in the same plan: ask the driver where the day's stop can also be a decent lunch stop rather than treating the two as separate errands.

Planning a day trip from Xi'an

Huaqing Palace and Mount Li, Hukou Waterfall on the Yellow River and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Xi'an day trip route — Huaqing Palace and Mount Li sits closest at 30 km, roughly 45 minutes by car, while Hukou Waterfall on the Yellow River reaches furthest at 300 km, about 270 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Famen Temple and the Tang Treasure, Taibai Mountain and the Qinling Divide, Louguantai, the Terrace of the Laozi each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Xi'an itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions: Booking in Xian

Which day trip is worth the longest drive?

Hukou Waterfall, if you have a full day and a driver for two days. It is 300 kilometres from Xi'an on a route that crosses a provincial boundary, and the falls themselves are 30 metres wide with a cascade face of 30,000 square metres, which is not the same thing as the small cascades most visitors picture. The quality of the water changes completely with the season, and late autumn gives the clearest flow. If you only have one day free, the answer is Mount Hua instead, at half the distance and with the summit ridge as the day's high point.

Is Mount Hua a realistic day trip for a first-time visitor?

Yes, with an early start. It is 120 kilometres west of Xi'an, which is about two hours on the expressway, and the standard route uses a cable car to reach the upper section. The walk then runs along the Canglong Ridge to the South Peak at 2,154.9 metres before descending through the West Peak and the Plank Road. The difficulty is real but concentrated: the chain sections need both hands and the summit ridge is exposed. Two hours on the summit section and a 14:00 departure leaves the evening free.

What makes Famen Temple worth a special trip?

The underground chamber, found by accident during drainage work in 1987, which contained the Buddha's finger bone relic together with the eight-treasure casket, a small bronze pagoda and thousands of other Tang objects. The ritual around it is older than the finds: eight Tang emperors welcomed the relic to the capital six times and escorted it twice, which is why a temple in a small county attracted imperial attention for two hundred years. It is also the reason for the name, since the pagoda was built for the relic before the monastery was founded around it.

Can I see the border between northern and southern China in a day?

That is what the Qinling run does. The range is the Qinling-Huaihe line, the country's own division between the temperate north and the subtropical south, and the watershed between the Yellow and Yangtze river systems. The climate changes across it: the coldest month average sits above zero on the southern side and below it on the northern side, and the annual rainfall crosses 750 millimetres at the same boundary. On the northern slope, the vegetation runs from warm temperate mixed forest into deciduous oak.

Is there a shorter cultural excursion than a full day?

Louguantai is the closest of the six at 70 kilometres, about 80 minutes from the city, and it is the site where Laozi is said to have expounded the Dao De Jing to Yin Xi at the Hangu Pass. The core terrace sits at 580 metres above the plain with a view north across the Wei river valley, the site covers about 5 square kilometres, and the State Council listed it among the national key Daoist temples in 1983. It pairs naturally with the county town 15 kilometres further on or with the temples back in the city.

What is the best season for these trips?

April to June and September to October for most of them, which is also when the mountains are clear and the Wei valley is comfortable. Hukou is the exception: the clearest water runs from late September to mid-November, and the winter ice is a separate spectacle. Taibai Mountain is under snow until late spring and the plateau is exposed on every side, so the summer months are the only comfortable ones. Mount Hua's paths are icy in winter and close to the summit after heavy snow.
