Name Origin

Nantong means "southern passage" in Chinese. It is located on the north bank of the Yangtze River estuary in Jiangsu and is known for textiles and education.

Education & Universities

Nantong University, Nantong Institute of Technology, and Nantong Shipping College are main institutions.

Getting Around

Nantong Xingdong International Airport (NTG) has domestic flights. Nantong Railway Station connects to Shanghai and Nanjing. Buses and taxis serve the city.

Natural Resources & Environment

Nantong lies on the Yangtze River Delta with a humid subtropical climate. It has coastline, rivers, wetlands, and is known for its textile industry.

What to eat and what to bring home from Nantong

🛍️ Shopping in Nantong

Buy Nantong blue calico prints, textiles, seafood products, and local snacks. Main shopping is on Wenfeng Dashijie on Nanda Street, the city's busiest shopping street.

🍵 Tea Culture

Traditional Nantong teahouses serve loose-leaf tea in covered bowls, and the regional green teas are the usual house pour. Your driver can point you to a teahouse that specialists use.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

Nantong Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Nantong like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

The main sight of Nantong rewards an early start, before the tour coaches arrive. Ask your driver to wait close by so you can return to the car at your own pace.

🥢 Skip the tourist menu

Eat where the queues are shortest: the morning market and the small noodle shops a block back from the main square usually feed locals at a fraction of the tourist-restaurant price.

⏱️ Plan the driving time

Allow extra time between the airport or main station and the old town — the city spreads out, and the distance that looks short on a map can take half an hour in traffic.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Nantong

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Nantong

🍵 Tea Houses of Nantong

Tea culture runs through the day in Nantong: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
