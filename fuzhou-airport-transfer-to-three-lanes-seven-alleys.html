Changle stands about 50 km east of central Fuzhou on the Taiwan Strait. The usual route runs west on the airport expressway, then the 817 Road through Cangshan into the old town, ending at the Guanglu Lane end of Nanhou Street.

Why transfer to Three Lanes and Seven Alleys?

This is the largest surviving historic block at the centre of any Chinese city and the clearest example of the Tang-era ward layout, which is why it went on the UNESCO World Heritage Tentative List in 2013 and gained 5A status in 2015. Three parallel lanes on the west side and seven alleys on the east are strung along a single north-south spine, Nanhou Street, with whitewashed firewalls and dark tile roofs on every corner. Roughly 400 eminent residents have lived here over the centuries, which is why the quarter is sometimes called the Beverly Hills of imperial China.

Nothing here is a single monument you tick off and leave. The interest is in the doorways: Lin Zexu's former residence, the house of Yan Fu who translated Huxley and Spencer into Chinese, the Qing residence of Shen Baozhen, the home where Bing Xin grew up, and the water pavilion stage where Fujian opera and puppet shows are still put on. Walking the block end to end takes about forty minutes if you stop at every courtyard gate.

The case for arriving by car is practical rather than scenic. The lanes are one car wide in places and are closed to tourist coaches, so day-trip buses from the airport usually park outside the quarter and walk you in. A car drops you inside the boundary, which means you can leave a suitcase in the boot, split the group so half explores the west lanes while the other heads straight for Yijin Lane, or park once and walk the grid twice.

  • The quarter covers just 0.38 sq km but holds more than 100 protected historic buildings
  • Nanhou Street is the spine; the three lanes sit west of it and the seven alleys east
  • Individual residences charge separately, while the streets themselves cost nothing to walk
  • Water pavilion stage performances of Fujian opera run through the day in season

What to do in Three Lanes and Seven Alleys once you arrive

Start at the southern end of Nanhou Street and work north, because the grid is easiest to read that way. Guanglu Lane and Ta Alley are worth the first detour, and further up you will find the narrow Anmin Alley, where the eaves almost touch overhead. Most named houses open off the lanes on ticketed entry, and several are only staffed in the morning, so pick your two or three before you start walking rather than queueing at each door.

Lunch in the quarter is a good reason to slow down. Small restaurants along Nanhou Street and the alleys behind it serve Fuzhou fish balls, pork dumplings with peanut sauce, and Buddha's hand fried with dried tangerine peel. In the evening the same street fills with residents and food stalls rather than tour groups, and if you can wait until after seven the traffic noise from Bayiqi Road drops away and the alleys feel genuinely residential.

  • Line 1 of the Fuzhou Metro reaches Dongjiekou on the eastern edge of the quarter, useful for the return leg
  • Bring cash or a local payment app for the ticketed houses; card acceptance is patchy in the small museums
  • Water pavilion stage show times are posted at the entrance and change through the year
  • Avoid weekends between 10:00 and 15:00, when the lanes fill with coach groups

Arriving at Fuzhou Changle International Airport

Changle is a single-terminal airport about 50 km east of the city, opened in 1997 and now handling well over 16 million passengers a year. The airport rail link reaches Fuzhou South Station but not the old town, so the final leg to the historic quarter is always by road. Traffic around Changle and the airport expressway slows in the late afternoon and during the evening rush, which is worth allowing for if you are meeting a flight the same day.

What the transfer costs

A one-way car from FOC to the Three Lanes and Seven Alleys area is priced on distance and vehicle class, and the quote covers fuel, tolls on the airport expressway and the driver's time waiting while you explore. Longer vehicles such as a seven-seat business van suit families or small groups travelling with cases. Share your flight number so the driver can track delays, and confirm in advance if you plan to leave luggage in the car while you walk the lanes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to drive from FOC airport to Three Lanes and Seven Alleys?

Changle Airport is roughly 50 km east of the old town, and the drive along the airport expressway and the 817 Road takes about an hour in normal conditions. Late afternoon congestion on the expressway can stretch that to 75 or 80 minutes. Allow a buffer of two hours from landing if you want to be walking the lanes by mid-morning.

Is there a subway from Fuzhou Changle airport to Three Lanes and Seven Alleys?

Not directly to the quarter. Fuzhou Metro Line 1 runs through the city and has a Dongjiekou station on the eastern side of the historic block, a short walk from the eastern alleys, but the airport itself is served by the airport rail link to Fuzhou South Station only. From there a metro and taxi combination still leaves a final street transfer.

Do I pay a ticket to enter Three Lanes and Seven Alleys?

No entry fee is charged for the streets themselves, and you can walk all of Nanhou Street, the three lanes and the seven alleys without paying. Individual sites inside the quarter do charge, and the former residences of Lin Zexu, Yan Fu and Bing Xin are each ticketed separately, with prices commonly in the region of 20 yuan per house.

How long should I allow to explore the quarter?

Two to three hours is realistic if you enter three or four ticketed houses and stop for tea or a meal. Walking the full grid without visiting inside the residences takes about forty minutes. Half a day leaves room for the water pavilion performance and a slow lunch without feeling hurried.

Can a tour bus drop passengers inside the lanes?

Not in the inner lanes. Large coaches are restricted in the historic quarter, so tour buses normally set down on the perimeter roads near Bayiqi Road or Yangqiao Road and visitors walk in from there. A private car can reach Nanhou Street itself, which saves a ten to fifteen minute walk each way for anyone carrying luggage.
