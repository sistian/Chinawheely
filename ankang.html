Name Origin of Ankang

Ankang (Chinese: 安康; pinyin: Ānkāng) is a prefecture-level city in the south of Shaanxi Province in the People's Republic of China, bordering Hubei province to the east, Chongqing municipality to the south, and Sichuan province to the southwest.

Ankang Education & Universities

Higher education in Ankang is provided by the city's local colleges, vocational schools and teacher-training institutions rather than by large research universities.

Getting Around Ankang

Getting around Ankang is mostly by road, with buses and taxis in the urban area and long-distance coaches to the surrounding counties.

Ankang Natural Resources & Environment

Ankang sits in its provincial hinterland, where the surrounding hills, farmland and rivers shape the local landscape, weather and food.

Travel Essentials for Your Ankang Trip

Make your Ankang journey smoother with these essential services.

🏯

🏞️ The Han River (汉江)

River & Nature

The Han River flows through Ankang, offering scenic boat tours and peaceful riverside parks. Rent a boat to enjoy sunset views over the water.

🏛️

🏯 Xunyang River Scenic Area (旬阳河)

Canyon & Scenic

A beautiful canyon with turquoise waters. The Xunyang Basin is famous for its Danjiang Bridge — a hauntingly beautiful suspension walkway.

⛰️

⛰️ Wulipo Karst Cave (乌鲤铺)

Karst & Caves

One of the largest karst cave systems in northern China. Explore vast underground chambers with stalactites and an underground river.

🌸

🛕 Nangang Mountain (南佛山)

Mountain & Buddhist

A sacred Buddhist mountain with ancient temples dating back 1,500 years. Hiking trails offer panoramic views of the surrounding Qinling Mountains.

🏖️

🌊 Yuping Lake (瀛湖)

Lake & Recreation

The largest freshwater lake in Shaanxi, created by the Danjiangkou Dam. Perfect for boat trips, fishing, and enjoying lakeside villages.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Ankang

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Ankang

🍵 Tea Houses of Ankang

Tea culture runs through the day in Ankang: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
