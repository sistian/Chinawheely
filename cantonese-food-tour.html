Cantonese Food Tour Guide

Explore the world's most celebrated regional Chinese cuisine -- delicate dim sum, silky char siu, snow-white chicken, and the freshest seafood simply prepared.

Iconic Cantonese Dishes

Cantonese cuisine values freshness, texture, and natural flavour above all. "If it isn't fresh, don't cook it" is the chef's golden rule.

🥟

Dim Sum (点心)

Small dishes for breakfast or lunch -- har gow (shrimp dumplings), siu mai (pork dumplings), cheung fun (rice noodle rolls), char siu bao. Served from bamboo steamers in teahouses.

🍗

White Cut Chicken (白切鸡)

A whole chicken poached just until cooked, then ice-bathed. Served with ginger-scallion sauce and sesame oil. The true test of a Cantonese chef -- the skin must be silky, the meat just cooked.

🍖

Char Siu (叉烧)

Marinated pork neck or belly, skewered and roasted over open flame until lacquered with a sweet, glossy glaze. Served over rice or in noodle soup. Made fresh daily at old-school BBQ shops.

🦐

Steamed Fish

Whole fish steamed with ginger, scallion, and soy sauce. Cantonese chefs judge a restaurant by their steamed fish -- the flesh must be just opaque, the sauce perfect.

🥘

Clay Pot Rice (煲仔饭)

Rice cooked in a clay pot until the bottom forms a golden crust. Topped with waxed meat, Chinese sausage, or a soft egg. Eaten slowly from the pot, scraping up the crispy rice.

🥣

Slow-Simmered Soup (老火汤)

A defining feature of Cantonese dining -- soups simmered for hours with herbs, meat, and medicinal ingredients. Believed to nourish the body and restore balance.

🦆

Roasted Duck

Whole duck roasted until the skin is mahogany-coloured and crisp, the meat tender and juicy. Cantonese roast duck is lighter and less fatty than Peking duck -- more about the meat than the skin.

🍮

Egg Tarts (蛋挞)

The Portuguese egg tart introduced to Hong Kong and refined into a Cantonese icon -- flaky pastry shell with a wobbly, caramelised custard that is just set. Best eaten warm.

🏮 Guangzhou: Birthplace of Cantonese Cuisine

Guangzhou (Canton) is where Cantonese cuisine was refined over centuries of trade, immigration, and culinary obsession. These institutions have defined the cuisine for generations.

🥟

Bai Zhan Ting (百丈亭)

Established: 1885 (over 139 years)

Specialty: Guangzhou's most famous teahouse -- a legendary institution that defined the yum cha (drinking tea, eating dim sum) culture. Four generations of the same family have maintained the same recipes for over a century.

Address: 1 Disheng Lane, Yuexiu District, Guangzhou (广州市越秀区地藏巷1号)

Nearest Metro:

Must Order: Har Gow, Siu Mai, Phoenix Claw, Lo Mai Gai

🍗

Ji Jia Bun (济记粉面)

Established: 1963 (over 60 years)

Specialty: Beef offal and offal noodle soup. A Guangzhou institution beloved for its rich beef broth, thin rice noodles, and generous portions of tendon, tripe, and beef ball. Locals line up before 7am.

Address: 68 Disheng Lane, Yuexiu District, Guangzhou (广州市越秀区地藏巷68号)

Nearest Metro:

Must Order: Beef Offal Noodles, Beef Ball Noodles

🦆

Guangzhou Roast Duck House (广州烧鸭店)

Established: 1956 (over 68 years)

Specialty: Cantonese-style roasted duck and pork. The old-school BBQ shop uses traditional clay ovens and produces some of the most fragrant roast duck in Guangzhou -- skin lacquered, meat tender.

Address: 88 Beijing Road, Yuexiu District, Guangzhou (广州市越秀区北京路88号)

Nearest Metro:

Must Order: Roast Duck, Char Siu, Soy Sauce Chicken

🥘

Wen Kee Clay Pot Rice (文记煲仔饭)

Established: 1971 (over 53 years)

Specialty: Clay pot rice made to order. Wen Kee fires each pot individually over high heat, producing an exceptionally thick, golden rice crust. Topped with your choice of Cantonese cured meats.

Address: 23 Shamian Island, Liwan District, Guangzhou (广州市荔湾区沙面岛23号)

Nearest Metro:

Must Order: Chinese Sausage & Egg Clay Pot Rice, Pork Belly & Egg Clay Pot Rice

🥣

Huang's Old Soup (黄氏老火汤)

Established: 1952 (over 72 years)

Specialty: Slow-simmered medicinal soups. Each soup is tailored to a different body type and season -- pork rib soup with lotus seed, fish head soup with tofu, herbal soup with black chicken.

Address: 5 Shangxiajiu Pedestrian Street, Liwan District, Guangzhou (广州市荔湾区上下九步行街5号)

Nearest Metro:

Must Order: Lotus Seed & Pork Rib Soup, Fish Maw Soup, Herbal Chicken Soup

🍜

Pan Xi Siu Mai (潘西烧麦)

Established: 1978 (over 46 years)

Specialty: Open-kitchen dim sum house famous for its oversized siu mai (pork dumplings) and har gow with thick, bouncy shrimp. The quality of the shrimp is legendary -- sourced fresh daily from Hainan.

Address: 18 Liwan Road, Liwan District, Guangzhou (广州市荔湾区荔湾路18号)

Nearest Metro:

Must Order: Har Gow, Jumbo Siu Mai, Lo Mai Gai

🧡 Shenzhen: Modern Cantonese Dining

Shenzhen's rapid growth means truly old restaurants are rare -- but the city attracts the best Cantonese chefs from Hong Kong and Guangzhou, creating a dynamic food scene with strong roots in tradition.

🥟

She Jia (蛇家)

Established: 1993 (over 31 years)

Specialty: Shanghainese and Cantonese dim sum fusion in Shenzhen's original premium dining mall. Famous for its handmade xiaolongbao alongside authentic Cantonese har gow.

Address: 2F, COCO Park, Futian District, Shenzhen (深圳市福田区Coco Park 2楼)

Nearest Metro:

Must Order: Dim Sum Set, Shanghai Xiaolongbao, Egg Tarts

🦐

Yan Jue (晏记海鲜)

Established: 1989 (over 35 years)

Specialty: Seafood hotpot and steamed fish. Yan Jue sources live seafood from Dapeng Peninsula daily -- the quality of the crab, grouper, and prawns is among the best in Shenzhen.

Address: 88 Dapeng Old City, Dapeng Peninsula, Shenzhen (深圳市大鹏新区大鹏古城88号)

Nearest Metro: Taxi required (approx. 90 min from city centre)

Must Order: Steamed Grouper, Salt & Pepper Mantis Shrimp, Crab Hot Pot

🍲

Jiang's Kitchen (江师傅)

Established: 1987 (over 37 years)

Specialty: Traditional Cantonese home cooking by Chef Jiang -- a Guangzhou native who moved to Shenzhen in the 1980s. Known for its silky steamed fish, perfect char siu, and silky-smooth congee.

Address: 12 Shuibei Road, Luohu District, Shenzhen (深圳市罗湖区水贝路12号)

Nearest Metro:

Must Order: Steamed Sea Bass, Char Siu over Rice, Century Egg Congee

🥢 Beyond Guangzhou: Cantonese Heritage in Hong Kong & Macau

The Cantonese diaspora from Guangzhou settled in Hong Kong and Macau, developing distinctive local styles that remain connected to the mother tradition.

🥟

Tim Ho Wan (添好运) -- Hong Kong

Established: 2009 (over 15 years, Michelin 3-star)

Specialty: Michelin three-star dim sum at street prices. Chef Mak Kwai-pui, formerly of Lung King Heen, created this humble open-kitchen paradise. The baked-char-siu bao is world-famous.

Address: 9 Fuk Wing Street, Sham Shui Po, Hong Kong (香港深水埗福荣街9号)

Nearest MTR: Sham Shui Po Station, MTR

Must Order: Baked Char Siu Bao, Har Gow, Steamed Egg Cake

🍗

Yat Lok (一乐烧鹅) -- Hong Kong

Established: 1950s (over 70 years, Michelin 1-star)

Specialty: Roast goose -- not duck. The skin is lacquered and crisp, the meat intensely flavoured from the glaze. One of Hong Kong's most decorated old restaurants, still in its original location.

Address: 35-37 Stanley Village Road, Stanley, Hong Kong (香港赤柱Stanley Village Road 35-37号)

Nearest MTR: Taxi from South Horizons or Chai Wan MTR

Must Order: Roast Goose over Rice, Soy Sauce Chicken

🥐

Lei Kwan (荔湾茶餐厅) -- Macau

Established: 1974 (over 50 years)

Specialty: Macanese-Cantonese fusion. Lei Kwan has been serving the Macau Portuguese-Chinese community for half a century -- try the egg tarts, Portuguese egg tarts, and pork chop buns.

Address: 22 Rua de Santa Catarina, Macau (澳门圣安多尼堂区圣娘堂街22号)

Nearest Bus: Bus 3, 3A from Macau Ferry Terminal

Must Order: Portuguese Egg Tart, Pork Chop Bun, Milk Tea
