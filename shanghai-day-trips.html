Overview: Day Trips from Shanghai

Shanghai's own water towns are the reason drivers come here rather than the airport, and they are a genuinely different country from the Bund: canals, stone bridges, boatmen poling under willows. Within an hour of the city you can be in Zhujiajiao or Fengjing, both officially designated Chinese historical and cultural towns, or across the water at the Shanghai Disney Resort. Chongming Island is the outlier — 1,225 square kilometres of flat farmland and forest, two hours from the centre by road, and the closest thing Shanghai has to real countryside.

Zhujiajiao Water Town (朱家角古镇)

Zhujiajiao is the closest and best-preserved of Shanghai's Jiangnan water towns, about 50 km west of the Bund in Qingpu District, and it has been a settlement for over 1,700 years. Its name means 'Zhu family corner' after the family that dominated it, and it is laid out around a single canal, the Caohe, crossed by five bridges of which the 72-span Fangsheng Bridge is the best known. Thirty-nine narrow lanes run off the main street, and the town has been used as a film location often enough that it now runs tourist shuttles to the old streets of neighbouring Shanghai during the day.

How the day runs: Leave the city centre at 8:30 am and allow 70 minutes on the Shanghai–Kunshan expressway toward the Hongqiao interchange, then the last stretch through Qingpu farmland. Park at the north entrance car park, which is on the correct side of the canal for the main street. Walk east along Caohe Street past the Qing Post Office and the City God Temple, then cross Fangsheng Bridge, the highest stone arch bridge in the area at around 5.7 m, which gives the classic view down the canal. Continue to the Kezhi Garden, one of Shanghai's few surviving Ming private gardens, for an hour, and eat lunch on a rowing boat or at the riverside restaurants along Caohe Street. Aim to be back at the car by 3 pm, before the weekend coach groups arrive from the city.

50 km from Shanghai · about 70 min by car from Shanghai · half-day trip · best in March to May and September to November; the town floods its lower lanes with water in high summer, which is atmospheric but blocks foot traffic.

  • The long distance from the Bund is the main reason a car beats the bus here; the tourist coach from Shanghai South Railway Station takes over 90 minutes.
  • Small electric buggies run around the old town for a few yuan and are genuinely useful on the far side of the canal.
  • Take cash: many small stalls and boat operators do not accept mobile payment, even in the middle of the tourist core.

Fengjing Ancient Town (枫泾古镇)

Fengjing sits on the very southwest tip of Shanghai where the municipality meets Zhejiang province, and unlike Zhujiajiao it has kept working homes and small workshops rather than turning into a single retail strip. It was listed as a Chinese historical and cultural town in 2005 and won a place on the second China Top 100 Towns list in 2023. The old town preserves roughly 487,500 square metres of Ming and Qing architecture along a 3.2 km main street, with 52 bridges over the waterways. The distinctive feature is the painted wooden eaves of the old houses, which is why the town was nicknamed the town of the thousand paintings.

How the day runs: Allow 85 minutes from the city via the G50 or G60, easing off the expressway through Jinshan District's farmland, which is part of the appeal. Park at the east entrance and walk the main street from east to west along the Huangpu tributary, about 3.2 km in total. Allow an hour and a half for this stretch if you are going to look at the buildings rather than photograph them, because the painted eaves are the reason to come. Detour to the Former Residence of the Ding Family and the 300-year-old Wu family compound near the western end. Lunch is simple noodle and wonton food around the central square. Turn back at 3 pm to allow for the return leg against late-afternoon traffic.

60 km from Shanghai · about 85 min by car from Shanghai · half-day trip · best in October to December, when the town runs a lantern festival and the streets are strung with red and gold light.

  • The town is split into an old section and a new commercial zone; the interesting walking is entirely in the old section to the east.
  • Fengjing pairs well with a stop in the nearby Zhangjiang area if you want to add a second small town to the day.
  • Weekday mornings are the only time you will have the painted houses to yourself.

Shanghai Disney Resort (上海迪士尼度假区)

The resort in Chuansha Town, Pudong, opened in 2016 and is the largest Disney theme park by footprint anywhere, at 91 hectares, with a second Disney town and 1,220 hotel rooms across two hotels. The choice of mainland China's first park matters because it was built with Chinese input from the start: the centrepiece is a 14.6 hectare garden rather than a hub-and-spoke Main Street, and the Enchanted Storybook Castle is the tallest Disney castle ever built, four storeys and 60 m. The result is a park that looks deliberately unlike the Florida or Paris layouts.

How the day runs: Pre-book the date ticket online, which is mandatory and separately priced from the park ticket, and arrive for the 8 am opening. The first hour is the only time to ride TRON Lightcycle Power Run and Roaring Rapids without a 90-minute queue. Take the 30-minute boat between the two halves of the park to save the walk, then break for lunch around 11:30 before the afternoon parade. The fireworks show over the lake is worth positioning for at least 45 minutes ahead. If you are staying at the resort hotels, build the day around the evening fireworks and use the extra early entry the hotel guests receive. Drive out via the Disney exit rather than fighting the general traffic toward the city.

40 km from Shanghai · about 55 min by car from Shanghai · full-day trip · best in Mid-September to mid-November and March to mid-April; late June to early September brings heat, afternoon thunderstorms and the longest queues.

  • The resort has its own dedicated exit on the Shanghai–Luchao road, which is why the drive feels shorter than the distance suggests.
  • The Shanghai Disney Resort app handles queue times and shows a wait-time map that is more accurate than the posted boards.
  • The two hotels and Disney town are free to enter without a park ticket if you want a half-day and an evening fireworks show.

Chongming Island and Dongping Forest Park (崇明岛)

Chongming is Asia's largest alluvial island and China's second largest island under mainland administration, covering 1,225 square kilometres with 80 km of east-west extent and 13 to 18 km north to south. It sits in the mouth of the Yangtze and has been building seaward at roughly 143 m a year through silt deposition, adding about 487 hectares annually. The island's flat terrain makes it Shanghai's agricultural hinterland, producing much of the city's milk, vegetables and meat, and Dongping National Forest Park in the island's centre is one of the largest man-planted forests in China, planted from 1992 onwards to stop wind-blown sand.

How the day runs: This is a long drive, so leave at 7 am and use the Chongming Bridge and tunnel rather than the ferry. Aim for the east end near the Chongming Dongtan wetland at the coast, where the migrating cranes and geese gather from November to March, and where the 2013 Dongtan International Stork Park became a nesting site for the species that gives the area its name. On the way back, stop at Dongping Forest Park, where the planted pine and poplar forest covers a large area on the island's western flatlands and has actual riding and camping facilities. Lunch is simple farmhouse cooking around the Minglun or Chenjia town area. Be back on the mainland by 6 pm; the bridge traffic after dark is the worst part of the day.

80 km from Shanghai · about 100 min by car from Shanghai · full-day trip · best in April for the cherry blossom plantations on the eastern end and October for birdwatching in the Yancheng coastal reserve; summer is hot and humid.

  • The Chongming cross-river bridge has a toll, and a passenger ferry also runs from the Wusongkou pier in about 70 minutes if you want a crossing with a view.
  • Chongming is flat and long, so distances between the farm sites and the eastern wetland are larger than they look on a map.
  • Stork and crane numbers peak between November and February, so this trip is a winter trip if that is the bird you want.

Qibao Ancient Town (七宝古镇)

Qibao is the one ancient town that sits inside the Shanghai metropolitan area, just 15 km southwest of the centre in Minhang District, which makes it a genuinely short outing rather than an excursion. Its development goes back to the Five Dynasties period and it took its name from Qibao Temple, with the town proper taking shape by the Northern Song. The old street runs along the Puhuitang river under a row of stone bridges, and the town covers 18.10 square kilometres with a registered population of 86,300. Qibao is also known for its fighting crickets, a cottage tradition that survives on the stone bridges.

How the day runs: Reach it by driving out along Humin Road or the S4, which takes about 35 minutes and saves the 50 minutes the metro-and-bus combination takes from People's Square. Park outside the old town, because the lanes inside are pedestrian-only and closed to cars at weekends. Walk the main street past the old bridge and the cotton and spinning workshops, then stop at the Qibao Exhibition Hall, which uses the old post office building. The night market on Zhongyi Road opened for trial operation in December 2020 with more than 100 stalls of Southeast Asian, Taiwanese and Hong Kong street food. Two to three hours is plenty, and the drive back is quick.

15 km from Shanghai · about 35 min by car from Shanghai · half-day trip · best in Any time, though the town is busiest at weekends and during the New Year period when the alleys fill with lantern stalls.

  • A single Shanghai museum ticket, which has been free since March 2008, covers a great many of the city's smaller historic sites.
  • The town is a metro line away, so it is a good rainy-day substitute for a longer drive.
  • Cricket fighting survives here as a cottage tradition; you will occasionally see small groups with cic boxes on the stone bridges.

Getting around the outer districts

Shanghai's ring road system is unforgiving at rush hour. The Inner Ring and the elevated roads around the Huangpu can add 40 minutes to a journey that should take 20, and outbound traffic to Zhujiajiao and Chongming funnels through the same handful of interchanges. Leaving the city centre before 8 am makes the difference between a relaxed day and a day spent in traffic.

Parking at the water towns is straightforward and cheap, usually a flat 10 to 20 RMB for the day, but the old town cores themselves are pedestrianised, so a car gets you to the edge and no further. Budget ten minutes' walk from the car park to the first bridge.

Chongming is the exception. It is 80 km each way with almost nothing on the road for the last 40 km, so the total time for the day is dominated by distance rather than congestion. Fill the tank before leaving the city.

Planning a day trip from Shanghai

Qibao Ancient Town, Chongming Island and Dongping Forest Park and the 3 stops in between set the two ends of this Shanghai day trip route — Qibao Ancient Town sits closest at 15 km, roughly 35 minutes by car, while Chongming Island and Dongping Forest Park reaches furthest at 80 km, about 100 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Zhujiajiao Water Town works best March to May and September to November; the town floods its lower lanes with water in high summer, which is atmospheric but blocks foot traffic., while Fengjing Ancient Town suits October to December, when the town runs a lantern festival and the streets are strung with red and gold light. — in Shanghai that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

Shanghai Disney Resort, Chongming Island and Dongping Forest Park, Qibao Ancient Town each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Shanghai itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which water town should I choose between Zhujiajiao and Fengjing?

Zhujiajiao if you want the classic canal-and-bridge postcard and the shortest drive, roughly 50 km and 70 minutes with a Ming private garden and a working boat scene. Fengjing if you prefer a smaller town with more surviving Ming and Qing domestic architecture, less retail and quieter lanes. It sits further southwest at the provincial border and takes about 85 minutes. Both are officially listed Chinese historical and cultural towns.

How much time do I need at the Shanghai Disney Resort?

A full day, and it is a long one. The park opens at 8 am, the fireworks show runs in the evening, and the queues for the headline rides peak between 11 am and 3 pm. Arrive at opening, take the first hour for TRON Lightcycle Power Run before the queues build, and use the boat transport across the park to avoid losing an hour walking. If you only have six hours, buy the cheaper date-based ticket and skip the fireworks.

Is Chongming Island worth the drive for a day trip?

Yes, if what you want from Shanghai is farmland, wetlands and open sky rather than architecture. The island is 1,225 square kilometres with 80 km of east-west extent, and the eastern Dongtan wetland is a genuine birding site where cranes gather from November through February. The trade-off is distance: it is about 100 minutes each way with a tolled bridge crossing, so you need a full day and should leave early.

Can I reach Qibao Ancient Town without a car?

Easily, and it may be the better option. The town is served by Line 9 of the Shanghai Metro, and the walk from Qibao station to the old town entrance takes around ten minutes. Driving is worth it only if you want to combine Qibao with other stops in Minhang or Qingpu in the same day, since parking at the old town fills up badly at weekends.

What is the best season for the Shanghai water towns?

Autumn, from late September to November, when the willow trees along the canals turn and the crowds thin after the National Day holiday. Spring from March to May is also good, particularly for the plum blossom and the tulip plantings in the Jiangnan garden at Zhujiajiao. Midsummer is hot and humid, and in heavy rain the lower lanes in Zhujiajiao genuinely flood.

Are the water towns busy with day-trippers, and is that a problem?

On weekends in spring and autumn, yes, particularly Zhujiajiao, where coach groups arrive from the city from around 9 am. This is exactly why a car helps: you can arrive at opening, walk the main canal before the coaches, and choose a smaller side lane for the second hour. Weekday mornings are transformative, and several of the older houses are still family homes rather than shops.
