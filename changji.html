Name Origin

Changji was established in 1954 as the Changji Hui Autonomous Region and made an autonomous prefecture in 1955. The name comes from the Changji river and the old city of Manas (阜康县一带), and the prefecture's history reaches back to the Tang-dynasty Beiting Protectorate (北庭都护府), founded here in 702 to administer the whole northern Tianshan.

Education & Universities

Higher education is provided by Xinjiang University (Changji campus), Changji College and local vocational colleges, with strengths in teacher training, medicine, agriculture and animal husbandry.

Getting Around

Getting around Changji is by road and rail. The Lanzhou–Xinjiang railway stops at Changji Station, and the G30 expressway runs through Fukang, Jimsar, Qitai, Hutubi, Manas and Wenchang, connecting them all to Urumqi, 40 km to the west. There is no passenger airport inside the prefecture, so most travellers arrive via Urumqi.

Natural Resources & Environment

Changji lies on the northern slope of the Tianshan, divided into four units: the Tianshan mountains in the south, the sloping alluvial plain below them, the Gurbantünggüt Desert to the north, and the Beitaishan range in the east. It has about 190 mm of rain a year and 2,700 hours of sunshine — one of the sunniest regions in China.

Food & Shopping in Changji

What to eat and what to bring home from Changji

🍽️ Must-Try Changji Cuisine

Changji's cooking is Hui, Uyghur and Han at once: hand-pulled noodles (拉条子), big plate chicken (大盘鸡), lamb skewers off the charcoal, and the melon and grape harvests of the northern oases.

🛍️ Shopping in Changji

Changji's markets and the Fukang and Jimsar bazaars sell Hami melons, raisins, walnuts and the region's wheat and lamb. Changji is also a long-standing centre of Hui noodle-making, so a bag of the dried noodles travels well.

🍵 Tea & Drinks

Tea in Changji is poured with milk and salt, the way it is done across the northern Xinjiang oases, and shared from one bowl at the table — the host refills it until everyone has had their share.

🚗 Private Car for a Food Crawl

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts in Changji

The living traditions of Changji — the crafts, festivals and food that are still part of daily life.

🏛️ Heritage & Landmarks

The Hui Lawton embroidery (回族民间刺绣), the Beiting heritage at Jimsar and the Uyghur muqam of the oases are the region's three living traditions. The Hui community here is old and deeply rooted, and its Huiyuan (回民文化园) in Changji city and the old mosque districts are worth a walk.

Changji Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Changji like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

Tianchi is 90 minutes from Urumqi, but the road up from Fukang is beautiful and almost empty in the early morning — start at dawn.

🥢 Skip the tourist menu

Best Time to Visit Changji

April to June for the mountain roads and the wildflowers; September and October for the melon harvest and the clearest air on the Tianshan slopes.

🌸 Spring

Chinese spring · March–May — the desert roads open, the apricot blossom comes out in the oases and Tianchi is clear before the summer crowds. (daytime 5.6–26.2°C, nights around -3.9°C; about 20 mm of precipitation a month)

☀️ Summer

Chinese summer · June–August — the high season in the mountains, with Tianchi busiest at midday and empty on an early start. (daytime 30.9–32.6°C, nights around 17.4°C; about 22 mm of precipitation a month)

🍂 Autumn

Chinese autumn · September–October — melon, grape and apple season in the oases, and the first snow on the Bogda peaks. (daytime 3.4–25.6°C, nights around -4.6°C; about 15 mm of precipitation a month)

❄️ Winter

Chinese winter · November–February — cold and dry with heavy snow on the mountain roads; the desert towns are quiet and clear. (daytime -9.1–-5.5°C, nights around -18.4°C; about 9 mm of precipitation a month)

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Changji

What to drink in Changji, where to drink it, and how the locals do it.

🍵 Tea Houses of Changji

Tea in Changji is poured with milk and salt, the way it is done across the northern Xinjiang oases, and shared from one bowl at the table — the host refills it until everyone has had their share.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Ask your driver to stop at a house where the tea is brewed properly: a small pot, several cups, and a host who will explain how the leaves change as the water cools. It is the cheapest and most relaxing hour of the day.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Pour for others before yourself, and pour with the other hand as a small sign of respect. Tapping two fingers on the table is the traditional way to say thank you.

Most houses will refill your covered bowl for free — there is no need to order again.

Tea Culture & Teahouse Guide in Changji

Where to sit down for a bowl of tea in Changji

🍵 Tea Houses of Changji

Tea culture runs through the day in Changji: loose-leaf houses serve the region's own greens in covered bowls, and the older teahouses around the old town are where the city's social life happens.

🫖 A Gongfu Tasting

Most visitors try a gongfu-style tasting session — a host brews one tea at a time and explains how the leaves open — and pair it with the local snacks sold in the same establishments.

💡 Tea Etiquette Tips

Ask your driver for the teahouse that locals favour rather than the loudest one on the tourist route; the best rooms are usually a short walk from the market, and a quiet hour there is the cheapest and most rewarding way to understand the city's pace.
