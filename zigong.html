Zigong at a Glance

Zigong (自贡) — "Salt Capital" — is a city in southern Sichuan Province, China, at the foot of the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau. The city is famous as China's historic salt production center (盐都) — producing salt for over 2,000 years — and as one of the world's richest dinosaur fossil sites, with the Zigong Dinosaur Museum built directly over the extraordinary Dashanpu fossil deposit.

The Salt Capital of China

Zigong's identity is rooted in salt. The city has been a center of salt production for over 2,000 years — from the Han Dynasty through to the modern era. The salt wells of Zigong produced brine (salt water) from deep underground deposits, and the city became the most important salt production and trading center in southwestern China. The name "Zigong" (自贡) — literally "Self-Dedication" or "voluntarily contributing" — reflects the city's historical obligation to supply salt to the imperial court.

What makes Zigong's salt history extraordinary is the depth of the wells. By the Song Dynasty (960–1279 CE), Zigong salt well drillers had developed techniques to drill wells over 1,000 meters deep — reaching underground brine reservoirs that no other civilization had accessed. This deep drilling technology is considered one of China's most significant pre-modern engineering achievements, and the techniques used are the direct ancestors of modern oil and gas drilling. The world's first deep drill well was sunk in Zigong, not Pennsylvania.

Name Origin

The name Zigong (自贡) dates to the Tang Dynasty (618–907 CE) and reflects the city's historical obligation to supply salt to the imperial government. The characters 自 (zì, "self, oneself") and 贡 (gòng, "tribute") together mean "voluntarily offering tribute" — referring to the salt tax and tribute that the city contributed to the imperial court. The city developed as a major salt industrial center under state supervision, with its administrative apparatus organized around the salt trade.

The Dinosaur Capital of Southern China

Zigong's other great claim to fame is dinosaurs. The Dashanpu Dinosaur Fossil Site (大山铺恐龙化石群) — discovered in 1972 during construction of a salt well — is one of the most extraordinary dinosaur fossil deposits in the world. The site, covering approximately 7,000 square meters, has yielded tens of thousands of fossil fragments representing multiple dinosaur species, as well as amphibian, mammal, and other vertebrate fossils from the Jurassic period (approximately 160 million years ago).

The Zigong Dinosaur Museum — built directly over the fossil site, so visitors look down at fossils still embedded in the original quarry floor — is one of the largest dinosaur museums in the world and the first museum in China to be built over a live dinosaur excavation site. The museum's collection includes complete skeletons of massive sauropods, fearsome theropods, and armored stegosaurs — all from Zigong's Jurassic fossil beds.

Geography & Location

Zigong is located in southern Sichuan Province, at the transition between the Sichuan Basin and the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau. The city sits along the Tongguan River (通关河) — a tributary of the Yangtze — which historically served as the transport route for salt shipments. Zigong is approximately 200km from Chengdu (Sichuan's capital) and 300km from Chongqing. The city is connected to the Chengdu-Chongqing high-speed rail network via Zigong Station and Zigong East Station.

Top Zigong Attractions

From the world-class dinosaur museum to 2,000 years of salt heritage, explore Sichuan's salt capital with a private driver.

🦕

🏛️ Zigong Dinosaur Museum (自贡恐龙博物馆)

Dashanpu · World's largest Jurassic dinosaur site

The Zigong Dinosaur Museum — built directly over the Dashanpu fossil excavation site — is one of the world's great dinosaur museums. Walk through the main hall and look down at dinosaur fossils still embedded in the original Jurassic quarry floor. The museum's collection includes massive sauropod skeletons, theropod reconstructions, and the complete fossil record of Zigong's Jurassic ecosystem. One of China's most impressive natural history museums.

🏭

🏛️ Zigong Salt Museum (自贡盐业历史博物馆)

2,000 years · Deep well drilling · Salt trade

The Zigong Salt Museum — housed in a historic salt merchant guild hall (西秦会馆) — documents 2,000 years of salt production in Zigong. Exhibits include ancient drilling tools, models of traditional salt wells, salt trade documents, and the remarkable story of how Zigong's engineers developed deep drilling techniques that prefigured modern petroleum engineering. The building itself — an elaborate Qing Dynasty guild hall — is a masterpiece of traditional Sichuanese architecture.

🏮

🏯 Zigong Lantern Festival (自贡灯会)

Chinese New Year · Largest lantern display in China

The Zigong Lantern Festival (自贡灯会) — held annually during the Chinese New Year period (late January to mid-February) at the Zigong Lantern Park (彩灯公园) — is one of China's most spectacular cultural events. Millions of visitors come to see the illuminated lanterns — massive sculptural displays tens of meters tall, depicting dragons, flowers, legends, and modern themes, all lit by LED and powered by creative engineering. Zigong's lantern-making tradition dates back centuries.

🏘️

🏘️ Shenbao Street (仙滩 / 釜溪步行街)

Historic salt merchant street · Traditional architecture

Shenbao Street (仙滩) and the surrounding Fuxi River (釜溪河) old district are the historic heart of Zigong's salt trade. The area has narrow lanes, traditional courtyard houses, old bridge crossings, and the atmosphere of the 19th-century salt merchant city. The Fuxi River was the main transport route for salt barges, and the old streets along its banks were lined with salt merchant houses, guild halls, and warehouses.

🛢️

🛕 Historic Salt Well Sites (古盐井)

Tang–Qing dynasty · Deep drilling sites · Industrial heritage

Several historic salt well sites around Zigong preserve the physical remains of the ancient salt industry — including the Donny Well (燊海井), one of the world's first deep drill wells at over 1,000 meters depth, sunk during the Qing Dynasty and still preserved as a historic site. The site shows the complete infrastructure of traditional salt production — the derrick, the brine pipelines, and the evaporation furnaces.

🌿

🛕 Rongzhou Pagoda & Fuxi River (荣州大佛)

Ancient Buddhist cliff statue · Riverside scenery

The Rongzhou Fuxi Giant Buddha (荣州大佛) — a massive Buddhist statue carved into the cliff face above the Fuxi River — is one of the tallest ancient Buddha statues in Sichuan Province, standing approximately 23 meters tall. The statue dates to the Tang Dynasty and is set in a dramatic natural cliff setting overlooking the river. The surrounding area has a pleasant riverside promenade and traditional architecture.

Zigong's Best Photo Spots

Capture Sichuan's salt capital from its most evocative angles.

🛕 🦕 Dinosaur Museum — Fossil Quarry Floor

Best with available light

The main hall of the Zigong Dinosaur Museum — where the floor is the original Jurassic quarry, with dinosaur fossils still embedded in the rock — is one of the most dramatic museum spaces in China. The fossils, the quarry walls, and the careful lighting make for extraordinary photography. Morning light through the high windows is best. Wide-angle and macro lenses are both useful here.

🏯 🏮 Lantern Festival — Night Sculptures

Best at night, Chinese New Year period

The Zigong Lantern Festival at night — when the massive LED lantern sculptures are fully illuminated — is one of the most spectacular nighttime displays in China. The lantern park (彩灯公园) transforms into an extraordinary world of light, color, and scale. The best photography is after 8 PM when the full illumination is active. Tripod recommended for the best results.

🏭 Salt Museum — Qing Dynasty Guild Hall

Best with available light

The Salt Museum's building — the 西秦会馆 (Xiqin Guild Hall), a Qing Dynasty salt merchant guild hall — is as impressive as its collection. The elaborate wooden carvings, painted ceilings, stone lions, and traditional Sichuanese architectural details make for excellent architectural photography. The central courtyard is particularly beautiful, with light falling through the roof into the ceremonial hall.

🛢️ Donny Well — Ancient Drilling Rig

Best morning light

The Donny Well (燊海井) — the preserved Qing Dynasty deep drill well site — with its wooden derrick, brine channels, and evaporation furnaces, is a remarkable piece of industrial heritage photography. The morning light on the wooden structure and the steam rising from the brine vats creates an atmospheric scene. The site is most photogenic in the morning.

🌉 Fuxi River — Old Bridges at Dusk

Best at dusk

The Fuxi River (釜溪河) old district — with its traditional bridges, stone river banks, and old courtyard houses — is most atmospheric at dusk when the lanterns come on along the river and the reflections shimmer in the water. Walk the river promenade from the old stone bridge for the best views of the traditional architecture and the city's historic salt transport waterway.

⛏️ Historic Salt Well Derricks — Industrial Landscape

Best year-round

The historic salt well areas surrounding Zigong — with their old wooden derricks, brine pipelines, and traditional evaporating ponds — create a distinctive industrial landscape unlike anywhere else in China. These sites, some still active, combine industrial heritage with the rural Sichuan landscape. A drive through the countryside south of the city reveals the best-preserved historic well sites.

Food & Shopping in Zigong

🍜 Must-Try Zigong & Sichuan Cuisine

Zigong Salt-Baked Chicken (自贡盐帮鸡)

The signature dish of Zigong — chicken baked in a salt crust, using the same coarse salt for which the city is famous. The salt seals in the chicken's juices while baking, producing meat that is aromatic, tender, and infused with salt flavor. A specialty of the salt merchant era, still served at traditional Zigong restaurants. A must-try in Zigong.

Spicy Salt Crispy Duck (冷吃鸭)

Cold spicy duck — a Zigong specialty of dried, spiced duck meat tossed with Sichuan pepper, chili, and garlic — is the city's most famous snack. The duck is first salt-cured, then deep-fried until crispy, then tossed in the spicy sauce. Sold at street food stalls and as a packaged souvenir. The numbing Sichuan pepper heat is the defining characteristic.

Zigong Noodles (自贡水面)

Zigong's unique noodle style — a thin, wheat noodle served in a clear or spicy broth with pickled vegetables and minced pork — is a beloved breakfast and lunch staple. The noodles are called 水面 (shuimian, "water noodles") and are made fresh daily at small noodle shops throughout the city. Best at the morning noodle shops near the Fuxi River old district.

Sichuan Hotpot (四川火锅)

Sichuan hotpot — with its signature malachite (copper-green) broth, numbing Sichuan pepper, and fresh meat and vegetable dipping items — is a must in Sichuan. Zigong has excellent hotpot restaurants, some specializing in the local "salt capital" version with extra salt-preserved and dried ingredients. Best for dinner in the cooler months.

River Shrimp with Spicy Salt (香辣虾)

Freshwater shrimp from the Fuxi River and Tongguan River — quickly wok-fried with Sichuan pepper, chili, and garlic — make for an excellent local specialty. The shrimp are small but sweet and tender, and the spicy, numbing sauce is the perfect Sichuanese treatment. Available at riverside restaurants and the Fuxi River food stalls.

🛍️ Shopping Destinations

Zigong Salt Museum Gift Shop

The Zigong Salt Museum has an excellent gift shop selling reproductions of ancient salt artifacts, salt crystal decorative objects, and books on Zigong's salt history. The best-quality souvenirs from the city are found here. Also sells packages of Zigong's famous salt-baked chicken spices for home cooking.

Shenbao Street & Fuxi River Night Market

The Shenbao Street area and the Fuxi River food stalls are the heart of Zigong's street food and local product scene. In the evening, the area comes alive with food vendors selling cold spicy duck, Zigong noodles, grilled skewers, and local specialties. The old street also has small shops selling local crafts and food products.

🎁 What to Buy in Zigong

Zigong Salt Crystal Decorations

Coarse salt crystals from Zigong — pressed into decorative shapes, sold as lamp lighters, or packaged as bath salts — are sold at the Salt Museum and at specialty shops. The salt from Zigong is known for its purity and mineral content. Small decorative items make distinctive and lightweight souvenirs of the "Salt Capital."

Cold Spicy Duck (冷吃兔 / 冷吃鸭)

The famous Zigong cold spicy duck (and cold spicy rabbit) — dried, spiced, and packaged for travel — is sold at food stalls and specialty shops throughout Zigong. The packaged version travels well and can be kept for weeks. A spicy, aromatic snack that captures Zigong's culinary character. Available at the night market and at railway station shops.

Dinosaur Model Souvenirs

Dinosaur model replicas — from small plastic toys to detailed resin skeleton models — are sold at the Zigong Dinosaur Museum gift shop and at street stalls near the museum. The quality and variety at the museum gift shop are best. Children's dinosaur toys and educational fossil replicas are particularly popular with families.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts of Zigong

Traditional crafts and cultural traditions of Sichuan's salt capital.

🛢️ Zigong Deep Well Drilling Technology

The ancient craft of deep well drilling — developed in Zigong over centuries to reach underground brine reservoirs at depths exceeding 1,000 meters — is one of China's most significant pre-modern engineering traditions. The drilling tools, the bamboo-lined boreholes, and the brine lifting equipment represent a sophisticated industrial technology that predated modern petroleum engineering. The Donny Well (燊海井) site preserves the complete equipment of a Qing Dynasty deep well.

Where to see: Donny Well historic site, Zigong Salt Museum.

🏮 Zigong Lantern Making (自贡彩灯)

The craft of lantern making in Zigong — creating the massive sculptural illuminated lanterns for the annual lantern festival — combines traditional Chinese lantern-making with large-scale sculpture, engineering, and LED lighting technology. The lantern makers (花灯艺人) use steel armatures, silk covers, and thousands of LED lights to create the extraordinary lantern displays. The craft is practiced year-round in Zigong's lantern workshops, with the largest and most skilled workshops producing the festival displays.

Where to see: Zigong Lantern Park (Chinese New Year), lantern workshops year-round.

🍗 Salt-Baked Chicken Preparation (盐帮鸡制作)

The craft of preparing Zigong's signature salt-baked chicken — selecting the right chicken, preparing the coarse salt bed, controlling the baking temperature — is a culinary tradition passed down through the city's restaurant families. The technique, which uses coarse salt both as cooking medium and flavoring, reflects Zigong's deep connection to its salt heritage. Many Zigong restaurants guard their family salt-baked chicken recipe as closely as any industrial secret.

Where to experience: Traditional Zigong restaurants, Fuxi River food district.

🥢 Zigong Culinary Tradition (盐帮菜)

The distinctive Zigong culinary style — "Yanban cuisine" (盐帮菜, "Salt Guild cuisine") — developed in the city's salt merchant era, when wealthy salt merchants demanded sophisticated restaurant cooking. Yanban cuisine is characterized by its bold use of Sichuan pepper and chili, its emphasis on dried and salt-preserved ingredients, and its elaborate preparation techniques. The style is distinct from generic Sichuanese cuisine and is considered one of Sichuan's four major regional culinary traditions.

Where to experience: Zigong's traditional restaurants, Fuxi River restaurant district.

🦕 Dinosaur Fossil Excavation & Preservation

The scientific craft of dinosaur fossil excavation and preservation — developed at the Dashanpu site and practiced by the Zigong Dinosaur Museum's team — represents a specialized expertise in vertebrate paleontology. The museum's team has pioneered techniques for extracting and preserving the extraordinary fossil finds from the Jurassic beds. The museum regularly conducts public fossil preparation demonstrations.

Where to see: Zigong Dinosaur Museum fossil preparation lab (when demonstrations are scheduled).

🏺 Salt Merchant Guild Hall Architecture

The traditional architecture of Zigong's salt merchant era — courtyard houses, guild halls, and warehouses — represents a distinctive Sichuanese commercial architectural tradition. The 西秦会馆 (Xiqin Guild Hall) — now the Salt Museum — is the finest surviving example, with elaborate wood carvings, painted ceilings, stone reliefs, and a floor plan designed for ceremonial and commercial functions. The building's preservation and adaptive use as a museum is itself a form of cultural heritage conservation.

Where to see: Zigong Salt Museum (西秦会馆), Fuxi River old district.

Zigong Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Zigong like a local.

🦕

Go to the Dinosaur Museum First Thing

The Zigong Dinosaur Museum is most peaceful and least crowded in the first hour after opening (9 AM). On weekends and during school holidays, it can get very busy with Chinese family groups. If you want to photograph the quarry floor without crowds and have time to read the exhibits properly, arrive at opening time. The museum takes 2–3 hours to explore properly.

🏮

Visit the Lantern Festival on a Weeknight

The Zigong Lantern Festival is most crowded on weekends and public holidays during the Chinese New Year period. If you can visit on a weekday evening, you will have a much better experience — the lantern displays are equally spectacular and the park is significantly less crowded. The illumination turns on at dusk (around 6–7 PM depending on season).

🍗

Eat Salt-Baked Chicken at the Fuxi River Restaurants

The riverside restaurants along the Fuxi River (釜溪河) — particularly the ones near the old stone bridge — serve the best salt-baked chicken in Zigong. These small, family-run places have been perfecting the same recipe for decades. Follow the locals — if a restaurant has a queue, it's the right one. Eat it with cold beer and pickled vegetables for the full experience.

🛢️

Visit the Donny Well on Your Way In or Out of the City

The Donny Well (燊海井) — the preserved Qing Dynasty deep drill well — is located on the southern edge of the city, about 15 minutes from the city center. It takes about 45 minutes to an hour to visit. Ask your driver to stop on the way in from the railway station or on the way to lunch — it's a quick detour that gives you a tangible connection to Zigong's 2,000-year salt heritage.

🌶️

Ask for Extra Sichuan Pepper at the Night Market

Zigong's cooking uses Sichuan pepper generously — more so than Chengdu or Chongqing. When ordering cold spicy duck or other dishes at the Shenbao Street night market, don't be shy about asking for extra Sichuan pepper (花椒). The local version of "spicy" is defined by the numbing quality as much as the heat. A truly Zigong experience means your lips going slightly numb — that's correct.

🦕

Look for the Small Dinosaur Carvings in Old Buildings

Zigong's dinosaur heritage is everywhere — including in unexpected places. Look for small carved dinosaur motifs on old buildings, street lamp posts, and manhole covers throughout the city. The city has embraced its dinosaur identity in ways both grand (the museum) and humble (dinosaur-shaped street drain covers). Finding these small details adds a layer of fun to exploring the city on foot.

Best Time to Visit Zigong

Each season reveals a different face of Sichuan's salt capital.

🌸

Spring

Mar–May · 12–26°C

Excellent. Spring is the most comfortable season for visiting Zigong — warm days (20–26°C in April and May), cool evenings, and low humidity. The dinosaur museum and salt heritage sites are at their best. Spring is also the season for Zigong's famous cold spicy duck — at its best freshly made. May is particularly pleasant before the summer heat sets in.

Highlights: Comfortable weather, outdoor sightseeing, best cold spicy duck season

☀️

Summer

Jun–Aug · 24–34°C

Hot and humid. Zigong's summer is hot and humid — temperatures regularly exceed 33°C in July and August, and the Sichuan humidity can be oppressive. The Fuxi River evening atmosphere is pleasant for evening walks and night market visits, but daytime sightseeing is uncomfortable. Summer is low season for tourism, so hotel prices drop. If visiting in summer, plan outdoor activities for early morning or evening.

Highlights: Low hotel prices, evening Fuxi River atmosphere, night market

🏮

Winter (Chinese New Year)

Jan–Feb · 4–12°C · Lantern Festival

The most magical time to visit Zigong is during the Chinese New Year period — when the Zigong Lantern Festival (自贡灯会) transforms the city with millions of illuminated lanterns. This is Zigong's peak tourist season — the lantern park is extraordinarily crowded on weekends but spectacular at any time. Book accommodation well in advance for the Chinese New Year period. Winter temperatures are cool (4–12°C) but manageable.

Highlights: Zigong Lantern Festival (Jan–Feb), magical nighttime illumination

🍂

Autumn

Sep–Nov · 14–26°C

Excellent. Autumn is another excellent season for Zigong — temperatures are comfortable (16–24°C in September and October), the humidity drops after summer, and the city is at its most comfortable for sightseeing. The dinosaur museum, salt museum, and outdoor heritage sites are all pleasant to visit. Hotel prices normalize after the Chinese New Year peak. October is particularly recommended.

Highlights: Best weather of the year, comfortable outdoor sightseeing, post-summer crowds gone
