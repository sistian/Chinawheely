West Lake covers 6.39 square kilometres on the western edge of Hangzhou, measuring 3.2 kilometres north to south and 2.8 kilometres east to west. A scenic destination since the Tang dynasty, it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape in 2011. Four causeways cross the water, three artificial islands sit offshore, and Gushan is the sole natural island. From Xiaoshan Airport the road distance is about 30 kilometres across the Qiantang River, roughly 50 minutes outside peak hours.

Hangzhou Xiaoshan International Airport (HGH)

30 km from the Hangzhou terminal, about 50 minutes by car.

Xiaoshan Airport opened on 30 December 2000 on the south bank of the Qiantang River, 27 kilometres east of central Hangzhou. The run into town follows the Airport Expressway over the Xixing Bridge and then threads west through the riverside districts to the eastern shore of West Lake, on the far side of the former walled city.

Why transfer to West Lake?

The lake is a constructed landscape as much as a natural one. Bai Juyi dredged it in 822 and the northern causeway still carries his name; Su Shi built the Su Causeway from dredged silt in 1089 and marked out three stone pagodas that became the origin of the Three Ponds Mirroring the Moon. The Yanggong Causeway followed a 1508 dredging, and Ruan Gong Islet was piled up in 1809. Each work period reshaped the same sheet of water, and the layers remain legible on a walk around the shore.

Recognition is unusually dense for a single site. The Ten Scenes of West Lake were formalised under the Qing emperors, with the Kangxi Emperor's inscriptions carved into steles and the Qianlong Emperor's poems added on the reverse. The landscape has influenced garden design in China, Japan and Korea, and replicas appear in imperial gardens in Beijing. Leifeng Pagoda, which collapsed in 1924, was rebuilt on its original spot in 2002, restoring the south-bank silhouette opposite Baochu Pagoda on the north.

  • Surface area of 6.39 square kilometres, 3.2 kilometres north to south
  • UNESCO World Heritage cultural landscape since 2011
  • Su Causeway dates to 1089, Bai Causeway commemorates the 822 dredging
  • Leifeng Pagoda rebuilt in 2002 on the site of the 1924 collapse
  • First major Chinese attraction to abolish entrance fees, free since 2002

What to do at West Lake once you arrive

The circuit is walkable in sections, and the layout is summarised locally as one hill, two pagodas, three islets, four causeways and five lakes. Gushan, the natural island, connects to the north shore by Xiling Bridge and the Bai Causeway, and holds Zhongshan Park plus the Zhejiang Provincial Museum in the grounds of a former Qing detached palace. The Broken Bridge at the east end of the Bai Causeway is the standard starting point for the northern shore, and the Xiling Seal Society sits on the slope just above it.

Crossing the water passes the three islets: Lesser Yingzhou, laid out between 1607 and 1611, the Mid-Lake Pavilion and Ruan Gong Islet. Three Ponds Mirroring the Moon occupies the largest islet, and its stone pagodas appear on the reverse of the one-yuan banknote. For the west side, the Su Causeway runs about 2.8 kilometres through six arched stone bridges, and the Longjing tea villages sit a short drive further into the hills past Lingyin Temple. The 2024 National Day week drew 4.43 million visits in seven days, so weekday mornings are measurably quieter.

  • Longxiangqiao station on Metro Line 1 is the closest stop to the eastern shore
  • The lake itself is free; only the pagodas, islet ferries and a few gardens charge entry
  • Rowed boats and electric ferries operate from hubs on the north and south shores
  • Combine the lake with Lingyin Temple to the west, founded in 326 AD in the hills above Feilai Peak

Arriving at Hangzhou Xiaoshan International Airport

Xiaoshan replaced the old Jianqiao airfield at the end of 2000 and now works through four passenger terminals: Terminals 1 and 3 handle domestic flights, the smaller Terminal 2 takes international and regional services, and Terminal 4, the newest, accepts both. Construction of the original field began in July 1997. Allow around twenty minutes from touchdown for checked bags and any immigration formalities before you step into the arrivals hall.

A booked car meets you with a name board and tracks the flight number, so an early morning or delayed arrival needs no separate phone coordination. The drive west uses the Airport Expressway and the Xixing Bridge across the Qiantang, then continues through the downtown blocks to the lake; budget closer to 70 minutes at the evening peak rather than the 50-minute clear run. Metro Line 1, Line 7 and the Line 19 airport express also serve the terminal, as an alternative when the roads jam.

What the transfer costs

Private sedans between Xiaoshan and the lake are generally quoted at 120 to 180 yuan, with the price covering fuel, driver and the expressway tolls on the airport section. Two travellers with two hold bags fit a sedan without difficulty; families of five or six usually move to an MPV class at roughly 160 to 220 yuan for the same run.

Bookings that continue past a set waiting window are billed as a half-day or day package rather than as a single transfer. The default free waiting allowance at arrivals is 60 to 90 minutes, which is ample for a terminal change between Terminals 3 and 4 on foot. Night flights landing after the metro closes carry a modest surcharge, typically 20 to 30 percent above the daytime rate.

Ask to be dropped at Hubin Road or the Broken Bridge car park on the north-east corner rather than on a causeway, since vehicles are restricted on the Bai and Su causeways at busy times. For hotel pickups, most lakefront properties sit one block east of the shore, so a short walk completes the last stretch.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Hangzhou Xiaoshan Airport from West Lake?

The airport stands 27 kilometres east of central Hangzhou, and the lake sits on the west side of the core, so the road distance comes to roughly 30 kilometres via the Airport Expressway and the Xixing Bridge. Clear-traffic driving time is about 50 minutes, while the evening peak can push it towards 70 minutes.

Is there a metro from Xiaoshan Airport to West Lake?

Yes. Line 1, Line 7 and the Line 19 airport express all call at the terminal. Line 1 runs across the city to Longxiangqiao, the station closest to the eastern shore of the lake; count on roughly an hour with the transfer included. A private car remains the faster choice with luggage or outside metro hours.

Do I need tickets to enter West Lake?

No. The lake has been free to enter since 2002, when it became the first major Chinese attraction to cancel admission fees. Separate charges apply to Leifeng Pagoda, the islet ferries, and a handful of gardens such as the one at Fish Viewing at the Flower Pond, but the shoreline and both famous causeways cost nothing.

What are the Ten Scenes of West Lake?

They are a fixed catalogue of viewpoints dating to the Southern Song and standardised under the Qing emperors, including Spring Dawn on the Su Causeway, Curved Yard and Lotus Pool in Summer, Autumn Moon over the Calm Lake, and Lingering Snow on the Broken Bridge. The Kangxi Emperor's inscribed titles stand on stone steles beside the spots, and a new set of ten was voted in 1984.

Which pagoda should I visit, Leifeng or Baochu?

Leifeng Pagoda on the south shore was rebuilt in 2002 on the ruins of the tower that collapsed in 1924, and you can climb it for a view over the outer lake. Baochu Pagoda on the north side on Baoshi Hill is a slender structure on a hilltop path and cannot be entered. Most itineraries pair Leifeng with a ferry to the Three Ponds islet.
