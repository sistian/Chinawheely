Overview: Day Trips from Wuhan

Wuhan is a city of three towns on two rivers, and its day trips exploit that geography better than most. The Yangtze River Bridge is the one piece of the city's 1950s infrastructure that is still admired, East Lake is a 33 square kilometre inland sea with 100 km of shoreline, and the Mulan mountains an hour to the north give genuine elevation with a 600 m peak and a forest that is older than most of the country.

East Lake and Moshan Hill (东湖)

East Lake covers 33 square kilometres of water, six times the area of Hangzhou's West Lake, and until the city's expansion made Tangxun Lake larger it was the biggest city lake in China. The eastern shore is lined with 100 km of shoreline, and the wooded hills of Moshan on the western side rise to 120 m and hold the 600-year-old temple of Lingxiu, the oldest building in Wuhan. Moshan also has an observatory, a botanical garden covering more than 72 hectares with over 4,000 plant species, and a sculpture collection of more than 100 pieces set across the hillsides.

How the day runs: Arrive at the Tingtao (Listening to the Waves) scenic area by 8 am, which is the north-western section with the longest tree-lined causeway and the best of the morning light on the water. Rent a bicycle or an electric two-wheeler and ride the lakeside road to Moshan, which takes about 40 minutes and gives the best combination of water and wooded hills. Break at the botanical garden, then continue to the Tingtao Peninsula for the lawn and the old German-style villas. Have lunch in the lakeside area rather than driving back into town for it. Return by mid-afternoon, or stay for the evening when the lake reflections and the city glow on the far shore are at their best.

8 km from Wuhan · about 25 min by car from Wuhan · half-day trip · best in March to May for the cherry blossom around Moshan, and October to November for the lake and the willows; summer brings heavy humidity on the water.

  • The lakeside roads are car-free in large sections, which makes cycling the correct way round the perimeter.
  • East Lake is large enough that trying to drive the whole 100 km shoreline in one day is a mistake; pick two sections.
  • The Hubei Provincial Museum sits directly on the western shore and is a natural midday stop on the same trip.

Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge (武汉长江大桥)

The first bridge across the Yangtze, built between 1955 and 1957, and the first of its kind anywhere on the river. It spans 1.6 km from Turtle Hill in Hanyang on the north bank to Snake Hill in Wuchang on the south, carried on eight piers with a double-deck steel truss above and a roadway below. At the western end stands the 1957 memorial monument and the Wang Jinshan, the first of the 1954 flood-crush brigades, who worked day and night for two and a half years on the cofferdam. The bridge was listed as a major historical and cultural site in 2013.

How the day runs: Go to the Hanyang side, where the monument stands at the foot of Turtle Hill, at 7 am for the cleanest light and the quietest platform. Walk out onto the bridge itself, which is a public pedestrian walkway with the river 40 m below on both sides and the truss framing the sky overhead. The best frame is from the monument terrace looking back along the bridge deck to the Wuchang bank with the towers and the city behind, which compresses the whole structure into one line. Take the tram or bus across to the Wuchang side for the Shouyi Plaza and the riverside promenade below Snake Hill, and look back for the other classic view of the bridge with the city behind it. Add the Yellow Crane Tower a short walk uphill if the afternoon is clear. Two hours is plenty.

6 km from Wuhan · about 25 min by car from Wuhan · half-day trip · best in October to April, with clear conditions for the view across the river; in summer the humidity makes distant subjects soft.

  • The bridge is a working road and rail crossing, so the pedestrian walkway on the deck has separate access hours and gets closed for maintenance periods.
  • Stand at the western Hanyang end for the view along the deck with Wuchang behind, and at the eastern end for the reverse.
  • The bridge is best on a still winter morning when the river surface holds a reflection of the truss.

Mulan Mountain (木兰山)

Mulan Mountain in Huangpi District is 70 km north of Wuhan, with a main peak of 600 m and a total area of more than 30 square kilometres that includes a 30 million cubic metre reservoir and more than 10,000 mu of forest. The mountain is named for Mulan, and the wider area has been a film location repeatedly because the 1958 opera movie Mulan Goes to the Mountain and the later television series were both shot here. The Song dynasty poet Du Mu's verse about the mountain is carved on a cliff face, and the surroundings include a Tang-style walled village with a restored general's residence.

How the day runs: Leave at 7 am on the S13 or the Wuhan–Xiaogan expressway and allow around two hours. Arrive at the scenic gate area and take the shuttle bus up the mountain road to the main peak, which saves the steepest 10 km of climbing. The summit terrace looks out over the Mulan reservoir and the forest below, which is the reason to come. Walk down through the Tang-style village, where the Ming and Qing houses and the Du Mu cliff inscription are on the way. Have lunch at the mountain resort or in the village. If the season is right, add the associated Mulan Lake, formed by the dam, for the water views. Be back on the road by 4 pm to beat the Sunday evening return traffic.

75 km from Wuhan · about 110 min by car from Wuhan · full-day trip · best in October to December for clear mountain air and autumn colour, and May for the azalea bloom; the summit road can be icy in January.

  • Mulan Mountain and Mulan Lake are separate ticketed areas close to each other, so most visitors combine them on the same day.
  • The distance is measured from central Wuhan and the motorway section is fast, but the last 20 km through local roads is slow.
  • Autumn weekends draw heavy traffic; a weekday gives you the forest to yourself.

Hubei Provincial Museum (湖北省博物馆)

The museum was founded in 1953 during the construction of the East Lake Scenic Area and holds more than 240,000 objects, of which some 14,000 are the best of the collection and more than 900 are national first-class cultural relics. Its centrepiece is the Zenghou Yi chime bells, a set of 65 bronze bells from a tomb of Marquis Yi of Zeng dated 433 BC, whose tones cover three octaves and a half and carry a pitch notation system inscribed more than 2,000 years ago. The other major draw is the Sword of Goujian, with its 875 bronze characters inlaid in gold. The 2011 extension echoes the bronze drum form of the bells.

How the day runs: Come for the morning and start with the Zenghou Yi bells, which are displayed in a dedicated darkened gallery with the original bronze vessels in their excavation positions. The chime bell performance, where a small ensemble plays the bells on a fixed day each week, is scheduled in the afternoon, so plan around it if it is running. The sword gallery is on the same floor and can be done quickly. Allow three hours for the main route and the special exhibitions, which rotate through the gallery spaces. The glass-roofed atrium and the bronze drum exterior make good architectural frames before and after. Lunch at the museum's own restaurant rather than leaving and losing an hour of parking time.

9 km from Wuhan · about 30 min by car from Wuhan · half-day trip · best in Any time; it is entirely indoors, which makes it the reliable choice on a wet or very hot day.

  • The chime bell concert is free and extremely popular, and tickets are handed out at a set time in the morning, so arrive early for that.
  • The museum is free but requires advance registration through its official WeChat account, and it is closed on Mondays.
  • Because it is inside the East Lake Scenic Area, it combines naturally with a morning or afternoon on the lake.

Hubu Alley and the Wuchang food streets (户部巷)

Hubu Alley is a 150 m long, 4 m wide lane in Wuchang that runs north to south connecting Minzu Road and Ziyou Road, named after the Ministry of Revenue that stood beside it and recorded in a Ming dynasty gazetteer. It is a genuine old lane of brick-and-timber houses with its original appearance largely intact, and it has become known as the first lane of Wuhan-flavoured breakfast. The Xie's mianwo shop established there in the 1940s and Shi Popo's hot dry noodles are the two names that carry the place, with the mianwo, a fermented mung bean batter fried into a bowl, being the defining Wuhan breakfast.

How the day runs: Go at 7 am, when the morning crowd is at its peak and the steam and the noise are at their most. Walk the full 150 m of the lane, stopping at Shi Popo's hot dry noodles and at Xie's mianwo, and do the hot dry noodles properly: the noodles are prefaced with sesame paste, then topped with pickled long beans, preserved egg, pickled turnip and chopped spring onion, and mixed thoroughly. Around 9:30 the queue has gone and the shutters start coming down. Afterwards walk north to the Wuchang riverfront and the Shouyi plaza below Snake Hill, or head east to the Yellow Crane Tower on Snake Hill, which is a ten-minute walk uphill. Two hours covers the alley and one of those extensions comfortably.

4 km from Wuhan · about 20 min by car from Wuhan · half-day trip · best in Early morning, any time of year, because the lane is at its best before 9 am and empty of heat by then.

  • Eat the mianwo while it is hot and straight from the pan; it collapses within a few minutes of leaving the oil.
  • The yellow crane tower ticket includes the separate cost of the cable car up the hill, which saves walking in summer.
  • Jiqing Street, a short walk away, is the better evening food street; the saying goes that you eat Hubu Alley for breakfast and Jiqing Street for dinner.

Guiyuan Temple and Hanyang (归元寺)

Guiyuan Temple in Hanyang, founded in 1658 during the Qing Kangxi reign, is the largest Buddhist temple in the Wuhan area, with a complex of more than 500 halls and about 30,000 square metres of buildings. Its most remarkable treasure is the 500 Arhats hall, where 500 life-size painted clay arhat statues, each individually modelled with distinct faces, line the walls of a cloister the length of the complex. The temple also holds a 2 tonne jade Buddha carved from a single piece of Burmese jade and a set of iron tripods cast during the Ming.

How the day runs: Arrive at 9 am and go straight to the Arhats hall, which is the reason to come and is best before the tour groups. The cloister is long and narrow with the figures on both sides, so walk it slowly and work along the wall in one direction. The jade Buddha is in the main hall and the iron tripods in the Thousand Buddha Pavilion at the back. Have lunch in the temple's vegetarian restaurant, which is a genuine institution in Wuhan. Then walk out to the restored Hanyang old streets around Qintai, and if the weather is good the riverside and the Yangtze Bridge can be seen from the western approach. Two to three hours is a good length.

15 km from Wuhan · about 35 min by car from Wuhan · half-day trip · best in October to April, with the courtyard and the vegetarian restaurant at their best in the cool months; the area is also very lively at the Lantern Festival in February.

  • The vegetarian restaurant is worth the detour on its own; the mushroom and bean curd dishes are the local benchmark.
  • The temple is a working monastery with resident monks, so dress respectfully and keep noise down in the halls.
  • Hanyang also has the Guiyuan Temple and Hanyang Riverbank heritage district, which combines well with the bridge at a separate time of day.

Crossing the rivers in Wuhan

Wuhan is defined by two rivers meeting, and the bridges are the only way to cross them quickly. The traffic between the three original towns — Wuchang on the south bank of the Yangtze, Hanyang on the north bank where the Han River joins, and Hankou on the north bank of the Han — is what makes the city sprawl the way it does. The Yangtze River Bridge is the historic crossing and the Wuhan Bridge downstream of it is the modern workhorse.

The expressways radiate from the city and the drive to Mulan Mountain takes around 110 minutes, which is the only trip in this group that genuinely needs a full day. Everything else is within 35 minutes of the centre.

One practical note for early mornings: the hot dry noodle and mianwo stalls are the point of Hubu Alley, and they are open from about 5:30 am, which means being on the road before 7 am if you want to eat at the counter with everyone else.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much of East Lake can I realistically see in half a day?

The lake is 33 square kilometres with 100 km of shoreline, so not all of it. The realistic plan is two sections: the Tingtao area in the north-west, which has the tree-lined causeway and the best water views in the morning, and the wooded Moshan hills on the western shore with the botanical garden and the sculpture collection. Cycling the perimeter road between them takes about 40 minutes and is the best use of the time.

Is the Wuhan Yangtze River Bridge open to pedestrians?

Yes, there is a pedestrian walkway on the deck with separate access at each end, and it is the only way to stand on the bridge itself with the Yangtze 40 m below. The structure spans 1.6 km from Turtle Hill in Hanyang to Snake Hill in Wuchang, and the best photographs come from the Hanyang monument terrace looking along the deck toward the Wuchang bank. Check access hours before going, because the walkway closes periodically for maintenance.

What should I eat at Hubu Alley and when should I go?

Go between 6:30 and 9 am, when the stalls are at full volume. The two signature items are Xie's mianwo, a fermented mung bean batter fried in a wok until it forms a bowl with a crisp lace edge, and the hot dry noodles, which are mixed with sesame paste, pickled long beans, preserved egg, pickled turnip and spring onion. For dinner, the same advice points the other way: Jiqing Street, a short walk away, is the evening food street.

Is the chime bell performance at the Hubei Provincial Museum worth rearranging my morning for?

Yes, if it is scheduled. The Zenghou Yi chime bells are a set of 65 bronze bells from 433 BC covering three and a half octaves, with a pitch notation system inscribed on them more than 2,000 years ago, and hearing them played on the originals is a genuinely rare experience. Tickets for the short performance are limited and distributed at a set time in the morning, so arrive early and ask. The museum is otherwise free but requires advance WeChat registration and closes on Mondays.

How far is Mulan Mountain and should I combine it with Mulan Lake?

About 70 to 75 km north of central Wuhan, roughly two hours by motorway, which makes it a full-day trip rather than an excursion. Mulan Mountain has the 600 m peak, the forest and the Tang-style village, while Mulan Lake is the 30 million cubic metre reservoir below it. They are separate ticketed areas a short distance apart and combining them in one day works well if you have the energy.

Which Wuhan day trip works best with children?

The Hubei Provincial Museum, which is entirely indoors and has the chime bells and the Sword of Goujian to look at, plus the bell concert if it is running. The second best is East Lake, where the causeway cycling and the boating on the lake are both good for younger children, and the weather does not matter as much as it would at an outdoor site.
