Overview: Day Trips from Nanjing

Nanjing's outskirts hold two of the most consequential sites in Chinese history. On Purple Mountain, the Ming Xiaoling mausoleum and the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum sit on the same axis of national memory, and the Linggu Temple complex next to them contains a brick vaulted hall with no beams at all. An hour east, Tangshan has hot springs at 50 to 60 degrees Celsius that were the most famous spa in the country before the war, and where the Nanjing Man fossils were excavated in the 1990s.

Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum (明孝陵)

The Ming Xiaoling is the tomb of the Yongle Emperor, Zhu Di, and it took 25 years to build from 1381, with 500,000 labourers reported on the project. The site was chosen for feng shui reasons, which is why the Spirit Way exists at all: it is 2.4 km long and lined with pairs of stone figures, horses, camels, elephants and mythical beasts, positioned to confuse any spirit that might trouble the emperor. The Hall of Soul Worship is one of the few surviving great Ming timber halls, and the complex became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003.

How the day runs: Enter through the Sifangcheng gate at 8 am and walk the 2.4 km Spirit Way, which is the best part and takes an easy hour with the stone animals on both sides. The Sifangcheng stele pavilion at the start carries the 'Sifangcheng' inscription carved by a prisoner during the Ming, which is a good opening frame. Continue to the Wengzhong path, the Sifangcheng itself, then to the Lingxing Gate and the 700-year-old ginkgo and cypress trees that the Wanli Emperor planted for his parents. Enter the Treasure Vault precinct through the Wenlingmen red gate and see the Treasure Vault itself, a 30 m high earthen cone with its revetted stone base still intact. Allow three hours including the walk back.

12 km from Nanjing · about 35 min by car from Nanjing · half-day trip · best in Late March to mid-April for the wisteria on the Spirit Way, and mid-November for the ginkgo and maple colour, which is also the busiest fortnight of the year.

  • There is a shuttle bus between the Sifangcheng gate and the Treasure Vault, which saves walking the Spirit Way twice.
  • The crimson leaves of the maples along the Spirit Way in late November are the reason for the November crowds.
  • The whole site is inside the Zhongshan Mountain scenic area, so it combines naturally with the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum on the same ridge.

Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum (中山陵)

The Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum was begun in 1926 on the southern slope of Purple Mountain, designed by the architect Yang Tingbao, and completed in 1929. The approach is deliberately theatrical, a 480 m ceremonial road with 392 stone steps in eight sections, framed at the top by a stone ceremonial arch and at the bottom by a semicircular stone ramp, which together form the shape of a lantern. The main hall is roofed in a blue-glazed tile chosen because blue was associated with the sky and the afterlife, and it is a faithful rendering of the formal hall of the Forbidden City.

How the day runs: Approach from the Bo'ai Arch at the bottom of the path, where the Chinese characters for 'fraternity, diligence, integrity, sacrifice' are carved in stone, and walk up the 392 steps at a steady pace. The 480 m climb takes about 15 minutes and the gradient is gentle enough for most visitors. At the top, the Hall of Soul Worship opens onto a terrace with a view over the whole city, the Yangtze and Xuanwu Lake, which is the best panorama in Nanjing. The building itself is best shot from the upper terrace looking back down the axis toward the arch, which is why the classic composition includes the whole 480 m of steps. Allow two hours, and add the adjacent Linggu Temple if you are there in the morning when the pine grove is empty.

12 km from Nanjing · about 35 min by car from Nanjing · half-day trip · best in March to April for the plum and the wisteria, and October to November for the maple on the approach, with clear conditions for the panorama from the top.

  • There are 392 stone steps and no escalator, so the mausoleum is not the easiest choice for anyone with limited mobility.
  • The stone ceremonial arch at the top of the path is the best single architectural frame in the complex.
  • The blue-glazed roof reads very differently against an overcast sky than under hard sun, so it is worth a return visit in different conditions.

Linggu Temple and the Purple Mountain Forest (灵谷寺)

Linggu Temple occupies the valley at the southeastern foot of Purple Mountain, and the reason to visit is a single extraordinary building. The Wuliang Hall, also called the Beamless Hall, was built in 1381 as the sarcophagus chamber for the Yongle Emperor, and it is constructed entirely of brick and stone in a corbelled vault with no timber beams at all, which is why it has stood for over 600 years. The original temple, the Kaishan Chan Monastery, dated to 514 AD. The Linggu Pagoda built beside it between 1931 and 1933 is 60.5 m tall.

How the day runs: Go in the morning, when the valley is quiet and the light reaches the floor of the pine grove. Start with the Wuliang Hall, which is a short walk from the car park and is the reason to come, and then walk up to the Linggu Pagoda, which is a 500-step climb through trees with occasional views out over the mountain. Continue to the National Revolutionary Army soldiers' cemetery and the Linggu Deep Pines stele, which stands on a bixi base with an inscription written by the park management after the original tombstone text was erased. Have lunch in the valley at the temple's vegetarian restaurant, which has been there since the Republican period. Two to three hours is comfortable.

13 km from Nanjing · about 40 min by car from Nanjing · half-day trip · best in October to November for the pine and maple colour in the valley, and any winter day for the bare-brick architecture of the Wuliang Hall.

  • The Wuliang Hall's brick vault means the interior stays cool and dim, so it is a good place to practise a slow exposure.
  • The valley floor is lined with mature pines, and the annual autumn colour here is the reason many Nanjing residents come.
  • The pagoda's upper storeys have views across to the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum on the same ridge.

Tangshan Hot Springs and the Nanjing Man Site (汤山温泉)

Tangshan, 29 km east of the city, was the most famous spa resort in the Jiangnan region before the 1937 war, with Chiang Kai-shek's private villa there and the Republic of China infantry school founded in 1932. The water keeps a constant 50 to 60 degrees Celsius all year, and the surrounding area is 29.74 square kilometres with attractions including the Longchang Temple, the Yangshan stele quarry and the Tangshan limestone caves. Workers digging in the Hulu Cave in the 1990s found the Nanjing Man hominin fossils, dated to around 300,000 years ago, making the area a UNESCO geopark site.

How the day runs: Drive out along the S122 for about 45 minutes and turn at Tangshan town, where the resort hotels and public baths are grouped. Go for a soak rather than a schedule, since the best facility to use is the Tangshan Hot Spring Public Bathhouse, which draws its water from the springs and is where local families go rather than the resort pools that use treated water. Try the outdoor pools in the Hulu Cave area, where the water emerges from the actual karst system. Have lunch in town. If you have the energy, add the Tangshan Museum of Homo Nanjingensis, which is small and displays the fossil material. Head back by mid-afternoon to avoid the Sunday evening rush on the return.

30 km from Nanjing · about 45 min by car from Nanjing · half-day trip · best in October to April, when the spring water at 50 to 60 degrees Celsius is a comfortable soak; summer afternoons are too hot for a long session in the water.

  • The difference matters: some resort hotels use heated and treated water, while the public bathhouses draw from the springs directly.
  • Bring your own towel, and be prepared for a 15-minute queue for the most popular public bathhouse on weekends.
  • The 2015 national tourism resort designation brought new investment and higher prices in the resort hotels than in the public facilities.

Qixia Temple and Qixia Mountain (栖霞寺)

Qixia Temple was founded in 489 AD as the private retreat of the recluse Ming Sengshao, and became the ancestral temple of the Sanlun school of Buddhism, one of the four great Chan monasteries of the Tang. The Sui dynasty relic pagoda, an eight-sided five-storey stone pagoda, was designated a national key cultural relic protection unit in 1988, and the Thousand Buddha Cliff nearby was protected in 2001. The grottoes contain 515 Buddha figures carved into the cliff face, and a study published in 2000 identified the Flying Apsaras murals as the only surviving complete murals inside the Thousand Buddha Cliff.

How the day runs: Arrive at 9 am to get ahead of the tour buses, which are the main problem here on weekends. Follow the path up past the Thousand Buddha Cliff, where the red maple grows directly out of the rock above the carved figures, and then down to the temple complex. Spend an hour on the Sui pagoda, which is the oldest structure and has an unusual octagonal stone form that photographs better from a low angle. The grottoes are best seen in raking light, which means either early morning or late afternoon; at midday the cliff face is flat and the carvings disappear. Eat at one of the vegetarian restaurants by the temple gate, then drive back via the G42. Three hours is enough.

22 km from Nanjing · about 40 min by car from Nanjing · half-day trip · best in Mid-November to early December for the red maple on the cliff above the Thousand Buddha Grottoes, and March for the plum blossom.

  • The maples were planted on the cliff above the grottoes specifically so that the leaves would frame the carvings.
  • The temple was destroyed by fire in the Taiping Rebellion in 1853 and again during the 1937 war, and the surviving structure is largely a 20th-century rebuild.
  • Qixia Mountain's red maple festival in November draws very large crowds, so a weekday is much better.

Niushoushan and the Buddha Relic Pagoda (牛首山)

Niushoushan is a low hill of 242.9 m in Jiangning District, named for the two peaks at its summit that look like a bull's horns, and it has been a Buddhist site since the Southern Dynasties, where the Chan master Fachang founded the Niutou school of Zen in 643. It also holds the Southern Tang dynasty tombs of Li Yu and Li Jing, and the Ming tomb of the admiral Zheng He. The reason the site is on every visitor's list now is what was unearthed in July 2008: a brick casket containing a Buddha skull bone relic found in the underground palace of the ruined Changgan Temple, now sheltered in the 89.3 m tall Foding Palace.

How the day runs: Allow a full day because the site is large and the entrance is on the far side of the hill from the car park. Start with the Foding Palace, which is built into a deep artificial pit so that you descend into it, and where the relic chamber is at the centre. Take the escalator route around the outside of the building for the long roof view, then walk to the Foding Pagoda and the twin towers, the Tang Pagoda and the Song Pagoda, on the summit ridge. The relic chamber is only open on designated days, so check before travelling. Have lunch in the park's vegetarian restaurant. Walk out through the scenic zone rather than taking the shuttle, so you pass the Zheng He tomb and the Yue Fei defensive earthworks on the southern flank, where a 200 m stretch of the 860-year-old red-brown stone rampart survives. Leave by 4 pm.

25 km from Nanjing · about 45 min by car from Nanjing · full-day trip · best in October to April; the valley has a museum atmosphere that works in any weather, and the exposed ridge paths are best outside summer.

  • The main hall is a deep-pit building, so you enter below ground level, which is disorienting and worth photographing.
  • The Niutou Zen connection makes this the ancestral site of one of the major schools of Chinese Buddhism.
  • Book the relic chamber in advance, since access is limited to designated days and by reservation.

Getting out to Purple Mountain and beyond

Most of Nanjing's best day trips are within 45 minutes of the centre, which makes the city unusually easy to day-trip from. The Purple Mountain sites sit 12 to 13 km out on the eastern side, and the expressway and surface road connections are good enough that a car beats the bus for anything involving several stops on one ridge.

The one trip worth planning properly is Tangshan. It is only 30 km, but the Sunday afternoon return on the S122 slows to a crawl, and the hot spring facilities fill up from around 3 pm, so an early start and an early departure is the whole trick.

Purple Mountain also has a scenic area shuttle bus system that covers the Ming Xiaoling, the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, Linggu Temple and the Purple Mountain Observatory. If you are visiting three or four of these, a car is still faster, but the shuttle is cheap and it removes the problem of parking at each site separately.

Planning a day trip from Nanjing

Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum, Tangshan Hot Springs and the Nanjing Man Site and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Nanjing day trip route — Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum sits closest at 12 km, roughly 35 minutes by car, while Tangshan Hot Springs and the Nanjing Man Site reaches furthest at 30 km, about 45 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum works best Late March to mid-April for the wisteria on the Spirit Way, and mid-November for the ginkgo and maple colour, which is also the busiest fortnight of the year., while Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum suits March to April for the plum and the wisteria, and October to November for the maple on the approach, with clear conditions for the panorama from the top. — in Nanjing that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

Linggu Temple and the Purple Mountain Forest, Tangshan Hot Springs and the Nanjing Man Site, Qixia Temple and Qixia Mountain each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Nanjing itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much time should I allow for the Ming Xiaoling and the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum together?

Half a day each is comfortable, or three hours total if you take the shuttle bus between them and keep moving. The Ming Xiaoling's Spirit Way alone is 2.4 km lined with stone animals, and the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum has 392 stone steps over 480 m. Both sit on the same ridge with the Linggu Temple a short walk away, so a single car covers all three easily.

What makes the Wuliang Hall at Linggu Temple worth the detour?

It has no beams at all. The hall, built in 1381 as the Yongle Emperor's sarcophagus chamber, is a corbelled brick and stone vault that has stood for over 600 years without structural timber, which is why it survived when almost everything around it was destroyed. It is inside the Linggu Temple valley on Purple Mountain, a short walk from the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, and the interior is dim, which makes it a good place to slow down.

Is the Qixia Temple maple really as good as people say?

The planting is deliberate: red maples were put on the cliff directly above the Thousand Buddha Cliff so their leaves would frame the carvings, and 515 Buddha figures sit in the rock below them. The best light on the combination is late afternoon or early morning, because at midday the cliff face is flat and the relief disappears. It is very crowded during the November maple festival, so a weekday is worth planning around.

How does the Tangshan hot spring compare to the resort hotels?

The public bathhouses draw water from the springs and keep it at 50 to 60 degrees Celsius year round, whereas many of the resort hotels use heated and treated water. Tangshan was the most famous spa in the Jiangnan region before the war, with Chiang Kai-shek's villa there, and it became a national tourism resort in 2015. Go for the public facilities rather than the resort pools if you want the genuine thing.

Can I see the Buddha skull relic at Niushoushan?

It depends on the day. The relic was found in July 2008 in a brick casket in the underground palace of the ruined Changgan Temple, and it is now enshrined in the Foding Palace, a deep-pit building 220 m long, 160 m wide and 89.3 m tall. Access to the relic chamber is limited to designated days and normally requires advance booking, so check the schedule before making the 25 km drive out to Jiangning.

Which Nanjing day trip is best for someone who does not want to walk much?

Tangshan. The hot spring soak requires almost no exertion, the resort has indoor pools, and the town itself has a museum on the Nanjing Man fossils. The Xuanwu Lake and the Presidential Palace are also short walks on flat ground, while the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum's 392 steps and the Ming Xiaoling's 2.4 km Spirit Way are the two to pick carefully.
