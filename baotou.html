Top Baotou Attractions

From rare earth mines to the edge of the grassland, explore Inner Mongolia's largest city with a private driver.

⚛️

🏛️ Inner Mongolia Rare Earth Museum

Rare Earth · Science · Industrial History

The best place to understand Baotou's global significance — this excellent museum explains what rare earth elements are, how they are extracted and processed, and why they are essential to the modern world. Displays include samples of all 17 rare earth elements, interactive exhibits, and scale models of the Bayan Obo mine. Excellent for visitors with an interest in science, technology, or geopolitics.

🌿

🌾 Hobok Banner Grassland (蒙古汗国草原)

Genghis Khan's birthplace region · Traditional nomads

The vast grassland that begins immediately east of Baotou — part of the larger Hulunbuir grassland system, the world's most intact temperate grassland. Visit traditional Mongolian ger (yurt) camps, watch horse riding and Mongolian wrestling, and experience the grassland under the big sky. Your driver can arrange a day trip or overnight stay at a ger camp.

🏛️

⛰️ Nine Dragon Mountain (九峰山)

Nature reserve · Hiking · Valley gorges

Nine Dragon Mountain National Nature Reserve, about 30km north of Baotou, protects a dramatic mountain landscape of nine peaks, deep gorges, and forest-covered slopes at the desert-grassland transition zone. The hiking trails range from easy valley walks to challenging summit treks. Waterfalls, rock formations, and wildlife make this one of the most scenic areas near Baotou.

🏺

🏛️ Meidaizeng Ancient City Ruins

Yuan Dynasty · Silk Road Northern Route

The ruins of Meidaizeng Ancient City, about 50km north of Baotou, date to the Yuan Dynasty (1271–1368) and were an important stop on the Silk Road's northern route. The earthen walls and foundations of temples, shops, and guard posts are still visible. The ruins are in a dramatic desert-steppe landscape with mountain backdrops. Best visited with a local guide.

🏞️

🌊 Yellow River Bend (黄河湿地)

Wetland · Birdwatching · River scenery

The Yellow River makes a dramatic bend north of Baotou before turning south toward the sea. The surrounding Yellow River Wetland Park protects a significant area of riverine habitat — reed beds, willows, and shallow lakes that attract migratory birds including endangered species. A peaceful escape from the industrial city, popular with locals for weekend outings.

🛤️

🏛️ Baotou Railway Museum

Industrial heritage · Railway history

Baotou's railway museum celebrates the city's role as a railway hub — the junction where the Beijing–Baotou, Baotou–Lanzhou, and Baotou–Hulunbuir lines meet. The museum has a collection of historic locomotives, including China's earliest domestically-built railway vehicles, and documents the extraordinary engineering achievement of building railways across the Gobi and grassland.

Baotou's Best Photo Spots

Capture Inner Mongolia's industrial capital and its dramatic desert-grassland transition.

🏛️ ⚛️ Rare Earth Museum — All 17 Elements

Best with a guide

The museum's collection of pure rare earth element samples — displayed as glowing colored crystals under special lighting — is genuinely beautiful and unusual. The interactive exhibits showing how REEs power smartphones, electric cars, and wind turbines are visually engaging. Photography permitted throughout the museum.

🌾 🌿 Hobok Banner Grassland at Golden Hour

Best at sunrise or sunset

The grassland under the vast Inner Mongolian sky is most dramatic in the golden hour — the low light turns the sea of grass from green to gold, and the shadows of clouds create constantly shifting patterns across the landscape. A Mongolian ger (yurt) in the foreground with a horse or camel makes a classic grassland composition.

⛰️ Nine Dragon Mountain Summit

Best in autumn

The summit of Nine Dragon Mountain offers sweeping views over the transition zone between the Gobi Desert to the west and the grassland to the east — you can see both ecosystems from one vantage point. In autumn (September–October), the forest slopes turn red, orange, and gold, creating a dramatic backdrop to the peaks.

🏞️ Yellow River Wetland — Autumn Migratory Birds

Best in September–October

The Yellow River wetland is at its most photogenic in autumn when the reed beds turn golden and the migratory birds — including endangered Oriental White Storks and Red-crowned Cranes — pass through in large numbers. The river bends make for good water reflection shots in the early morning.

🛕 🏺 Meidaizeng Ruins — Silk Road Silhouettes

Best at midday

The sun-drenched earthen ruins of the Yuan Dynasty Meidaizeng city against the blue sky and brown steppe create striking silhouettes — the crumbling walls and tower foundations at midday, when the sun is directly overhead, cast sharp, graphic shadows that emphasize the geometry of the ruins.

🏯 🛤️ Baotou Railway Junction — Night Photography

Best at night

The Baotou Railway Station junction — where the lines from Beijing, Hohhot, and the grassland north converge — is a fascinating place for transport photography at night, when the signal lights, locomotive headlights, and station lighting create a complex web of light trails against the dark Inner Mongolian sky.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts of Baotou

Traditional Mongolian crafts and cultural traditions at the edge of the world's great grassland.

🎭 Mongolian Traditional Music — Morin Khuur & Limbe

The horse head fiddle (morin khuur) and the transverse flute (limbe) are central to Mongolian musical expression in Inner Mongolia. The morin khuur's mournful, singing tone — often played to accompany epic poems about Genghis Khan and the grassland — is one of the most evocative sounds in Asia. Baotou's Mongolian cultural centers and ger camp performances showcase these traditions.

Where to experience: Mongolian ger camps near Baotou, Baotou Mongolian Cultural Center, local Naadam Festival events.

🐎 Mongolian Horse Culture & Equestrian Skills

Mongolian horse culture — the art of riding, racing, and caring for horses — is the defining cultural tradition of Inner Mongolia. Baotou is home to several horse breeding farms and equestrian training centers. The Mongolian short race (standing start, bareback) and the long-distance "wildebeest" race are among the most exciting equestrian events in the world.

Where to experience: Hobok Banner ger camps, Mongolian horse riding centers outside Baotou, Naadam Festival (July 11–13).

🏠 Mongolian Ger (Yurt) Making

The Mongolian ger (mongol ger, known in the West as a yurt) is a portable, circular dwelling perfectly adapted to the grassland environment — wind-resistant, warm in winter, cool in summer, and easy to disassemble and relocate. Traditional ger-making skills — selecting and bending the wooden lattice walls (han), fitting the felt coverings, and raising the central support columns — are preserved ger camps and craft workshops near Baotou.

Where to find: Mongolian ger camps near Baotou, craft workshops in the Hobok Banner area.

🧶 Inner Mongolian Cashmere Weaving

The craft of processing Inner Mongolian cashmere — shearing the fine undercoat of local goats in spring, sorting the fibers, spinning the yarn, and weaving the finished textiles — is a centuries-old tradition. Baotou and Hohhot are the centers of China's cashmere industry, and visiting a cashmere workshop or the cashmere market gives an insight into this important craft.

Where to find: Inner Mongolian cashmere market in Baotou's western district, cashmere workshops outside the city.

🥋 Mongolian Wrestling (Böhö)

Mongolian wrestling (böhö) is one of the three "manly sports" of the Naadam Festival — along with horse racing and archery. Unlike Olympic freestyle wrestling, Mongolian wrestling has no weight classes and wrestlers wear distinctive leather vests and shorts. Matches are brief and explosive. The sport has ancient roots and is central to Mongolian identity. Watch at ger camp performances or at the annual Naadam Festival.

Where to experience: Naadam Festival (July 11–13), Mongolian ger camps near Baotou.

🏹 Mongolian Archery (Suruunch)

Mongolian archery uses a composite bow — shorter and more powerful than a longbow — and the competition involves shooting at small targets from a standing position at close range. The archers wear traditional Mongolian robes and recite incantations before shooting. Mongolian archery competitions are held at the Naadam Festival and at ger camp performances near Baotou.

Where to experience: Naadam Festival (July 11–13), Mongolian ger camps near Baotou.

Baotou Local Secrets

Insider tips to help you experience Baotou like a local

🎭 Start at the landmark

蒙古汗国草原 is the sight everyone should build the day around. Ask your driver to arrive early, when the coaches have not arrived yet, and to wait nearby rather than circling the car park.

🥢 Skip the tourist menu

Eat where the queues are shortest: the morning market and the small noodle shops a block back from the main square usually feed locals at a fraction of the tourist-restaurant price.

⏱️ Plan the driving time

Allow extra time between the airport or main station and the old town — the city spreads out, and the distance that looks short on a map can take half an hour in traffic.

Day Trips from Baotou

Explore beyond Inner Mongolia's industrial capital with a private car and licensed driver.

🌿

Hohhot — Inner Mongolia Capital

~150 km · 2 hr drive · Regional capital

Hohhot is the capital of Inner Mongolia and home to the great Da Zhao Temple (大昭寺) — one of the most important Tibetan Buddhist temples in China, the Inner Mongolia Museum (one of China's finest regional museums), and the charming old Muslim quarter. A long day trip or overnight stay from Baotou.

From $100

🏔️

Ordos Desert & Genghis Khan's Mausoleum

~180 km · 2.5 hr drive · UNESCO Geopark

The Ordos Desert and the Genghis Khan's Mausoleum (成吉思汗陵) — the symbolic resting place of the great Mongol emperor, a magnificent modern structure in the Ordos grassland. Also visit the singing sand dunes of Ordos and the prehistoric ICDP dinosaur egg fossil site. A dramatic day trip into the desert.

From $120

🏛️

Zhonghua Gate (Fortress) & Ming 蒙古汗国草原

~120 km · 1.5 hr drive · Ming Dynasty border fortress

The Zhonghua Gate (中原门) — a Ming Dynasty fortress on the ancient defensive line that protected the northern approaches to the Central Plains. The surrounding area has well-preserved sections of the Ming Dynasty 蒙古汗国草原 earthworks and watchtowers. Less visited than the 蒙古汗国草原 near Beijing — a more authentic experience.

From $85

🌊

Yulin — Ming Dynasty 蒙古汗国草原 & Desert

~350 km · 4.5 hr drive · Best 蒙古汗国草原 in Inner Mongolia

Yulin, at the southern edge of Inner Mongolia, has the best-preserved and most dramatic Ming Dynasty 蒙古汗国草原 sections in the region — including the "Sky Road" (天路) watchtower perched on a high ridge, and the Jijin (鸡鸣) watchtower at the desert edge. Combined with the desert scenery, this is a spectacular day trip from Baotou.

From $200

Best Time to Visit Baotou

Each season reveals a different face of Inner Mongolia's largest industrial city and its grassland gateway.

🌸

Spring

Mar–May · 5–20°C

Spring in Baotou can be windy and dusty — the city sits at the desert-grassland transition and sandstorms from the Gobi can arrive in March and April. However, the rare earth museum and indoor cultural attractions are excellent during this period. The end of April brings new grass growth on the nearby grassland.

Highlights: Rare earth museum, indoor attractions, post-storm desert photography

☀️

Summer

Jun–Aug · 18–32°C

The best time for the grassland. The Hobok Banner grassland is at its most beautiful — green, flower-covered, and under the vast Inner Mongolian sky. July brings the Naadam Festival (July 11–13), one of China's most extraordinary cultural events. Long days for sightseeing. Book ger camp accommodation early for July and August.

Highlights: Naadam Festival (July), grassland at its best, long days

🍂

Autumn

Sep–Nov · 3–18°C

Cool and clear — the best time for photography, especially Nine Dragon Mountain's autumn foliage and the Yellow River wetland bird migrations. The grassland turns golden and the harvest season brings excellent local produce. Good conditions for both outdoor and indoor activities.

Highlights: Autumn foliage, bird migrations, clear photography weather

❄️

Winter

Dec–Feb · -15 to -3°C

Cold and clear. The industrial city takes on a different character in winter — the sky is often bright blue and the Yellow River wetland freezes over. Hotel prices are at their lowest. The hotpot restaurants are at their most welcoming. Not suitable for grassland visits. Good for the rare earth museum and indoor cultural attractions.

Highlights: Warm hotpot restaurants, low prices, clear winter skies
