Overview: Photography in Jinan

Jinan is a city of water in a plain. More than 70 named artesian springs push up through the karst aquifer beneath the streets, feeding a moat, a lake and a chain of spring pools, each with its own pavilion, stone basin and local legend. That water gives the photography a subject entirely different from the usual Chinese city itinerary: reflections, ripples, spray and vapour. Add the carved slopes of Qianfo Mountain, a 611 AD stone pagoda and a 197-metre solitary hill, and the day has range.

Baotu Spring Pool and Guanlan Pavilion (趵突泉)

Baotu Spring is three jets that rise in a tight line and surge together, which is why the Qianlong Emperor named it the Number One Spring under Heaven. The pool is the subject of a long photographic argument, and the local guide is specific: the Guanlan Pavilion sits on the west side, but the best viewing position is on the east and north sides, where the north edge is closest to the spring eyes. That tells you the photograph should be shot looking slightly down and across the water at the jets rather than from the pavilion side, so the columns of water are separated against the dark basin.

best light: Early morning in late spring, when the three jets run hard and the mist catches the first light off the water. · spring / pavilion

Getting there: Baotu Spring South Road 1, in the Lixia District of the old city, about 2 km from the centre. The park is a short walk from the old commercial streets, and metro Line 3 to Wuyingshan stops nearby. Entry is ticketed through the south gate and the main pool is a few minutes inside.

  • Shoot from the north side of the pool, where the three jets line up cleanly and the dark basin gives contrast.
  • A long exposure of 1/4 to 1/2 second turns the surge into soft vertical streaks while the pavilion stays sharp.
  • The spring is monitored, with yellow, orange and red warnings issued as the water level falls below 28.15, 28 and 27.60 metres, so low water means weak jets and fewer people.

Black Tiger Spring and the South Moat (黑虎泉)

Black Tiger Spring is the main spring of the second largest of Jinan's spring groups, which runs westward along both banks of the south moat for about 700 metres from the Jiefang Pavilion and contains 14 separate spring pools. The name comes from the sound of the water, recorded in the Jin dynasty Spring Record as gushing audibly into the city moat. Photographically the group beats a single spring because the pools vary in size and shape, from wide stone basins to narrow slots under rock, and the moat water links them into a single frame.

best light: Late afternoon, when low sun comes through the plane trees along the moat and lights the spray above the pools. · spring / moat street

Getting there: On the southeast corner of the old city moat, in the Lixia District, directly opposite the Jiefang Pavilion and one street away from Baotu Spring. Walking distance from the Baotu Spring park, about 10 minutes. Street parking is restricted in the old city, so arrive on foot or by taxi.

  • Work the smaller northern pools rather than the main one, where the crowd concentrates.
  • The rock edges and long exposure water make the moat the best place in Jinan to practise a graduated filter.
  • Shoot along the moat line with a long lens so the 700-metre run of pools compresses into a single repeating pattern.

Daming Lake, Lixia Pavilion and the Iron Duke Shrine (大明湖)

Daming Lake covers 0.58 square kilometres after the 2010 dredging, with an average depth of 2 metres and total storage of about 830,000 cubic metres, and it holds nine small islands. That water comes from the spring groups of the city, which is why the level stays fairly constant all year. The photographic anchor is the Lixia Pavilion, whose east-facing corridor carries a couplet taken from a Du Fu poem and written by the calligrapher He Shaoji: the pavilion has stood here since ancient times, and Jinan has always been home to famous scholars.

best light: Sunset in July and August, when the lotus is open and the low sun rakes across the water behind the pavilions. · lake / classical pavilion

Getting there: In the Lixia District, north of the historic city centre, about 3 km from the main hotels. The park gates, including the south gate with the Guo Moruo calligraphy plaque, are all on city streets and easy to reach by taxi or metro Line 3 to Daming Lake. The lake circuit is around 6 km on foot.

  • The lakeside willow and lotus rows are the subject; leave the pavilions for the reflections rather than the architecture.
  • Golden hour from the west shore looks back across the water to the pavilion islands.
  • A polariser controls the sheen on the water in bright sun and stops the sky from washing out the reflections.

Wulongtan and the Crescent Spring (五龙潭)

Wulongtan, also called Longju Spring or Huiwan Spring, measures about 70 metres long, 35 metres wide and 6 metres deep, making it the deepest spring in Jinan, and it sits with the old Warm Spring, Guanjia Pool, Huima Spring, Crescent Spring, Lian Spring and Yu Spring as the Wulongtan group. It is also the only one of the four main spring groups not in the Lixia District. Its distinctive detail is the Crescent Spring inside the basin, the highest water level of the Seventy-Two Famous Springs, which only begins to spout when the level reaches 29 metres.

best light: Midday is acceptable here because the pool is deep and shaded; better in the afternoon when the water clarifies after the crowds thin. · deep spring / garden

Getting there: Kuangshi Street 18, Tianqiao District, one street away from the Baotu Spring park and about 2 km from the centre. Entry is ticketed, with the Mingshi Pavilion inside the grounds. Metro Line 1 to Gongyanyuan or Line 3 both leave a short walk away.

  • Climb the Mingshi Pavilion for the overhead frame of the long rectangular pool.
  • The depth of the basin makes it a good place for a long exposure that flattens the ripples into a smooth dark plane.
  • The surrounding pavilions and covered corridors are half-hidden in trees, so watch for gaps in the foliage.

Qianfo Mountain Grottoes (千佛山)

Qianfo Mountain rises to 285 metres on the southern edge of Jinan and covers 1.518 square kilometres, with the scenic area covering 164.88 hectares. It takes its name from the thousands of Buddha images carved into the rock during the Sui dynasty, when Buddhism was flourishing between 581 and 600 CE and the Qianfo Temple was built, later rebuilt and renamed Xingguochan Temple in the Tang period. The carvings are the subject, not the mountain: they run in niches along the cliff faces at several levels, weathered to a soft grey, and the value of the shot is the way the figures emerge from shadow.

best light: Early morning light from the east on the upper rock faces, before the slope fills with visitors. · rock-cut sculpture / hillside

Getting there: About 2.5 km southeast of the city centre, a 15-minute drive, in the Lixia District near Jingyi Road. The main gate and the mountain path up to Xingguochan Temple are all walkable, and metro Line 3 to Wuyingshan is a short taxi ride away. The scenic area is a national 4A attraction.

  • Raking side light in the early morning picks out the relief depth; flat midday sun makes the niches read as flat panels.
  • Work the upper cliff paths above the main terrace, where the carving groups are larger and the crowds thinner.
  • The view north to the Yellow River from the midway gate is a separate landscape frame worth making.

Simen Pagoda (四门塔)

The Simen Pagoda is a single-storey pavilion-style stone tower with a square plan, 15.04 metres tall, built in the seventh year of the Daye era of the Sui dynasty, 611 CE, and it is regarded as the oldest surviving stone pagoda in China. Its central chamber is lit by a stone pillar at the core, with a seated Buddha on each of the four exterior faces, and the Sui dating was only established after an inscription was found, since it had been attributed to the Eastern Wei period for centuries.

best light: Low winter or early spring sun, when the relief panels on all four faces catch the light at an angle. · stone pagoda / Sui dynasty

Getting there: In Liubu, Licheng District, about 30 km south-east of central Jinan and roughly 45 minutes by car. The site is small and rural, with parking close to the tower and the Pumi Temple grottoes a short drive further along the same road.

  • A 50mm lens frames the whole square tower cleanly; a wide angle exaggerates its height beyond what it is.
  • Shoot all four faces, because the Buddha images differ and the light will favour a different one each hour.
  • The pale limestone blows out in strong sun, so pull exposure down half a stop and check the highlights.

Huabuzhu Mountain Summit (华不注山)

Huabuzhu Mountain is a 197-metre cone of blue-grey rock standing isolated on the plain about 7 kilometres from the centre of Jinan, on the south bank of the Yellow River. Its name is a pun on the words for an unopened flower bud, which is what the shape resembles. Li Daoyuan described it in the Water Classic as a single peak standing apart from the hills, tooth-like against the sky, and the Yuan painter Zhao Mengfu made it the subject of his Autumn Colours on the Que and Hua Mountains. The climb is short and steep, roughly 30 minutes for 197 metres.

best light: Late afternoon, when the sun drops behind the peak and the plain below fills with the long shadows of autumn. · hilltop / panorama

Getting there: In the Licheng District, about 9 km from central Jinan and 25 minutes by car, with a road to the base and a stepped path to the summit. The mountain sits close to Huquan Spring at its foot, so the two work as a single outing. Parking is informal on the approach lanes.

  • The classic frame is the view west and north across the plain toward the Yellow River, which is the composition Zhao Mengfu used.
  • Climb in the last two hours of the afternoon for the strongest side light on the rock face.
  • A wide lens from the summit includes the low city in the foreground, which gives the scale of the plain.

Camera gear for Jinan

Water is the defining element of the Jinan subject, so a polarising filter is the single most useful accessory. The spring pools, the moat and Daming Lake all produce surface sheen that eats contrast, and a polariser will separate the reflections from the water without the flatness of a strong graduated filter.

On exposure, springs are bright and reflective at once, so the readings are awkward. Metering for the highlights and pulling down one or two stops keeps the white spray from clipping and turns the basin dark, which is exactly the contrast the images need.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best spring to photograph in Jinan?

Baotu Spring is the famous one and gives the strongest single subject, with three jets rising in a line. But the local guidance is that the best position is on the east and north sides of the pool rather than from the Guanlan Pavilion on the west, because you get the jets separated against the dark basin.

Is Daming Lake worth photographing, or is it just a park?

It is worth it, but the subject is the water and the trees rather than the architecture. The lake covers 0.58 square kilometres with an average depth of 2 metres and holds about 830,000 cubic metres, fed by the city's spring groups, which keeps the level stable year round. Nine small islands hold pavilions that reflect well at sunset, and the willow and lotus rows along the shore give the frame its texture.

How much time do I need for Qianfo Mountain?

Two hours is comfortable. The mountain rises to 285 metres and covers 1.518 square kilometres, with the scenic area at 164.88 hectares, and the carved niches run along several levels of cliff face, so the walking is spread out rather than steep. The morning is better, because the eastern light falls across the carvings before the slope fills with visitors, and the view north to the Yellow River from the midway gate is worth making on the way down.

Do the Jinan springs photograph reliably, or do they sometimes dry up?

They are monitored and not always at full flow. Baotu Spring stopped flowing entirely on 30 March 1976, and again for 926 days between 14 March 1999 and 17 September 2001, before resuming on 6 September 2003. Water level warnings are issued at 28.15 metres, 28 metres and 27.60 metres for yellow, orange and red. Spring and early summer give the strongest flow, and a check of the current level before travelling avoids a wasted trip.

Is Simen Pagoda a good photo subject for a small ancient tower?

It is, and it is rare. It is regarded as the oldest surviving stone pagoda in China, built in 611 CE, 15.04 metres tall, with a square plan and a Buddha on each of the four faces. The compact geometry suits a study shot rather than a wide landscape, so a moderate lens at a low viewpoint works better than a wide angle.

Can I get the classic view from Huabuzhu Mountain in one morning?

Yes, and it is short. The climb from the base takes about 30 minutes for 197 metres, and the whole mountain is 9 km from central Jinan, about 25 minutes by car. Go late in the afternoon for the side light on the rock and the long shadows across the plain, then combine it with Huquan Spring at the foot of the hill.
