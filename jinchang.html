Jinchang (Chinese: 金昌; pinyin: Jīnchāng) is a prefecture-level city in the centre of Gansu province, People's Republic of China, bordering Inner Mongolia to the north.

Education & Universities

Jinchang is served by local colleges, vocational schools and teacher-training institutions rather than by large research universities.

Getting Around

Getting around Jinchang relies on road transport, with buses and taxis covering the urban area and long-distance coaches serving the surrounding counties.

Natural Resources & Environment

Jinchang sits in its provincial hinterland, where the surrounding hills, rivers and farmland shape the local landscape and cuisine.

Intangible Cultural Heritage & Living Crafts of Jinchang

Traditional crafts and cultural traditions at the Gobi Desert edge of the Silk Road.

🐪 Hexi Corridor Camel Wool Crafts

Camel wool (驼绒) weaving is a traditional craft along the Hexi Corridor, using the thick winter coats of Bactrian camels that roam the Tengger Desert edge. The wool is spun into yarn and woven into blankets, scarves, and coats of exceptional warmth. Jinchang's camel wool products are considered among the finest in Gansu — available at local markets and the night market.

Where to find: Jinchang Night Market, local specialty stores.

🎭 Silk Road Folk Music & Dance

The Hexi Corridor has a rich tradition of Silk Road folk music — featuring the pipa (lute), suona (double-reed horn), and Mongolian throat singing that reflects the cultural crossroads nature of the corridor. Jinchang's local cultural center regularly performs traditional Hexi folk music, including pieces that blend Han Chinese, Mongolian, and Tibetan musical traditions.

Where to experience: Jinchang Cultural Center (金昌市文化馆), local festivals.

🏺 Gansu Pottery & Earthenware

The Gansu region has a long tradition of earthenware pottery — unglazed terracotta vessels for water storage, cooking, and burial. The distinctive ochre and terracotta tones of Gansu pottery reflect the desert landscape. Local workshops in Jinchang produce both traditional functional earthenware and contemporary sculptural ceramics.

Where to find: Jinchang markets, local craft workshops.

✂️ Gansu Paper-Cutting (剪纸)

Paper-cutting is a beloved folk art throughout Gansu Province, with designs that reflect the Silk Road heritage — camels, horses, flying dragons, and geometric patterns borrowed from Central Asian textile designs. Jinchang's paper-cutting tradition features bold, graphic designs in red paper — particularly popular during Chinese New Year and weddings.

Where to find: Folk craft markets, Jinchang Cultural Center.

🐎 Mongolian Horse Head Fiddle (马头琴)

The Mongolian communities near Jinchang keep the tradition of the morin khuur (horse head fiddle) alive. This two-stringed instrument with a carved horse head is central to Mongolian musical expression — its mournful, singing tone perfectly captures the atmosphere of the Gobi Desert steppe. Performances are occasionally held at the Jinchang Cultural Center.

Where to experience: Jinchang Cultural Center, local Naadam festival events (July–August).

🍜 Gansu Noodle-Making Tradition

The art of hand-pulled noodles (拉面) is deeply embedded in Gansu's food culture. In Jinchang, the local variant — slightly thicker and with a different gluten texture than Lanzhou noodles — is taught in local noodle restaurants. Some restaurants offer hands-on noodle-making experiences where visitors can learn to pull their own noodles.

Where to experience: Local noodle restaurants near Jinchang Railway Station area.
