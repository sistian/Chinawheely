Overview: Day Trips from Chengdu

Chengdu stands on the Chengdu Plain, a basin of alluvial soil between the Longmen range to the north and the Qionglai Mountains to the west, and every day trip below follows one of those two lines. North-west runs the Min River corridor, where the Dujiangyan scheme at 60 kilometres still irrigates the plain and Mount Qingcheng rises 68 kilometres away with its 1,600-metre summit. West and south-west lead to the museum culture of Guanghan and to the Anning tributaries. South, past the river towns, the mountains rise to the Leshan Buddha at 130 kilometres and to the 5,364-metre head of Xiling. The distances here are short enough to fill a single day comfortably and long enough that the return drive is a real part of the plan.

The Dujiangyan Irrigation System (都江堰)

The Dujiangyan scheme stands on the Min River 60 kilometres upstream of Chengdu and is the oldest waterworks in China still in daily operation. The Qin governor Li Bing and his son directed its construction between about 256 and 251 BC. The site was listed as a national key cultural relic in 1982 and was inscribed together with Mount Qingcheng as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000. The headworks divide the river into three parts: the Fish Mouth, a semicircular divider 80 metres long, 39.1 metres at its widest and 6.6 metres high; the Flying Sand Weir, which spills floodwater and vents sediment; and the Bottle-Neck Channel cut into the cliff, 14.3 metres wide at the base and 18.8 metres high, which admits the inner river to the plain.

How the day runs: Leave the city at 07:00 and be at the Yulei Temple bridge area in about 75 minutes. Walk out to the Fish Mouth and follow the Bottle-Neck Channel upstream to the Fulong Temple on the right bank, then continue along the inner river to the Flying Sand Weir, about 2 kilometres of walking in total. Cross at Anlan Bridge for the return leg, allowing an hour for the museum beside the weir, which documents the 1974 changeover from temporary bamboo cribs to permanent gates. End at the old town of Guanxian, where the West Street shops and the Qingcheng stone tablets line the former river course. A departure at 16:30 puts you back in central Chengdu around 18:00.

60 km from Chengdu · about 75 min by car from Chengdu · full-day trip · best in March to May and September to November for clear low-water views of the headworks; the summer monsoon raises the river and the clear-weather sightlines of the weir disappear.

  • The Bottle-Neck Channel is carved into rock and its upper rim is narrow; the marked walkway gives a better view of the water gauge than the lower riverside path.
  • Under the Anlan Bridge the river bed is open stonework, and the pale colouring of the silt there is the clearest evidence of how much sediment the weir actually removes.
  • Guides at the headworks often begin at different points along the site, so confirm the meeting place in the old town rather than assuming it is at the ticket gate.

Mount Qingcheng and its Daoist Temples (青城山)

Mount Qingcheng stands 68 kilometres from central Chengdu, 10 kilometres south-west of Dujiangyan, and its summit, the Laoxiaoding, reaches 1,600 metres. It is one of the birthplaces of Daoism in China and the mountain carries the epithet of being the most secluded of the country's sacred peaks. The front mountain, the main scenic area, covers about 15 square kilometres, and the massif takes its name from 36 peaks arranged in a ring. The principal buildings are Jianfu Palace, Laojun Pavilion, Yuanming Palace, Shangqing Palace and Tianshi Cave, with the Shangqing Palace standing at the summit. The ancient building group was added to the seventh batch of national key cultural relic sites in 2013. Qingcheng and Dujiangyan were inscribed as a single World Heritage Site in 2000.

How the day runs: Depart at 07:30 and allow 90 minutes to the front mountain gate. Take the shuttle or the cable car to the mid-level temples rather than walking the whole approach, and see Tianshi Cave and the Shangqing Palace on the outward leg, where the number of stairs is lower. Continue to Laojun Pavilion and the summit ridge for about two hours, then descend through the tree-lined paths on the far side. Total walking on the standard loop is around 6 kilometres with 800 metres of ascent. Reserve the final hour for the Jianfu Palace precinct at the entrance, where the mountain gate and the old cypress stand, and leave by 15:30 for the return run into Chengdu.

68 km from Chengdu · about 90 min by car from Chengdu · full-day trip · best in April to June and September to October; the front mountain paths are shaded and busy in summer and the stair sections can be slick after rain.

  • The 36 peaks are arranged in a ring, but only a handful are marked on the main route; the named viewpoints above the tree line are the ones worth the extra climb.
  • The rear mountain is a separate and much less visited area reached by a different road, with the Wulonggou valley running about 8 kilometres into the hills.
  • Cable car hours are shorter than the walking hours, so a late-morning cable car can end your ascent well before the afternoon descent you have planned.

Sanxingdui Museum and the Guanghan Site (三星堆)

The Sanxingdui site lies on the west side of Guanghan, 40 kilometres from Chengdu, on the banks of the Yazi River, and takes its name from three connected loess mounds. The occupation runs from about 2,800 BC to 1,100 BC in four phases: Baodun culture, two phases of the Sanxingdui culture, and the Twelve Bridges culture, whose main remains are at the Jinsha site. It was listed as a provincial protected unit in 1987 and as a third-batch national key cultural relic site in January 1988. Systematic excavation began in 1986, when two sacrificial pits yielded more than 1,700 bronze, jade, lacquer and ceramic objects, 80 tusks and over 4,600 cowrie and bronze-shell pieces. Six further pits were announced in March 2021.

How the day runs: Leave at 09:00 and reach the new museum hall in Guanghan in about an hour, which allows two full hours for the excavation pits and the bronze gallery before the midday crowds. The reconstruction of a standing bronze figure and the tree-and-bird altarpiece anchor the main hall. Continue to the older site mound in the afternoon for the outdoor trenches, which take about 45 minutes and show how the sacrificial pits sat in relation to the original river terrace. Finish with the bronze tree gallery or the cultural relics hall. Total time on site is around four hours, which leaves the return drive for late afternoon and puts you back in Chengdu before the evening traffic builds.

40 km from Chengdu · about 60 min by car from Chengdu · half-day trip · best in All year for the museum halls; the outdoor excavation area and the mound are best in the cooler months from October to March.

  • The old pit hall and the new hall are separate buildings with a shuttle between them, so allow transfer time rather than assuming they are the same visit.
  • The 2,800 to 1,100 BC chronology is divided into four named phases, and the panels in each gallery are arranged by phase rather than by object type.
  • Ground-level photography is allowed in the pit halls but the reconstructed figure and the largest bronzes are behind glass in the new building.

The Leshan Giant Buddha (乐山大佛)

The Leshan Giant Buddha, the Great Maitreya Statue of Lingyun Temple, is carved into Qiluan Peak of Lingyun Mountain at the point where the Min, Qingyi and Dadu rivers meet. It is the tallest stone Buddha statue in the world. Work began in 713, the first year of the Kaiyuan era, was interrupted twice, and finished in 803, giving a total construction period of about 90 years under three successive overseers. Wei Gao, then military governor of Xichuan, contributed 500,000 cash and wrote the inscription carved on the cliff beside the niche. The statue was listed as a national key cultural relic in 1982, and in December 1996 the Mount Emei and Leshan Buddha area was inscribed as a mixed World Heritage Site.

How the day runs: Start from Chengdu at 06:30 for the 130-kilometre run, which reaches the Lingyun Mountain parking area at around 09:00. Take the stair path beside the cliff, allowing 90 minutes for the climb and the descent through the nine-storey pavilion structure built into the rock. Cross by the boat to the confluence viewpoint, where the three rivers and the local ferry traffic are visible from the opposite bank, and eat there rather than at the base. The adjacent Lingyun Temple and the Wuyou Temple stele are on the same site. Depart by 15:30, which returns you to Chengdu at about 18:00 after the expressway section north of Leshan.

130 km from Chengdu · about 150 min by car from Chengdu · full-day trip · best in October to March for the riverside climb; summer brings both heat and monsoon rain to the confluence.

  • The statue was originally painted and partly gilded, and traces of pigment survive in sheltered areas; the modern surface colour is mostly from early twentieth-century restoration.
  • The head of the statue was rebuilt after damage in 1925 and again in the 1930s, which is why the hair arrangement differs from the rest of the figure.
  • A spiral staircase within the rock leads to the former wooden pavilion levels, and it is narrow and one-way during busy periods.

Huanglongxi Ancient Town and the Jin River (黄龙溪)

Huanglongxi stands 40 kilometres south-west of Chengdu where the Luxi River meets the Jin River, on the boundary of Shuangliu, Pengshan and Renshou counties. The town has a documented history of more than 2,100 years and has been a transshipment point on the Jin River waterway since the Han dynasty. The old town preserves its original layout of seven streets and nine lanes, with 76 surviving houses from the Ming and Qing dynasties, and the fire dragon lantern dance and the Fuheng boatmen's songs are both on the national intangible heritage list. Three temples stand on the main street, forming what is locally called a single street with three temples. Six banyan trees in the town are more than a thousand years old.

How the day runs: Leave at 09:00 and arrive at Huanglongxi in about an hour, which leaves the late morning for the main street, the Zhenjiang Temple at its southern end and the Gulong Temple at its northern end. The covered market and the old river quay are the busier parts in the middle of the day. Take the covered lane network, where the fish and eel alley and the herb merchants' lanes preserve the older plot divisions. The banyan trees stand at the river end and at the town centre, and the Jin River ferry crossing gives a short ride across the water. Be back in Chengdu by 14:30 if you want the afternoon free.

40 km from Chengdu · about 60 min by car from Chengdu · half-day trip · best in All year; the dragon boat festival in early summer and the lantern season in winter both fill the main street with crowds.

  • The town is entered through the ancient gate at the southern end of the main street, and vehicles are kept outside, so the approach takes longer than the map distance suggests.
  • The dragon boat festival and the lantern displays both draw large crowds, and access to the main street is then controlled in one direction only.
  • The Jin River flood plain is soft underfoot in summer and the riverside paths flood after heavy rain in the upper catchment.

Xiling Snow Mountain and the Daxuetang Snowfield (西岭雪山)

Xiling Snow Mountain lies in Dayi County within the Qionglai range, 110 kilometres west of Chengdu, and its principal peak, Daxuetang, reaches 5,364 metres, the highest point in the city administrative area. The mountain is the origin of the Jian River, and the name appears in the Tang poet Du Fu's line about a thousand years of snow framed by a window and boats moored to the east. The scenic area and the Huashuiwan hot spring resort form the modern visitor complex, with a ski area among them. The massif is known for the transition from the low fir forest of the foothills to the alpine zone above the tree line, a climb of more than 3,000 metres.

How the day runs: Depart at 07:00 and allow two hours to the Huashuiwan base in Dayi County, where the coach transfer to the upper station runs. Take the cable car to around 3,000 metres and walk the ridge path to the Daxuetang viewing platform, about 40 minutes in each direction, with the snowfield and the eastern horizon on a clear morning. Descend by early afternoon to the tree line and take the lower valley path past the fir forest before returning to the base. Keep the total walking to under 10 kilometres, since altitude and the return drive are the two constraints that matter. Leave the area by 15:30 for the drive back to Chengdu.

110 km from Chengdu · about 120 min by car from Chengdu · full-day trip · best in January to March for the ski area; the upper trail to the snowfield is only reliably clear of snow from April to early June.

  • The upper section carries snow into early June and the lower valley path is often wet and muddy, so bring waterproof footwear rather than assuming a summer walk.
  • The cable car runs to a fixed upper station, and the final approach to the snowfield is on foot whatever the conditions above.
  • Weather on the ridge can turn within an hour, and the views from the viewing platform depend on cloud clearing rather than on the time of day.

Planning day trips from Chengdu

The plain makes these trips easy in one respect and deceptive in another. Because Chengdu sits at only about 500 metres and the routes radiate outward through a flat basin, the drive times look generous until you meet the traffic on the Chengdu–Dujiangyan and Chengdu–Leshan corridors at the weekend peak. The Sanxingdui and Huanglongxi runs are short enough to leave late and still have an afternoon; Qingcheng, Leshan and Xiling are not, and each of the three rewards an early departure rather than a long lunch before leaving the city.

Two practical points cover most of the ground. Sichuan rain runs from May to September, and in that window the paths above Dujiangyan and on the Qingcheng ridges are slick in a way the dry-season maps do not show, so a waterproof layer matters more than extra water. And the high routes sit several hundred metres above the city, so the temperature on the Daxuetang ridge in the middle of the day can be several degrees below what the valley reports. Local buses cover the shorter destinations well, including the runs to Huanglongxi and to Sanxingdui, and using one removes the parking search that adds thirty minutes at the end of the day.

Sanxingdui Museum and the Guanghan Site, The Leshan Giant Buddha and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Chengdu day trip route — Sanxingdui Museum and the Guanghan Site sits closest at 40 km, roughly 60 minutes by car, while The Leshan Giant Buddha reaches furthest at 130 km, about 150 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Sanxingdui Museum and the Guanghan Site, The Leshan Giant Buddha, Huanglongxi Ancient Town and the Jin River each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Chengdu itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Chengdu day trip deserves the longest drive?

The Leshan Giant Buddha, at 130 kilometres and about two and a half hours each way, is the furthest of the six in any practical sense. The statue itself is 71 metres high, stands at the confluence of three rivers, and takes roughly 90 minutes to climb down the cliff stair and back up through the rock pavilion, so the return leg is what justifies the early start. Xiling Snow Mountain is comparable in distance but ends on a ridge at 3,000 metres, which leaves less room for a slow day.

Can Dujiangyan and Mount Qingcheng be done in one day?

Yes, and it is a common pairing because the two are 10 kilometres apart and share the World Heritage inscription of 2000. Do Dujiangyan first, walking the headworks in the cool of the morning, then drive ten minutes to Qingcheng and take the cable car up to the temples and the summit ridge. The two together need an early departure, a good pair of shoes for the Dujiangyan riverbank paths and about ten hours from the city centre.

Is Sanxingdui worth half a day rather than a full one?

Half a day is enough if you are selective. The excavation pits and the new bronze hall together take two hours, and the older site mound with its open trenches adds another 45 minutes. The site is only 40 kilometres from central Chengdu, roughly an hour by road, so the remaining hours are better spent at the museum complex than in the car. Allow more time if the bronzes are a serious interest for you, since the gallery is arranged by excavation phase and rewards unhurried reading.

What is the hardest part of the Qingcheng climb?

The approach through the front mountain, not the summit ridge. The main gate sits at about 1,000 metres and the summit is 1,600 metres, so the gain is short but concentrated, and the stairs between the mid-level temples and Laojun Pavilion are stepped and narrow. Taking the cable car or the shuttle to the middle of the mountain cuts the climb to roughly 800 metres of ascent over about six kilometres of walking, which is comfortable for most visitors in a single morning.

Is Huanglongxi suitable for a short trip with children?

It suits a half-day visit well because the old town is flat, compact and 40 kilometres from the centre, so an hour of driving covers the round trip. The seven streets and nine lanes are short, the three temples sit on the main street, and the banyan trees give shade in summer. The rain-softened river paths and the crowd control during the dragon boat and lantern festivals are the two points to check before setting out with a group.

Do these trips work in winter?

Dujiangyan, Sanxingdui, Huanglongxi and the Qingcheng front mountain all work in winter and are at their quietest then. Leshan is also fine, though the riverside climb is exposed to wind. Xiling changes character completely: January to March is the ski season, and the summit snowfield trail is only reliably clear of snow from April to early June, so the same trip becomes a winter sports outing rather than a ridge walk.
