Overview: Photography in Nanchang

Nanchang photographs along the Gan river, where a tower rebuilt in 1989 on a plan drawn by Liang Sicheng stands on a platform marking the line of a vanished city wall, and inland, where a 1927 hotel that served as a revolutionary headquarters and a brick pagoda of the Tang period stand within a few hundred metres of each other. These seven locations work in a single day, and each of them has documented dimensions, dates and protection status.

Tengwang Pavilion (滕王阁)

The present Pavilion of Prince Teng was completed on 8 October 1989 on the site of a tower first raised in 653 AD by Li Yuanying as governor of Hongzhou, and destroyed or rebuilt 29 times over the centuries since. The architect Liang Sicheng supplied the design; the building is reinforced concrete finished in Song dynasty style, 57.5 metres tall, nine storeys, with a floor area of 13,000 square metres. It stands on a 12-metre concrete platform marking the line of the old city wall, on the east bank of the Gan river.

best light: Late afternoon, when the sun crosses the Gan river from the west and the stacked eaves of the nine storeys separate into readable layers. · tower / landmark

Getting there: In the north-west of the city centre in Xihu district, on the east bank of the Gan river, reached by metro and by local buses on the riverside road; the garden around the building is entered from the road above.

  • The concrete platform is 12 metres high, so the ground-level and terrace views are not interchangeable; the terrace is the one that shows all nine storeys.
  • The structure is concrete behind Song style decoration, so the eaves are heavy and the shadow lines are deeper than a timber building gives.
  • The Mao Zedong tablet is lit separately from the facade, so meter for the tablet if it is in frame rather than for the whole wall.

Bayi Nanchang Uprising Memorial Hall (八一南昌起义纪念馆)

The museum was founded in 1956 on the site of the old uprising headquarters and opened on 1 October 1959, and it holds the title of a national first-class museum. The main building was the Jiangxi Grand Hotel, built in 1923: five storeys of grey brick and timber, 26 metres wide and 42 metres deep, 23.5 metres high, with a courtyard plan and 96 rooms. The old sites were listed among the first group of national key cultural relics in 1961. The site was repaired in 2007 and opened free of charge from 28 January 2008, with 1.528 million visitors that year and 2.035 million in 2009.

best light: Morning, when the grey brick of the former hotel is lit flat and the courtyard reads by proportion rather than by texture. · revolutionary history museum

Getting there: At 380 Zhongshan Road in Xihu district, in the Guangrunmen area of the old city, a short walk from the Wanshou Palace and from the Jiangxi Grand Hotel's original block.

  • The plan is a loop around a courtyard, so the interiors are lit from above and a slower shutter is needed than outside.
  • The 2007 restoration used flat grey brick with new mortar; in raking light the difference between old and rebuilt fabric shows along the window heads.
  • Free entry since 2008 means the hall fills in the morning; the courtyard frames are emptiest in the first hour after opening.

Shengjin Pagoda (绳金塔)

Shengjin Pagoda stands at 165 Shengjin Tower Street in Xihu district, just inside the old Jinxian gate of the former city wall, and is an eight-sided, seven-storey pavilion-style brick pagoda. It was founded in the Tang dynasty in the Tianyou era, between 904 and 907 AD, and the name comes from an iron coffer, four lengths of gold rope, three swords and 300 relics said to have been uncovered when it was built. It collapsed in the 48th year of the Kangxi reign and was rebuilt in the 52nd, with completion the following year. The most recent large-scale repair was carried out in 2000.

best light: The hour before sunrise in autumn, when the eight-sided outline separates from the sky and the brick faces stay in shadow. · brick pagoda

Getting there: In Xihu district in the old city, on the east side of Shengjin Tower Street, within the street that carries the pagoda's name and close to the Bayi Square metro and bus interchange.

  • Seven brick storeys on an octagonal plan compress with distance, so a long lens is better than a wide one for the upper tiers.
  • The pagoda stands among later buildings of similar height, and the separation between old tower and new block is easiest to see in the first light.
  • Autumn evenings carry a local lantern event on the site, which adds light sources the structure was never designed for.

Wanshou Palace and Wanshou Palace Historic District (万寿宫)

The Taoist temple of Wanshou Palace, also called Miaoji Wanshou Palace and popularly known as the Iron Pillar Palace, was founded in the sixth year of the Yongjia era, 312 AD, for Xu Jingyang, ancestor of the Jingming school. It stood on Cuihua Street inside the Guangrunmen gate, in what became one of Nanchang's busiest trading quarters, and was burned completely during the Cultural Revolution. A replica was raised on the shore of Xianghu lake in 2000, and in 2013 a decision was taken to rebuild the temple on the original plot, with the old quarter remade as the Wanshou Palace historic district; it opened on 6 February 2021.

best light: Blue hour, when the gateway arch, the timber hall fronts and the shop signs in the historic district come up together against a still-coloured sky. · Taoist temple and rebuilt street district

Getting there: At the former Cuihua Street site inside Guangrunmen in Xihu district, in the old city centre between the uprising memorial hall and the Wanshou Palace shopping streets.

  • The 2021 opening drew enough footfall that the temple was suspended on 11 February, so check the current status before a long morning.
  • The district is a working commercial quarter, so shopfronts and signage dominate after dark rather than the temple front.
  • The original gate had stone lions six to seven chi high and a paved approach; the reconstruction follows that pattern rather than a free design.

Bada Shanren Memorial Hall, Qingyunpu (八大山人纪念馆)

The memorial hall occupies Qingyunpu, a Taoist abbey with a recorded history of about 2,500 years, set in the Meihu scenic area at 259 Qingyunpu Road in the Qingyunpu district. The painter Bada Shanren, born Zhu Da in Nanchang in 1626, lived in retreat here. The complex was protected as a provincial cultural relic in 1957 and listed among the national key cultural relics in 2006. The museum founded in 1959 was the first memorial museum for an ancient painter in the country, and between 2009 and 2010 the old buildings were repaired, the displays renewed and new gallery space added.

best light: Soft light through the garden canopy in the late morning, when the whitewashed walls and the pond edges hold detail without hard shadow. · artist memorial museum in a historic garden

Getting there: In the Mehu scenic area of Qingyunpu district on the south side of the city, reached by bus and by taxi from the old city; the garden is entered through the museum gate on Qingyunpu Road.

  • The complex is a Taoist precinct with a Jiangnan garden layout, so the axis runs along water and pavilions rather than along a street.
  • Paintings in the collection are shown under controlled light; photograph the garden and the architecture, and treat the galleries as a separate set of frames.
  • The pond and the lotus pool at the old calligraphy basin give reflections that hold after the trees have filled in.

Bayi Uprising Monument Tower (八一南昌起义纪念塔)

The tower at the southern end of Bayi Square was approved as a project in 1957 for the thirtieth anniversary of the uprising, with the site fixed at the old Fushan Square, but construction did not begin. Work started on 1 August 1977 at what was then People's Square, renamed Bayi Square for the occasion, and the tower was completed on 8 January 1979. It stands 45.5 metres high, and an extension carried out between 2001 and 2004 added 8.1 metres, giving 53.6 metres. Built in four parts, it carries three granite relief panels on its eastern, southern and western faces.

best light: Midday is acceptable for the granite reliefs; the better light is early morning, when the shaft is lit from the east side and the relief panels stay readable. · monument

Getting there: At the southern end of Bayi Square on Bayi Avenue in Xihu district, on the main north-south axis of the city and directly served by the Bayi Square metro and bus station.

  • The relief panels face three directions, so no single frontal position covers all of them; walk a quarter turn between frames.
  • The crown is a granite Hanyang rifle together with the August 1 military flag, and both are small details that need a long lens.
  • The 2001 to 2004 extension means the proportions differ from the 1979 photographs; the older proportions are visible in the shaft joint.

Youmin Temple (佑民寺)

Youmin Temple lies north of Bayi Park on the east shore of Donghu lake, and was founded during the Tianjian era of the Liang dynasty, 503 AD, on a house donated to the temple. The founding story records a well beside the site where a dragon was said to stir, and a large Buddha image was cast to settle it, giving the temple the name Dafosi; it was renamed Kaiyuan Temple in the Tang dynasty and rebuilt seven times under other names. It is a working temple rather than a museum, with a monastic layout of halls and side courtyards set against the lake.

best light: Early morning, when the incense smoke from the side halls thins and the courtyard water surfaces clear. · historic temple

Getting there: North of Bayi Park on the east side of Donghu lake, reached by bus along the lake road from the city centre, in the Xihu district of the old city.

  • It is an active monastery, so approach the halls quietly and keep the working quarters out of the frame.
  • The lake side gives a wider view of the roofs and the pagoda line, while the entrance court is the tighter architectural subject.
  • Morning light reaches the courtyards before the surrounding blocks, which is what keeps the sightlines open.

Camera gear for Nanchang

A long lens is the more useful tool in this city than a wide one. Tengwang Pavilion is a nine-storey stack that needs distance to read as a stack, and the Song style rooflines separate cleanly when compressed. The Bayi Uprising memorial hall and Shengjin Pagoda both sit in dense low-rise fabric, where a 70 to 200 millimetre range isolates a single structure from the buildings behind it. A polariser earns its place on the pond at Youmin Temple, the lotus basin at Qingyunpu and the wet stone of the old city approach to the pagoda, all of which give back a sheen that flattens contrast.

Exposure is decided by the mix of surfaces rather than by the scene. The pavilion is bright concrete and white detail in the middle of the day, so metering for the highlights and pulling down a stop keeps the roof edges from clipping, while the grey brick of the 1923 hotel and the red granite of the monument tower both hold shadow detail that a wide exposure will lose. Nanchang is a humid inland city with a summer monsoon, so expect flat light after rain and clear air in autumn, when the light comes low from the east across the Gan river valley and the river surface holds the pavilion without wind.

Frequently Asked Questions

How old is the pavilion in Nanchang really?

The site is nearly 1,400 years old. The tower was first built in 653 AD by Li Yuanying and has been destroyed or rebuilt 29 times since. The building that stands there now was completed on 8 October 1989 to a design by the architect Liang Sicheng, is 57.5 metres tall, has nine storeys and covers 13,000 square metres of floor space. The reconstruction works from Song dynasty forms, so the building reads as a historical style rather than a copy of any single earlier state.

Why is the tower standing on a concrete platform?

The platform is 12 metres high and stands for the old city wall that used to run along that line, which no longer survives. It is the reason the pavilion appears higher above the river bank than any surviving structure nearby, and it gives the building a level terrace that the 1989 garden was laid out around.

Is the uprising memorial hall the original building?

Yes in outline and in fabric, but it was repaired at large scale in 2007 before it opened free to the public on 28 January 2008. The building it occupies was the Jiangxi Grand Hotel, built in 1923, five storeys of grey brick and timber, 26 metres wide and 42 metres deep with 96 rooms around a courtyard. The old sites of the general headquarters, of the He Long, Ye Ting and Zhu De headquarters and of the Zhu De officers' school were listed among the first group of national key cultural relics in 1961.

What is the oldest structure in this list?

Shengjin Pagoda, which was founded in the Tang dynasty in the Tianyou era, between 904 and 907 AD, as an eight-sided, seven-storey brick pavilion. It fell in the 48th year of the Kangxi reign, was rebuilt in the 52nd year and finished the year after that, and had its most recent large restoration in 2000. It is counted among the best preserved ancient buildings in Nanchang.

Which of these sites needs the least time?

The monument tower and the memorial hall are both in Bayi Square, within a few hundred metres of each other, and each can be worked in under an hour. The pavilion complex is the largest but is a single structure with a defined terrace, so the time goes into waiting for the light rather than into moving between subjects.

What is the best light in Nanchang?

Early morning for the old city, because the sun rises over the hills east of the city and reaches the low streets first, and late afternoon for the pavilion, which faces the river bank from the north-west of the centre. The tower at Bayi Square is lit from the east side in the morning, which leaves the three granite relief panels readable, and the Wanshou Palace district is a blue-hour subject, with the gateway arch and the shop signs lit against a coloured sky.
