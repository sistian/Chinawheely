Overview: Day Trips from Changsha

Changsha is a river city first. The Xiang runs through it, and the long sandbar in the middle of the river is where the city takes its own name, from the sand that made the crossing shallow. Most of what is worth a day is inside the ring road: an academy on the western bank that has been teaching since 976, three Western Han tombs whose contents are now in the provincial museum a few kilometres away, and a kiln site that invented a decorating technique. Further out are the memorial towns and the imperial tomb in the hills, which are the two places a day trip usually means.

Orange Isle in the Xiang (橘子洲)

The sandbar sits in the middle of the Xiang in the Yuelu district, running about 5 kilometres north to south and narrowing from 320 metres at the widest to 170 metres at the narrowest, and it is also called Jizhou, Juzhou, Shuilluzhou or Shuizhou depending on the text. It formed in 305, in the second year of the Yongxing reign of the Jin emperor Hui, when the river dropped a long bank of sand, and one account of the city's name connects the two directly. Tang dynasty oranges grew here in quantity and sold across the Jianghan region, and Du Fu wrote of the fat soil of Jizhou. The Qing county gazetteer divided the bar into three parts, Niutou, Shuiliu and Fujia, which historically appeared joined only at very low water, giving rise to the saying that when the three isles connect, a zhuangyuan is born. The river view here is one of the eight scenes of Xiaoxiang, the evening snow on the river and the sky.

How the day runs: The bar is 5 kilometres long, so the sensible approach is to enter at one end and walk out at the other rather than double back. Start at the southern end, where the southern bank and the head of the sandbar meet, and walk north along the centre line, which takes about 50 minutes at a normal pace with stops. The stone terrace at the northern end and the riverside avenue are the two places to pause. Cross back by the bridge at the far end and take the car, which takes 15 minutes each way. Finish with the Hua Ting pavilion and the Yuelu Academy on the west bank if the afternoon is free.

5 km from Changsha · about 15 min by car from Changsha · half-day trip · best in October to November for the citrus that gave the sandbar its name; spring and autumn for the light on the river.

  • The bar is bare in the middle and wooded at the ends, so the light and the shade both change along the walk.
  • Bicycles are hired on the island and the length of it suits a bicycle better than it suits a car.
  • The far end is a long walk from the parking; use the bridge at the north end to return rather than retracing the sandbar.

Yuelu Mountain and the Academy (岳麓山与岳麓书院)

The mountain occupies 35.2 square kilometres on the west bank of the Xiang, and its highest point, Yunlu Peak, reaches 300.8 metres and is counted among the seventy-two peaks of Nanyue. The Southern and Northern Dynasties text of the Nan Yue Record describes the range as a foot of the mountain whose head is Huiyan, and the site has held four institutions at once: the Confucian Yuelu Academy, the Buddhist first patriarchy of Hunan at Lushan Temple, the twenty-third Daoist blessed land, and the burial ground of revolutionary martyrs. The temple dates from 268, and Li Yong wrote its stele in 730. The academy was founded in 976 by the governor of Tanzhou, Zhu Dong, on the site of a monastic school, entered its northern Song peak between 1008 and 1017, and in 1015 Emperor Zhenzong personally summoned its head, Zhou Shi. It became Hunan Higher Academy in 1903 and is still a teaching institution of Hunan University. It was listed as a third-batch national key cultural relic in 1988 and the range became a national key scenic area on 17 May 2002, rated 5A in January 2012.

How the day runs: Enter from the east gate by the academy at the foot of the hill and visit the academy first, before the coach groups reach it, allowing 45 minutes for the lecture hall, the garden and the stele. Then walk up through the Aiwan Pavilion and past Lushan Temple to Yunlu Palace, which takes about an hour and a half for the round trip. The cable car saves the steepest section if the day is hot. Come back down by the road on the west side of the mountain, which is also the route for the Xinhai Society's former site and the memorials to the martyrs of the 1911 revolution and the war against Japan.

8 km from Changsha · about 25 min by car from Changsha · half-day trip · best in All year, with autumn colour on the paths in late October and November.

  • The academy and the Aiwan Pavilion are on opposite sides of the summit loop, so the walk between them through Lushan Temple is the scenic part.
  • The stele of Lushan Temple from 730 is the oldest inscription on the mountain and is easy to walk past.
  • The New People's Study Society site at the west side explains the academy through the twentieth century and is usually the quieter half of the visit.

Mawangdui and the Hunan Provincial Museum (马王堆汉墓与湖南省博物馆)

Three Western Han tombs at Mawangdui, in the Furong district beside the Liuyang river, held the chancellor of the Changsha kingdom, Li Cang, his wife Xin Zhui and his son or brother Li Xi. The first tomb was opened in December 1971 and the other two between 1973 and 1974, and the excavation produced the body of Xin Zhui along with a large body of grave goods, which in 2009 was named one of the ten most important archaeological discoveries of Changsha in recent years. The site was listed as a seventh-batch national key cultural relic on 3 May 2013, and the finds have all moved into the Hunan Provincial Museum. The museum covers 49,000 square metres, was founded in March 1951 and opened in July 1956, added the Mawangdui gallery in July 1974 and a new building in January 2003, was named one of the first batch of national first-class museums in May 2008, closed for rebuilding in June 2012 and reopened in November 2017. It holds more than 180,000 objects, among them the silk painting, the plain gauze robe and the silk manuscripts from tomb one.

How the day runs: Go to the museum first, in the morning, since the Mawangdui gallery is the reason to come and it fills with groups by mid-morning. Allow two hours for the tomb display and the reconstructed pit area, then take the short drive to the Mawangdui site itself, where the mound and the three tomb markers are marked out on the ground. The whole thing fits inside a half-day because both are in the north of the city, about 20 minutes apart.

6 km from Changsha · about 20 min by car from Changsha · half-day trip · best in All year; the museum is indoors and works as the wet-season answer.

  • The well-preserved body and the garments are shown in separate galleries; the textile section explains the weave, which is the part visitors miss.
  • The museum closed for rebuilding between 2012 and 2017, so older photographs of the displays do not match the current layout.
  • The site and the museum together tell one story; the museum alone leaves the scale of the mounds unexplained.

The Changsha Kiln at Tongguan (铜官窑遗址)

The kiln that history calls Changsha Kiln is properly the Tongguan Kiln, a folk kiln of the Tang and Five Dynasties in the Wangcheng district, at Dingziwan and around Tongguan town on the east bank of the Xiang. It produced celadon and, more unusually, applied colour under the glaze, a technique not seen before in the excavated material and treated as the origin of the underglaze decoration that runs through Song painted ceramics. The kiln site appears in no historical text at all, which is why it took archaeology to establish it, and research has traced it back to the Yuezhou kilns, beginning in the early Tang, peaking in the middle and late Tang and declining from the end of the Five Dynasties. It was listed as a third-batch national key cultural relic in 1988 and was approved as one of the first batch of national archaeological site parks in 2010, opening in 2012. Its wares circulated along the middle and lower Yangtze and were exported to Japan, Korea, Siam, the southern seas and as far as West Asia and Africa.

How the day runs: Drive north for 40 kilometres to the Tongguan area and allow the afternoon. The site is spread over two kiln districts, Tongguan and Shizhu lake, so pick one and do it properly rather than trying to cover both. The kiln mounds and the firing remains are the subject, and the museum section explains the glazes. The trip is best combined with the Liuyang River sites or a drive along the east bank, and it is the least crowded of the six.

40 km from Changsha · about 50 min by car from Changsha · half-day trip · best in All year; the site is open ground with little shade, so spring and autumn are better.

  • The underglaze colour decoration is the technical point of the site, so find the display of the painted wares first.
  • The site is exposed and there is little shelter, so plan the middle hours of the day for the museum section if there is one.
  • The export finds were found across East Asia and West Asia, which explains why the site matters outside Chinese ceramic history.

Shaoshan and Huaminglou (韶山与花明楼)

Shaoshan is a county-level city administered by Xiangtan, with an area of 247 square kilometres, standing where Xiangtan, Ningxiang and Xiangxiang meet. The name comes from a hillock in the north-west of Xiangtan county, named for the legend that the emperor Shun played the Shao music there and phoenixes gathered. Mao Zedong was born there on 26 December 1893 and spent his childhood in the village, and the memorial complex at the site became one of the standard pilgrimages of the twentieth century; the administrative status swung repeatedly, and in December 1990 the State Council approved replacing the Shaoshan district with the county-level city of Shaoshan, administered by Xiangtan. Twenty kilometres away at Huaminglou in Ningxiang is the house where Liu Shaoqi was born, built in 1871 as an ordinary brick-and-timber courtyard house of the Jiangnan type, opened as a museum in 1959 after provincial protection, closed in October 1966 and restored after his rehabilitation in 1980.

How the day runs: Leave Changsha at 07:30 for the 110-kilometre run and reach Shaoshan around 10:00, allowing for the holiday traffic that builds on the approach. The main site takes two hours, and the house and the memorial square are the core of it. Lunch in the town, then drive the 20 kilometres to Huaminglou in Ningxiang for the Liu Shaoqi house, which is a smaller and quieter site and needs an hour and a half. Leave at 16:00 to get back before the evening traffic builds, which puts you in Changsha around 18:30.

110 km from Changsha · about 150 min by car from Changsha · full-day trip · best in All year, with the quietest conditions in the weeks either side of the public holidays.

  • The two sites are 20 kilometres apart and are almost always visited together; the second is much quieter than the first.
  • Both are working memorial museums with their own rules about photography and quiet, and the house at Huaminglou is small enough that a group fills it.
  • The approach roads are the slow part of the day, so leave early rather than adding hours to the visits.

The Yan Emperor Mausoleum at Yanling (炎帝陵)

The Yan Emperor is a legendary ruler rather than a historical one, and there are several claimed tombs of him in China, including the site in Yanling county in Zhuzhou, the one at Baoji in Shaanxi, the one at Gaoping in Shanxi and the one at Shangqiu in Henan. The Yanling tomb is the one recognised by successive central governments, appearing in the Song, Ming and Qing collections of state regulations, with emperors sending officials to make sacrifice there. The Han had a cult at the site, and the temple was founded in the fifth year of the Qiande reign of the Song emperor Taizu, 965, when the location was established and a temple built in front of the tomb with an image kept in it. The Chronicles of the Emperors by Huangfu Mi record that the Yan Emperor reigned 120 years and was buried in Changsha, and the southern Song scholar Luo Mi wrote in his Lu Shi that he was buried at the end of the tea country of Changsha, which is why the place is called Chaling. The site is a national key cultural relic.

How the day runs: Leave Changsha at 08:00 for the 120-kilometre run to Yanling county in Zhuzhou, arriving around 10:30. The complex is a sequence of courtyards at the foot of the mountain with the tomb behind them, so give it two hours, walking rather than rushing. The stele gallery and the temple buildings are the strongest parts, and the sacrificial court explains the continuity of state worship at the site. Lunch in the county town, and return by 15:00, which puts you back in Changsha around 17:30 with time to spare.

120 km from Changsha · about 140 min by car from Changsha · full-day trip · best in October to April; the complex is at the foot of the mountains and holds a festival in the early summer.

  • The identification of this site as the true tomb rests on the Song and later state records rather than on the Han-period evidence, which is the point to understand before visiting.
  • The complex is arranged along one axis, so the walk from the gate to the tomb is the structure of the visit.
  • The name Chaling itself is usually read as marking the burial place of the Yan Emperor at the tail of the tea country of Changsha.

Planning day trips from Changsha

Three of the six destinations are inside the city and need no planning beyond a taxi ride, which is the first thing to understand about Changsha day trips: the river island, the mountain with the academy and the two Han tombs with their museum are all within a few kilometres of each other and can be done in a single day on foot with a car for the gaps. The other three are excursions of 40 to 150 kilometres, and their timings are dominated by road conditions rather than by anything to see.

The second point is the river. The Xiang is the spine of the city and the reason for its name, and the crossings matter: the sandbar in the middle of the river is reached on foot, from either bank, and it is a five-kilometre walk rather than a drive. For anything to the west of the water, the academy and the mountain, and for anything to the east, the kilns and the Hunan Provincial Museum, the same river crossing is the thing to allow time for.

Planning a day trip from Changsha

Orange Isle in the Xiang, The Yan Emperor Mausoleum at Yanling and the 4 stops in between set the two ends of this Changsha day trip route — Orange Isle in the Xiang sits closest at 5 km, roughly 15 minutes by car, while The Yan Emperor Mausoleum at Yanling reaches furthest at 120 km, about 140 minutes on the road — and a private car keeps the whole loop moving at your own pace rather than a timetable's.

Orange Isle in the Xiang works best October to November for the citrus that gave the sandbar its name; spring and autumn for the light on the river., while Yuelu Mountain and the Academy suits All year, with autumn colour on the paths in late October and November. — in Changsha that seasonal difference matters more than the distance between the two, so pick the stop that matches the month you are actually travelling in and let the rest of the day follow from that.

Mawangdui and the Hunan Provincial Museum, The Changsha Kiln at Tongguan, Shaoshan and Huaminglou each carry a seasonal note of their own, so check those against the dates you actually have before you fix the Changsha itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions: Booking in Changsha

Which day trip is the essential one?

For most visitors it is the pair of Yuelu Mountain and the Yuelu Academy, 8 kilometres from the centre, where the academy was founded in 976 and was still one of the four great academies of imperial China, with its hall, garden and stele standing on the mountain that also holds a Buddhist temple founded in 268 and a Daoist site. For the archaeology it is Mawangdui and the museum, three Western Han tombs opened between 1971 and 1974 that produced the body of Xin Zhui and a body of textiles and manuscripts now kept a short drive away.

Why is the river island called Orange Isle?

Because oranges grew on it. The sandbar formed in 305, in the second year of the Yongxing reign, and Tang dynasty mandarins from the bar were sold across the Jianghan region, which Du Fu wrote about. The island has also been called Jizhou, Juzhou and Shuiliuzhou, and the Qing county gazetteer divided it into three sections, Niutou, Shuiliu and Fujia, which historically joined only when the river was very low. It became part of the Yuelu Mountain scenic area in 2001.

Is the Changsha Kiln worth the drive north?

It is the only place in the country where the underglaze colour decoration that runs through later Song painted ceramics can be seen in the place it was developed. The site is at Tongguan in Wangcheng district, 40 kilometres north, and it appears in no dynastic history at all, which is why it took excavation to identify it. It was listed as a third-batch national key cultural relic in 1988 and became a national archaeological site park in 2012, and it was quietly one of the most important ceramics sites in the world.

How far is Shaoshan and can both memorial sites be done in one day?

Yes, and they are normally done together. Shaoshan is a county-level city of 247 square kilometres administered by Xiangtan, and the Liu Shaoqi house at Huaminglou in Ningxiang is 20 kilometres from it, so the pair works as one loop of about 110 kilometres from Changsha. The house is an ordinary Jiangnan courtyard building from 1871, opened as a museum in 1959 and restored after the rehabilitation of 1980. Leave early, because the approach roads rather than the visits set the pace.

What is the Yan Emperor Tomb actually?

One of several claimed tombs of a legendary ruler, and the one recognised by successive central governments from the Song dynasty onwards, with emperors sending officials to make sacrifice. The temple was founded in 965 under the Song, and the Han period tradition records that the Yan Emperor reigned 120 years and was buried in Changsha. The site is in Yanling county in Zhuzhou, about 120 kilometres from the city, and it is a national key cultural relic.

What about the Hunan Provincial Museum?

It holds more than 180,000 objects, including the silk painting, the plain gauze robe and the silk manuscripts from tomb one at Mawangdui, and it was the first building in the province built as a museum for that material. It was founded in 1951, opened in 1956, gained the Mawangdui gallery in 1974 and a new building in 2003, closed for rebuilding in 2012 and reopened in November 2017. The name changed to the Hunan Museum in 2022 and back again in October 2025.
